7.10 Positive solutions and limits of normal graphsA1376C328CF6

A1376C328CF6

This section supplies the analytic steps behind separated-sheet convergence in [ChMin], Section 33, pp. 79–82. We retain Harnack and Schauder estimates as external inputs and prove the normalization, linearization and compactness deductions. The scalar mean-curvature convention is $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $, so its derivative at a minimal hypersurface is $-J$.

Theorem 7.10.1 (Harnack with bounded lower-order coefficients).C122EB493E51 Let $n\geq 1$, $R>0$, and let $A=(a^{ij})$ be symmetric and measurable on $B_{2R}\subset \mathbb R^ n$, with $\lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I$ for $0<\lambda \leq \Lambda $. Let $b,c$ be bounded measurable coefficients. If $u\in H^1(B_{2R})$ is nonnegative and solves

\[ \operatorname {div}(A Du)+b\cdot Du+cu=0 \]

weakly, then

\[ \operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ R}u\leq C\operatorname *{ess\, inf}_{B_ R}u, \]

where $C$ depends on $n,\lambda ,\Lambda ,R\| b\| _\infty ,R^2\| c\| _\infty $. No sign assumption on $c$ is required for this inequality for nonnegative solutions. The Harnack estimate is an external analytic input from [ChMin], Appendix B, p. 87, and [SimonPDE], Chapter 18. The nonnegativity and interior margin, implicit in the shorthand appendix statement, are explicit here.

Lemma 7.10.2 (Compactness after normalizing a positive solution).A22B04D02BB2 Let $\Sigma $ be a connected smooth manifold of dimension $n\geq 1$, exhausted by connected relatively compact open sets $U_ j$, and fix $p\in U_1$. Let $L_ j$ be smooth second-order operators on $U_ j$ whose coefficients converge in $C^\infty $ on every compact subset to those of a locally uniformly elliptic operator $L$. Assume their principal coefficients are symmetric. If $v_ j>0$, $L_ jv_ j=0$, and $v_ j(p)=1$, then a subsequence converges in $C^\infty $ on compact subsets to $v>0$ with $Lv=0$ and $v(p)=1$.

Proof

On a coordinate chart write $L_ j=a_ j^{ab}\partial _{ab}+b_ j^ a\partial _ a+c_ j$. Rewriting its principal part as $\partial _ a(a_ j^{ab}\partial _ b)$ changes the drift to $b_ j^ b-\partial _ a a_ j^{ab}$. Smooth coefficient convergence supplies uniform ellipticity and bounds for this drift and the potential on every fixed compact chart. Theorem 7.10.1 therefore applies uniformly there. Given a compact set $K\subset \Sigma $, use connectedness to join $p$ to finitely many coordinate balls covering $K$ by a finite chain of overlapping smaller balls inside a larger relatively compact open set. For all sufficiently large $j$ these balls lie in $U_ j$. Iterating Harnack along the chain gives $C_ K^{-1}\leq v_ j\leq C_ K$ on $K$. Interior Schauder estimates, including the higher estimates in Theorem 7.5.8, give uniform bounds for all derivatives on smaller charts. Arzela–Ascoli and a diagonal subsequence yield smooth local convergence. The positive lower bounds and normalization pass to the limit, and coefficient convergence gives $Lv=0$. Neither the Harnack iteration proof nor Schauder theory is being reproved here. $\square$

Lemma 7.10.3 (The equation for the gap between minimal normal graphs).B17410FAAB18 Let $\Sigma ^ n$ be a smooth two-sided minimal hypersurface in a smooth Riemannian manifold, with $n\geq 1$. Suppose $u_{1,j},u_{2,j}$ define minimal normal graphs on an exhaustion and converge to zero smoothly on compact subsets. There are linear operators $L_ j$, locally uniformly elliptic for large $j$, such that

\[ L_ j(u_{2,j}-u_{1,j})=0,\qquad L_ j\longrightarrow J=\Delta _\Sigma +|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu ) \]

in the smooth coefficient topology on compact subsets.

Proof

Pull scalar mean curvature of the graph back to $\Sigma $ and denote the resulting operator by $\mathcal H(u)$. In a normal coordinate tube $F(x,s)=\exp _ x(s\nu )$, the induced metric and graph normal depend smoothly on $(x,u,Du)$. Tracing the second fundamental form shows that $\mathcal H$ is quasilinear of order two. More explicitly, its coefficient of $\partial _{ab}u$ is

\[ -g_ u^{ab}\langle \partial _ sF,\nu _ u\rangle . \]

The inner product is positive for small $C^1$ graphs; hence the negative of the linearization is elliptic. The remaining coefficients of the linearization depend smoothly on $(x,u,Du,D^2u)$ and the ambient metric. For $w_ j=u_{2,j}-u_{1,j}$, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives

\[ 0=\mathcal H(u_{2,j})-\mathcal H(u_{1,j}) =\int _0^1 D\mathcal H(u_{1,j}+tw_ j)[w_ j],dt. \]

Set $L_ j=-\int _0^1D\mathcal H(u_{1,j}+tw_ j)\, dt$. On each compact set all interpolating graphs tend smoothly to zero, so the coefficients converge smoothly to $-D\mathcal H(0)=J$ by Proposition 7.6.2. This also gives uniform local ellipticity. This is the operator Taylor expansion in [ChMin], Proposition 33.3, with its sign fixed by the stated scalar convention. $\square$

Corollary 7.10.4 (Comparable sheet gaps on compact subsets).747E58F53F15 Under the hypotheses of Theorem 7.6.5, write $w_ j=u_{2,j}-u_{1,j}>0$. For every compact $K$ in the interior there is $C_ K\geq 1$ such that, for all large $j$,

\[ C_ K^{-1}\leq w_ j(x)/w_ j(p)\leq C_ K\qquad (x\in K). \]

If a normalized subsequence tends to $v$ and $q$ is another interior point, normalization at $q$ instead gives the limit $v/v(q)$.

Proof

Apply the Harnack-chain step of Lemma 7.10.2 to the operators from Lemma 7.10.3. This gives the bounds without a priori control on the rate at which the unnormalized gap tends to zero. For the last assertion divide $w_ j(x)/w_ j(p)$ by $w_ j(q)/w_ j(p)$; the denominator converges to the positive number $v(q)$. $\square$

Proposition 7.10.5 (Positive supersolutions and the quadratic form).8E562438D97E Let $(\Sigma ,g)$ be smooth, let $q$ be smooth, and set $J=\Delta +q$ and $Q(\phi )=\int (|\nabla \phi |^2-q\phi ^2)$. For every smooth $w>0$ and compactly supported smooth interior test $\phi $,

\[ Q(\phi )=\int w^2|\nabla (\phi /w)|^2+\int \frac{-Jw}{w}\phi ^2. \]

In particular $Jw\leq 0$ implies $Q\geq 0$ on such tests. This extends the positive-solution identity used in [ChMin], Proposition 33.4.

Proof

Write $\phi =w\eta $. Expand $|\nabla (w\eta )|^2$ and group the terms other than $w^2|\nabla \eta |^2$ as $\nabla w\cdot \nabla (w\eta ^2)-qw^2\eta ^2$. Their integral is $-\int w\eta ^2(\Delta w+qw)$ by integration by parts. Substitution of $\eta =\phi /w$ proves the identity. Compact support removes boundary terms, and $w$ has a positive minimum on the support, so every expression is well defined. $\square$

Proposition 7.10.6 (A positive Jacobi field on a closed manifold has simple kernel).3A7A095E3DA2 Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, connected and closed, and let $q$ be smooth. If $w>0$ satisfies $(\Delta +q)w=0$, then the smooth kernel of $J=\Delta +q$ consists exactly of the constant multiples of $w$.

Proof

For any $Jf=0$, integration by parts gives $Q(f)=-\int fJf=0$. Proposition 7.10.5, applied on the compact manifold with no boundary, gives $0=\int w^2|\nabla (f/w)|^2$. Thus $f/w$ is constant by connectedness. Conversely every constant multiple of $w$ lies in the kernel. Closedness is essential to this integration argument; no corresponding assertion for all solutions on a noncompact manifold is made. $\square$

Proposition 7.10.7 (Accumulating minimal normal graphs force degeneracy).8A40075C4DD7 Let $\Sigma ^ n$ be a smooth closed two-sided embedded minimal hypersurface, $n\geq 1$. Suppose nonzero smooth functions $u_ j\to 0$ in $C^\infty (\Sigma )$ define minimal normal graphs. Then $J$ has a nonzero smooth kernel element. Consequently, if $\ker J=0$, the zero graph is isolated among minimal normal graphs in the smooth topology. The functions $u_ j$ need not have a common sign. Compare the nullity mechanism in [Sharp17], Theorem 2.3 and its discussion.

Proof

Apply the averaged linearization of Lemma 7.10.3 with $u_{1,j}=0$ and $u_{2,j}=u_ j$, and set $v_ j=u_ j/\| u_ j\| _{C^0}$. Then $L_ jv_ j=0$, $L_ j\to J$ smoothly and $\| v_ j\| _{C^0}=1$. A finite covering of the closed hypersurface and interior Schauder estimates yield uniform bounds in every $C^ k$ norm. A subsequence converges smoothly to $v$ with $Jv=0$ and $\| v\| _{C^0}=1$. If the zero graph were not isolated in the metrizable smooth topology, a sequence of distinct minimal graphs converging smoothly to zero would contradict the trivial-kernel assumption. This is local isolation of normal graphs, not a global finiteness theorem for minimal hypersurfaces. $\square$
