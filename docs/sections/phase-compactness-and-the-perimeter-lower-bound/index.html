12.3 Phase compactness and the perimeter lower boundB25785170C6E

B25785170C6E

The energy bound alone forces two phases and a finite-perimeter interface; no Euler equation is needed. We give the details of [ChAC], Proposition 3.5 and Exercise 3.1, using the BV compactness already established in Theorem 4.2.7. Throughout this section $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ is bounded and open.

Lemma 12.3.1 (Quartic coercivity and concentration at the wells).F41C9F46FACF For every real $s$,

\[ W(s)\geq \frac18|s|^4-\frac14, \qquad \operatorname {dist}(s,\{ -1,1\} )^2\leq 4W(s). \]

Thus if $E_\varepsilon (u;U)\leq C$, then

\[ \int _ U|u|^4\leq 8C\varepsilon +2|U|, \qquad \int _ U\operatorname {dist}(u,\{ -1,1\} )^2\leq 4C\varepsilon . \]

In particular the measure where the distance from the wells exceeds $\delta >0$ is at most $4C\varepsilon /\delta ^2$.

Proof

Subtracting $|s|^4/8-1/4$ from $W(s)$ gives $(s^2-2)^2/8$. Also $4W(s)=(|s|-1)^2(|s|+1)^2\geq (|s|-1)^2$. Integrate and use $\int W(u)\leq C\varepsilon $; Chebyshev's inequality gives the last statement. These are the coercivity and well-concentration estimates used in [ChAC], pp. 8–9. $\square$

Proposition 12.3.2 (Binary phases and perimeter normalization).AC8E360762C1 For a measurable set $E\subset U$, write $u_ E=2\chi _ E-1$. Then $u_ E\in BV(U)$ if and only if $E$ has finite perimeter in $U$, and

\[ Du_ E=2D\chi _ E,\qquad |Du_ E|=2|D\chi _ E|, \qquad D\Phi (u_ E)=c_ WD\chi _ E. \]

For two measurable sets, $\| u_ E-u_ F\| _{L^1(U)}=2|E\triangle F|$.

Proof

Differentiate the affine expressions in distributions; the constant function has zero derivative relative to $U$. Since $\Phi (u_ E)=c_ W\chi _ E-c_ W/2$, the last derivative identity follows. The distance identity is pointwise. This is the two-phase specialization of Example 4.4.12; in particular $|D\Phi (u_ E)|=(c_ W/2)|Du_ E|$, retaining the factor two in [ChAC], Proposition 3.5. $\square$

Theorem 12.3.3 (Compactness of bounded diffuse-interface energy).95CAAB2DF3CE Let $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ and $u_ j\in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U)$ satisfy $\sup _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)<\infty $. A subsequence converges in $L^ p(U)$ for every $1\leq p<4$ to $u_ E=2\chi _ E-1$, where $E$ has finite perimeter in $U$. Along this subsequence $\Phi (u_ j)\to \Phi (u_ E)$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ and

\[ c_ W P(E;U)\leq \liminf _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U). \]

No boundary regularity or boundary trace condition is assumed.

Proof

Lemma 12.3.1 bounds $u_ j$ in $L^4(U)$. The cubic growth of $\Phi $ bounds $\Phi (u_ j)$ in $L^1(U)$, and Lemma 12.2.8 bounds their total variations. BV compactness and a diagonal almost-everywhere extraction give $\Phi (u_ j)\to v$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and almost everywhere. Continuity of $\Phi ^{-1}$ gives $u_ j\to u_0=\Phi ^{-1}(v)$ almost everywhere. Fatou and $\int W(u_ j)\to 0$ show $u_0\in \{ -1,1\} $ almost everywhere.

The $L^4$ bound gives uniform integrability on the finite-measure set $U$. More explicitly, $\int _ A|u_ j|\leq C|A|^{3/4}$, and truncation at a large value makes the remaining tails uniformly small. Dominated convergence for the bounded truncations then gives $L^1(U)$ convergence. Interpolation between this convergence and the uniform $L^4$ bound gives convergence in every $L^ p$, $p<4$.

Write $u_0=u_ E$. Lower semicontinuity of variation applied to $\Phi (u_ j)$ gives $c_ WP(E;U)\leq \liminf _ j\int _ U|D\Phi (u_ j)|\leq \liminf _ jE_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)$. Here the lower-semicontinuity theorem permits local $L^1$ convergence even when the variation is measured on all of $U$. The finite volume gives $u_ E\in L^1(U)$, so $E$ has finite relative perimeter. This fills out [ChAC], Proposition 3.5; quartic coercivity upgrades the local convergence there to global convergence on bounded $U$. $\square$

Theorem 12.3.4 (The perimeter lower bound for a prescribed limit).D022437E926D Suppose $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$ and $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1(U)$. Then

\[ \liminf _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\geq \begin{cases} c_ WP(E;U),& u=2\chi _ E-1\text{ a.e.},\ E\text{ of finite perimeter},\\ +\infty ,& \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

If the energies are uniformly bounded and $u=u_ E$, the localized bound $c_ WP(E;A)\leq \liminf _ j E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;A)$ holds for every open $A\subset U$.

Proof

If the liminf is finite, select a subsequence attaining it with bounded energy and apply Theorem 12.3.3. Its phase limit must be the prescribed $L^1$ limit. This proves the global assertion, including infinite perimeter. For any fixed open $A$, restrict the sequence to $A$ and repeat the same argument for its energy liminf; $A$ is bounded and the prescribed convergence restricts to it. The subsequence used to prove the inequality may depend on $A$, but the inequality concerns the original sequence. This is the liminf conclusion of [ChAC], Proposition 3.5. $\square$

Proposition 12.3.5 (The limiting energy measure dominates perimeter).0AB8B0DD0379 Assume the hypotheses of Theorem 12.3.3, with $u_ j\to u_ E$ in $L^1(U)$. If a subsequence of the energy measures converges weakly on compactly supported continuous tests in $U$ to $\mu $, then

\[ \mu \geq c_ W|D\chi _ E| \]

as Radon measures on $U$.

Proof

Pass further to the primitive-convergent subsequence in Theorem 12.3.3; this preserves $\mu $ and $u_ E$. For $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ n)$, integration by parts and the pointwise energy inequality give

\[ \left|\int _ U\Phi (u_ j)\operatorname {div}X\right| \leq \int _ U|X|\, d\mu _{\varepsilon _ j}^{u_ j}. \]

Pass to the limit. The resulting bound says that the vector measure $D\Phi (u_ E)$ has total variation at most $\mu $, by the dual characterization of total variation with localized test fields. Proposition 12.3.2 identifies this variation as $c_ W|D\chi _ E|$. Equality is an additional assertion and need not hold. $\square$

Corollary 12.3.6 (Energy convergence forces equipartition).194D96DE584A Suppose $u_ j\to u_ E$ in $L^1(U)$, $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, and $E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\to c_ WP(E;U)<\infty $. Then

\[ \int _ U|D\Phi (u_ j)|\to c_ WP(E;U),\qquad |\xi _{\varepsilon _ j}^{u_ j}|(U)\to 0. \]

Each of the gradient and potential energies tends to $c_ WP(E;U)/2$.

Proof

For every subsequence, bounded energy gives a further subsequence whose primitives converge locally to $\Phi (u_ E)$. Lower semicontinuity gives the lower bound $c_ WP(E;U)$ for their variations, while the energy inequality gives the matching upper bound. The subsequence criterion therefore gives convergence along the whole sequence. Its energy defect tends to zero. Proposition 12.2.11 makes the total variation of discrepancy tend to zero. Since the sum of the two energies converges to $c_ WP(E;U)$ and their difference tends to zero, each converges to half the sum. $\square$

Example 12.3.7 (Two disappearing layers can retain energy).D80927740693 Let $U=(-1,1)$, $a_\varepsilon =\sqrt\varepsilon $, and

\[ u_\varepsilon (x)=q\left(\frac{a_\varepsilon -|x|}\varepsilon \right), \qquad q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2). \]

These $H^1$ functions converge to $-1$ in $L^1(U)$, but their energy measures converge to $2c_ W\delta _0$. Indeed, on each half interval the change of variables $t=(a_\varepsilon -|x|)/\varepsilon $ gives energy tending to $\int _{\mathbb R}(q')^2=c_ W$. Outside any neighborhood of zero the energy tends to zero by exponential decay. The positive-phase interval has length $2a_\varepsilon \to 0$, and pointwise convergence holds away from zero, proving the phase convergence by bounded convergence. The limiting phase has perimeter zero. These are competitors, not claimed solutions of the Euler equation; the example explains why a phase limit alone does not record all interface multiplicity.
