9.2 Multiplicity and the Morse indexD58BB7C941EF

D58BB7C941EF

The coefficients of a sweepout do not determine the multiplicities of its varifold limit. Index estimates require their own hypotheses in addition to the existence and regularity theory.

Definition 9.2.1 (Bumpy metrics and the index of a minimal cycle).BE8BE104CF66 A smooth metric is bumpy if no smooth closed immersed minimal hypersurface admits a nonzero normal Jacobi field. For an embedded minimal cycle $V=\sum _ j m_ j|\Sigma _ j|$ with distinct disjoint components, define $\operatorname {index}(V)=\sum _ j\operatorname {index}(\Sigma _ j)$; the integers $m_ j$ do not multiply the index. Multiplicity one means that every $m_ j$ is one. This convention is used in [MNIndex], Section 1. A disconnected hypersurface is allowed.

Theorem 9.2.2 (Index equality under a critical-set multiplicity hypothesis).5CC6B5664356 Let $g$ be a smooth bumpy metric on a closed connected $M^{n+1}$ with $2\leq n\leq 6$. Use the $\mathbf F$-continuous sweepout convention of [MNIndex]. Let $\Pi $ be a homotopy class of $p$-sweepouts on a fixed finite $p$-dimensional simplicial complex, with $\mathbf L(\Pi )=\omega _ p(M,g)$, and let $\Phi _ i$ be a minimizing sequence in $\Pi $. Suppose that every embedded minimal cycle in its critical set has multiplicity one. Then that critical set contains an embedded minimal cycle $\Sigma $ satisfying

\[ \operatorname {Area}_ g(\Sigma )=\omega _ p(M,g),\qquad \operatorname {index}(\Sigma )=p. \]

Source: [MNIndex], Main Theorem 1.3, p. 5. The proof remains external. The multiplicity assumption concerns the whole indicated critical set, not merely one selected element; this theorem does not by itself prove multiplicity one.
