2.2 Hausdorff measure and density2E7A24F3D4E0

2E7A24F3D4E0

Hausdorff measure gives a notion of $m$-dimensional size before any tangent plane is known. We use the normalization in [Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 2.

Definition 2.2.1 (Normalized Hausdorff measure).04D5BCC54A0E Let $m\geq 1$ be an integer and $A\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$. For $\delta >0$ define

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m_\delta (A)=\inf \left\{ \sum _{i=1}^{\infty }\omega _ m\left(\frac{\operatorname {diam}E_ i}{2}\right)^ m: A\subset \bigcup _ iE_ i,\quad \operatorname {diam}E_ i<\delta \right\} , \qquad \mathcal{H}^ m(A)=\lim _{\delta \downarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^ m_\delta (A). \]

The empty cover gives value zero for $A=\varnothing $. The limit exists in $[0,\infty ]$ by monotonicity as $\delta $ decreases. We set $\mathcal{H}^0$ equal to counting measure. These are Borel regular outer measures. With this normalization $\mathcal{H}^ m=\mathcal{L}^ m$ on $\mathbb {R}^ m$; that identification is a cited result, not part of the definition. See [Sim18], Chapter 1, equations (2.1)–(2.5) and Theorem 2.6, pp. 11–14, and [Fed69], Section 2.10.2(1), p. 171.

Proposition 2.2.2 (Lipschitz distortion of Hausdorff measure).2921BEDC2A31 Let $A\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$, let $f:A\to \mathbb {R}^ q$ be $L$-Lipschitz with $0<L<\infty $, and let $m\geq 1$. Then

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m(f(A))\leq L^ m\mathcal{H}^ m(A). \]

In particular, Lipschitz maps take $\mathcal{H}^ m$-null sets to $\mathcal{H}^ m$-null sets. A constant map also has this null-set property. This follows directly from the covering definition in [Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 2.

Proof

Intersect each set in a $\delta $-cover of $A$ with $A$ and take its image under $f$. The image diameters are at most $L$ times the original diameters. Thus

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m_{L\delta }(f(A))\leq L^ m\mathcal{H}^ m_\delta (A). \]

Let $\delta \downarrow 0$. For a constant map the image is empty or a singleton, whose $\mathcal{H}^ m$ measure is zero when $m\geq 1$. $\square$

Definition 2.2.3 (Density).B88FEB8AC934 For a Radon measure $\mu $ on $U$, a point $x\in U$, and $m\geq 1$, define

\[ \Theta ^{*m}(\mu ,x)=\limsup _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ m r^ m}, \qquad \Theta _*^ m(\mu ,x)=\liminf _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ m r^ m}. \]

When these agree, their common value is the density $\Theta ^ m(\mu ,x)$. The values may be infinite. For $\mu =\mathcal{H}^ m\llcorner M$ we also write $\Theta ^ m(M,x)$. See [Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 3, p. 14.

Example 2.2.4 (A plane with multiplicity).F2FA6522C7DE Let $P$ be an affine $m$-plane, let $\theta >0$, and set $\mu =\theta \mathcal{H}^ m\llcorner P$. For every $x\in P$ and $r>0$,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))=\theta \omega _ mr^ m,\qquad \Theta ^ m(\mu ,x)=\theta . \]

Indeed, a Euclidean isometry identifies $P\cap B_ r(x)$ with an $m$-dimensional ball, and the normalized Hausdorff measure agrees with Lebesgue measure on that plane. This model fixes the normalization used for tangent planes and varifolds below.
