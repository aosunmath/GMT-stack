14.5 Rescaled flow and self-similar models35D4E6FDB765

35D4E6FDB765

Logarithmic time converts a finite-time singularity into a long-time problem. This is a change of variables before it is a compactness theorem. We retain the scale-one shrinker convention $\vec H+x^\perp /2=0$ throughout.

Proposition 14.5.1 (Logarithmic rescaling at a finite time).F139BDB06172 Fix a point $x_0$ and a time $T$ later than the times under consideration. Set

\[ \tau =-\log (T-t),\qquad \widetilde F(p,\tau )=(T-t)^{-1/2}(F(p,t)-x_0). \]

Then the normal velocity of the rescaled hypersurfaces is

\[ (\partial _\tau \widetilde F)^\perp = \vec H_{\widetilde\Sigma _\tau }+\tfrac 12\widetilde F^\perp . \]

If the original flow is Type I at $T$, its rescaled curvature is uniformly bounded. This last assertion supplies a curvature bound, not a claim of convergence or uniqueness of a limiting shrinker.

Proof

Since $dt/d\tau =T-t$ and $(T-t)^{-1/2}=e^{\tau /2}$, differentiation yields $\partial _\tau \widetilde F=\widetilde F/2+(T-t)^{1/2}\vec H$. The second term is the mean curvature vector of the dilated hypersurface. Project normally to get the equation. Proposition 14.3.12 gives $|\widetilde A|^2=(T-t)|A|^2$, proving the curvature assertion. Huisken [Hui90], Section 2, uses instead $(2(T-t))^{-1/2}$ and $-\frac12\log (T-t)$; his rescaled equation has coefficient one instead of $1/2$. $\square$

Proposition 14.5.2 (Gaussian area dissipates along rescaled flow).714B0C7286EF For a smooth closed rescaled flow satisfying Proposition 14.5.1,

\[ \frac{d}{d\tau }F_{0,1}(\widetilde\Sigma _\tau ) =-(4\pi )^{-n/2}\int _{\widetilde\Sigma _\tau } \left|\vec H+\tfrac 12x^\perp \right|^2e^{-|x|^2/4}\, d\mu . \]

Consequently, if the rescaled flow exists for all $\tau \geq \tau _0$, then the time integral of the nonnegative expression on the right, with its minus sign removed, is finite.

Proof

Insert the rescaled normal velocity in Proposition 14.2.4. Tangential velocity contributes only a divergence. Integrate in $\tau $ and use $F_{0,1}\geq 0$. This is [Hui90], Corollary 3.2, after converting the Gaussian normalization. $\square$

Corollary 14.5.3 (Times with small Gaussian dissipation).E1D33EF33BA1 Under the long-time hypothesis in Proposition 14.5.2, there are $\tau _ j\to \infty $ such that

\[ \int _{\widetilde\Sigma _{\tau _ j}} \left|\vec H+\tfrac 12x^\perp \right|^2e^{-|x|^2/4}\, d\mu \longrightarrow 0. \]

If, in addition, these slices converge smoothly with multiplicity one on compact subsets to a smooth hypersurface $\Sigma _\infty $, then $\Sigma _\infty $ satisfies the self-shrinker equation. The compactness assumption is separate from the dissipation conclusion.

Proof

A nonnegative integrable function on $[\tau _0,\infty )$ has a sequence of times tending to infinity at which it tends to zero. For the second assertion, multiply the integrand by a nonnegative compactly supported ambient cutoff. Smooth convergence permits passage to the limit in this localized integral, which is bounded above by the full dissipation. The limit integral is zero for every cutoff; continuity and the positive Gaussian weight force $\vec H+x^\perp /2=0$ on $\Sigma _\infty $. No assertion about mass escaping to infinity or uniqueness follows from this local argument. Compare [WhiMCF15], Section 6. $\square$

Proposition 14.5.4 (Equality in monotonicity gives self-similar motion).5900E7823B75 Suppose a smooth closed flow has constant Gaussian integral centered at $(x_0,T)$ on a time interval $(a,b)\subset (-\infty ,T)$. Then throughout that interval

\[ \vec H+\frac{(x-x_0)^\perp }{2(T-t)}=0. \]

Equivalently, the images $(T-t)^{-1/2}(\Sigma _ t-x_0)$ are independent of time and form a self-shrinker in the project's scale-one normalization.

Proof

Theorem 14.4.3 gives zero integral of a continuous nonnegative function at each time, and hence the displayed pointwise equation. By Proposition 14.5.1, the rescaled velocity is tangent everywhere. A time-dependent tangential vector field on the compact parameter manifold can be removed by reparametrization, so the rescaled images are constant. The equation then reads $\vec H+x^\perp /2=0$. Conversely that equation makes the monotonicity integrand zero. $\square$

Definition 14.5.5 (Translators and self-expanders).2B1C5447089D A smooth hypersurface is a translator with velocity $v\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ if $\vec H=v^\perp $. Its translates $\Sigma _ t=\Sigma +tv$ have normal velocity $\vec H$. A scale-one self-expander satisfies $\vec H-x^\perp /2=0$; its expanding family is $\Sigma _ t=\sqrt t\, \Sigma $, $t>0$. These are exact solution classes, not automatic classifications of Type II singularities. Compare the translating models discussed in [HP99], Section 4, and [WhiMCF15], Section 9.3.

Example 14.5.6 (The grim reaper and its cylindrical products).652E4AB4233E The graphs

\[ u(x,t)=-\log (\cos x)+t,\qquad -\pi /2<x<\pi /2, \]

give a translating curve-shortening flow. Indeed $u_ t=1$, $u_ x=\tan x$, and $u_{xx}=\sec ^2x$, so $u_ t=u_{xx}/(1+u_ x^2)$. Taking the product with $\mathbb R^{n-1}$ gives a translating hypersurface flow in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ with the same scalar graphical equation. Its slope is unbounded near the boundary of the graph domain, even though the complete translating hypersurface has no boundary there. The intrinsic length to either end diverges because $\sqrt{1+u_ x^2}=1/\cos x$. Compare the translator discussion in [HP99], Section 4.
