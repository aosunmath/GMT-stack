4.3 Perimeter blow-ups and the direct method69083B262DD9

69083B262DD9

Compactness and lower semicontinuity establish existence before smoothness is known. The reduced-boundary blow-up identifies the local geometric object, but by itself does not make every topological boundary point regular. In this section $n\geq 2$.

Definition 4.3.1 (Measure-theoretic interior and exterior).A0CDC4480E37 For a measurable set $E\subset \mathbb R^ n$, define

\[ E^{(1)}=\left\{ x:\lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|E\cap B_ r(x)|}{|B_ r(x)|}=1\right\} , \qquad E^{(0)}=\left\{ x:\lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|E\cap B_ r(x)|}{|B_ r(x)|}=0\right\} . \]

The essential boundary is $\partial ^ eE=\mathbb R^ n\setminus (E^{(0)}\cup E^{(1)})$. These definitions are unchanged by a Lebesgue-null modification of $E$. They differ from the topological interior, exterior, and boundary, which depend on the chosen representative. Compare the measure-density framework of [Sim18], Chapters 1 and 3.

Theorem 4.3.2 (Half-space blow-up at a reduced-boundary point).DA28F4FE0B6B Let $E$ have locally finite perimeter in $U$, and let $x\in \partial ^*E\cap U$. With outward normal $\nu _ E(x)$, set $E_{x,r}=(E-x)/r$. As $r\downarrow 0$,

\[ \chi _{E_{x,r}}\longrightarrow \chi _{\{ y:y\cdot \nu _ E(x)<0\} } \quad \text{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n), \]

and the perimeter measures converge locally to $\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner \nu _ E(x)^\perp $. In particular,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|E\cap B_ r(x)|}{\omega _ nr^ n}=\frac12, \qquad \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{P(E;B_ r(x))}{\omega _{n-1}r^{n-1}}=1. \]

This is the blow-up conclusion in the proof of [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, especially equations (9)–(13), pp. 98–100, with the project's outward-normal convention. The substantial blow-up and density argument remains external. The convergence holds as $r\downarrow 0$, not merely for one selected subsequence.

Example 4.3.3 (A null modification can change the whole topological boundary).9BC8B81C327D The set $E=\mathbb Q^ n$ is null and dense, and its complement is also dense. Thus its topological boundary is all of $\mathbb R^ n$, while $\chi _ E=0$ almost everywhere, $P(E;U)=0$, $E^{(0)}=\mathbb R^ n$, and its reduced and essential boundaries are empty. Perimeter measures are therefore attached to measurable sets modulo null sets, not to arbitrary topological representatives.

Proposition 4.3.4 (Existence with a fixed volume in a compact container).D065989825F9 Let $K\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be compact and $v\geq 0$. Suppose there is at least one measurable $E\subset K$ almost everywhere with $|E|=v$ and finite total perimeter. Then the infimum of $P(E;\mathbb R^ n)$ over this class is attained. No regularity of $K$ is needed for this existence statement, and no smoothness of the minimizing boundary is concluded.

Proof

Choose a minimizing sequence with bounded total perimeter. All its characteristic functions are supported in $K$ and have fixed $L^1$ norm $v$. Corollary 4.2.8 gives a globally $L^1$-convergent subsequence whose limit remains supported in $K$ and has volume $v$. Proposition 4.1.3 shows that this limit attains the infimum. This is a direct application of the BV compactness theorem in [Sim18], Chapter 2. $\square$

Proposition 4.3.5 (A local direct method with a bulk term).8128DEB47531 Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be bounded and open, let $K\Subset U$ be compact, let $E_0$ have finite perimeter in $U$, and let $g\in L^1(U)$. Then

\[ \mathcal J_ g(E)=P(E;U)-\int _{E\cap U}g(x)\, dx \]

attains its infimum among measurable sets $E$ with $\chi _ E=\chi _{E_0}$ almost everywhere on $U\setminus K$. The compactly contained set of allowed changes is part of the boundary-data formulation. This extends the constant bulk term in Definition 11.1.1.

Proof

The functional is bounded below by $-\| g\| _{L^1(U)}$, and $E_0$ is an admissible finite-energy competitor. A minimizing sequence therefore has uniformly bounded perimeter. Corollary 4.2.8 gives a subsequence converging locally in $L^1$ and, after a further extraction, almost everywhere. The limit agrees with $E_0$ on $U\setminus K$. Dominated convergence, using $|g\chi _ E|\leq |g|$, shows convergence of the bulk integrals. Perimeter lower semicontinuity then proves attainment. The argument uses no trace-continuity assertion at $\partial U$, because every competitor is fixed outside $K$. $\square$

Proposition 4.3.6 (The Euler equation on a smooth minimizing interface).FCE1DA6B5E31 Suppose $g\in C^1(U)$ and $E$ minimizes the functional in Proposition 4.3.5. On any open region $W\subset \operatorname {int}K$ where $E$ is represented by a domain with smooth boundary, that boundary satisfies

\[ H=g \]

with respect to the outward unit normal and the trace convention $H=\operatorname {div}_{\partial E}\nu $. This is an interior conclusion; it makes no assertion about contact with the container or about singular points.

Proof

Use the flow of a smooth vector field compactly supported in $W$ to vary $E$. Both positive and negative variation times are admissible. If $f=X\cdot \nu $ is the normal speed, the first variation of perimeter is $\int _{\partial E}Hf\, dA$, while the transport formula for the bulk integral gives $\int _{\partial E}gf\, dA$. Minimality makes their difference zero. Every compactly supported smooth normal speed on the interface can be extended to an ambient field, so $H-g=0$ there. Compare Proposition 11.1.2 for constant $g$. $\square$
