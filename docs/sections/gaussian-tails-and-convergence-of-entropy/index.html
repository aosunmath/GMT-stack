14.10 Gaussian tails and convergence of entropyE19583DFFAC6

E19583DFFAC6

Local convergence of measures controls compact tests. Gaussian integrals need tail control, and entropy adds a supremum over a noncompact parameter space. We separate these steps. Use $F_{c,t}$ and $\lambda $ from Definition 14.4.6, with dimension $n\geq 1$, and write $\rho _{c,t}(x)=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x-c|^2/(4t)}$. The area-growth estimates extend the argument of [CM12], Lemma 7.28, pp. 788–789, to Radon measures.

Proposition 14.10.1 (Lower semicontinuity of Gaussian area and entropy).7E46AD688EDF If nonnegative Radon measures $\mu _ j$ on $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ converge locally weakly to $\mu $, then for every $c$ and $t>0$,

\[ F_{c,t}(\mu )\leq \liminf _ j F_{c,t}(\mu _ j),\qquad \lambda (\mu )\leq \liminf _ j\lambda (\mu _ j). \]

The values may be infinite, and no uniform growth bound is required.

Proof

Choose compactly supported continuous cutoffs $0\leq \chi _ R\leq 1$ increasing to one. Local weak convergence gives $\int \chi _ R\rho _{c,t}\, d\mu =\lim _ j\int \chi _ R\rho _{c,t}\, d\mu _ j\leq \liminf _ jF_{c,t}(\mu _ j)$. Monotone convergence in $R$ proves the first inequality. The same argument bounds each $F_{c,t}(\mu )$ by $\liminf _ j\lambda (\mu _ j)$; take the supremum in $c,t$. This last step does not interchange a limit and a supremum as an equality. $\square$

Lemma 14.10.2 (Uniform polynomial Gaussian tails).52A6BD047625 Suppose $\lambda (\mu )\leq \Lambda $. Fix $q\geq 0$, $A\geq 0$ and $0<a\leq b<\infty $. Then

\[ \sup _{|c|\leq A,\ a\leq t\leq b} \int _{|x|>R}(1+|x|)^ q\rho _{c,t}(x)\, d\mu (x)\longrightarrow 0 \]

as $R\to \infty $, uniformly over all such measures. More precisely, for $R\geq 2A+2$ the integral is at most

\[ C(n,a)\Lambda \sum _{j=\lfloor R\rfloor }^{\infty } (j+2)^{n+q}\exp \left(-\frac{(j-A)^2}{4b}\right). \]
Proof

Proposition 14.4.9 bounds $\mu (B_{j+1}(0))$ by $C(n)\Lambda (j+1)^ n$. In the annulus $j\leq |x|<j+1$, the polynomial factor is at most $(j+2)^ q$, $|x-c|\geq j-A$, and the prefactor is at most $(4\pi a)^{-n/2}$. Sum these bounds. The series converges, with tails tending to zero independently of the measure and parameters. The same estimate with the bounded inner ball included gives uniform finite polynomial Gaussian moments. This is the annular method of [CM12], (7.31)–(7.32). $\square$

Theorem 14.10.3 (Gaussian convergence on compact parameter sets).951933FB16BD Suppose nonnegative Radon measures $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \mu $ locally in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $\lambda (\mu _ j)\leq \Lambda $. Then

\[ F_{c,t}(\mu _ j)\longrightarrow F_{c,t}(\mu ) \]

uniformly for $(c,t)$ in every compact subset of $\mathbb R^{n+1}\times (0,\infty )$. In particular the conclusion applies to the weight measures of locally convergent varifolds with uniformly bounded entropy. This uses the Gaussian tail estimates associated with [CM12], Lemma 7.28.

Proof

Proposition 14.10.1 gives $\lambda (\mu )\leq \Lambda $. For fixed $(c,t)$, insert a cutoff on $B_ R$; local weak convergence handles the cutoff Gaussian, while Lemma 14.10.2 makes both omitted tails uniformly small. This proves pointwise convergence.

On $|c|\leq A$, $a\leq t\leq b$, the parameter derivatives of the kernel are

\[ \nabla _ c\rho _{c,t}=\frac{x-c}{2t}\rho _{c,t},\qquad \partial _ t\rho _{c,t}=\left(\frac{|x-c|^2}{4t^2}-\frac n{2t}\right)\rho _{c,t}. \]

The moment bounds give a uniform bound for their integrals on a slightly larger compact parameter box. Differentiation under the integral follows by the same domination. Thus all $F_{c,t}(\mu _ j)$ and the limit function have a common Lipschitz constant there. Approximate the compact parameter set by a finite net: pointwise convergence on the net and the common Lipschitz bound give uniform convergence. No control of parameters with $t\to 0$, $t\to \infty $, or $|c|\to \infty $ is asserted. $\square$

Example 14.10.4 (Entropy can drop when a plane escapes to infinity).939147F750E3 Let $P_ h=\{ x_{n+1}=h\} $ and $\mu _ j=\mathcal H^ n\llcorner P_0+\mathcal H^ n\llcorner P_ j$. Then $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \mathcal H^ n\llcorner P_0$ locally, while $\lambda (\mu _ j)=2$ and $\lambda (P_0)=1$. Thus entropy is not upper semicontinuous even with a common entropy bound and a fixed component.

Proof

The second plane eventually misses every compact set. If the center has last coordinate $a$, Example 14.4.10 gives

\[ F_{c,t}(\mu _ j)=e^{-a^2/(4t)}+e^{-(a-j)^2/(4t)}\leq 2. \]

For fixed $j,c$ this tends to two as $t\to \infty $, proving the entropy value. At each fixed finite center and scale, the second term tends to zero as $j\to \infty $, in agreement with Theorem 14.10.3. The optimizing scales are not confined to a compact parameter set. $\square$

Proposition 14.10.5 (Entropy controls the Gaussian second moment).572E84F0FD10 For every nonnegative Radon measure with finite entropy,

\[ \int |x|^2\rho _{c,t}(x)\, d\mu (x) \leq 2\lambda (\mu )\bigl (|c|^2+C_ n t\bigr ),\qquad t>0. \]

Here $C_ n$ depends only on the dimension. This is the measure version of [CM12], Lemma 7.28.

Proof

Apply the geometric translation and dilation $z=(x-c)/\sqrt t$ from Proposition 14.4.7, including the factor $t^{-n/2}$ in the transformed measure $\widetilde\mu $. The integral becomes $\int |c+\sqrt t z|^2\rho _{0,1}(z)\, d\widetilde\mu $. The inequality $|c+\sqrt t z|^2\leq 2|c|^2+2t|z|^2$ bounds its first part by $2|c|^2\lambda (\mu )$. The area bound and unit annular sum in Lemma 14.10.2 bound the second moment of $\widetilde\mu $ by $C_ n\lambda (\widetilde\mu )=C_ n\lambda (\mu )$. $\square$

Proposition 14.10.6 (Entropy is unchanged by a Euclidean product).510CE72B5070 Let $\mu $ be a nonnegative Radon measure on $\mathbb R^{m+1}$ with Gaussian normalization of dimension $m\geq 1$. For $k\geq 1$, give $\mu \times \mathcal L^ k$ the normalization of dimension $m+k$. Then

\[ F^{m+k}_{(c,b),t}(\mu \times \mathcal L^ k)=F^ m_{c,t}(\mu ), \qquad \lambda _{m+k}(\mu \times \mathcal L^ k)=\lambda _ m(\mu ). \]
Proof

The nonnegative product Gaussian factors. Tonelli's theorem and $\int _{\mathbb R^ k}(4\pi t)^{-k/2}e^{-|z-b|^2/(4t)}\, dz=1$ give the first equality, even when the other factor is infinite. Taking suprema gives the second. Translation of the Gaussian center in a product direction has no effect; this will matter in Proposition 14.11.6. $\square$

Proposition 14.10.7 (Small-scale Gaussian limits on a proper embedded hypersurface).EC6BCD371796 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be smooth, properly embedded, without boundary, and with polynomial area growth. Then

\[ \lim _{t\downarrow 0}F_{c,t}(\Sigma )= \begin{cases} 1,& c\in \Sigma ,\\ 0,& c\notin \Sigma .\end{cases} \]

In particular $\lambda (\Sigma )\geq 1$ when $\Sigma $ is nonempty. This is the local statement in [CM12], Lemma 7.2(3), with properness explicit.

Proof

At $c\in \Sigma $, represent a neighborhood as one smooth graph over its tangent plane, with graph value and derivative zero at $c$. Rescaling by $t^{-1/2}$ gives convergence to that plane on bounded sets. In a fixed small graph neighborhood the area Jacobian is bounded and the tangential Gaussian dominates an integrable Gaussian on the tangent plane; hence its integral tends to one by dominated convergence. Proper embeddedness allows this neighborhood to exclude all other sheets.

Outside a fixed ambient ball of radius $\delta $ about $c$, for $0<t\leq t_*$ the Gaussian contribution is bounded by

\[ (4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-\delta ^2/(8t)} \int _\Sigma e^{-|x-c|^2/(8t_*)}\, dA, \]

which tends to zero; the last integral is finite by polynomial area growth. If $c\notin \Sigma $, properness makes $\Sigma $ closed, so its distance from $c$ is positive and the same estimate applies to the whole surface. This is the static counterpart of Proposition 14.4.5; multiplicity or nonproper accumulation would change the argument. $\square$
