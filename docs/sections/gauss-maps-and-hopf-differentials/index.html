11.8 Gauss maps and Hopf differentialsF402186F356A

F402186F356A

Harmonic maps connect CMC surface theory to complex analysis. The sources are [Hel01], Theorem 2.1, p. 18, and Chapter 4, pp. 34–37. We use the trace $H$ and the sign $h(X,Y)=\langle D_ X\nu ,Y\rangle $ throughout; the source uses the opposite second fundamental form and averaged mean curvature. Basic complex differentiation and Liouville's theorem are explicit complex-analytic background inputs. Global conformal parametrization of a topological sphere is not proved in this section.

Definition 11.8.1 (Harmonic maps to a unit sphere).5F0BF2179EDD A smooth map $u:(M,g)\to S^{N-1}\subset \mathbb R^ N$ is harmonic if

\[ \Delta _ gu+|du|_ g^2u=0. \]

Equivalently the Euclidean vector $\Delta _ gu$ is normal to the target sphere at $u$.

Proposition 11.8.2 (The sphere-valued energy equation).DB2FAFFE267A A smooth map $u:(M,g)\to S^{N-1}$ is harmonic precisely when it is stationary for the Dirichlet energy under all smooth target-tangent variations compactly supported in the interior. For each test variation, evaluate $E_ U(u)=\frac12\int _ U|du|^2$ on a relatively compact domain $U$ containing its support; no finite total energy on a noncompact $M$ is assumed.

Proof

For a field $V$ along $u$ satisfying $\langle V,u\rangle =0$, the variation $(u+tV)/|u+tV|$ stays on the sphere and has initial velocity $V$. Differentiating the energy and integrating by parts gives $E'(0)=-\int \langle \Delta u,V\rangle $. Vanishing for all such fields is exactly the vanishing of the tangential projection of $\Delta u$. Since $\Delta |u|^2=2\langle \Delta u,u\rangle +2|du|^2=0$, its normal component is $-|du|^2u$. This gives the displayed harmonic-map equation. Boundary values are held fixed by the compact support of the test. $\square$

Proposition 11.8.3 (The Gauss-map tension of a Euclidean hypersurface).70AB98B22DB0 For a smooth two-sided hypersurface immersion $F:\Sigma ^ n\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ with global unit normal $\nu $, the Gauss map satisfies

\[ |d\nu |^2=|A|^2,\qquad \Delta _\Sigma \nu +|A|^2\nu =\nabla H. \]

The right side is viewed as a tangent vector in Euclidean space. Thus on a connected hypersurface the Gauss map is harmonic if and only if $H$ is constant. In dimension two this is the Ruh–Vilms statement recalled in [Hel01], Theorem 2.1.

Proof

The Weingarten formula in our convention is $D_{e_ i}\nu =Ae_ i$, giving the energy identity. At a point choose a geodesic orthonormal frame. The ambient derivative decomposition gives

\[ \Delta \nu =\sum _ i D_{e_ i}(Ae_ i) =\sum _ i(\nabla _{e_ i}A)e_ i-\sum _ i h(e_ i,Ae_ i)\nu . \]

The last sum is $-|A|^2\nu $. Contracting Codazzi gives $\sum _ i(\nabla _{e_ i}A)e_ i=\nabla H$. These tensorial formulas hold at every point. The sphere-valued harmonic-map equation therefore reduces to $\nabla H=0$, which is equivalent to constant $H$ on a connected hypersurface. $\square$

Lemma 11.8.4 (Conformal invariance of the harmonic-map equation in two dimensions).1896C8DC240D For a two-dimensional domain, replacing $g$ by $\widetilde g=e^{2\lambda }g$ preserves the harmonicity of a sphere-valued map and its Dirichlet energy. In a conformal coordinate $z=x+iy$ with $g=e^{2\lambda }(dx^2+dy^2)$, the equation is

\[ u_{xx}+u_{yy}+(|u_ x|^2+|u_ y|^2)u=0. \]
Proof

The coordinate divergence expression gives $\Delta _{\widetilde g}=e^{-2\lambda }\Delta _ g$ on scalar functions in dimension two, and applies componentwise to $u$. Also $|du|_{\widetilde g}^2=e^{-2\lambda }|du|_ g^2$. Thus the equation is multiplied by the same positive factor. The area element is multiplied by $e^{2\lambda }$, canceling the change of energy density. Substitution into a conformal coordinate proves the flat-coordinate form. This cancellation is specific to a two-dimensional domain. $\square$

Proposition 11.8.5 (The Hopf differential of a harmonic map).C6B4DB72F83C For a smooth harmonic map $u$ from a Riemann surface to a unit sphere, the expression

\[ \Phi _ u=\langle u_ z,u_ z\rangle \, dz^2, \qquad \partial _ z=\tfrac 12(\partial _ x-i\partial _ y), \]

is a globally defined holomorphic quadratic differential. The pairing is the complex bilinear extension of the Euclidean inner product, not the Hermitian pairing. It vanishes exactly when $u$ is weakly conformal, meaning $|u_ x|=|u_ y|$ and $\langle u_ x,u_ y\rangle =0$ in conformal coordinates. Compare [Hel01], Chapter 4, p. 34; the source multiplies our coefficient by four.

Proof

For a holomorphic coordinate change $z=z(w)$, the chain rule gives $u_ w=u_ z z_ w$ and hence the coefficient transforms by $z_ w^2$, as required for a quadratic differential. By the preceding lemma, $u_{z\bar z}=-(|u_ x|^2+|u_ y|^2)u/4$. Since $\langle u,u_ z\rangle =0$, differentiation gives

\[ \partial _{\bar z}\langle u_ z,u_ z\rangle =2\langle u_{z\bar z},u_ z\rangle =0. \]

Finally $4\langle u_ z,u_ z\rangle =|u_ x|^2-|u_ y|^2-2i\langle u_ x,u_ y\rangle $, proving the equivalence with weak conformality. No assertion of immersion follows at points where $du=0$. $\square$

Lemma 11.8.6 (No nonzero holomorphic quadratic differential on the Riemann sphere).41369D5435BF Every holomorphic quadratic differential on the standard Riemann sphere vanishes identically. Therefore every smooth harmonic map from that sphere to a unit sphere is weakly conformal.

Proof

Write the differential in the finite chart as $q(z)dz^2$, where $q$ is entire. In the coordinate $w=1/z$ at infinity its coefficient is $\widetilde q(w)=q(1/w)w^{-4}$. Smoothness and holomorphicity near $w=0$ make this coefficient bounded there. Hence $q(z)=z^{-4}\widetilde q(1/z)=O(|z|^{-4})$ at infinity. Liouville's theorem implies that the bounded entire function $q$ is constant, and the decay makes the constant zero. Apply Proposition 11.8.5 to obtain the last assertion. This is the argument of [Hel01], Theorem 4.1, p. 35. $\square$

Theorem 11.8.7 (Hopf rigidity for a conformally parametrized CMC sphere).BB55E1795C93 Let $F:S^2\to \mathbb R^3$ be a smooth conformal immersion from the standard Riemann sphere, with constant scalar mean curvature $H$. Then $H\ne 0$ and its image is a round sphere of radius $2/|H|$. This is [Hel01], Theorem 4.2, pp. 36–37, with the conformal-parametrization hypothesis stated explicitly.

Proof

The global orientation gives a smooth Gauss map. By Proposition 11.8.3 and Lemma 11.8.4, it is harmonic for the standard conformal structure and hence weakly conformal by Lemma 11.8.6. At a principal orthonormal frame, $d\nu =A$, so weak conformality says $\kappa _1^2=\kappa _2^2$. If $H=0$, the identity $\Delta F=\vec H=0$ makes every coordinate function harmonic on a compact connected surface and therefore constant, contradicting immersion. Thus $H\ne 0$. The equality of squares together with $\kappa _1+\kappa _2=H\ne 0$ implies $\kappa _1=\kappa _2=H/2$ at every point.

Put $\lambda =H/2$. Since $\lambda $ is constant and nonzero, differentiating $F-\nu /\lambda $ gives zero. Its value is a fixed center $c$, and $|F-c|=1/|\lambda |=2/|H|$. The image is open in that sphere by the inverse function theorem and closed by compactness, so it is the entire sphere. This proves the claimed image statement without assuming embeddedness. $\square$

Remark 11.8.8 (The topological-sphere formulation and its analytic input).E258EB566499 To conclude the same theorem for a smooth CMC immersion of an abstract surface merely known to be diffeomorphic to $S^2$, apply the genus-zero uniformization theorem to its induced metric and reparametrize conformally. Uniformization is an external analytic input here; a topological identification alone is not a conformal parametrization. The local geometric proof above remains valid for every immersed conformal sphere and does not classify CMC immersions of positive genus.
