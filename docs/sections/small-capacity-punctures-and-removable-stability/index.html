7.11 Small-capacity punctures and removable stability63C90C629FF2

63C90C629FF2

The cutoffs here shrink around a missing point, in contrast with the large-radius cutoffs used in Bernstein arguments. We work on an already smooth manifold with a locally bounded potential; removal of a singularity of the hypersurface itself is a different regularity problem. The geometric source is [ChMin], Lemma 33.5, pp. 81–82, and the cutoff discussion in [Sharp17], p. 324.

Definition 7.11.1 (Local cutoffs avoiding a closed set).B8E027663FF9 A closed measure-zero subset $E$ of a smooth manifold $\Sigma $ has the cutoff property used here if, for every compact $K\subset \Sigma $, there are locally Lipschitz functions $0\leq \eta _ j\leq 1$ on a neighborhood of $K$, vanishing on an open neighborhood of $E\cap K$, such that $\eta _ j\to 1$ almost everywhere on $K$ and $\int _ K|\nabla \eta _ j|^2\to 0$. The property must hold also on larger compact neighborhoods containing the support of any test function. This explicit local zero-capacity condition is all that is used below; broader equivalences for capacity are not assumed. Compare the cutoff mechanism in [ChMin], Lemma 33.5.

Lemma 7.11.2 (A point has cutoffs of arbitrarily small energy in dimension at least two).4E098CFF9186 On a smooth $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with $n\geq 2$, a single interior point has the property in Definition 7.11.1. The cutoffs can equal one outside a shrinking coordinate ball. Their Dirichlet energies are $O(\varepsilon ^{n-2})$ for $n>2$ and $O(1/|\log \varepsilon |)$ for $n=2$.

Proof

Use normal coordinates centered at the point, where the metric and volume density are uniformly comparable with their Euclidean counterparts in a fixed small ball. For $n>2$, take a radial cutoff zero for $r\leq \varepsilon $, one for $r\geq 2\varepsilon $, and with derivative bounded by $2/\varepsilon $. Its energy is at most $C\varepsilon ^{-2}\varepsilon ^ n$. For $n=2$ and $0<\varepsilon <1$, take zero for $r\leq \varepsilon ^2$, one for $r\geq \varepsilon $, and

\[ \eta _\varepsilon (r)=\frac{\log (r/\varepsilon ^2)}{\log (1/\varepsilon )} \quad (\varepsilon ^2<r<\varepsilon ). \]

Its energy is bounded by

\[ \frac{C}{\log (1/\varepsilon )^2}\int _{\varepsilon ^2}^{\varepsilon }\frac{dr}{r} =\frac{C}{\log (1/\varepsilon )}. \]

Extend each cutoff by one outside the chart. They are locally Lipschitz, vanish near the point, and converge to one away from it. A point has zero Riemannian volume, so the almost-everywhere requirement follows. The estimates are the small-radius logarithmic cutoff argument of [ChMin], Lemma 33.5. $\square$

Lemma 7.11.3 (Finite unions and approximation of test functions).04A378869597 Finite sets on a smooth manifold of dimension at least two have the cutoff property. More generally, if a closed set $E$ has that property, every $\phi \in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$ can be approximated in $H^1$ by functions $\phi _ j\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma \setminus E)$ whose supports lie in a fixed compact set. For any locally bounded $q$, the quadratic forms $\int (|\nabla \phi _ j|^2-q\phi _ j^2)$ converge to the form of $\phi $.

Proof

For finitely many points take the product of their cutoffs. Each factor lies in $[0,1]$, so for $N$ factors its squared gradient is at most $N$ times the sum of the squared gradients. Pointwise convergence off the finite set and vanishing energy follow from Lemma 7.11.2. For a general $E$, choose a compact neighborhood $K$ of $\operatorname {spt}\phi $ and cutoffs there. The functions $\phi \eta _ j$ vanish near $E$ on their support and

\[ \nabla (\phi \eta _ j-\phi )=(\eta _ j-1)\nabla \phi +\phi \nabla \eta _ j. \]

Dominated convergence controls the first term and the $L^2$ error, while the vanishing energy controls the second term. Smooth approximation of each compactly supported Lipschitz function inside the open set $\Sigma \setminus E$, with error tending to zero in $H^1$, gives the required smooth sequence. The supports can remain in one fixed compact neighborhood. Since $q$ is bounded there, $H^1$ convergence also gives convergence of the potential term, by $L^2$ convergence and Cauchy–Schwarz. $\square$

Theorem 7.11.4 (Stability extends across sets with the cutoff property).04F4CF70F97D Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, $q\in L^\infty _{\mathrm{loc}}(\Sigma )$, and let $E\subset \Sigma $ be closed with the property in Definition 7.11.1. If

\[ \int _\Sigma (|\nabla \phi |^2-q\phi ^2)\geq 0 \quad \text{for all }\phi \in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma \setminus E), \]

then the same inequality holds for all $\phi \in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$. In particular, on a smooth two-sided minimal hypersurface of dimension at least two, stability away from finitely many points implies stability everywhere. This supplies the smooth-manifold cutoff deduction in [ChMin], Lemma 33.5, and [Sharp17], p. 324.

Proof

Approximate an arbitrary compactly supported smooth test by Lemma 7.11.3. Each approximant has nonnegative quadratic form, and the forms converge, so the limiting form is nonnegative. For a smooth minimal hypersurface the Jacobi potential $q=|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )$ is bounded on compact subsets. Finite sets satisfy the cutoff property in dimension at least two, so the general result applies. This proof does not require extending a positive Jacobi function across the missing points, nor does it prove that an initially singular hypersurface becomes smooth. $\square$

Example 7.11.5 (Removing a point can change stability in dimension one).CEDADA80CB0B On $(-1,1)$ the quadratic form

\[ Q(f)=\int _{-1}^1\left(|f'|^2-\frac{\pi ^2}{2}f^2\right) \]

is nonnegative on $C_ c^\infty ((-1,1)\setminus \{ 0\} )$ but negative on some $C_ c^\infty (-1,1)$ test.

Proof

On each unit-length half-interval the positive sine function solves $w''+\pi ^2w=0$. Applying Proposition 7.10.5 with potential $\pi ^2$ gives $\int |f'|^2\geq \pi ^2\int f^2$ for compactly supported tests there. Splitting a test supported away from zero into its two halves proves nonnegativity for the displayed smaller potential. On the full interval, $f(x)=\cos (\pi x/2)$ belongs to $H_0^1(-1,1)$ and has $\int |f'|^2=(\pi ^2/4)\int f^2$, so $Q(f)<0$. Smooth compactly supported approximations in $H_0^1$ retain strict negativity. Thus the dimension restriction in point-capacity removal cannot be discarded. $\square$

Corollary 7.11.6 (Ordered sheets away from finitely many points imply global stability).7D9C1F01B117 Let $\Sigma ^ n$ be a smooth connected closed two-sided embedded minimal hypersurface, $n\geq 2$, and let $P\subset \Sigma $ be finite. Suppose on an exhaustion of $\Sigma \setminus P$ there are two globally ordered minimal normal graphs whose functions converge smoothly to zero on compact subsets. Then $\Sigma $ is stable. In particular this configuration is impossible if the ambient Ricci curvature is strictly positive along $\Sigma $.

Proof

A connected manifold of dimension at least two remains connected after finitely many points are removed: a path meeting those points can be rerouted in small punctured coordinate balls. Theorem 7.6.5 therefore gives a positive Jacobi function on $\Sigma \setminus P$, and Proposition 7.6.3 gives stability there. Theorem 7.11.4 extends it across $P$. If the ambient Ricci curvature is positive, the global test one instead gives

\[ Q(1)=-\int _\Sigma \bigl (|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )\bigr )\, dA<0, \]

contradicting stability. The existence of globally ordered normal sheets and a smooth two-sided limit are hypotheses; numerical multiplicity alone has not established them here. $\square$
