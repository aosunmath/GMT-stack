5.11 Flat continuity and exceptional slicing levels51856B9B2D29

51856B9B2D29

The lower and upper slices are meaningful as distributional currents even at exceptional levels. Their flat norm is controlled by the mass in a thin slab. Throughout this section $T$ is a compactly supported normal $m$-current in $\mathbb R^ N$, $m\geq 1$, and $f$ is locally Lipschitz near its support. Flat norms are the real-current norms of Definition 5.3.14. These deductions refine the distinction between the two one-sided slices in [Sim18], equations (4.6)–(4.10), pp. 184–185.

Definition 5.11.1 (Lower and upper distributional slices).2303BA9586EA For every real $t$, set

\[ B_ t^- =\partial (T\llcorner \{ f<t\} )-(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f<t\} , \qquad B_ t^+ =-\partial (T\llcorner \{ f>t\} )+(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f>t\} . \]

Since $\partial T=\partial (T\llcorner \mathbb R^ N)$, the upper slice can also be written

\[ B_ t^+=\partial (T\llcorner \{ f\leq t\} )-(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f\leq t\} . \]

Neither formula alone guarantees finite mass at every level. They agree with the usual geometric slice at almost every level when $T$ is integral. The signs agree with [Sim18], equations (4.6)–(4.7).

Proposition 5.11.2 (Support and finite flat norm at every level).775CD4FED890 Both $B_ t^-$ and $B_ t^+$ are compactly supported currents with

\[ \operatorname {spt}B_ t^\pm \subset \operatorname {spt}T\cap \{ f=t\} , \qquad \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_ t^\pm ) \leq \mathbf M(T)+\mathbf M(\partial T). \]

Finite flat norm here does not imply finite mass. Compare the support assertion in [Sim18], equation (4.8).

Proof

For the lower slice use the flat decomposition with filling $T\llcorner \{ f<t\} $ and residual $-(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f<t\} $. Both have finite mass and compact support in $\operatorname {spt}T$, since $\operatorname {spt}\partial T\subset \operatorname {spt}T$. This proves the norm bound. Use the sublevel formula with $\leq $ for the upper slice.

On the open set $\{ f<t\} $, the lower slice acts as $\partial T-\partial T=0$; on $\{ f>t\} $, both terms vanish. These statements are local: every test compactly supported in either open set has that same property on the support of its derivative. A partition of unity for a compact test support disjoint from the level extends this to all tests off $\{ f=t\} $. The same argument applies to the upper slice. Tests off $\operatorname {spt}T$ vanish as well, proving the inclusion. $\square$

Proposition 5.11.3 (A slab controls the flat change of a slice).93AF8799FE31 For $s<t$,

\[ B_ t^- -B_ s^- =\partial (T\llcorner \{ s\leq f<t\} ) -(\partial T)\llcorner \{ s\leq f<t\} , \]

and hence, with $\mu =\| T\| +\| \partial T\| $,

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_ t^- -B_ s^-) \leq \mu (\{ s\leq f<t\} ). \]

The analogous upper-slice bound uses $\{ s<f\leq t\} $. This is a flat-norm consequence of the restriction identities in [Sim18], equations (4.6)–(4.7).

Proof

Subtract the two lower-sublevel definitions and use the exact indicator identity $\chi _{\{ f<t\} }-\chi _{\{ f<s\} }=\chi _{\{ s\leq f<t\} }$. The two resulting terms are an admissible real flat decomposition. Their mass sum is at most the displayed slab measure. Subtracting the closed-sublevel formulas for the upper slices gives the stated alternative. The choice of strict and nonstrict endpoints matters at levels carrying mass. $\square$

Theorem 5.11.4 (One-sided continuity and a countable jump set).A8942ED69FBA The map $t\mapsto B_ t^-$ is left continuous in real flat norm and has right limit $B_ t^+$ at every $t$. The map $t\mapsto B_ t^+$ is right continuous and has left limit $B_ t^-$. Their difference is

\[ B_ t^+-B_ t^-= \partial (T\llcorner \{ f=t\} )-(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f=t\} . \]

Both maps are continuous at every $t$ such that $\mu (\{ f=t\} )=0$, where $\mu =\| T\| +\| \partial T\| $. Consequently any discontinuities lie in a countable set. These conclusions explain the one-sided conventions in [Sim18], p. 184.

Proof

If $s\uparrow t$, the sets $\{ s\leq f<t\} $ shrink to the empty set, so finite-measure continuity from above and Proposition 5.11.3 give $B_ s^-\to B_ t^-$. If $s\downarrow t$, compare the lower slice at $s$ to the closed-sublevel formula at $t$. Their flat distance is bounded by $\mu (\{ t<f<s\} )$, which again tends to zero. Thus the right limit is $B_ t^+$. The other two limits follow from the same calculation with the endpoint conventions reversed.

Subtract the open- and closed-sublevel formulas to get the jump identity and its bound $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_ t^+-B_ t^-)\leq \mu (\{ f=t\} )$. This gives continuity whenever the latter measure is zero. The finite measure $f_\# \mu $ on $\mathbb R$ has at most countably many atoms: for each positive integer $j$, it has only finitely many atoms of size at least $1/j$, and their union contains every atom. Therefore the possible discontinuity set is countable. A positive level mass is only a sufficient location for a possible jump; the displayed current difference can cancel. $\square$

Proposition 5.11.5 (Bounded variation of the flat-valued slice map).7473A8FE1715 For every finite increasing sequence $t_0<\cdots <t_ k$,

\[ \sum _{i=1}^ k\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_{t_ i}^- -B_{t_{i-1}}^-) \leq \mu (\{ t_0\leq f<t_ k\} ) \leq \mathbf M(T)+\mathbf M(\partial T). \]

In particular, for every finite set of levels $F$,

\[ \sum _{t\in F}\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_ t^+-B_ t^-) \leq \mathbf M(T)+\mathbf M(\partial T). \]

This is a direct quantitative consequence of the one-sided slicing formulas of [Sim18], Section 4.

Proof

Apply the slab estimate to each adjacent pair. The half-open slabs are pairwise disjoint and their union is the outer slab, so their measures add exactly. For jumps, use the bound by $\mu (\{ f=t\} )$ and disjointness of distinct level sets. No completeness assertion for the space of flat currents is needed: all one-sided limits have already been exhibited by explicit currents. $\square$

Corollary 5.11.6 (Flat continuity of almost-everywhere geometric slices).62CA9DD59508 If $T$ is compactly supported and integral, choose the full-measure set of good levels from Proposition 5.10.5 and Proposition 5.10.6. At a good level $t$ with $\mu (\{ f=t\} )=0$, every sequence of good levels $t_ j\to t$ satisfies

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(S_{t_ j}-S_ t)\longrightarrow 0. \]

This conclusion concerns flat convergence, and does not assert convergence of slice masses. It combines the geometric slicing theorem with the exceptional-level distinction in [Sim18], Lemma 4.5 and equations (4.6)–(4.7).

Proof

At each good level $S_ t=B_ t^-$. Theorem 5.11.4 makes the latter map continuous at the stated non-atomic level. Substituting the equal currents proves the claim, regardless of which side the sequence approaches from. $\square$
