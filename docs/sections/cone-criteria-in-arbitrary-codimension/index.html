3.7 Cone criteria in arbitrary codimension531296D48743

531296D48743

The reduced-boundary graph argument has a version for an $m$-plane in $\mathbb R^ n$, with $1\leq m<n$. The normal component is now a vector in an $(n-m)$-dimensional space. We supply the details needed for the converse implication in [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 1.6, printed pp. 88–90. A coordinatewise scalar extension is sufficient; an optimal vector-valued extension theorem is not needed.

Definition 3.7.1 (Projection distance and the cone outside a plane).FC9C64E80FA4 For an $m$-dimensional linear plane $P\subset \mathbb R^ n$, let $p_ P$ be its orthogonal projection and $q_ P=I-p_ P$. Put

\[ d(P,Q)=\| p_ P-p_ Q\| _{\mathrm{op}},\qquad C_\alpha (x,P)=\{ z:|q_ P(z-x)|\geq \alpha |z-x|\} ,\quad 0<\alpha <1. \]

The cone consists of directions whose angle from the plane is bounded below. This is equivalent to the complementary-plane convention in [Sim18], equations (1)–(3), p. 88. For codimension one it agrees with Definition 4.13.1 after choosing a unit normal.

Lemma 3.7.2 (Finite nets of planes).286A09C1E40A The $m$-dimensional planes form a compact metric space under $d$. In particular, for each $\eta >0$ finitely many open $d$-balls of radius $\eta $ cover all of them. Also $\| q_ P-q_ Q\| _{\mathrm{op}}=d(P,Q)$.

Proof

Identify a plane with its real $n\times n$ orthogonal projection matrix. Such matrices are exactly those satisfying $A^*=A$, $A^2=A$ and $\operatorname {tr}A=m$. These polynomial conditions are closed. A real symmetric idempotent has eigenvalues zero or one, so these matrices are bounded and the trace condition makes their rank exactly $m$. They form a compact subset of the finite-dimensional matrix space. The operator norm induces its usual topology, and compactness gives a finite subcover of the cover by all open balls of radius $\eta $. The equality for complementary projections follows from $q_ P-q_ Q=-(p_ P-p_ Q)$. This justifies the finite orientation selection in the cone proof. $\square$

Lemma 3.7.3 (A ball forced outside a nearby plane).78A96A322A6D Let $x\ne y$, $d_0=|y-x|$, and let $P,P_ x$ be $m$-planes with $d(P,P_ x)\leq 1/8$. If $|q_ P(y-x)|>d_0/2$, then

\[ B_{d_0/32}(y)\subset C_{1/4}(x,P_ x)\cap B_{2d_0}(x). \]

This is the vector-normal version of Lemma 4.13.3.

Proof

The projection estimate gives $|q_{P_ x}(y-x)|>3d_0/8$. For $|z-y|<d_0/32$, use $\| q_{P_ x}\| _{\mathrm{op}}=1$ to obtain $|q_{P_ x}(z-x)|>11d_0/32$, while $|z-x|<33d_0/32<2d_0$. Their ratio exceeds $1/3$ and hence $1/4$. No sign choice for a normal vector is involved, so the calculation works in every codimension. $\square$

Proposition 3.7.4 (Uniform plane-cone bounds imply a vector-valued graph).3D34669B9F06 Let $\mu $ be Radon on an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ and $S\subset U$. Suppose $c,\rho >0$, an $m$-plane $P$, and $m$-planes $P_ x$ for $x\in S$ satisfy $d(P_ x,P)\leq 1/8$, $B_\rho (x)\subset U$, and, for every $0<r<\rho $,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))\geq cr^ m,\qquad \mu (C_{1/4}(x,P_ x)\cap B_ r(x))\leq \delta r^ m, \qquad \delta =\frac{c}{2\cdot 64^ m}. \]

If $\operatorname {diam}S<\rho /2$, then $S$ lies on the graph over $P$ of a map $h:P\to P^\perp $ with Lipschitz constant at most $\sqrt{(n-m)/3}$. On the projected subset $p_ P(S)$ the original height map has the sharper bound $1/\sqrt3$.

Proof

If a pair $x,y\in S$ had $|q_ P(y-x)|>|y-x|/2$, Lemma 3.7.3 would give

\[ c(d_0/32)^ m\leq \mu (B_{d_0/32}(y)) \leq \mu (C_{1/4}(x,P_ x)\cap B_{2d_0}(x)) \leq \delta (2d_0)^ m=\tfrac 12c(d_0/32)^ m, \]

where $d_0=|y-x|<\rho /2$. This is impossible. Hence every pair satisfies $|q_ P(y-x)|\leq |y-x|/2$. Orthogonality gives $|p_ P(y-x)|\geq \sqrt3|y-x|/2$ and $|q_ P(y-x)|\leq |p_ P(y-x)|/\sqrt3$. Projection is injective, and the height map on its image is $h_0(p_ Px)=q_ Px$ with the claimed bound.

Choose an orthonormal basis of $P^\perp $. Each of the $n-m$ components of $h_0$ is scalar $1/\sqrt3$-Lipschitz. Proposition 3.4.2 extends it to all of $P$ with that bound. The Euclidean norm of the vector of component differences is at most $\sqrt{(n-m)/3}$ times the base distance. These extensions form $h$. If $S$ is empty take $h=0$. The resulting whole graph is a Lipschitz image of $\mathbb R^ m$; the possibly enlarged extension constant has no effect on containment or rectifiability. $\square$

Theorem 3.7.5 (Pointwise plane-cone bounds give a countable graph cover).0535AECAFAE7 Let $\mu $ be Radon on an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, and let $S\subset U$. Suppose that for each $x\in S$ there is an $m$-plane $P_ x$ for which

\[ \liminf _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{r^ m}>0,\qquad \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (C_{1/4}(x,P_ x)\cap B_ r(x))}{r^ m}=0. \]

Then $S$ is contained in countably many whole Lipschitz graphs over $m$-planes. No measurability of the plane selection is needed for this containment assertion.

Proof

For positive integers $k,j$, retain those points for which $B_{1/j}(x)\subset U$ and the lower bound with $c=1/k$ and cone upper bound with $\delta =c/(2\cdot 64^ m)$ both hold for all $0<r<1/j$. The two limits imply that these countably many classes cover $S$: first choose a smaller positive lower constant, then a sufficiently small common scale at the given point. Split each class using the finite $1/8$-net of planes from Lemma 3.7.2, allowing overlap. Intersect with a countable cover of space by balls of diameter less than $1/(2j)$. Each resulting piece satisfies Proposition 3.7.4. There are countably many pieces and hence countably many graphs. This construction uses uniform inequalities only on the defined pieces, not an unsupported uniform convergence assertion on $S$ itself. $\square$

Lemma 3.7.6 (A plane tangent supplies density and cone decay).487ED9476A1A Let $\mu $ be Radon on an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, let $x\in U$, and suppose

\[ r^{-m}(\eta _{x,r})_\# \mu \rightharpoonup \mathcal H^ m\llcorner P, \qquad \eta _{x,r}(y)=(y-x)/r, \]

locally as $r\downarrow 0$, where $m\geq 1$. Then $\mu (B_ r(x))/(\omega _ mr^ m)\to 1$ and, for every $0<\alpha <1$,

\[ r^{-m}\mu (C_\alpha (x,P)\cap B_ r(x))\to 0. \]

This is the content of [Sim18], Chapter 3, Remark 1.5(3), p. 87.

Proof

The ball mass follows by continuous cutoffs as in Corollary 3.6.3. For the cone, its rescaling inside $B_1$ lies in the compact set $K=\{ |q_ Pz|\geq \alpha |z|\} \cap \overline B_1$. Its intersection with $P$ is only the origin, which has zero $\mathcal H^ m$ measure for $m\geq 1$. The upper bound for compact sets under weak Radon convergence gives a limiting upper mass of zero. These two arguments use different continuity facts: the unit sphere has zero plane measure, whereas the closed cone compact set itself has zero plane measure. $\square$

Theorem 3.7.7 (Almost-everywhere plane tangents imply rectifiability).1C1255DA63AF Let $M\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable and locally finite in $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, with $1\leq m<n$. If $T_ xM$ in Definition 3.1.3 exists at almost every point of $M$, then $M$ is countably $m$-rectifiable. This proves the converse in [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 1.6, pp. 88–90.

Proof

Use Lemma 3.6.1 to regard $\mu =\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$ as a locally finite Radon measure. Let $S\subset M$ be the full-measure set on which the tangent exists, and assign $P_ x=T_ xM$. Lemma 3.7.6 supplies both hypotheses of Theorem 3.7.5, with positive density $\omega _ m$. Thus $S$ lies in countably many Lipschitz graphs, each a Lipschitz image of $\mathbb R^ m$. The remaining subset of $M$ has zero $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, exactly the allowed exception in Definition 3.1.1. Nothing in this argument identifies a merely positive finite density with a plane tangent; the directional cone decay is also required. $\square$
