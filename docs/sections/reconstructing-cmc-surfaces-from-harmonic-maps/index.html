11.9 Reconstructing CMC surfaces from harmonic maps70665B2BAC39

70665B2BAC39

The construction in [Hel01], Chapter 2, pp. 19–21, turns a harmonic sphere-valued map into CMC surfaces. We derive the formulas with the convention $h(X,Y)=\langle D_ X\nu ,Y\rangle $ and trace mean curvature. The period condition and the possible failure of immersion are part of the statement.

Lemma 11.9.1 (The closed reconstruction form).28CB34BE815C Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^2$ be open and let $u:\Omega \to S^2$ be smooth and harmonic. The $\mathbb R^3$-valued one-form

\[ \alpha =(u\times u_ y)\, dx-(u\times u_ x)\, dy \]

is closed. On a simply connected domain there is a smooth map $B:\Omega \to \mathbb R^3$ with $dB=\alpha $, unique up to translation. It satisfies $\Delta B=2u_ x\times u_ y$.

Proof

The coefficient of $dx\wedge dy$ in $d\alpha $ is $-u\times (u_{xx}+u_{yy})$, which vanishes because $\Delta u=-|Du|^2u$. On a simply connected domain a closed form has a primitive: integrate along a path from a fixed base point; Stokes' theorem applied to a homotopy shows path independence, component by component. Differentiating this integral gives the form, and any two primitives have zero derivative and differ by a constant. Finally

\[ B_{xx}+B_{yy}=u_ x\times u_ y-u_ y\times u_ x+u\times (u_{yx}-u_{xy})=2u_ x\times u_ y. \]
$\square$

Proposition 11.9.2 (The global period obstruction).0F5256B6DCF4 On a connected, not necessarily simply connected, planar domain, the form in Lemma 11.9.1 has a single-valued primitive if and only if its integral around every closed piecewise smooth curve is zero. Without that condition the construction still works locally and on the universal cover.

Proof

The integral of $dB$ around a closed curve is zero by the fundamental theorem of calculus. Conversely, vanishing periods make the path integral from a base point independent of the chosen path: traverse one path and then the reverse of the other. Local differentiation produces a smooth primitive. On the universal cover, the pulled-back form is closed and the domain is simply connected, so the preceding lemma applies in local coordinates. Different lifts of a point can give values of $B$ differing by a period vector. Thus local solvability alone does not imply a single-valued map on the original domain. $\square$

Lemma 11.9.3 (The algebra of the two reconstructed maps).7DBEE7E31AA0 Assume $dB=\alpha $ and set $X_\varepsilon =B+\varepsilon u$ for $\varepsilon \in \{ 1,-1\} $. Write

\[ E=|u_ x|^2+|u_ y|^2,\quad d=u\cdot (u_ x\times u_ y),\quad \lambda _\varepsilon ^2=E-2\varepsilon d. \]

Then $\lambda _\varepsilon ^2\ge 0$, and

\[ |(X_\varepsilon )_ x|^2=|(X_\varepsilon )_ y|^2=\lambda _\varepsilon ^2,\qquad (X_\varepsilon )_ x\cdot (X_\varepsilon )_ y=0, \]
\[ (X_\varepsilon )_ x\times (X_\varepsilon )_ y=-\varepsilon \lambda _\varepsilon ^2u, \qquad \Delta X_\varepsilon =-\varepsilon \lambda _\varepsilon ^2u. \]
Proof

Put $a=u_ x$, $b=u_ y$ and $Jv=u\times v$ for vectors tangent to $S^2$ at $u$. Then $J^2=-I$, $|Jv|=|v|$, and

\[ (X_\varepsilon )_ x=Jb+\varepsilon a,\qquad (X_\varepsilon )_ y=-Ja+\varepsilon b=-\varepsilon J(X_\varepsilon )_ x. \]

Since $a\cdot Jb=-d$, the first squared norm is $E-2\varepsilon d$; the second and the orthogonality follow from the last relation. For a tangent vector $v$, $v\times Jv=|v|^2u$, proving the cross-product formula. The previous lemma and harmonicity give $\Delta X_\varepsilon =2a\times b-\varepsilon Eu=(2d-\varepsilon E)u$, as claimed. $\square$

Theorem 11.9.4 (CMC reconstruction on the immersion locus).E1BAF4699366 Under the preceding hypotheses, $X_\varepsilon $ is a conformal immersion on the open set $\{ \lambda _\varepsilon ^2>0\} $. Its induced metric is $\lambda _\varepsilon ^2(dx^2+dy^2)$, and its trace scalar mean curvature relative to the chosen normal $u$ is $H=\varepsilon $. Its mean curvature vector is $-\varepsilon u$. At a point where $\lambda _\varepsilon =0$, its entire differential vanishes; no immersion assertion is made there. This is the reconstruction of [Hel01], pp. 19–21, with signs and normalization made explicit.

Proof

The positive conformal factor gives a rank-two differential by Lemma 11.9.3. Since both derivatives are perpendicular to $u$, this is a unit normal. For the induced metric, $\Delta _ g X_\varepsilon =\lambda _\varepsilon ^{-2}\Delta X_\varepsilon =-\varepsilon u$. The immersion identity $\Delta _ gX=\vec H=-Hu$ yields $H=\varepsilon $. The parameter orientation instead gives unit normal $-\varepsilon u$, relative to which the scalar trace is $-1$. At a zero of the conformal factor, both coordinate derivatives have norm zero. Thus the formula can produce a degenerate map, even a constant map, as well as an immersion. $\square$

Proposition 11.9.5 (Hopf differential and degeneracy of reconstruction).A418F2471861 Let $q=\langle u_ z,u_ z\rangle $ be the coefficient of the Hopf differential. Then

\[ \lambda _+^2\lambda _-^2=16|q|^2. \]

If $\Omega $ is connected and $q$ is not identically zero, both reconstructions are immersions away from a discrete set. At a zero of $q$, at least one reconstruction has zero differential. A conformal harmonic map can therefore make one reconstruction degenerate everywhere.

Proof

Since $a,b$ lie in the plane perpendicular to $u$, $d^2=|a|^2|b|^2-(a\cdot b)^2$. Consequently

\[ (E-2d)(E+2d)=(|a|^2-|b|^2)^2+4(a\cdot b)^2=16|q|^2. \]

Both factors are nonnegative. The Hopf coefficient is holomorphic by Proposition 11.8.5, so its zeros are discrete unless it vanishes identically, by the identity theorem for holomorphic functions. At a zero the product vanishes, and the differential of the corresponding reconstruction is zero. The last possibility is realized explicitly in Example 16.8.3. $\square$
