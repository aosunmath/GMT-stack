4.9 Polar alignment and perimeter growthAA1E42250DFB

AA1E42250DFB

The reduced-boundary definition contains only a limiting average of the distributional derivative. We develop its quantitative consequences before using any rectifiability theorem. Throughout this section $n\geq 2$, $E$ has locally finite perimeter in an open set $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, and

\[ \mu =|D\chi _ E|,\qquad D\chi _ E=-\nu \mu ,\qquad |\nu |=1\quad \mu \text{-almost everywhere}. \]

Here $\nu $ is a measurable outward polar field, and $\nu _0=\nu _ E(x)$ is its limiting average at $x\in \partial ^*E$. This is the outward convention of Definition 4.1.4. The source argument is [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, equations (1)–(6), printed pp. 97–98. None of the results below uses the De Giorgi structure theorem.

Lemma 4.9.1 (The squared polar excess is an average defect).667EA6E6C7BA For $x\in \partial ^*E$ and all sufficiently small $r>0$, define

\[ a(r)=\frac{1}{\mu (B_ r(x))}\int _{B_ r(x)}\nu \cdot \nu _0\, d\mu , \qquad \varepsilon (r)=\frac{1}{\mu (B_ r(x))} \int _{B_ r(x)}|\nu -\nu _0|^2\, d\mu . \]

Then $\varepsilon (r)=2(1-a(r))\to 0$. In particular,

\[ \int _{B_ r(x)}|\nu -\nu _0|\, d\mu \leq \sqrt{\varepsilon (r)}\, \mu (B_ r(x)), \qquad a(r)\geq \tfrac 12 \]

for every sufficiently small radius. This expands the polar-component estimates in [Sim18], equations (1)–(2).

Proof

The denominators are positive because $x$ belongs to the support of $\mu $. Both polar vectors have unit length, so $|\nu -\nu _0|^2=2-2\nu \cdot \nu _0$ almost everywhere. Integrating gives the identity. The reduced-boundary definition says that the average of $\nu $ tends to $\nu _0$, so $a(r)\to 1$. Cauchy–Schwarz with respect to the finite measure on the ball gives the first estimate. The last assertion follows from the limit, for all sufficiently small $r$, without selecting a sequence. $\square$

Corollary 4.9.2 (Tangential and negative polar components disappear).E35EC14C05C1 Let $P=I-\nu _0\otimes \nu _0$ be projection onto $\nu _0^\perp $, and let $s_- =\max \{ -s,0\} $. Under Lemma 4.9.1,

\[ \int _{B_ r(x)}|P\nu |\, d\mu \leq \sqrt{\varepsilon (r)}\mu (B_ r(x)), \qquad \int _{B_ r(x)}(\nu \cdot \nu _0)_-\, d\mu \leq \tfrac 12\varepsilon (r)\mu (B_ r(x)). \]

The normal component need not be nonnegative at every point of a small ball. These estimates, rather than pointwise sign assertions, pass to blow-up limits; compare [Sim18], pp. 97–98.

Proof

Since $P\nu _0=0$, one has $|P\nu |\leq |\nu -\nu _0|$. Apply the preceding lemma. For $-1\leq s\leq 1$, $s_-\leq 1-s$. With $s=\nu \cdot \nu _0$, integration gives the second estimate from the exact excess identity. $\square$

Lemma 4.9.3 (Radial phase volumes are absolutely continuous).8CD253C511F3 If $B_ R(x)\Subset U$, set $V(r)=|E\cap B_ r(x)|$ and $W(r)=|B_ r(x)\setminus E|$ for $0\leq r<R$. These functions are absolutely continuous, and for almost every $r$,

\[ V'(r)=\int _{\partial B_ r(x)}\chi _ E\, d\mathcal H^{n-1},\qquad W'(r)=n\omega _ n r^{n-1}-V'(r), \qquad 0\leq V'(r),W'(r)\leq n\omega _ n r^{n-1}. \]

Representatives of $E$ differing on a volume-null set give the same identities for almost every radius. This is the radial differentiation used in [Sim18], equations (4)–(6).

Proof

Translate $x$ to zero and use polar coordinates and Tonelli:

\[ V(r)=\int _0^ r s^{n-1}\int _{S^{n-1}}\chi _ E(s\theta )\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}(\theta )\, ds. \]

The integrand is measurable and bounded by $n\omega _ n s^{n-1}$. The fundamental theorem for Lebesgue integrals proves absolute continuity and the derivative formula. The sphere integral equals the displayed derivative after dilation. Subtract $V$ from $\omega _ n r^ n$ to obtain the claims for $W$. Fubini makes a volume-null modification invisible on almost every sphere. $\square$

Lemma 4.9.4 (A radial flux bound without a smooth boundary).714C12C35564 For any unit vector $e$ and almost every $r\in (0,R)$ with $B_ R(x)\Subset U$,

\[ \left|\int _{B_ r(x)}\nu \cdot e\, d\mu \right|\leq V'(r). \]

The corresponding bound for $U\setminus E$ has $-\nu $ in place of $\nu $ and $W'(r)$ in place of $V'(r)$. This is a distributional version of the flux estimate in [Sim18], equations (3)–(5); no boundary trace on the sphere is required.

Proof

Choose a smooth nonincreasing function $h$ on $\mathbb R$, equal to one on $(-\infty ,0]$ and zero on $[1,\infty )$, with $\int _0^1-h'(s)\, ds=1$. Put $\phi _\delta (y)=h((|y-x|-r)/\delta )$ for $0<\delta <R-r$. It is smooth, including near $x$, because it is constant there. The identity $D\chi _ E=-\nu \mu $ gives

\[ \int \phi _\delta \nu \cdot e\, d\mu =\int _ E e\cdot D\phi _\delta \, dy, \qquad \left|\int _ E e\cdot D\phi _\delta \right| \leq \int _0^1(-h'(s))V'(r+\delta s)\, ds. \]

For almost every $r$, the right side tends to $V'(r)$ by Lebesgue differentiation applied to the integrable radial derivative from Lemma 4.9.3. The exceptional radii where $\mu (\partial B_ r(x))>0$ are at most countable, since these spheres are disjoint and $\mu $ is finite on each smaller compact ball. At every remaining radius, dominated convergence gives convergence of the left integral to its open-ball integral. Taking absolute values proves the result. The complement satisfies $D\chi _{U\setminus E}=-D\chi _ E$ within $U$, so the same proof applies to it. $\square$

Proposition 4.9.5 (Perimeter growth at a reduced-boundary point).D17532795B67 For $x\in \partial ^*E$ there is $r_0>0$ such that for almost every $0<r<r_0$,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))\leq 2V'(r),\qquad \mu (B_ r(x))\leq 2W'(r). \]

Moreover, at every $0<r<r_0$,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))\leq 2n\omega _ n r^{n-1}. \]

The radius $r_0$ can depend on $E$ and $x$, but the upper-growth constant depends only on $n$. This is the compactness bound in [Sim18], equation (6), made valid at all small radii.

Proof

Choose $r_0$ so that Lemma 4.9.1 gives $a(r)\geq 1/2$ for all $r<r_0$, and the closed ball of radius $r_0$ lies in $U$. The flux lemma with $e=\nu _0$ gives $\mu (B_ r)/2\leq \int _{B_ r}\nu \cdot \nu _0\, d\mu \leq V'(r)$ at good radii. For the complement take outward limiting vector $-\nu _0$; the polar dot product is unchanged, and the same reasoning gives the bound with $W'$. Lemma 4.9.3 bounds either derivative by $n\omega _ n r^{n-1}$. Finally, for any radius $r<r_0$ choose good radii $s_ j\downarrow r$ with $s_ j<r_0$. Monotonicity gives $\mu (B_ r)\leq \mu (B_{s_ j})\leq 2n\omega _ n s_ j^{n-1}$; taking the limit proves the all-radius bound without assuming the sphere has zero perimeter mass. $\square$

Lemma 4.9.6 (Scaling the distributional derivative and its variation).A0C5D3D85441 For $x\in U$ and $r>0$, write $F_ r(y)=x+ry$, $U_{x,r}=F_ r^{-1}(U)$ and $E_{x,r}=F_ r^{-1}(E)$. Then in $U_{x,r}$,

\[ D\chi _{E_{x,r}}=r^{1-n}(F_ r^{-1})_\# D\chi _ E, \qquad |D\chi _{E_{x,r}}|=r^{1-n}(F_ r^{-1})_\# \mu . \]

Thus the outward polar field is $\nu (x+ry)$ almost everywhere for the rescaled perimeter measure. This supplies the measure scaling used in [Sim18], equation (8).

Proof

For a compactly supported smooth vector field $X$ on $U_{x,r}$, change variables in $\int \chi _{E_{x,r}}\operatorname {div}X$. The chain rule contributes $r$ when the test is expressed as $X((z-x)/r)$ in the original variable, whereas volume contributes $r^{-n}$. The distributional integration-by-parts identity gives the first formula. A bijective measurable change of variables preserves the norm of the polar vector; its pushforward is still unit almost everywhere. The vector-measure polar representation therefore gives the second formula. This is scalar dilation in fixed Euclidean coordinates; no tangent-plane measure formula is being assumed. $\square$
