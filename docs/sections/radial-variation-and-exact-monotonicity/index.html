6.6 Radial variation and exact monotonicity349010E50BF4

349010E50BF4

Throughout this section $1\leq m<N$, $V$ is an $m$-varifold in an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, and $\mu =\| V\| $. Balls are open. The calculations develop [Sim18], Chapter 8, equations (3.3)–(3.6), pp. 240–241, including the endpoint convention needed for general varifolds.

Definition 6.6.1 (Mass ratio and radial tilt).7577F97C39D0 For $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$, put

\[ f_ a(r)=r^{-m}\mu (B_ r(a)),\qquad T_ a(x,S)=\frac{|\pi _{S^\perp }(x-a)|^2}{|x-a|^{m+2}}\quad (x\ne a), \]

and set $T_ a(a,S)=0$. The normalized mass ratio is $f_ a(r)/\omega _ m$. Radial tilt vanishes when the displacement $x-a$ lies in the plane recorded by the varifold; this does not require that the varifold be rectifiable. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 8, (3.3)–(3.6), pp. 240–241.

Lemma 6.6.2 (Divergence of a radial test field).FF971C87180B Write $r=|x-a|$. If $X(x)=\gamma (r)(x-a)$ is $C^1$, then for $r>0$

\[ \operatorname {div}_ S X=m\gamma (r)+r\gamma '(r) -\frac{\gamma '(r)}r|\pi _{S^\perp }(x-a)|^2. \]

At $a$, if $\gamma $ is constant near zero, the divergence is $m\gamma (0)$.

Proof

For a unit vector $e\in S$, differentiation gives $\langle D_ eX,e\rangle =\gamma +\gamma '\langle x-a,e\rangle ^2/r$. Sum over an orthonormal basis of $S$ and use $|\pi _ S(x-a)|^2=r^2-|\pi _{S^\perp }(x-a)|^2$. The value at the center follows directly from the linear field there. $\square$

Proposition 6.6.3 (Exact stationary annular identity).A2E5563F6D93 If $V$ is stationary and $0<\sigma <\rho <R$, then

\[ f_ a(\rho )-f_ a(\sigma ) =\int _{G_ m(B_\rho (a)\setminus B_\sigma (a))}T_ a(x,S)\, dV(x,S). \]

The annulus includes its inner sphere and excludes its outer sphere. No assumption about the mass of either sphere is imposed.

Proof

First suppose both spheres have zero $\mu $ mass. Use the radial cutoff

\[ \gamma (r)=\begin{cases} \sigma ^{-m}-\rho ^{-m},& 0\leq r\leq \sigma ,\\ r^{-m}-\rho ^{-m},& \sigma <r<\rho ,\\ 0,& r\geq \rho . \end{cases} \]

Smooth the two corners, keeping the derivative uniformly bounded and the support in a fixed compact subset of $U$. Away from the two spheres the fields and their derivatives converge. Dominated convergence in first variation is therefore valid. Lemma 6.6.2 gives

\[ 0=m\sigma ^{-m}\mu (B_\sigma )-m\rho ^{-m}\mu (B_\rho ) +m\int _{G_ m(B_\rho \setminus B_\sigma )}T_ a\, dV. \]

For arbitrary radii choose continuity radii $\sigma _ j\uparrow \sigma $ and $\rho _ j\uparrow \rho $, with $\sigma _ j<\rho _ j$. Such choices exist because disjoint positive-mass spheres are at most countable in a compact ball. Open-ball masses converge from below. The annular indicators converge to that of $\{ \sigma \leq |x-a|<\rho \} $; the integrands are bounded on a common annulus separated from $a$. Dominated convergence proves the identity, including both endpoint conventions. $\square$

Corollary 6.6.4 (Density defect and absence of atoms).4EA95AAE7E1A For stationary $V$, $\mu (\{ a\} )=0$ and

\[ f_ a(\rho )-\omega _ m\Theta ^ m(\mu ,a) =\int _{G_ m(B_\rho (a))}T_ a\, dV<\infty \qquad (0<\rho <R). \]
Proof

Theorem 6.1.5 bounds $\mu (B_ r(a))$ by $Cr^ m$ for small $r$, which excludes an atom since $m\geq 1$. In Proposition 6.6.3 send $\sigma \downarrow 0$. The left side converges by the definition of density, and monotone convergence applies on the right. The omitted fiber over $a$ has zero mass. $\square$

Corollary 6.6.5 (Equality and quantitative radial tilt).E28E9DD8CC63 If $0<\sigma <\rho <R$, then $f_ a(\rho )=f_ a(\sigma )$ precisely when $x-a\in S$ for $V$-almost every $(x,S)$ with $\sigma \leq |x-a|<\rho $. Moreover

\[ \int _{G_ m(B_\rho \setminus B_\sigma )}|\pi _{S^\perp }(x-a)|^2\, dV \leq \rho ^{m+2}\bigl (f_ a(\rho )-f_ a(\sigma )\bigr ). \]
Proof

The integrand in Proposition 6.6.3 is nonnegative and its denominator is positive on the annulus. An integral of such a function vanishes exactly when the numerator vanishes almost everywhere. Multiplication by the upper bound $|x-a|^{m+2}\leq \rho ^{m+2}$ gives the estimate. This controls radial tilt, not distance from a fixed plane. $\square$

Example 6.6.6 (A stationary varifold with zero density on its support).9198A856EB15 Fix an $m$-plane $P\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $m<N$. The general varifold $V=\mathcal L^ N\otimes \delta _ P$ is stationary, has full support, and has zero $m$-density at every point.

Proof

In coordinates adapted to $P$, first variation is the integral over $\mathbb R^ N$ of $\sum _{i=1}^ m\partial _ iX_ i$, which vanishes by Fubini and compact support. Its normalized mass ratio is $(\omega _ N/\omega _ m)r^{N-m}$, tending to zero. Its weight is not $m$-rectifiable. Thus Corollary 6.3.4 cannot be extended to arbitrary stationary varifolds. $\square$

Example 6.6.7 (A stationary varifold that charges a sphere).2B91CC016F24 In $\mathbb R^2$ put $e_\theta =(\cos \theta ,\sin \theta )$ and define a general $1$-varifold by

\[ V(\varphi )=\int _0^{2\pi }\varphi (e_\theta ,T_{e_\theta }S^1)\, d\theta +\int _0^{2\pi }\int _1^\infty \varphi (r e_\theta ,\mathbb R e_\theta )\, dr\, d\theta . \]

It is stationary, $\| V\| (S^1)=2\pi $, and

\[ \| V\| (B_ r(0))=\begin{cases} 0,& 0<r\leq 1,\\ 2\pi r,& r>1.\end{cases} \]

Consequently the mass ratio of a general stationary varifold need not be continuous or absolutely continuous in radius.

Proof

Both terms define locally finite nonnegative measures on the Grassmann bundle. Integration by parts along the circle gives first variation $\int _0^{2\pi }X(e_\theta )\cdot e_\theta \, d\theta $. Along each outward ray it gives $-X(e_\theta )\cdot e_\theta $, so their sum vanishes. The ray endpoints have zero length measure. For $r>1$ their total mass in the ball is $2\pi (r-1)$, in addition to the circle mass. For $r\leq 1$ the open ball contains neither term. Outside the circle the weight has planar density $1/|x|$, so this is not a rectifiable $1$-varifold. The circle supplies exactly the radial-tilt jump in Proposition 6.6.3. $\square$

Proposition 6.6.8 (No stationary varifold supported in a compact interior set).2C80FDE3480E If $V$ is stationary in $U$ and $\operatorname {spt}\| V\| $ is compactly contained in $U$, then $V=0$.

Proof

Choose a smooth compactly supported cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of the support and multiply it by $x-a$ for any fixed $a$. Its tangential divergence is $m$ on the support. Stationarity gives $0=m\| V\| (U)$, so the weight and hence the varifold vanish. The compact-containment assumption rules out boundary terms at $\partial U$. $\square$
