5.9 Tangential Lipschitz calculus for currentsF8362824DD01

F8362824DD01

The passage from a smooth cutoff to a Lipschitz slicing function requires convergence on the rectifiable support, which can have zero ambient volume. We make this step explicit. The source is [Sim18], Chapter 6, Lemma 4.5, pp. 182–183; the countable $C^1$ decomposition and Euclidean area formula now have local proofs, with Whitney extension retained as an external input to the decomposition.

Proposition 5.9.1 (Scalar coarea on a rectifiable curve).36CCCA6345A8 Let $M\subset U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ be a measurable countably one-rectifiable set of locally finite $\mathcal H^1$ measure, and let $f:U\to \mathbb R$ be locally Lipschitz. Define $\nabla ^ Mf$ by differentiating on $C^1$ curve pieces. For every nonnegative measurable $g$,

\[ \int _ M g|\nabla ^ Mf|\, d\mathcal H^1 =\int _{\mathbb R}\sum _{x\in M\cap f^{-1}(t)}g(x)\, dt. \]

The formula is understood with measurable representatives; exceptional null subsets of $M$ contribute zero for almost every $t$. In particular the critical part $\{ \nabla ^ Mf=0\} $ contributes no points to almost every fiber. This is the counting-measure endpoint of the scalar coarea identity used in [Sim18], Chapter 6, Lemma 4.1.

Proof

Use the countable $C^1$ decomposition to partition $M$, up to an $\mathcal H^1$-null set, into disjoint measurable pieces contained in injective regular curve charts $\gamma :I\to \mathbb R^ N$. Subdivide the charts so that they and their inverses on their images are Lipschitz on the relevant relatively compact intervals. The weighted area formula, Proposition 3.3.1, first gives arclength integration with factor $|\gamma '|$. The ordinary chain rule for the Lipschitz function $f\circ \gamma $ gives

\[ |(f\circ \gamma )'|=|\nabla ^ Mf|\, |\gamma '| \]

almost everywhere on each piece. Apply the one-dimensional weighted area formula to $f\circ \gamma $, inserting the indicator of that piece and the weight $g\circ \gamma $. The resulting sum counts exactly its points at level $t$, since $\gamma $ is injective. Sum over the disjoint pieces by Tonelli. The omitted null set has an $\mathcal L^1$-null image under the locally Lipschitz scalar map, by the Hausdorff-measure Lipschitz bound on a countable compact cover. Thus it affects no almost-everywhere assertion. Setting $g$ equal to the critical-set indicator proves the final assertion. This endpoint does not require applying the strict inequality $k<m$ in Theorem 3.3.3 with $k=m=1$. $\square$

Lemma 5.9.2 (Weak tangential convergence of ambient mollifications).4AAAD381706B Let $M$ be countably $m$-rectifiable with locally finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, and let $d\mu =\theta \, d\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$ be locally finite, where $\theta \geq 0$ is measurable. Suppose $h$ is locally Lipschitz in an ambient open neighborhood. On a fixed compact subset, let $h_\varepsilon $ be smooth ambient mollifications of $h$. If $v$ is a $\mu $-integrable tangent vector field supported in that compact set, then

\[ \lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\int _ M v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh_\varepsilon \, d\mu =\int _ M v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh\, d\mu . \]

This is a weak convergence statement against fixed $L^1(\mu )$ fields. It does not assert pointwise convergence of ambient derivatives on $M$. See [Sim18], p. 182, equation (1) in the proof of Lemma 4.5.

Proof

Localize to a slightly larger compact neighborhood on which $h$ has a finite Lipschitz constant $L$. A cutoff and an extension can be used before convolution; sufficiently small convolution radii give uniform convergence to $h$ and a uniform gradient bound on the compact set being tested.

First work in a relatively compact regular $C^1$ chart $F:V\subset \mathbb R^ m\to M_ j$, with $DF$ and the inverse metric bounded. The functions $h_\varepsilon \circ F$ converge uniformly to $h\circ F$, while their first derivatives are uniformly bounded. For a smooth compactly supported scalar test $a$, integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ V a\, \partial _ i(h_\varepsilon \circ F) =-\int _ V(\partial _ i a)(h_\varepsilon \circ F) \longrightarrow \int _ V a\, \partial _ i(h\circ F). \]

Approximation of an $L^1$ test by smooth tests, together with the uniform derivative bound, extends this convergence to every fixed $L^1$ coefficient. Expressing $v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh_\varepsilon $ in chart coordinates introduces only bounded coefficients from $DF$ and the inverse metric. The factor $\theta $ and the chart area density are absorbed into the $L^1$ test; the indicator of a measurable rectifiable piece is absorbed there as well. No derivative of a merely continuous chart metric is taken.

Partition $M$ into disjoint measurable pieces covered by such charts, using the countable $C^1$ decomposition. A finite number of compact chart pieces captures all but an arbitrarily small amount of $\int |v|\, d\mu $. On that remainder both tangential gradients have the same uniform bound, so the difference of the integrals is bounded by a constant times this small tail. First pass to the limit on the finite union, then let the tail tend to zero. This proves the assertion for the original field. $\square$

Lemma 5.9.3 (Smooth scalar chain rule on a rectifiable support).15EB78009E28 Under the preceding rectifiability hypotheses, let $f$ be locally Lipschitz and $Q\in C^1(\mathbb R)$. Then

\[ \nabla ^ M(Q\circ f)=Q'(f)\nabla ^ Mf \quad \mathcal H^ m\text{-almost everywhere on }M. \]

On each compact set where $f$ and $Q'$ are bounded, the composition is Lipschitz. This is the smooth-outer-function case of the chain rule in [Sim18], Chapter 6, p. 183, equation (4).

Proof

In each $C^1$ chart the Lipschitz function $f\circ F$ is differentiable almost everywhere by Theorem 3.1.2. The ordinary chain rule there gives $D(Q\circ f\circ F)=Q'(f\circ F)D(f\circ F)$. Passing from coordinate differentials to the tangential gradient proves the identity on that chart. The countable decomposition removes a single $\mathcal H^ m$-null set. The mean value theorem bounds the Lipschitz constant of the composition on each relevant compact neighborhood. $\square$

Definition 5.9.4 (Tangential contraction of a rectifiable current).058A5F0F3915 Let $T=\llbracket M,\theta ,\tau \rrbracket $ be an integer rectifiable $m$-current of locally finite mass, $m\geq 1$, and let $h$ be locally Lipschitz. Set

\[ (T\llcorner d^ Mh)(\omega ) =\int _ M\langle \tau ,d^ Mh\wedge \omega \rangle \theta \, d\mathcal H^ m. \]

Here $d^ Mh$ can be extended to the ambient cotangent space by zero on the normal space; any extension gives the same evaluation on $\tau $. Its mass measure is bounded by $|\nabla ^ Mh|\, \| T\| $. This convention extends the smooth contraction in Definition 5.3.4; compare [Sim18], Chapter 6, equation (4.3) and the proof of Lemma 4.5.

Proposition 5.9.5 (Boundary of a Lipschitz scalar restriction).79C5D9AEC72E Let $T=\llbracket M,\theta ,\tau \rrbracket $ be locally integer rectifiable and locally normal, with $m\geq 1$, and let $h$ be locally Lipschitz. Then

\[ \partial (T\llcorner h)=(\partial T)\llcorner h-T\llcorner d^ Mh. \]

Thus $T\llcorner h$ is locally normal, although its multiplicities need not be integers, and

\[ \| \partial (T\llcorner h)\| \leq |h|\, \| \partial T\| +|\nabla ^ Mh|\, \| T\| . \]

These are measure inequalities on relatively compact sets. The argument expands the Lipschitz approximation step in [Sim18], Lemma 4.5, pp. 182–183.

Proof

Fix a smooth compactly supported $(m-1)$-form $\omega $. Apply Proposition 5.3.5 to ambient mollifications $h_\varepsilon $. Uniform convergence on the support of the tests gives convergence of $(T\llcorner h_\varepsilon )(d\omega )$ and $((\partial T)\llcorner h_\varepsilon )(\omega )$, since both currents have locally finite mass. The remaining term is

\[ \int _ M\langle \tau ,d^ Mh_\varepsilon \wedge \omega \rangle \, d\| T\| . \]

Its integrand is the pairing of $\nabla ^ Mh_\varepsilon $ with a fixed bounded measurable tangent field determined by $\tau $ and $\omega $. Lemma 5.9.2 gives its limit. This proves the current identity. Contraction of a unit simple $m$-vector with a covector of norm $a$ has mass norm at most $a$; the integral representation therefore gives the stated bound on every Borel set by domination of vector-measure variation. The right side is locally finite. Integer multiplicity of $T$ also ensures locally finite $\mathcal H^ m$ on its nonzero multiplicity support, as required in the tangential-calculus lemmas. $\square$
