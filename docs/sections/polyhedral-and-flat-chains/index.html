5.2 Polyhedral and flat chains4791813D2C61

4791813D2C61

The flat norm makes boundary continuous while allowing thin fillings to become small. We begin with integer coefficients and polyhedral chains, following [WhiCho14], Sections 2.1–2.3. General coefficient groups will require separate hypotheses and are not silently included in the statements here.

Definition 5.2.1 (Integer polyhedral chains).79AE1902199F The group $\mathcal{P}_ m(\mathbb {R}^ n;\mathbb {Z})$ consists of finite integer sums of oriented compact convex $m$-dimensional polyhedra, modulo subdivision and reversal of orientation. After a common subdivision, a chain can be written $P=\sum _ i a_ i[\sigma _ i]$ with pairwise disjoint relative interiors and with repeated pieces combined. Its mass and boundary are

\[ \mathbf{M}(P)=\sum _ i|a_ i|\mathcal{H}^ m(\sigma _ i),\qquad \partial P=\sum _ i a_ i\partial [\sigma _ i], \]

where faces carry their induced orientations. For $m=0$ use counting measure and set the boundary to zero. These constructions respect subdivision, and $\partial ^2P=0$. See [WhiCho14], Definition 2.4 and equation (2.1), pp. 4–5.

Definition 5.2.2 (Flat norm and completion).394A15B53A4E For $P\in \mathcal{P}_ m(\mathbb {R}^ n;\mathbb {Z})$, define

\[ \mathcal{F}(P)=\inf \{ \mathbf{M}(R)+\mathbf{M}(S): R\in \mathcal{P}_ m(\mathbb {R}^ n;\mathbb {Z}), S\in \mathcal{P}_{m+1}(\mathbb {R}^ n;\mathbb {Z}),\ P=R+\partial S\} . \]

When $m=n$, the group of $(n+1)$-chains is zero. This flat norm is symmetric, subadditive, and vanishes only at zero, so $d(P,Q)=\mathcal F(P-Q)$ is a translation-invariant metric; a proof is given in Proposition 5.3.16. For a compact set $K$, let $\mathcal{P}_ m(K)$ be the chains supported in $K$, with the ambient flat norm. The compactly supported flat chains are the union over compact $K$ of the metric completions of $\mathcal{P}_ m(K)$, using the natural inclusions. This is the convention of [WhiCho14], equation (2.2) and the discussion on pp. 5–6. The fillings in the displayed infimum are not required to lie in $K$.

Proposition 5.2.3 (Boundary is continuous in the flat norm).FEAB4217D841 For every integer polyhedral chain $P$,

\[ \mathcal{F}(P)\leq \mathbf{M}(P),\qquad \mathcal{F}(\partial P)\leq \mathcal{F}(P). \]

The boundary therefore extends continuously to the completions in Definition 5.2.2. See [WhiCho14], Section 2.3, pp. 6–8.

Proof

Take $R=P$ and $S=0$ for the first inequality. If $P=R+\partial S$, then $\partial P=\partial R$. In the definition of $\mathcal{F}(\partial P)$ use residual chain zero and filling $R$. Thus $\mathcal{F}(\partial P)\leq \mathbf{M}(R)\leq \mathbf{M}(R)+\mathbf{M}(S)$. Take the infimum over decompositions of $P$. Applying the same estimate to differences gives the continuous extension on each completion. $\square$

Example 5.2.4 (Thin boundaries and loss of mass).2113703E7856 In $\mathbb {R}^2$ let $Q_\varepsilon $ be the oriented rectangle $[0,1]\times [0,\varepsilon ]$, with $\varepsilon >0$. Then

\[ \mathcal{F}(\partial Q_\varepsilon )\leq \mathbf{M}(Q_\varepsilon )=\varepsilon , \qquad \mathbf{M}(\partial Q_\varepsilon )=2+2\varepsilon . \]

Thus $\partial Q_\varepsilon $ converges to zero in the flat norm as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, although its mass tends to $2$. The first estimate uses $Q_\varepsilon $ as a filling and the second sums its four edge lengths. This is the example in [WhiCho14], Example 2.7, p. 6. Flat convergence does not imply convergence of mass.
