12.2 Transition profiles and phase-field energyA0A93B912E49

A0A93B912E49

We separate the elementary energy identities from the later convergence of interfaces. The reference is [ChAC], Sections 2–3, in the June 29, 2023 revision. Throughout, $W(s)=(1-s^2)^2/4$ and $c_ W=2\sqrt2/3$, as in Definition 12.1.1. The Laplacian has the sign $\Delta =\operatorname {div}\nabla $.

Definition 12.2.1 (Local diffuse-interface energy).CF4888DCD29F For a bounded open set $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, $n\geq 1$, and $u\in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U)$, define the energy measure and its mass by

\[ d\mu _\varepsilon ^ u=\left(\frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2+\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \right)dx, \qquad E_\varepsilon (u;A)=\mu _\varepsilon ^ u(A) \]

for Borel $A\subset U$. As a functional on $L^1(U)$, $E_\varepsilon (u;U)=+\infty $ outside this Sobolev domain. No boundary value is prescribed by this definition. This is the localized form of [ChAC], Definition 2.1.

Proposition 12.2.2 (Truncation to the two wells decreases energy).A13F5CC7640E Let $T(s)=\max \{ -1,\min \{ s,1\} \} $. For finite-energy $u$ on $U$,

\[ E_\varepsilon (T(u);A)\leq E_\varepsilon (u;A) \]

for every Borel $A\subset U$. If a prescribed Sobolev trace already takes values in $[-1,1]$, truncation preserves that trace.

Proof

The Sobolev chain rule gives $|D(T(u))|\leq |Du|$ almost everywhere. The potential is unchanged where $|u|\leq 1$, and becomes zero where $|u|>1$. Thus both energy terms decrease pointwise. Trace compatibility with Lipschitz composition gives the final assertion. This is a useful variational consequence of the potential chosen in [ChAC], Definition 2.1; it does not require the Euler equation. $\square$

Proposition 12.2.3 (Maximum bound for smooth critical points).D9C3CB6B8ECD A smooth solution of $\varepsilon ^2\Delta _ g u=u(u^2-1)$ on a closed Riemannian manifold satisfies $-1\leq u\leq 1$. The same conclusion holds for $u\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\overline U)$ on a bounded Euclidean domain when its boundary values lie in $[-1,1]$.

Proof

A maximum larger than one must occur at an interior point, where $\Delta _ g u\leq 0$, while $u(u^2-1)>0$. This contradicts the equation. At a minimum below minus one the two signs are reversed, giving the other bound. Compactness supplies the extrema; connectedness is unnecessary. This proves [ChAC], Exercise 2.1(b), and the stated boundary version. $\square$

Proposition 12.2.4 (Zero energy and connected components).C2E265EAC5A8 On a connected bounded open Euclidean domain, a finite-energy function has zero energy if and only if it equals $+1$ or $-1$ almost everywhere. On a closed manifold the same assertion holds on each connected component; different components may choose different signs.

Proof

Both integrands are nonnegative. Zero energy implies $Du=0$ and $W(u)=0$ almost everywhere. A Sobolev function with zero weak gradient is constant on each connected component: mollification makes it constant on interior balls, and overlapping balls propagate the constant. The zeros of $W$ are precisely $\pm 1$. Conversely either constant has zero energy. The argument in coordinate charts proves the manifold statement. The connectedness qualification refines the observation following Exercise 2.1 in [ChAC]. $\square$

Lemma 12.2.5 (The one-dimensional Hamiltonian).9904DDC4B1C1 If $u\in C^2(I)$ solves $\varepsilon ^2u''=W'(u)$ on an interval, then

\[ \frac{\varepsilon ^2}{2}(u')^2-W(u) \]

is constant on $I$.

Proof

Its derivative is $u'(\varepsilon ^2u''-W'(u))=0$. The identity is the conservation law in [ChAC], Section 2.2. It applies to solutions of the ODE, whereas the energy inequalities below apply to arbitrary competitors. $\square$

Theorem 12.2.6 (Classification of finite-energy entire transition profiles).E6898370A6C4 Let $u\in C^2(\mathbb R)$ solve $\varepsilon ^2u''=W'(u)$. Its total one-dimensional energy is finite if and only if it is one of

\[ u\equiv \pm 1,\qquad u(t)=\pm \tanh \left(\frac{t-t_0}{\varepsilon \sqrt2}\right), \quad t_0\in \mathbb R. \]

Every nonconstant profile in this list has energy $c_ W$.

Proof

Finiteness gives points $t_ j\to \infty $ at which both $(u'(t_ j))^2$ and $W(u(t_ j))$ tend to zero. Lemma 12.2.5 therefore has constant zero, so $\varepsilon |u'|=\sqrt{2W(u)}$. If $u$ reaches either well, its derivative there is zero; uniqueness for the smooth second-order ODE makes it constant. Otherwise it stays in one of $(-\infty ,-1)$, $(-1,1)$, or $(1,\infty )$ and $u'$ never vanishes.

In the middle interval, separation of variables gives exactly the two translated hyperbolic-tangent families. In either exterior interval, choose the time direction in which $|u|$ increases. The time to reach infinity is finite, because $\int _ a^\infty (s^2-1)^{-1}ds<\infty $ for $a>1$. This contradicts an entire solution. Finally the equipartition identity and substitution $s=u(t)$ give energy $\int _{-1}^1\sqrt{2W(s)}ds=c_ W$ for the nonconstant profiles; the constants have zero energy. This supplies the details of [ChAC], Lemma 2.3. $\square$

Definition 12.2.7 (The phase primitive).B4E1EE0417C5 Set

\[ \Phi (t)=\int _0^ t\sqrt{2W(s)}\, ds =\frac1{\sqrt2}\int _0^ t|1-s^2|\, ds. \]

It is odd, continuously differentiable, strictly increasing, and maps $\mathbb R$ onto $\mathbb R$. Its inverse is continuous. In particular $\Phi (\pm 1)=\pm c_ W/2$ and $|\Phi (t)|\leq C(1+|t|^3)$. Strict increase follows because its derivative vanishes only at two points. This is the primitive in [ChAC], Section 3.1.

Lemma 12.2.8 (The Sobolev primitive and Modica's energy inequality).EFCBE4321CEF If $U$ is bounded and $u\in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U)$, then $\Phi (u)\in W^{1,1}(U)$ and

\[ D\Phi (u)=\sqrt{2W(u)}Du,\qquad |D\Phi (u)|\leq \frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2+\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \quad \text{almost everywhere}. \]

Consequently $|D\Phi (u)|(A)\leq E_\varepsilon (u;A)$ for every Borel $A\subset U$.

Proof

The cubic growth gives $\Phi (u)\in L^1$. Truncate $u$ to $[-k,k]$ and apply the Lipschitz Sobolev chain rule to the corresponding bounded-range primitive. The gradients converge in $L^1$, since they are bounded by $C(1+|u|^2)|Du|$, which is integrable by Cauchy–Schwarz and $u\in L^4$. The functions converge in $L^1$ by the cubic bound. Passing to weak derivatives proves the chain rule. Finally apply $2ab\leq a^2+b^2$ with $a=\sqrt{\varepsilon /2}|Du|$ and $b=\sqrt{W(u)/\varepsilon }$. This proves [ChAC], equation (3.1), including its Sobolev justification. It is an energy inequality, not the pointwise gradient estimate for entire solutions also associated with Modica's name. $\square$

Proposition 12.2.9 (A sharp bound for a one-dimensional transition).FE09C7FC0E55 For $u\in H^1(a,b)$, using its continuous representative,

\[ \int _ a^ b\left(\frac\varepsilon 2|u'|^2+\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \right)dt \geq |\Phi (u(b))-\Phi (u(a))|. \]

Equality holds exactly when $\varepsilon |u'|=\sqrt{2W(u)}$ almost everywhere and $\Phi (u)$ is monotone. For an entire finite-energy function with limits $-1$ and $+1$, the analogous bound is $c_ W$ and is attained by the increasing profile in Theorem 12.2.6.

Proof

Integrate Lemma 12.2.8, then use the fundamental theorem for the absolutely continuous function $\Phi (u)$. Equality in the first inequality requires $a=b$ in the square inequality, and equality between total variation and endpoint difference requires a derivative of constant sign almost everywhere. These are exactly the stated conditions. On the whole line, apply the bound on $(-R,R)$ and let $R\to \infty $. This makes the transition cost from [ChAC], Exercise 2.3, explicit for competitors as well as solutions. $\square$

Definition 12.2.10 (Energy discrepancy).F6D965E6AFE8 For a finite-energy function on $U$, its discrepancy is the signed measure

\[ d\xi _\varepsilon ^ u= \left(\frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2-\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \right)dx. \]

It compares gradient and potential energy. Vanishing is equipartition; no sign is asserted for a general function. This notation isolates the two terms appearing in [ChAC], Sections 2.2 and 3.1.

Proposition 12.2.11 (The energy defect controls discrepancy).1B56A14C4675 Let $E=E_\varepsilon (u;U)$ and $D=E-\int _ U|D\Phi (u)|$. Then

\[ D=\int _ U\left(\sqrt{\frac\varepsilon 2}|Du|-\sqrt{\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon }\right)^2dx, \qquad |\xi _\varepsilon ^ u|(U)\leq \sqrt{2ED}. \]

In particular $D\geq 0$, and $D=0$ is equivalent to equality of the two energy densities almost everywhere.

Proof

With $a,b$ as in Lemma 12.2.8, the defect is $\int (a-b)^2$. The discrepancy density is $(a-b)(a+b)$. Cauchy–Schwarz and $(a+b)^2\leq 2(a^2+b^2)$ prove the bound. This quantitative refinement of the square inequality will turn convergence of total energy into equipartition. $\square$
