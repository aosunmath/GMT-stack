10.5 Local area and diameter estimates from Willmore energy4FBE44263383

4FBE44263383

Throughout this section $F:M^2\to \mathbb R^ N$ is a smooth compact immersion of a nonempty surface without boundary. Set $X=F-y$, $r=|X|$, $M_ s=\{ r<s\} $, $A(s)=\mu (M_ s)$ and $\mathcal W(E)=\frac14\int _ E|\vec H|^2d\mu $. The cutoff calculation follows [SimW93], pp. 284–286. We keep the annular energy and the two boundary-radius terms explicit before deriving inequalities.

Proposition 10.5.1 (The two-radius square identity).1EB2A47C45B3 For positive regular values $0<\sigma <\rho $ of $r$, let $M_{\sigma ,\rho }=\{ \sigma <r<\rho \} $. Then

\[ \frac{A(\sigma )}{\sigma ^2} +\int _{M_{\sigma ,\rho }}\left|\frac{\vec H}4+\frac{X^\perp }{r^2}\right|^2d\mu =\frac{A(\rho )}{\rho ^2}+\frac14\mathcal W(M_{\sigma ,\rho }) +\frac1{2\rho ^2}\int _{M_\rho }X\cdot \vec H\, d\mu -\frac1{2\sigma ^2}\int _{M_\sigma }X\cdot \vec H\, d\mu . \]

There is no contribution from the intrinsic boundary of $M$, since it is empty.

Proof

Use the compactly supported Lipschitz ambient vector field

\[ V(X)=\bigl (\max (r,\sigma )^{-2}-\rho ^{-2}\bigr )_+X \]

in the first variation identity $\int \operatorname {div}_ MV=-\int \vec H\cdot V$. Its nonsmooth radii can be approximated by smooth radial cutoffs; the coefficients and tangential derivatives are uniformly bounded for fixed $\sigma >0$, and the two regular level sets have zero surface measure. Dominated convergence therefore justifies the Lipschitz test.

On $M_\sigma $ its tangential divergence is $2(\sigma ^{-2}-\rho ^{-2})$. On the annulus it is $2|X^\perp |^2/r^4-2\rho ^{-2}$, and outside $M_\rho $ it is zero. Substitution and division by two give

\[ \frac{A(\sigma )}{\sigma ^2}+\int _{M_{\sigma ,\rho }} \left(\frac{|X^\perp |^2}{r^4}+\frac{X\cdot \vec H}{2r^2}\right)d\mu =\frac{A(\rho )}{\rho ^2}+\frac1{2\rho ^2}\int _{M_\rho }X\cdot \vec H -\frac1{2\sigma ^2}\int _{M_\sigma }X\cdot \vec H. \]

Completing the square inside the annulus adds $|\vec H|^2/16$ on that annulus, exactly $\mathcal W(M_{\sigma ,\rho })/4$ on the right. This explains why the energy in the exact two-radius formula is annular, before any enlargement used in an estimate. $\square$

Proposition 10.5.2 (A corrected area ratio is monotone).06FDAA574FA5 For $s>0$ define

\[ \Phi _ y(s)=\frac{A(s)}{s^2}+\frac1{2s^2}\int _{M_ s}X\cdot \vec H\, d\mu +\frac14\mathcal W(M_ s). \]

Then $\Phi _ y$ is nondecreasing and

\[ \Phi _ y(s)=\pi m+\int _{M_ s\setminus F^{-1}(y)} \left|\frac{\vec H}4+\frac{X^\perp }{r^2}\right|^2d\mu , \qquad m=\# F^{-1}(y). \]

In particular $\Phi _ y(0+)=\pi m$ and $\Phi _ y(+\infty )=\mathcal W(F)/4$. The uncorrected area ratio need not be monotone for a nonminimal surface.

Proof

Move the inner curvature-radius term in Proposition 10.5.1 to the left and add $\mathcal W(M_\sigma )/4$ to both sides. The result is $\Phi _ y(\rho )-\Phi _ y(\sigma )$ equal to the nonnegative square integral over the annulus. The graph calculation of Lemma 10.4.2 gives $A(\sigma )/\sigma ^2\to \pi m$. It also gives $X^\perp =O(r^2)$ near each preimage, whence $\sigma ^{-2}\int _{M_\sigma }X\cdot \vec H=O(\sigma ^2)$; the inner energy tends to zero. If the pole misses the image, all three terms vanish for small $\sigma $. Letting $\sigma \downarrow 0$ proves the formula for regular $s$.

For any other $s>0$, choose regular values increasing to $s$. The corresponding open sublevel sets increase to $M_ s$. Monotone convergence for the nonnegative terms, and dominated convergence for the bounded signed curvature term, yield the same formula. It makes monotonicity immediate at every radius. Finally compactness of $M$ makes both radius-denominator terms tend to zero at infinity, while the energy tends to its full value. The correction terms involving mean curvature are essential in this computation. $\square$

Proposition 10.5.3 (A local comparison with an explicit constant).2124DA17B73F For $0<\sigma <\rho $,

\[ \frac{A(\sigma )}{\sigma ^2}\leq 3\frac{A(\rho )}{\rho ^2}+\frac52\mathcal W(M_\rho ). \]

If $y$ has $m$ preimages, then

\[ \pi m\leq 3\frac{A(\rho )}{\rho ^2}+\frac52\mathcal W(M_\rho ). \]

These estimates do not assume embeddedness or a pointwise bound on mean curvature.

Proof

First use regular radii in Proposition 10.5.1 and discard its square term. Put $a_ s=A(s)/s^2$ and $E=\int _{M_\rho }|\vec H|^2$. The two radius terms are bounded in absolute value by $\tfrac 12\sqrt{a_\rho E}$ and $\tfrac 12\sqrt{a_\sigma E}$, using $r\leq s$ on $M_ s$ and Cauchy–Schwarz. The annular energy term is at most $E/16$. Since

\[ \tfrac 12\sqrt{aE}\leq \tfrac 12a+\tfrac 18E, \]

we obtain $a_\sigma \leq a_\rho +E/16+(a_\rho +a_\sigma )/2+E/4$. Rearrangement gives $a_\sigma \leq 3a_\rho +5E/8$, which is the claim. Approximation from below by regular radii gives the formula at arbitrary radii; open sublevel sets and their integrals converge from below. Letting $\sigma \downarrow 0$ gives the multiplicity consequence by the local sheet area expansion. $\square$

Theorem 10.5.4 (Global energy controls every local area ratio).6850DAD0C60F For every $y\in \mathbb R^ N$ and $s>0$,

\[ \mu \bigl (F^{-1}(B_ s(y))\bigr )\leq \mathcal W(F)s^2. \]

The constant is independent of the total area, diameter and number of components. This is a consequence of the radial first-variation mechanism of [SimW93], pp. 284–285; the following proof retains separate inside and outside energies.

Proof

Fix a regular value $s$ and let $\rho \to \infty $ in Proposition 10.5.1. Compactness eliminates the outer radius terms. Let $a=A(s)/s^2$, $w=\mathcal W(M_ s)$ and $W=\mathcal W(F)$. Dropping the exterior square term gives

\[ a\leq \frac{W-w}{4}-\frac1{2s^2}\int _{M_ s}X\cdot \vec H \leq \frac{W-w}{4}+\sqrt{aw}. \]

The last estimate again uses $r\leq s$ and Cauchy–Schwarz. Consequently

\[ \left(\sqrt a-\frac{\sqrt w}{2}\right)^2\leq \frac W4, \qquad \sqrt a\leq \frac{\sqrt w+\sqrt W}{2}\leq \sqrt W. \]

This proves $a\leq W$. For arbitrary $s$, take regular values increasing to $s$ and use convergence of the open-ball area. No limit surface or weak mean-curvature theory enters the argument. $\square$

Corollary 10.5.5 (Willmore energy gives a Gaussian-area bound).779189C9B6ED Every smooth closed immersed surface in Euclidean space satisfies, for all centers $y$ and scales $t>0$,

\[ F_{y,t}(M)=\frac1{4\pi t}\int _ Me^{-|F-y|^2/(4t)}d\mu \leq \frac{\mathcal W(F)}\pi . \]

Thus the supremum over centers and scales obeys the same bound. This estimate is not asserted to be sharp.

Proof

For $r\geq 0$, write $e^{-r^2/(4t)}=\int _ r^\infty \frac{s}{2t}e^{-s^2/(4t)}ds$. Tonelli's theorem gives

\[ F_{y,t}=\frac1{8\pi t^2}\int _0^\infty s\, e^{-s^2/(4t)}A(s)\, ds. \]

The choice of open or closed sublevel sets does not affect this integral in $s$. Apply Theorem 10.5.4 and substitute $u=s^2/(4t)$. Since $\int _0^\infty s^3e^{-s^2/(4t)}ds=8t^2$, the result follows. Taking the supremum preserves the inequality. This connects an extrinsic bending-energy bound to the Gaussian functional used in mean curvature flow without claiming that Willmore energy is itself monotone along that flow. $\square$

Proposition 10.5.6 (A containing ball bounds area by energy).D11A3941C16D If $R>0$ and the image lies in $\overline B_ R(y)$, then $\mu (M)\leq R^2\mathcal W(F)$. Equality holds exactly when the immersion lies in $\partial B_ R(y)$ and is minimal as a surface of that sphere.

Proof

Use the vector field $F-y$ in the closed-surface first variation. Its tangential divergence is two, so

\[ 2\mu (M)=-\int _ M(F-y)\cdot \vec H \leq R\sqrt{\mu (M)}\left(\int _ M|\vec H|^2\right)^{1/2}. \]

This gives the inequality. Equality in the two estimates requires $|F-y|=R$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, and $\vec H$ to be a fixed negative scalar multiple of $F-y$. The first-variation integral identifies that scalar as $2/R^2$. For a surface in a radius-$R$ sphere, the Euclidean and spherical mean curvature vectors satisfy $\vec H_{\mathbb R^ N}=\vec H_{S^{N-1}(R)}-2(F-y)/R^2$. Therefore equality is equivalent to spherical minimality. Conversely that identity and $|F-y|=R$ give $\mathcal W=\mu (M)/R^2$ directly. $\square$

Theorem 10.5.7 (Area-energy bounds for extrinsic diameter).2289A5287A63 If $M$ is connected and nonempty, its extrinsic diameter $D=\operatorname {diam}F(M)$ satisfies

\[ \sqrt{\frac{\mu (M)}{\mathcal W(F)}}\leq D \leq \frac{29}{2\pi }\sqrt{\mu (M)\mathcal W(F)}. \]

The upper constant is merely an explicit choice. The qualitative estimate is Lemma 1.1 of [SimW93], p. 283; the proof below also works with immersion multiplicity.

Proof

Fix a point of the image. The entire image lies in its closed radius-$D$ ball, so Proposition 10.5.6 gives the lower estimate. Write $A=\mu (M)$, $W=\mathcal W(F)>0$ and $\ell =\sqrt{A/W}$. Choose image points whose distance is $D$ and use one as center $y_0$. Connectedness makes the continuous distance function on the image attain every value in $[0,D]$. Hence for $j=0,\ldots ,\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor $ choose image points $y_ j$ with $|y_ j-y_0|=j\ell $. Distinct points have pairwise distance at least $\ell $, so their open radius-$\ell /2$ balls are disjoint.

Each $y_ j$ has at least one preimage. Apply Proposition 10.5.3 with outer radius $\ell /2$ at that center and sum. Disjointness of the preimages of these balls gives

\[ \pi \bigl (1+\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor \bigr ) \leq \frac{12A}{\ell ^2}+\frac52W=\frac{29}{2}W. \]

Since $D/\ell <1+\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor $, the upper bound follows, with a non-strict inequality as stated. This is an extrinsic diameter bound; it does not control intrinsic distances along a surface with long narrow geometry. $\square$
