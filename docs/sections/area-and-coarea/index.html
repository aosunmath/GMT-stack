3.2 Area and coareaC07458D97A0A

C07458D97A0A

The area formula counts points in the fibers of a parametrization. The coarea formula integrates the measures of higher-dimensional fibers. We state Euclidean versions first, with different Jacobians made explicit.

Definition 3.2.1 (Area and coarea Jacobians).ECFDA4F8C960 For a linear map $L:\mathbb {R}^ m\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ with $m\leq n$, define its area Jacobian by

\[ J_ mL=\sqrt{\det (L^*L)}. \]

For $L:\mathbb {R}^ n\to \mathbb {R}^ m$ with $m\leq n$, define its coarea Jacobian by

\[ J_ mL=\sqrt{\det (LL^*)}. \]

Here $L^*$ is the Euclidean adjoint. Each quantity is zero when $L$ has rank less than $m$. For a locally Lipschitz map, use the appropriate expression with $L=Df(x)$ at points of differentiability, and set it to zero on the exceptional null set. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, equation (3.2), p. 57, and Chapter 3, equation (2.7), p. 93.

Theorem 3.2.2 (Area formula).893CBC15202C Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ m$ be open, let $m\leq n$, let $f:V\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then the multiplicity function on the right is $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and

\[ \int _ A J_ mDf(x)\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(x) =\int _{\mathbb {R}^ n}\mathcal{H}^0\bigl (A\cap f^{-1}(\{ y\} )\bigr )\, d\mathcal{H}^ m(y). \]

Both sides may be infinite. If $f$ is injective on $A$, the right side is $\mathcal{H}^ m(f(A))$.

A complete local proof is supplied by Theorem 3.13.8. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 3.3, p. 57. See also [Fed69], Theorem 3.2.3, p. 243. Rademacher's theorem supplies the derivative; the area formula is a further theorem and does not follow from differentiability alone.

Proof

Apply Theorem 3.13.8, which establishes both the measurable counting function and the displayed identity on an arbitrary measurable domain subset. Its full-rank part is partitioned into pieces with a controlled positive linear metric; applying the Hausdorff Lipschitz estimate to each piece and its inverse proves the multiplicity identity. An injective graph regularization shows that the rank-deficient part has null image. Rademacher and the Hausdorff Lipschitz bound remove the nondifferentiability set. Local extensions and a disjoint countable exhaustion prove the stated locally Lipschitz version. If the restriction to $A$ is injective, its counting function is exactly the indicator of $f(A)$, proving the last assertion. The normalization of Hausdorff measure and ordinary linear change of variables are retained as background inputs. If dimension $m=0$ is admitted, the domain is a subset of a single point and the identity is the equality of counting measures, with zero-dimensional Jacobian one. $\square$

Corollary 3.2.3 (Area of a scalar graph).C022A573234B Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ m$ be open, let $u:V\to \mathbb {R}$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m\{ (x,u(x)):x\in A\} =\int _ A\sqrt{1+|Du(x)|^2}\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(x). \]
Proof

The map $F(x)=(x,u(x))$ is locally Lipschitz and injective. At almost every point, $(DF)^*DF=I+Du\otimes Du$. This matrix has eigenvalue $1+|Du|^2$ in the direction of $Du$ and eigenvalue $1$ in the orthogonal directions; if $Du=0$, it is the identity. Thus $J_ mDF=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$. Apply Theorem 3.2.2. $\square$

Theorem 3.2.4 (Coarea formula).5830576492D6 Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be open, let $1\leq m<n$, let $f:V\to \mathbb {R}^ m$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then the fiber measure on the right is Lebesgue measurable and

\[ \int _ A J_ mDf(x)\, d\mathcal{L}^ n(x) =\int _{\mathbb {R}^ m}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\bigl (A\cap f^{-1}(\{ y\} )\bigr )\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(y). \]

The proof is now supplied below from the local area formula, Whitney approximation and direct slicing estimates. This is the Euclidean special case of [Sim18], Chapter 3, equation (2.8), p. 93; Chapter 2, Section 7 develops the smooth case. Compare the rectifiable-set coarea theorem in [Fed69], Theorem 3.2.22, p. 258. Unlike the area formula, the Jacobian here is $\sqrt{\det (Df(Df)^*)}$.

Proof

Cover $V$ by countably many relatively compact balls on which $f$ is Lipschitz. Partition $A$ among these balls by assigning a point to the first ball containing it. On each ball extend the components of $f$ to globally Lipschitz functions by Proposition 3.4.2, combine them into a globally Lipschitz vector map, and apply Proposition 3.14.8 to the bounded assigned piece. The extension agrees with $f$ on an open ball, so their derivatives agree there wherever they exist; the fibers restricted to the piece also agree. Sum the identities using disjointness and monotone convergence. Fiber measurability and almost-everywhere measurability of the fiber pieces follow from Lemma 3.14.2, applied on each bounded piece. This proves the displayed local formula, allowing infinite values. Euclidean area is proved locally, while Whitney extension remains an external input to the C1 approximation. The critical-set slicing argument does not appeal to this coarea formula. $\square$
