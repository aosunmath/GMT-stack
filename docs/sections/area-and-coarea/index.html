3.2 Area and coareaC07458D97A0A

C07458D97A0A

The area formula counts points in the fibers of a parametrization. The coarea formula integrates the measures of higher-dimensional fibers. We state Euclidean versions first, with different Jacobians made explicit.

Definition 3.2.1 (Area and coarea Jacobians).ECFDA4F8C960 For a linear map $L:\mathbb {R}^ m\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ with $m\leq n$, define its area Jacobian by

\[ J_ mL=\sqrt{\det (L^*L)}. \]

For $L:\mathbb {R}^ n\to \mathbb {R}^ m$ with $m\leq n$, define its coarea Jacobian by

\[ J_ mL=\sqrt{\det (LL^*)}. \]

Here $L^*$ is the Euclidean adjoint. Each quantity is zero when $L$ has rank less than $m$. For a locally Lipschitz map, use the appropriate expression with $L=Df(x)$ at points of differentiability, and set it to zero on the exceptional null set. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, equation (3.2), p. 57, and Chapter 3, equation (2.7), p. 93.

Theorem 3.2.2 (Area formula).893CBC15202C Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ m$ be open, let $m\leq n$, let $f:V\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then the multiplicity function on the right is $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and

\[ \int _ A J_ mDf(x)\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(x) =\int _{\mathbb {R}^ n}\mathcal{H}^0\bigl (A\cap f^{-1}(\{ y\} )\bigr )\, d\mathcal{H}^ m(y). \]

Both sides may be infinite. If $f$ is injective on $A$, the right side is $\mathcal{H}^ m(f(A))$.

Cited theorem; proof to be added. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 3.3, p. 57. See also [Fed69], Theorem 3.2.3, p. 243. Rademacher's theorem supplies the derivative; the area formula is a further theorem and does not follow from differentiability alone.

Corollary 3.2.3 (Area of a scalar graph).C022A573234B Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ m$ be open, let $u:V\to \mathbb {R}$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m\{ (x,u(x)):x\in A\} =\int _ A\sqrt{1+|Du(x)|^2}\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(x). \]
Proof

The map $F(x)=(x,u(x))$ is locally Lipschitz and injective. At almost every point, $(DF)^*DF=I+Du\otimes Du$. This matrix has eigenvalue $1+|Du|^2$ in the direction of $Du$ and eigenvalue $1$ in the orthogonal directions; if $Du=0$, it is the identity. Thus $J_ mDF=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$. Apply Theorem 3.2.2. $\square$

Theorem 3.2.4 (Coarea formula).5830576492D6 Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be open, let $1\leq m<n$, let $f:V\to \mathbb {R}^ m$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then the fiber measure on the right is Lebesgue measurable and

\[ \int _ A J_ mDf(x)\, d\mathcal{L}^ n(x) =\int _{\mathbb {R}^ m}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\bigl (A\cap f^{-1}(\{ y\} )\bigr )\, d\mathcal{L}^ m(y). \]

Cited theorem; proof to be added. This is the Euclidean special case of [Sim18], Chapter 3, equation (2.8), p. 93; Chapter 2, Section 7 develops the smooth case. Compare the rectifiable-set coarea theorem in [Fed69], Theorem 3.2.22, p. 258. Unlike the area formula, the Jacobian here is $\sqrt{\det (Df(Df)^*)}$.
