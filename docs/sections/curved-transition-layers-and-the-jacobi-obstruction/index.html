12.10 Curved transition layers and the Jacobi obstructionBB020B823488

BB020B823488

We follow the local calculations of [ChAC], Appendix B, Problem 3, pp. 29–31, with explicit signs, boundedness conditions and error norms. Let $\Sigma ^2\subset \mathbb R^3$ be a smooth two-sided embedded surface, $\nu $ a chosen unit normal, and $A=D\nu $. Work on a relatively compact coordinate patch $K$ inside a larger patch $U$ on which

\[ F(y,z)=y+z\nu (y),\qquad |z|<\rho , \]

is an injective tubular parametrization, with $I+zA$ uniformly positive definite on a neighborhood of $K\times [-\rho ,\rho ]$. All estimates below are uniform on this fixed tube over $K$, with constants depending on its geometry and the stated smooth functions. We write $H=\operatorname {tr}A$, $K_\Sigma =\det A$, and retain the quartic potential, profile $q$, and $\psi =q'$ from Section 12.8. No closed nonflat minimal surface in Euclidean space is assumed.

Lemma 12.10.1 (The exact metric, Jacobian and parallel curvature).5C6FEA2AE720 In these coordinates the Euclidean metric, volume density relative to $d\mu _\Sigma \, dz$, and parallel shape operator are

\[ g_{\mathbb R^3}=dz^2+g_ z,\qquad g_ z(v,w)=g((I+zA)v,(I+zA)w),\qquad D(y,z)=\det (I+zA)=1+Hz+K_\Sigma z^2, \]
\[ A_ z=A(I+zA)^{-1},\qquad H_ z=\partial _ z\log D=\sum _{i=1}^2\frac{\kappa _ i}{1+z\kappa _ i}, \qquad \partial _ zH_ z=-|A_ z|^2. \]

Here $\kappa _1,\kappa _2$ are the principal curvatures at $y$, and $A_ z$ is identified through the parallel tangent planes.

Proof

Differentiate $F$ tangentially to get $dF(v)=(I+zA)v$, while $\partial _ zF=\nu $ is orthogonal to every tangential derivative and has length one. This proves the metric formula. Diagonalizing $A$ gives the positive volume ratio $(1+z\kappa _1)(1+z\kappa _2)$ and its stated polynomial. The normal of each parallel surface is still $\nu (y)$, so differentiation along its tangent vector $(I+zA)v$ gives $Av$. Thus its shape operator is $A(I+zA)^{-1}$. Taking its trace and differentiating each eigenvalue proves both remaining formulas. Positivity and injectivity of the chosen tube are used to avoid a focal point or a self-intersection, not inferred merely from the formulas. $\square$

Proposition 12.10.2 (The Laplacian and the special cancellation for minimal surfaces).022784D6AABA For every smooth function $f$ in the tube,

\[ \Delta f=f_{zz}+H_ zf_ z+\Delta _{g_ z}f. \]

If $\Sigma $ is minimal and $a(y)=|A(y)|^2$, then

\[ D=1-\frac{a z^2}{2},\qquad H_ z=-\frac{az}{1-az^2/2}=-az+O(z^3). \]

For a smooth function $h(y)$ independent of $z$, $\Delta _{g_ z}h=\Delta _\Sigma h+O(|z|)$ uniformly on the fixed patch, with the last constant controlled by its first two derivatives and the local geometry.

Proof

In block diagonal coordinates the divergence formula for the Laplacian separates into $D^{-1}\partial _ z(Df_ z)$ and the tangential Laplace–Beltrami operator. Lemma 12.10.1 gives the coefficient of $f_ z$. For a minimal surface the principal curvatures are $\kappa ,-\kappa $, hence $a=2\kappa ^2$ and $D=1-az^2/2$. Differentiating its logarithm gives the exact rational expression. Its difference from $-az$ is $-a^2z^3/[2(1-az^2/2)]$, uniformly of order $z^3$ on the chosen tube. Finally the coefficients of $\Delta _{g_ z}$ and their first tangential derivatives vary smoothly with $z$. Subtract their values at zero in the coordinate formula; every difference is bounded by $C|z|$. This gives the last estimate without asserting an equality between the metrics at nonzero $z$. $\square$

Proposition 12.10.3 (When the distance profile is an exact solution).8A22C57A90B9 Put $u_\varepsilon (F(y,z))=q(z/\varepsilon )$ and $R_\varepsilon =\varepsilon ^2\Delta u_\varepsilon -W'(u_\varepsilon )$. Then $R_\varepsilon =\varepsilon H_ z\psi (z/\varepsilon )$, which is uniformly $O(\varepsilon )$, and is uniformly $O(\varepsilon ^2)$ when $\Sigma $ is minimal. If this profile is an exact solution throughout a tube for even one $\varepsilon >0$, then $A=0$ on its base; every connected base patch is contained in a plane. If $H(y_0)\ne 0$, the general $O(\varepsilon )$ estimate is sharp at $(y_0,0)$.

Proof

The equation $q''=W'(q)$ cancels the normal second derivative, leaving precisely the residual already identified in Proposition 12.4.4. The boundedness of $H_ z$ gives the general estimate. In the minimal case $|H_ z|\leq C|z|$, and $|t|\psi (t)$ is bounded, giving $C\varepsilon ^2$. If the residual vanishes identically, positivity of $\psi $ forces $H_ z=0$ for every $z$ in the tube. Differentiate at zero using Lemma 12.10.1: $0=-|A|^2$. The normal is therefore constant on each connected patch, and $y\cdot \nu $ is constant along it, proving planarity. Finally $R_\varepsilon (y_0,0)=\varepsilon H(y_0)\psi (0)$ proves sharpness when the mean curvature does not vanish. $\square$

Lemma 12.10.4 (The bounded odd normal corrector).C4389C0124F3 There is exactly one bounded solution $j$ of

\[ j''-Vj=t\psi ,\qquad j(0)=0. \]

It is odd, belongs to $H^2(\mathbb R)$, and is given by

\[ j(t)=\psi (t)\int _0^ t\psi (s)^{-2} \left(\int _{-\infty }^ s\tau \psi (\tau )^2\, d\tau \right)ds. \]

It is negative for $t>0$, orthogonal to $\psi $, and satisfies $|j|+|j'|+|j''|\leq C(1+t^2)e^{-\sqrt2|t|}$.

Proof

The total integral of $\tau \psi (\tau )^2$ is zero by oddness. Its primitive $I(s)$ from minus infinity is even and strictly negative at every finite $s$: for $s\leq 0$ this is immediate, and for $s>0$ use $I(s)=-\int _ s^\infty \tau \psi (\tau )^2d\tau $. Thus $\psi ^{-2}I$ is even and negative, proving the claimed parity and sign of the formula. Writing $j=\psi v$ gives

\[ (\psi ^2v')'=t\psi ^2,\qquad j''-Vj=\psi v''+2\psi 'v'=t\psi , \]

so the formula solves the correct equation with the prescribed value at zero.

For $s\geq 0$, the exponential bounds on $\psi $ give $|I(s)|\leq C(1+s)e^{-2\sqrt2s}$ and $\psi (s)^{-2}|I(s)|\leq C(1+s)$. Therefore $|v(t)|\leq C(1+t^2)$ and $|v'(t)|\leq C(1+|t|)$; parity handles the other end. These bounds give the estimates for $j$ and $j'$, and the differential equation gives the bound for $j''$. They imply $j\in H^2$ and justify its orthogonality to the even function $\psi $. The difference of two bounded solutions is a multiple of $\psi $ by Proposition 12.8.2; its zero value at the origin forces that multiple to vanish. Both boundedness and the value at zero are needed for this uniqueness statement. The linear coefficient is $V=W''(q)$, as dictated by linearization of $W'$. $\square$

Proposition 12.10.5 (A fourth-order residual for the unshifted corrected layer).D44443082E59 Assume $\Sigma $ is minimal and set $a=|A|^2$, $t=z/\varepsilon $ and

\[ u_\varepsilon =q(t)+\varepsilon ^2a(y)j(t). \]

Then $\| \varepsilon ^2\Delta u_\varepsilon -W'(u_\varepsilon )\| _{C^0(K\times (-\rho ,\rho ))} \leq C\varepsilon ^4$ for $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$. This is a local approximate solution, without imposed boundary values or a claim that it can already be corrected to an exact solution.

Proof

Since $W'(u)=u^3-u$, direct substitution and Lemma 12.10.4 give the exact residual

\[ \varepsilon (H_ z+az)\psi (t)+ \varepsilon ^3H_ z a j'(t)+\varepsilon ^4j(t)\Delta _{g_ z}a -3\varepsilon ^4q(t)a^2j(t)^2-\varepsilon ^6a^3j(t)^3. \]

The first term is bounded by $C\varepsilon |z|^3\psi (t)\leq C\varepsilon ^4$, because $|t|^3\psi (t)$ is bounded. In the second term use $|H_ z|\leq C|z|$ and boundedness of $tj'$. The remaining terms are of order at least four by bounded geometry and the corrector estimates. This proves the asserted uniform zeroth-order bound. The improved order uses the odd expansion of $H_ z$ for a two-dimensional minimal surface in Euclidean three-space; it is not an asserted dimension-independent cancellation. $\square$

Lemma 12.10.6 (Shifted layers and the cubic error).B460B0616D6E On a minimal patch let $h$ be smooth and put

\[ u_\varepsilon (y,z)=q(t-\varepsilon h(y))+\varepsilon ^2a(y)j(t). \]

With $a=|A|^2$ and $t=z/\varepsilon $, its residual is

\[ R_\varepsilon (y,\varepsilon t)=\varepsilon ^3 \left[-\psi (t)\Delta _\Sigma h+ ah\{ t\psi '(t)+W'''(q(t))\psi (t)j(t)\} \right]+O(\varepsilon ^4) \]

uniformly for $y\in K$, $|t|<\rho /\varepsilon $. The displacement of the profile's center is $\varepsilon ^2h$, not $\varepsilon h$.

Proof

Write $s=t-\varepsilon h$. Tangential differentiation gives $\Delta _{g_ z}q(s)=-\varepsilon \psi (s)\Delta _{g_ z}h+\varepsilon ^2q''(s)|D_{g_ z}h|^2$. Normal differentiation and expansion of the cubic potential give

\[ R_\varepsilon =\varepsilon H_ z\psi (s) -\varepsilon ^3\psi (s)\Delta _{g_ z}h +\varepsilon ^4q''(s)|D_{g_ z}h|^2 +\varepsilon ^2a\{ j''-W''(q(s))j\} +\varepsilon ^3H_ zaj'+\varepsilon ^4j\Delta _{g_ z}a -3\varepsilon ^4q(s)a^2j^2-\varepsilon ^6a^3j^3. \]

Use $H_ z=-a\varepsilon t+O(\varepsilon ^3|t|^3)$, $\psi (s)=\psi (t)-\varepsilon h\psi '(t)+O(\varepsilon ^2)$, and $W''(q(s))=V(t)-\varepsilon hW'''(q(t))\psi (t)+O(\varepsilon ^2)$. The two terms $-\varepsilon ^2at\psi $ and $+\varepsilon ^2at\psi $ cancel. The remaining cubic terms are precisely the displayed expression. The difference $\Delta _{g_ z}h-\Delta _\Sigma h$ contributes $O(\varepsilon |t|)$ multiplied by $\varepsilon ^3\psi (s)$, hence only $O(\varepsilon ^4)$.

Uniformity on the expanding interval in $t$ follows from exponential decay of every positive-order derivative of $q$ and the polynomial-times-exponential bounds on $j$. Taylor remainders for a shift of size at most $\varepsilon \| h\| _\infty $ have the same decay with a fixed larger constant; factors $|t|^ m$ are therefore harmless. These are $C^0$ estimates in the physical tube. Unscaled normal derivatives introduce powers of $\varepsilon ^{-1}$, so no assertion about errors of the same order in every unscaled derivative is made. Finally the center equation $s=0$ gives $z=\varepsilon ^2h$. $\square$

Proposition 12.10.7 (Projection onto translation produces the surface Jacobi operator).3B888F82739D Under Lemma 12.10.6,

\[ \int _{-\rho /\varepsilon }^{\rho /\varepsilon } R_\varepsilon (y,\varepsilon t)\psi (t)\, dt =-\varepsilon ^3c_ W(\Delta _\Sigma h+|A|^2h)+O(\varepsilon ^4) \]

uniformly for $y\in K$. Thus cancellation of the leading projected residual is the Jacobi equation on the interface. This is the solvability calculation in [ChAC], Problem 3(o)–(p); it does not supply a global inverse or a nonlinear gluing theorem.

Proof

All endpoint terms below vanish by the corrector and profile bounds. First,

\[ I_1=\int t\psi '\psi =-\frac12\int \psi ^2=-\frac{c_ W}{2}. \]

Differentiating $\psi ''-V\psi =0$ gives $\psi '''-V\psi '=V'\psi =W'''(q)\psi ^2$. The self-adjoint one-dimensional integration by parts then yields

\[ I_2=\int W'''(q)\psi ^2j =\int (\psi '''-V\psi ')j =\int \psi '(j''-Vj)=\int t\psi '\psi =-\frac{c_ W}{2}. \]

Pair the cubic residual with $\psi $: its coefficient is $-c_ W\Delta _\Sigma h+ah(I_1+I_2)=-c_ W(\Delta _\Sigma h+ah)$. Replacing the finite interval by the line in this coefficient incurs only an exponentially small error. The uniform remainder, paired with the integrable $\psi $, is still $O(\varepsilon ^4)$, without a factor equal to the length of the interval. This proves the formula and identifies exactly which geometric operator arises. $\square$
