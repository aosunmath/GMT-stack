14.7 Gaussian calculus on self-shrinkersF5F891ECCCF7

F5F891ECCCF7

We develop the weighted identities behind the variation theory of self-shrinkers. The primary reference is [CM12], Section 3.3, pp. 772–775. All integrals on immersed hypersurfaces are taken on the parameter manifold with its induced area measure, so multiplicity is retained. Write $[u]=\int _\Sigma u\rho \, dA$, where $\rho =(4\pi )^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4}$. Our scalar convention is $\vec H=-H\nu $ and $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $.

Definition 14.7.1 (Gaussian drift and polynomial area growth).0745A084E5F8 On a smooth immersed $n$-hypersurface $\Sigma \subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\geq 1$, define

\[ \mathcal D u=\Delta _\Sigma u-\tfrac 12\langle x^ T,\nabla _\Sigma u\rangle . \]

Polynomial area growth means that $\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma \cap B_ R(0))\leq C(1+R)^ d$ for all $R\geq 1$, for some finite $C,d$. The intersection here means the inverse image under the immersion. This condition controls Gaussian tails; properness separately makes ambient radial cutoffs compactly supported. See [CM12], Lemma 3.8 and the hypotheses of Lemma 3.25.

Lemma 14.7.2 (Weighted integration by parts with compact support).895D966424D5 On a smooth boundaryless immersed hypersurface, if $u\in C_ c^1(\Sigma )$ and $v\in C^2(\Sigma )$, then

\[ \mathcal Dv=\rho ^{-1}\operatorname {div}_\Sigma (\rho \nabla v), \qquad [u\mathcal Dv]=-[\langle \nabla u,\nabla v\rangle ]. \]

Neither the shrinker equation nor finite total Gaussian area is needed.

Proof

The tangential derivative of $\log \rho $ is $-x^ T/2$. Expand the divergence using the product rule. Integrate $\operatorname {div}_\Sigma (u\rho \nabla v)$; it has compact support and its integral is zero by the divergence theorem. This proves the identity, which is [CM12], Lemma 3.8. $\square$

Lemma 14.7.3 (Global weighted integration by parts).F4CA20B83C44 Suppose $\Sigma $ is a connected complete smooth immersed hypersurface without boundary, and $u,v\in C^2(\Sigma )$ satisfy

\[ [\, |u\nabla v|+|\nabla u||\nabla v|+|u\mathcal Dv|\, ]<\infty . \]

Then $[u\mathcal Dv]=-[\langle \nabla u,\nabla v\rangle ]$. If these conditions also hold with $u,v$ interchanged, then $[u\mathcal Dv]=[v\mathcal Du]$.

Proof

Take intrinsic distance cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ j\leq 1$, equal to one on the intrinsic ball of radius $j$ about a fixed point, zero outside radius $j+1$, and with $|\nabla \eta _ j|\leq 1$ almost everywhere. Completeness makes their supports compact. The compact-support identity extends to these Lipschitz tests by local smooth approximation. Apply it to $\eta _ ju$ and $v$:

\[ [\eta _ ju\mathcal Dv]=-[\eta _ j\langle \nabla u,\nabla v\rangle ] -[u\langle \nabla \eta _ j,\nabla v\rangle ]. \]

Dominated convergence applies to the first two terms. The last tends to zero since its absolute value is at most the tail integral of $|u\nabla v|\rho $. Interchanging the functions gives symmetry. These are the integrability requirements in [CM12], Corollary 3.10; completeness alone does not remove them. $\square$

Lemma 14.7.4 (Gaussian moments from area growth).3D797C21D18F If a smooth immersed hypersurface has polynomial area growth, then for every $a>0$ and $q\geq 0$,

\[ \int _\Sigma (1+|x|)^ q e^{-a|x|^2}\, dA<\infty . \]

The integral over $\{ |x|>R\} $ tends to zero as $R\to \infty $.

Proof

The part inside $B_1$ has finite area by hypothesis. On $B_{j+1}\setminus B_ j$, the integral is bounded by $C(j+2)^{q+d}e^{-aj^2}$. This series converges: the quadratic exponential eventually dominates any polynomial. Its tails tend to zero. This also justifies differentiating Gaussian integrals in center and positive scale on compact parameter sets, since each derivative is bounded by a polynomial times $e^{-a|x|^2}$ for some $a>0$. $\square$

Proposition 14.7.5 (Coordinate and radial drift identities).89901739599A On any smooth immersed self-shrinker $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$,

\[ \mathcal D x_ i=-\tfrac 12x_ i,\qquad \mathcal D|x|^2=2n-|x|^2,\qquad |\nabla |x|^2|^2=4|x^ T|^2. \]

These are pointwise identities and require no growth hypothesis.

Proof

The immersion satisfies $\Delta _\Sigma x=\vec H=-x^\perp /2$. The drift subtracts $x^ T/2$, giving $\mathcal Dx=-x/2$. The product rule for $\mathcal D$ and $\sum _ i|\nabla x_ i|^2=n$ give the radial identity. Finally $\nabla |x|^2=2x^ T$. Compare [CM12], Lemma 3.20. $\square$

Proposition 14.7.6 (Gaussian center, radius, and mixed moments).9410BE875CB7 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a connected complete smooth immersed self-shrinker without boundary and with polynomial area growth. Then

\[ [x]=0,\qquad [|x|^2]=2n[1],\qquad [x|x|^2]=0. \]

For every constant vector $v\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$,

\[ [\langle x,v\rangle ^2]=2[|v^ T|^2]. \]
Proof

All products of the coordinate functions, $|x|^2$, their first derivatives, and their drift derivatives that occur here are polynomially bounded. Lemma 14.7.4 therefore verifies each hypothesis of Lemma 14.7.3. Apply that lemma with $u=1$ and $v=x_ i$ or $|x|^2$ to obtain the first two identities from Proposition 14.7.5. Symmetry with $u=x_ i$, $v=|x|^2$ gives

\[ -\tfrac 12[x_ i|x|^2]=[|x|^2\mathcal Dx_ i] =[x_ i\mathcal D|x|^2]=-[x_ i|x|^2], \]

so this mixed moment is zero. Finally, setting $u=v=\langle x,v\rangle $ in integration by parts gives $-\tfrac 12[\langle x,v\rangle ^2]=-[|v^ T|^2]$. These are the identities of [CM12], Lemma 3.25. $\square$

Proposition 14.7.7 (The fourth moment and the scale variance).2AA75BC1A69D Let $\Sigma $ satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition 14.7.6 and be two-sided, with $H=\langle x,\nu \rangle /2$. Then

\[ [|x|^4]=4n(n+2)[1]-16[H^2], \qquad \left[\left(\tfrac {|x|^2}{4}-\tfrac n2\right)^2\right] =\tfrac n2[1]-[H^2]. \]

In particular $[H^2]\leq n[1]/2$.

Proof

Integration by parts with $u=v=|x|^2$ is justified as above and gives

\[ 2n[|x|^2]-[|x|^4]=-4[|x^ T|^2] =-4[|x|^2]+16[H^2]. \]

Insert $[|x|^2]=2n[1]$ to obtain the first formula. Expand the square and substitute both moment identities to get the second. Its left side is nonnegative. This is [CM12], Lemma 3.25 and Corollary 3.34. $\square$

Corollary 14.7.8 (A radial constraint for closed shrinkers).CF7BD7C076CF A connected smooth closed embedded self-shrinker $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ satisfies

\[ \min _\Sigma |x|\leq \sqrt{2n}\leq \max _\Sigma |x|. \]

If it lies entirely in $\overline B_{\sqrt{2n}}$ or entirely in its exterior $\{ |x|\geq \sqrt{2n}\} $, it is the round sphere of radius $\sqrt{2n}$ centered at zero.

Proof

The positive weighted measure and $[|x|^2-2n]=0$ force both inequalities. Under either one-sided radial condition, the continuous integrand has one sign and zero integral, so $|x|^2=2n$ everywhere. The inclusion in the sphere is a local diffeomorphism because both manifolds have dimension $n$ and the immersion has full rank. The image is open, and compactness makes it closed. The sphere is connected for $n\geq 1$, so the image is the whole sphere. Embeddedness identifies it with the round sphere rather than a covering parametrization. $\square$
