6.5 Smooth support and multiplicity86EE0C8A66A2

86EE0C8A66A2

Stationarity constrains both geometry and multiplicity. On a connected smooth support it forces constant multiplicity and minimality. Without stationarity a density can vary, and its derivative contributes to first variation. The main source is [Minter], Lemma 0.7 and its proof, pp. 8–9.

Lemma 6.5.1 (A distributionally constant function is constant).CB1D1F5465B7 Let $W\subset \mathbb R^ m$ be connected and open, and let $h\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(W)$. If

\[ \int _ W h\, \partial _ i\phi \, dx=0 \quad (\phi \in C_ c^\infty (W),\ i=1,\ldots ,m), \]

then $h$ is equal almost everywhere to one constant.

Proof

On a ball compactly contained in $W$, mollification gives smooth functions whose derivatives vanish on smaller concentric balls. Each mollification is constant on each such smaller ball. Its $L^1$ convergence shows that the limiting function is almost everywhere constant there as well: the constants converge, as seen by taking their averages. Overlapping balls have the same constant. Any two points of a connected open subset of Euclidean space can be joined by a finite chain of overlapping balls, so the local constants agree. This supplies the distributional step in [Minter], Lemma 0.7. $\square$

Proposition 6.5.2 (Tangential stationarity forces constant density).5832EF0DAF5B Let $M$ be a connected $C^2$ Riemannian manifold without boundary and let $\theta \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)$. If

\[ \int _ M\theta \operatorname {div}_ M Y\, dA=0 \quad \text{for all compactly supported }C^1\text{ tangent fields }Y, \]

then $\theta $ is constant almost everywhere.

Proof

In coordinates with volume element $\sqrt{g}\, dx$, the divergence is $\operatorname {div}_ M Y=(\sqrt g)^{-1}\partial _ i(\sqrt gY^ i)$. Choose $Y^ i=\phi /\sqrt g$ for one component and zero for the others. The hypothesis becomes $\int \theta \partial _ i\phi \, dx=0$. Lemma 6.5.1 gives a constant on each connected chart. Constants agree on overlaps and hence throughout connected $M$. The volume factor must be included in this coordinate argument; it is absorbed into the chosen test field. Compare [Minter], the second stage of the proof of Lemma 0.7. $\square$

Theorem 6.5.3 (Constancy on a smooth connected support).B43FE7AAC565 Let $V$ be a stationary integral $m$-varifold in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$. Suppose its support is contained in a connected, relatively closed, embedded $C^2$ $m$-submanifold $M\subset U$ without boundary in $U$. Then

\[ V=q|M|\quad \text{in }U \]

for an integer $q\geq 0$. If $V\ne 0$, then $q\geq 1$, $M$ is minimal, and $\operatorname {spt}\| V\| =M$ in $U$. This is the local smooth-support constancy theorem of [Minter], Lemma 0.7, with the connected portion and ambient open set specified.

Proof

Integrality gives a representation on a rectifiable set with integer density. Since the weight is supported on $M$, it is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$; extend the density by zero on the unused part of $M$. Call it $\theta \geq 0$.

We also identify the plane component. Locally choose $C^2$ defining functions $f_\alpha $ for $M$ whose gradients span its normal space, and a nonnegative cutoff $\eta $ supported in that coordinate neighborhood. The compactly supported field $X=\eta f_\alpha \nabla f_\alpha $ satisfies on $M$

\[ \operatorname {div}_ S X=\eta |\operatorname {proj}_ S\nabla f_\alpha |^2. \]

Stationarity forces this nonnegative integral to vanish. A countable cover and cutoffs positive on smaller neighborhoods show that almost every varifold plane is orthogonal to every normal gradient. Equality of dimensions implies $S=T_ xM$. Thus $V=V(M,\theta )$.

Every compactly supported $C^1$ tangent field on $M$ extends locally to an ambient test field; a partition of unity gives a compactly supported extension in $U$. Stationarity and Proposition 6.5.2 imply $\theta =q$ almost everywhere on connected $M$. Integrality makes $q$ a nonnegative integer. If it is positive, Proposition 6.5.5 gives $0=\delta V(X)=-q\int _ M\mathbf H_ M\cdot X\, dA$. Hence the continuous mean-curvature vector vanishes. A positive constant density gives positive mass in every neighborhood of every point of $M$, so its relative support is all of $M$. The defining-function argument follows [Minter], pp. 8–9. $\square$

Example 6.5.4 (A multiplicity jump produces first variation).A1F1A11532C8 On $P=\mathbb R^ m\times \{ 0\} \subset \mathbb R^{m+1}$, set $\theta =1$ when $x_1<0$ and $\theta =2$ when $x_1>0$. This defines an integral varifold with smooth planar support, but for a compactly supported ambient field $X$,

\[ \delta V(X)=-\int _{P\cap \{ x_1=0\} }X\cdot e_1\, d\mathcal H^{m-1}. \]

Indeed the two half-planes have opposite outward conormals, and their weights leave coefficient $1-2=-1$ on the interface. Thus the varifold is not stationary. Smoothness of support alone does not imply constant multiplicity, and the singular first variation is not represented by a function integrable with respect to the $m$-dimensional weight.

Proposition 6.5.5 (First variation of a smooth positive density).A4DAF9D70A92 Let $M\subset U$ be a relatively closed embedded $C^2$ submanifold without boundary in $U$, and let $\theta >0$ be $C^1$ on $M$. Assume the associated weight is locally finite. Then

\[ \delta V(M,\theta )(X) =-\int _ M(\mathbf H_ M+\nabla ^ M\log \theta )\cdot X\, \theta \, dA. \]

Here $\mathbf H_ M$ is the geometric mean-curvature vector, equal to $\Delta _ M F$ for the inclusion map. The tangential term records variation of the weight.

Proof

Decompose $X=X^ T+X^\perp $. The smooth divergence identity is $\operatorname {div}_ M X=\operatorname {div}_ M X^ T-\mathbf H_ M\cdot X$. Integration by parts against $\theta $ converts the first term to $-\nabla ^ M\theta \cdot X$. Factor out $\theta $ to obtain the formula. Relative closedness ensures that a compact ambient support meets $M$ in a compact set, so no boundary term occurs. This computes Definition 6.1.3 explicitly and complements the stationarity calculation in [Minter], Lemma 0.7. $\square$

Example 6.5.6 (Generalized mean curvature can have a tangential part).09CF92107610 On the same plane $P$, use the smooth positive density $\theta (x)=e^{x_1}$. The weight is locally finite and the generalized mean curvature in Definition 6.1.3 is $e_1$, entirely tangent to $P$. This varifold is rectifiable but not integral. Thus normality of generalized mean curvature is not a consequence of that definition for every rectifiable varifold; additional hypotheses, such as those in the integral-varifold theory, matter.
