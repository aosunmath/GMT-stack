4.1 Finite perimeter and reduced boundary5526969B30CA

5526969B30CA

A topological boundary can change drastically when a null set is added. The distributional derivative of the characteristic function provides a boundary notion that depends only on the measurable set. Fleming describes the development of this approach in [Fle16], Section 3, pp. 3–4. The precise statements below follow [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 2, and Chapter 3, Section 4.

Definition 4.1.1 (Locally bounded variation).4FF0E8DA6480 For $u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ and an open set $W\subset U$, put

\[ |Du|(W)=\sup \left\{ \int _ W u\, \operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n: X\in C_ c^1(W;\mathbb {R}^ n),\ |X|\leq 1\right\} . \]

We say $u\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ if this quantity is finite for every $W\Subset U$. Equivalently, its distributional derivative $Du$ is a vector-valued Radon measure, characterized by

\[ \int _ U u\, \operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n=-\int _ U X\cdot dDu. \]

The notation $|Du|$ then denotes its total variation measure. Proposition 4.6.1 proves this equivalence and identifies the measure with the displayed supremum. We write $u\in BV(U)$ when $u\in L^1(U)$ and $|Du|(U)<\infty $. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 2, pp. 52–53.

Definition 4.1.2 (Finite perimeter).4E14BC795E12 A Lebesgue measurable set $E\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ has locally finite perimeter in $U$ if $\chi _ E\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. Its perimeter in an open set $W\subset U$ is

\[ P(E;W)=|D\chi _ E|(W). \]

It has finite perimeter in $U$ if $P(E;U)<\infty $; this does not require $\mathcal{L}^ n(E\cap U)<\infty $. Sets equal almost everywhere have the same distributional derivative and perimeter. See [Sim18], Chapter 3, p. 96.

Proposition 4.1.3 (Lower semicontinuity of perimeter).D59D0CB3A71F Suppose $\chi _{E_ j}\to \chi _ E$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. For every open $W\subset U$,

\[ P(E;W)\leq \liminf _{j\to \infty }P(E_ j;W), \]

where the variational definition permits value $+\infty $ even before local finite perimeter is known.

Proof

For every $X\in C_ c^1(W;\mathbb {R}^ n)$ with $|X|\leq 1$, local $L^1$ convergence and boundedness of $\operatorname {div}X$ give

\[ \int _ W\chi _ E\operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n =\lim _ j\int _ W\chi _{E_ j}\operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n \leq \liminf _ j P(E_ j;W). \]

Take the supremum over $X$. This is the variational lower semicontinuity used in [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 2. $\square$

Definition 4.1.4 (Reduced boundary and outward normal).55A210572AB4 Let $E$ have locally finite perimeter in $U$. Its reduced boundary $\partial ^*E\cap U$ consists of points $x\in U\cap \operatorname {supp}|D\chi _ E|$ for which

\[ v_ E(x)=\lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{D\chi _ E(B_ r(x))}{|D\chi _ E|(B_ r(x))} \]

exists and has length one. We define the outward measure-theoretic unit normal by $\nu _ E(x)=-v_ E(x)$. For small $r$ the balls lie in $U$; membership in the support ensures that their denominators are positive. This is [Sim18], Chapter 3, equations (4.2)–(4.3), pp. 96–97, with the sign reversed to use the outward normal.

Theorem 4.1.5 (De Giorgi structure and Gauss–Green).DA1827FB809A Let $n\geq 2$ and let $E$ have locally finite perimeter in $U\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$. Then $\partial ^*E\cap U$ is countably $(n-1)$-rectifiable and

\[ |D\chi _ E|=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner (\partial ^*E\cap U), \qquad D\chi _ E=-\nu _ E\, \mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner (\partial ^*E\cap U). \]

Consequently, for every $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb {R}^ n)$,

\[ \int _ E\operatorname {div}X\, d\mathcal{L}^ n =\int _{\partial ^*E\cap U}X\cdot \nu _ E\, d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}. \]

Here $X$ is extended by zero outside $U$. The boundary measures in these identities are taken inside $U$.

Cited structure theorem; its proof remains to be added. See [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, pp. 97–100. The Gauss–Green identity follows immediately from the measure representation and Definition 4.1.1. The historical account in [Fle16], Section 3, uses the same outward-normal convention as this entry.

Example 4.1.6 (Smooth domains).589B34B9A3FE Let $E\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be a bounded open set with compact $C^1$ boundary. The classical divergence theorem identifies

\[ D\chi _ E=-\nu _ E\, \mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner \partial E, \qquad P(E;U)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial E\cap U). \]

The continuous outward normal has unit length, so the variation of the displayed vector measure is exactly the displayed surface measure. Thus the variational perimeter agrees with the usual boundary area. This also checks the sign in Theorem 4.1.5.
