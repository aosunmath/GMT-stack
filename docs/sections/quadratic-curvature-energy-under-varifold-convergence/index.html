6.12 Quadratic curvature energy under varifold convergenceED93D2FED53A

ED93D2FED53A

The first-variation convention is Definition 6.1.3, based on [Sim18], Chapter 8, equations (2.3)–(2.10), pp. 239–240. A singular boundary part must not be discarded when discussing continuity of curvature energy. We formulate an extended energy through smooth ambient tests and prove its lower semicontinuity using the representation results already established in the measure chapter.

Definition 6.12.1 (An extended quadratic first-variation energy).A25C61F4660D For an $m$-varifold $V$ in an open set $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, with weight $\mu =\| V\| $, define

\[ \mathcal E_ U(V)=\sup _{X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N)} \left\{ \delta V(X)-\int _ U|X|^2d\mu \right\} \in [0,+\infty ]. \]

The zero test makes the supremum nonnegative. This definition uses the entire first variation. In dimension two it agrees with one quarter of the squared generalized mean curvature when that curvature represents the first variation without a singular part and is globally square integrable, as proved next.

Theorem 6.12.2 (Finite quadratic energy is equivalent to square-integrable mean curvature).03E7797CCA57 The quantity $\mathcal E_ U(V)$ is finite if and only if there is $H\in L^2(\mu ;\mathbb R^ N)$ such that

\[ \delta V(X)=-\int _ UH\cdot X\, d\mu \quad \text{for every }X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N). \]

In that case $H$ is unique up to $\mu $-null sets and

\[ \mathcal E_ U(V)=\frac14\int _ U|H|^2d\mu . \]

The first variation therefore has no singular boundary part in $U$. This is a dual formulation of the mean-curvature representation in [Sim18], pp. 239–240; the $L^2$ equivalence is proved here.

Proof

Suppose $E=\mathcal E_ U(V)<\infty $. For a fixed test $X$, applying the definition to $tX$ for every real $t$ gives

\[ t\delta V(X)-t^2\| X\| _{L^2(\mu )}^2\leq E. \]

If $\| X\| _2=0$, this forces $\delta V(X)=0$. Otherwise optimize the quadratic polynomial in $t$ to obtain $|\delta V(X)|\leq 2\sqrt E\| X\| _2$. Thus the first variation is well defined and bounded on the $L^2$ equivalence classes of smooth compactly supported fields.

These fields are dense in $L^2(\mu ;\mathbb R^ N)$. Indeed Theorem 2.3.8 approximates each component by a continuous compactly supported function. Extending such a function by zero and mollifying gives uniform smooth approximation with support in a fixed compact neighborhood inside $U$; local finiteness of $\mu $ converts uniform convergence to $L^2$ convergence. The bounded functional extends to the Hilbert space. Lemma 5.7.1 gives a unique vector field $-H$ representing it, with $\| H\| _2\leq 2\sqrt E$.

Conversely, if such $H$ exists, completion of the square gives

\[ \delta V(X)-\int |X|^2d\mu =\frac14\int |H|^2d\mu -\int |X+H/2|^2d\mu . \]

The right side is bounded above by the claimed energy. Smooth compactly supported approximations to $-H/2$ in $L^2$ make the last term tend to zero, proving equality. The same density proves uniqueness. The representing vector measure $-H\mu $ is locally finite by Cauchy–Schwarz and local finiteness of $\mu $, and is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu $, so there is no additional singular first-variation measure. $\square$

Theorem 6.12.3 (Lower semicontinuity of quadratic varifold energy).365150F73639 If $V_ j\rightharpoonup V$ as varifolds in $U$, then

\[ \mathcal E_ U(V)\leq \liminf _{j\to \infty }\mathcal E_ U(V_ j). \]

In particular, if the liminf is finite, the limit has a generalized mean curvature in $L^2(\| V\| )$ representing its entire first variation, with the corresponding squared-norm bound. This argument uses the first-variation formula of [Sim18], equation (2.3), and does not assume integrality.

Proof

For a fixed $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N)$, both $(x,S)\mapsto \operatorname {div}_ SX(x)$ and $(x,S)\mapsto |X(x)|^2$ are continuous with compact support on the Grassmann bundle. Thus

\[ \delta V_ j(X)-\int |X|^2d\| V_ j\| \longrightarrow \delta V(X)-\int |X|^2d\| V\| . \]

Each term before the limit is bounded above by $\mathcal E_ U(V_ j)$. For every fixed test the limiting expression is therefore at most the liminf of the energies. Taking the supremum over tests proves the inequality, including an infinite liminf. If it is finite, apply Theorem 6.12.2 to identify the limit curvature and its norm. The proof does not interchange a supremum with a limit as an equality. $\square$

Proposition 6.12.4 (Comparison with the energy of a smooth immersion).DF57C01B9F0F Let $F:M^2\to \mathbb R^ N$ be a smooth closed immersion, and let $V_ F$ be its induced varifold with parameter multiplicity. Then

\[ \mathcal E_{\mathbb R^ N}(V_ F)\leq \mathcal W(F). \]

For an embedding there is equality. For every compact set $K\subset U$ and any varifold of finite quadratic energy,

\[ \| \delta V\| (K)\leq 2\sqrt{\mathcal E_ U(V)}\sqrt{\| V\| (K)}. \]
Proof

The smooth first variation gives $\delta V_ F(X)=-\int _ M\vec H_ F\cdot X(F)d\mu _ M$, and the weight identity gives $\int |X|^2d\| V_ F\| =\int _ M|X(F)|^2d\mu _ M$. Completing the square on the parameter surface bounds their difference by $\frac14\int _ M|\vec H_ F|^2$. Take the supremum. For an embedding, the geometric mean curvature defines a vector field on the image representing the entire first variation, so Theorem 6.12.2 gives equality. The weaker assertion for an arbitrary immersion suffices here and avoids requiring a separate identification of curvatures on coincident sheets.

For the last estimate, finite energy represents the first variation by $-H\| V\| $. Its total variation on $K$ is $\int _ K|H|d\| V\| $. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds this by $\sqrt{\| V\| (K)}\| H\| _{L^2(U)}$, and the norm identity gives the claimed constant. The same argument applies to relatively compact Borel sets. $\square$

Corollary 6.12.5 (An integral compactness consequence, with its external input).71DC1770F6D7 Let $V_ j$ be integral two-varifolds in $U$ with locally uniformly bounded weights and $\sup _ j\mathcal E_ U(V_ j)<\infty $. Then a subsequence converges to an integral two-varifold $V$, and $\mathcal E_ U(V)\leq \liminf _ j\mathcal E_ U(V_ j)$ after taking a subsequence attaining the energy liminf. The integrality conclusion uses the externally proved Allard compactness theorem 6.2.1; it is not a consequence of the Hilbert-space duality alone.

Proof

First pass to a subsequence whose energies tend to the liminf. Proposition 6.12.4 bounds the total variation of $\delta V_ j$ uniformly on each relatively compact set from the assumed weight and energy bounds. Apply Theorem 6.2.1, whose hypotheses are now verified, to obtain an integral varifold subsequential limit. Theorem 6.12.3 supplies the energy inequality. This proves the deduction conditional on the explicitly named external compactness theorem; it does not replace its geometric proof. $\square$

Corollary 6.12.6 (A bounded-energy sequence of closed surfaces has local compactness).12AB1072F745 Let $F_ j:M_ j^2\to \mathbb R^ N$ be smooth closed embedded surfaces with $\mathcal W(F_ j)\leq \Lambda $. Their induced varifolds have a subsequence converging locally to an integral two-varifold $V$ with $\mathcal E_{\mathbb R^ N}(V)\leq \Lambda $. Its weight satisfies

\[ \| V\| (B_ r(y))\leq \Lambda r^2 \]

for every ball, and has no atoms. The limit is allowed to be zero. Integrality again uses Theorem 6.2.1 externally.

Proof

Theorem 10.5.4 gives uniform mass bounds on every ball and hence on every compact set. Proposition 6.12.4 identifies the energy of each embedding with its classical Willmore energy. Corollary 6.12.5 gives the claimed limit and energy bound. Weak varifold convergence implies weak convergence of the weights, since the Grassmannian is compact. The open-set lower semicontinuity of Radon measures then yields $\| V\| (B_ r(y))\leq \liminf _ j\| V_ j\| (B_ r(y))\leq \Lambda r^2$. Let $r\downarrow 0$ to get $\| V\| (\{ y\} )=0$. No fixed area lower bound, tightness at infinity or nonvanishing limit was assumed. $\square$
