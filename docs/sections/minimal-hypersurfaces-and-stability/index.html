7.1 Minimal hypersurfaces and stability1DCFE80CFBCA

1DCFE80CFBCA

In the variational chapters we write $M^{n+1}$ for the ambient manifold and $\Sigma ^ n$ for a hypersurface. Thus $n$ is the hypersurface dimension. A surface has $n=2$. These conventions replace the Euclidean dimension letters used in the foundational chapters. The smooth theory connects the first variation of varifolds with elliptic equations and Morse index. Sources for this section are Marques' lectures, recorded by Montezuma, [MM], Sections 2 and 6, and Chodosh [ChMin], Sections 7 and 23.

Definition 7.1.1 (Mean curvature and minimality).28E53582FC82 For a smooth immersed hypersurface $\Sigma \subset M$, let

\[ B(X,Y)=(\nabla ^ M_ XY)^\perp ,\qquad \vec H=\operatorname {tr}_{T\Sigma }B. \]

We call $\Sigma $ minimal when $\vec H=0$. If a global unit normal $\nu $ exists, $\Sigma $ is two-sided. Our scalar convention is $H=\operatorname {div}_{\Sigma }\nu $, so that $\vec H=-H\nu $. With the outward normal, a Euclidean sphere of radius $R$ has $H=n/R$. Here $H$ is the sum of principal curvatures; in the Willmore energy we explicitly use their average. See [MM], Section 2, and [ChMin], Section 7.

Proposition 7.1.2 (First variation of a closed hypersurface).571E6CF1470F Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, compact, and without boundary. If $F_ t$ is a smooth variation with velocity $X$, then

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\operatorname {Area}(F_ t(\Sigma )) =\int _\Sigma \operatorname {div}_\Sigma X\, dA =-\int _\Sigma \langle \vec H,X\rangle \, dA. \]

For $X=f\nu $ this equals $\int _\Sigma Hf\, dA$. This is the smooth counterpart of the varifold first variation. Compare [MM], Theorems 2.3 and 2.6.

Proof

In local coordinates, differentiating the induced metric gives $\dot g_{ij}=\langle \nabla _ iX,e_ j\rangle +\langle e_ i,\nabla _ jX\rangle $. The identity $\frac{d}{dt}\sqrt{\det g}=\frac12\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1}\dot g)\sqrt{\det g}$ therefore gives $\dot{dA}=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X\, dA$. Decompose $X=X^ T+X^\perp $. Differentiating $\langle X^\perp ,e_ i\rangle =0$ along an orthonormal tangent frame yields $\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X^ T-\langle \vec H,X\rangle $. The tangential divergence integrates to zero because $\Sigma $ has no boundary. Finally substitute $\vec H=-H\nu $. $\square$

Theorem 7.1.3 (Second variation in the two-sided case).30FBA471ADE5 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset (M^{n+1},g)$ be a smooth, closed, two-sided minimal hypersurface. For a smooth normal variation with initial velocity $f\nu $, the second derivative of area is

\[ Q(f,f)=\int _\Sigma \bigl (|\nabla _\Sigma f|^2-(|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu ))f^2\bigr )\, dA. \]

With $\Delta _\Sigma =\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nabla _\Sigma $, define $J=\Delta _\Sigma +|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )$; then $Q(f,f)=-\int _\Sigma fJf\, dA$. Source: [MM], Theorem 6.2, specialized to a closed hypersurface; also [ChMin], Section 23.

Proof

Differentiate the first-variation formula $\frac d{dt}A(\Sigma _ t)=\int _{\Sigma _ t}H_ tf_ t\, dA_ t$. At a minimal initial hypersurface, the terms from differentiating $f_ t$ and $dA_ t$ vanish because $H_0=0$. Thus $A''(0)=\int _\Sigma f\dot H$. The Riemannian calculation in Proposition 11.3.4, which does not use the second variation, gives $\dot H=-\Delta _\Sigma f-(|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu ))f$. Integration by parts on the closed hypersurface proves the formula. A tangential initial velocity contributes the tangential derivative of $H_0=0$, so only the normal speed enters. The same proof works for variations compactly supported away from a boundary. $\square$

Definition 7.1.4 (Stability and Morse index).6E18930B316D For the hypersurface in Theorem 7.1.3, stability means $Q(f,f)\geq 0$ for every smooth $f$. The Morse index is the largest dimension of a linear subspace on which $Q$ is negative definite. A Jacobi field is a normal field in the kernel of the Jacobi operator. For a one-sided hypersurface, use sections of its normal line bundle, equivalently the appropriate equivariant functions on the two-sided cover. These are unrestricted area variations; CMC stability will use a volume constraint. See [MM], Section 9, and [MNIndex], Section 1.

Corollary 7.1.5 (Positive Ricci curvature gives an unstable direction).DAFF74D119A8 If $\operatorname {Ric}_ M>0$, every nonempty smooth closed two-sided minimal hypersurface has Morse index at least one.

Proof

Put $f=1$ in Theorem 7.1.3. The gradient term vanishes, while $|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )>0$ everywhere. Consequently $Q(1,1)<0$, and the span of the constant function is a negative direction. $\square$
