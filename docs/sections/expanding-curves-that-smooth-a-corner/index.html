16.5 Expanding curves that smooth a cornerEC3FC5C9B6AA

EC3FC5C9B6AA

Expanding solitons give nonstationary examples defined for positive times and converging to a nonsmooth initial cone. We construct an even convex graphical family directly. The primary comparison is [Hal12], Section 6, where expanding planar curves are studied in arc-length coordinates. Here the normalization is $\vec H=x^\perp /2$, so the time family is $\sqrt t\, \Sigma $.

Proposition 16.5.1 (The expander graph equation and its positive defect).19BF76D4A84D A graph $y=u(x)$ in $\mathbb R^2$ satisfies the scale-one expander equation exactly when

\[ u''=\frac12(1+(u')^2)(u-xu'). \]

If $u(0)=a>0$ and $u'(0)=0$, set $p=u'$ and $q=u-xp$. On every interval of existence with $x\geq 0$,

\[ p'=\tfrac 12(1+p^2)q,\qquad q'=-xp',\qquad q(x)=a\exp \left(-\frac12\int _0^ x s(1+p(s)^2)\, ds\right)>0. \]

In particular $p'>0$ and $0<q(x)\leq ae^{-x^2/4}$.

Proof

For the upward normal $\nu =(-p,1)/\sqrt{1+p^2}$, the coefficient of the mean curvature vector along $\nu $ is $u''/(1+p^2)^{3/2}$, while $\langle (x,u),\nu \rangle =(u-xp)/\sqrt{1+p^2}$. Their equality with factor one half gives the ODE. Differentiate $q=u-xp$ to obtain $q'=-xp'=-x(1+p^2)q/2$. Solving this scalar linear equation with $q(0)=a$ gives its positive exponential expression and Gaussian upper bound. The scalar convention $H=\operatorname {div}\nu $ is the negative of the coefficient of $\vec H$ here. $\square$

Lemma 16.5.2 (The expanding graph has bounded slope).959A9F743DAB For each $a>0$, the initial-value solution in Proposition 16.5.1 extends smoothly to all $x\geq 0$. For every $\varepsilon >0$ in its interval of existence and $x\geq \varepsilon $,

\[ 0<p(x)\leq p(\varepsilon )+\frac{q(\varepsilon )}\varepsilon . \]

It therefore has a finite positive limiting slope $\ell _ a=\lim _{x\to \infty }p(x)$.

Proof

The smooth ODE has a unique local solution for these initial data. Since $p'>0$ and $q>0$, integration of $q'=-xp'$ gives

\[ q(\varepsilon )-q(x)=\int _\varepsilon ^ x s p'(s)\, ds \geq \varepsilon (p(x)-p(\varepsilon )). \]

This proves the bound. Were the maximal endpoint finite and positive, choose a fixed positive $\varepsilon $ below it. The bound controls $p$, while $0<q\leq a$ controls $u=q+xp$ on that finite interval. Smooth ODE continuation then crosses the endpoint, a contradiction. Monotonicity and boundedness of $p$ give its finite limit, which is positive since $p(x)>0$ for $x>0$. $\square$

Theorem 16.5.3 (A complete convex expander asymptotic to two rays).56C7AF70B331 For every $a>0$, there is a unique smooth even solution $u_ a:\mathbb R\to \mathbb R$ of the expander graph equation with $u_ a(0)=a$ and $u_ a'(0)=0$. Its graph is properly embedded, complete, and strictly convex. For a finite $\ell _ a>0$,

\[ u_ a(x)-\ell _ a|x|\longrightarrow 0\quad (|x|\to \infty ), \qquad 0<u_ a(x)-\ell _ a|x|\leq a. \]

Its two asymptotic rays meet at the origin. This constructs a family; it does not assert that a prescribed cone angle determines a unique expander among arbitrary immersions.

Proof

The ODE is invariant under $x\mapsto -x$, so local uniqueness makes the solution even; Lemma 16.5.2 extends both halves. Positivity of $q$ gives $u_ a''>0$. As an entire smooth graph it is proper, embedded, and complete. Write $\ell =\ell _ a$. Boundedness of $p$ and the Gaussian bound for $q$ give $0<p'(x)\leq Ce^{-x^2/4}$, hence for $x\geq 1$,

\[ 0<\ell -p(x)\leq C\int _ x^\infty e^{-s^2/4}\, ds \leq \frac{2C}{x}e^{-x^2/4}. \]

Since $u_ a-x\ell =q-x(\ell -p)$, both terms tend to zero. Its derivative is $p-\ell <0$ for $x\geq 0$; thus it decreases from $a$ to zero and is strictly positive. Reflecting gives the negative half. These properties agree with the expanding-curve models in [Hal12], Section 6, after converting its dilation normalization. $\square$

Proposition 16.5.4 (A smooth flow converging uniformly to a corner).643DB83B70AB Let $u_ a$ be the expander above and define

\[ U(x,t)=\sqrt t\, u_ a(x/\sqrt t),\qquad t>0. \]

Its graphs solve curve shortening flow, and for every $x\in \mathbb R$,

\[ 0<U(x,t)-\ell _ a|x|\leq a\sqrt t. \]

Thus the graph functions converge uniformly on all of $\mathbb R$ to the Lipschitz corner $\ell _ a|x|$ as $t\downarrow 0$. Their products with $\mathbb R^ k$ give hypersurface flows with an initial crease.

Proof

Put $\xi =x/\sqrt t$. Direct differentiation gives $U_ t=(u_ a-\xi u_ a')/(2\sqrt t)$, $U_ x=u_ a'$, and $U_{xx}=u_ a''/\sqrt t$. The expander equation makes $U_ t=U_{xx}/(1+U_ x^2)$. The bound follows by multiplying the height excess in Theorem 16.5.3 by $\sqrt t$. Proposition 16.3.2 handles products. This is an explicit classical solution for $t>0$ with an initial trace; no uniqueness theorem for arbitrary weak flows from that trace is used. $\square$

Proposition 16.5.5 (Total curvature and maximum curvature of the expander).F456929A84ED The expander graph has finite total curvature

\[ \int _\Sigma |\kappa |\, ds=2\arctan \ell _ a<\pi . \]

Its maximum curvature is $a/2$, attained at its tip. Along its expanding flow the maximum is $a/(2\sqrt t)$.

Proof

Since $u_ a''>0$, the curvature magnitude is $p'/(1+p^2)^{3/2}$ and $ds=\sqrt{1+p^2}\, dx$. Their product is $(\arctan p)'dx$. The limiting slopes are $-\ell _ a$ and $\ell _ a$, proving the integral. By the equation, $|\kappa |=q/(2\sqrt{1+p^2})$. On $x>0$, $q$ strictly decreases and $p$ strictly increases, so the curvature decreases from $q(0)/2=a/2$. Evenness handles the other half. Dilation by $\sqrt t$ divides curvature by $\sqrt t$. $\square$
