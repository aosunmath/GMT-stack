6.4 Excess and Allard regularity7EB7A8003449

7EB7A8003449

Being close to a plane has several meanings. Height excess measures distance from the plane, tilt excess measures tangent directions, and a mass-ratio bound controls multiplicity. These quantities cannot be substituted for one another without a theorem.

Definition 6.4.1 (Height and tilt excess).2C463D5AB6FB Let $V$ be an $n$-varifold in $B_ R(a)\subset \mathbb R^{n+k}$ and $P$ a linear $n$-plane. With $\pi _ S$ denoting orthogonal projection onto $S$, set

\[ E_ h(V,a,R,P)^2=R^{-n-2}\int _{B_ R(a)}\operatorname {dist}(x,a+P)^2\, d\| V\| (x), \]
\[ E_ t(V,a,R,P)^2=R^{-n}\int _{G_ n(B_ R(a))}|\pi _ S-\pi _ P|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\, dV(x,S). \]

Here $|\cdot |_{\mathrm{HS}}$ is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. These are dimensionless quantities; our tilt normalization is fixed by this formula and need not equal another author's scalar-normal convention. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 5, and [Minter], Sections 2–3.

Proposition 6.4.2 (Excess is invariant under simultaneous rescaling).0938FA23F7B1 For $V_{a,R}$ as in Definition 6.3.1,

\[ E_ h(V,a,R,P)=E_ h(V_{a,R},0,1,P),\qquad E_ t(V,a,R,P)=E_ t(V_{a,R},0,1,P). \]

If the first variation is entirely represented by a mean-curvature field $H\in L^ p(\| V\| )$, its rescaled field is $H_ R(y)=R H(a+Ry)$, and

\[ \left(\int _{B_1}|H_ R|^ p\, d\| V_{a,R}\| \right)^{1/p} =R^{1-n/p}\left(\int _{B_ R(a)}|H|^ p\, d\| V\| \right)^{1/p}. \]
Proof

The mass factor is $R^{-n}$, distances scale by $R^{-1}$, and planes are unchanged. Substitute into the excess definitions. For the curvature formula, use the first-variation scaling from Proposition 6.3.2 and the identity $\delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot X\, d\| V\| $. The last equality is then a change of variables. $\square$

Theorem 6.4.3 (Allard regularity with supercritical mean curvature).AB385BD66E1E Fix $n,k\geq 1$ and $n<p<\infty $. There are $\varepsilon >0$ and $\gamma \in (0,1)$, depending only on $n,k,p$, with the following property. Let $V$ be an integral $n$-varifold in an open neighborhood of $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset \mathbb R^{n+k}$, with $a\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| $, and suppose its first variation in $B_ R(a)$ has no singular part:

\[ \delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot X\, d\| V\| \quad (X\in C_ c^1(B_ R(a);\mathbb R^{n+k})). \]

Assume $H\in L^ p(\| V\| \llcorner B_ R(a))$ and

\[ \frac{\| V\| (B_ R(a))}{\omega _ n R^ n}\leq 1+\varepsilon , \qquad R^{1-n/p}\left(\int _{B_ R(a)}|H|^ p\, d\| V\| \right)^{1/p}\leq \varepsilon . \]

Then in $B_{\gamma R}(a)$ the support of $V$ is a single $C^{1,1-n/p}$ graph over an $n$-plane, and $V$ has multiplicity one there. This is the integral-varifold specialization of [Sim18], Chapter 5, hypotheses (5.1) and Theorem 5.2, pp. 145–146, following [Allard72]. The source's density-lower-bound hypothesis is supplied here by integrality almost everywhere. The proof, including affine approximation and excess decay, remains external. General bounded first variation with a singular boundary term does not satisfy the stated hypothesis.

Corollary 6.4.4 (Density one forces a regular point).B253B69E9E98 If $V$ is stationary and integral and $\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)=1$, then $a$ is regular with multiplicity one. In particular one multiplicity-one planar tangent cone is enough to imply regularity and uniqueness of the tangent cone at $a$.

Proof

At a sufficiently small radius the mass ratio is at most $1+\varepsilon $, while the mean-curvature term is zero. Theorem 6.4.3 gives a $C^{1,\alpha }$ single graph. The stationary graph equation and elliptic bootstrapping make this graph smooth; this stationary consequence is also recorded in [Minter], Corollary 3.2. A multiplicity-one planar tangent cone has density one by Theorem 6.3.6. At a smooth point every blow-up is the ordinary tangent plane with its multiplicity. $\square$

Theorem 6.4.5 (A density gap above one).5DBE38E6CA27 There is $\delta =\delta (n,k)>0$ such that every support point $a$ of a stationary integral $n$-varifold in $\mathbb R^{n+k}$ with $\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)<1+\delta $ is regular with multiplicity one. Equivalently, every singular point has density at least $1+\delta $. This is [Minter], Corollary 3.2, pp. 44–45. Its compactness argument for cones and application of Allard regularity remain external.

Example 6.4.6 (Zero excess does not specify multiplicity).2D4CD27BD1E5 For $V=q|P|$ with integer $q\geq 2$, both excesses relative to $P$ vanish at every scale centered on $P$, but the mass ratio and density are $q$. Thus zero height and tilt excess do not imply a single sheet with multiplicity one. This varifold is nevertheless regular under Definition 6.3.5; high multiplicity and singular support are different issues.
