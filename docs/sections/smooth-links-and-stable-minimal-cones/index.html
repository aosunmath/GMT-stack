7.9 Smooth links and stable minimal cones0464EFF09404

0464EFF09404

Cone stability combines a spherical operator with a one-dimensional Hardy inequality. Here $n\geq 2$ is the cone dimension; its link has dimension $n-1$ and lies in the unit sphere $\mathbb S^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$. We assume a smooth closed connected embedded two-sided link, so the cone is smooth away from its vertex. These hypotheses exclude more general cones with singular links. Sources are [ChMin], Section 26, pp. 64–68, and the Simons identities in [HP99], Section 2.

Definition 7.9.1 (The cone over a spherical hypersurface).016DAF7407ED For a link $L\subset \mathbb S^ n$ as above, set

\[ C^*(L)=\{ rp:r>0,\ p\in L\} ,\qquad C(L)=C^*(L)\cup \{ 0\} . \]

The normal $\nu _ L$ to $L$ within the sphere extends to the cone by $\nu _ C(rp)=\nu _ L(p)$. The point $0$ is not included in the smooth domain $C^*(L)$. This is the cone parametrization used in [ChMin], Section 26.

Proposition 7.9.2 (Metric, curvature, and Laplacian of a cone).F3105662B738 Under $F(r,p)=rp$,

\[ g_ C=dr^2+r^2g_ L,\qquad dA_ C=r^{n-1}dr\, dA_ L, \qquad H_ C=r^{-1}H_ L,\qquad |A_ C|^2=r^{-2}|A_ L|^2, \]
\[ \Delta _ C v=\partial _{rr}v+\frac{n-1}{r}\partial _ rv+r^{-2}\Delta _ Lv. \]

In particular $C^*(L)$ is minimal precisely when $L$ is minimal in the unit sphere.

Proof

The radial vector is $p$ and an angular coordinate vector is $r\partial _ ip$. They are orthogonal and have the displayed metric. The normal is independent of $r$, so the radial principal curvature is zero; angular principal curvatures are those of the spherical link divided by $r$. The Euclidean derivative of $\nu _ L$ in a link direction has no radial component, because $\langle \nu _ L,p\rangle =\langle \nu _ L,\partial _ ip\rangle =0$. This gives the mean-curvature and norm formulas. The determinant of the metric is $r^{2(n-1)}\det g_ L$; the coordinate divergence formula for the Laplacian yields the last identity. Compare [ChMin], the calculation preceding Proposition 26.1. $\square$

Definition 7.9.3 (Stability away from a cone vertex).7D56079904BC For a minimal link $L$, the cone is stable away from its vertex if

\[ Q_ C(\phi ,\phi )=\int _{C^*(L)} \left(|\nabla _ C\phi |^2-|A_ C|^2\phi ^2\right)dA_ C\geq 0 \quad (\phi \in C_ c^\infty (C^*(L))). \]

Thus every test is supported in an annulus with positive inner radius. This definition does not assert regularity at the vertex or area minimization among integral-current or finite-perimeter competitors. It is the test class in [ChMin], Proposition 26.1.

Lemma 7.9.4 (The sharp radial Hardy inequality).B401AC1A32A6 Let $n\geq 2$ and $a=(n-2)/2$. For $\eta \in C_ c^\infty ((0,\infty ))$,

\[ \int _0^\infty |\eta '|^2r^{n-1}dr \geq a^2\int _0^\infty \eta ^2r^{n-3}dr. \]

The constant $a^2$ is sharp, including the value zero when $n=2$, and equality holds only for $\eta =0$ in this compactly supported class.

Proof

Put $t=\log r$ and $\psi (t)=r^ a\eta (r)$. Direct substitution gives

\[ \int _0^\infty \bigl (|\eta '|^2r^{n-1}-a^2\eta ^2r^{n-3}\bigr )dr =\int _{\mathbb R}|\psi '|^2dt, \qquad \int _0^\infty \eta ^2r^{n-3}dr=\int _{\mathbb R}\psi ^2dt. \]

The cross term integrates to zero because $\psi $ is compactly supported. A zero right side makes $\psi $ constant and thus zero. To prove sharpness, fix a nonzero $\chi \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$ and use $\psi _ T(t)=\chi (t/T)$. Its derivative-energy to $L^2$-norm ratio is $T^{-2}\int |\chi '|^2/\int \chi ^2\to 0$. This is the logarithmic substitution underlying [ChMin], Proposition 26.1, pp. 64–65. $\square$

Definition 7.9.5 (The spherical link Rayleigh quotient).C3BBE589F9AC For a smooth minimal link define

\[ \lambda _0(L)=\inf _{0\ne \psi \in C^\infty (L)} \frac{\int _ L(|\nabla \psi |^2-|A_ L|^2\psi ^2)\, dA_ L}{\int _ L\psi ^2\, dA_ L}. \]

This is the lowest Rayleigh value of $-\Delta _ L-|A_ L|^2$. It omits the ambient Ricci term $n-1$ that appears in the Jacobi operator for $L$ as a hypersurface of $\mathbb S^ n$. Consequently it is not that spherical hypersurface's full stability eigenvalue. See [ChMin], Section 26, p. 64.

Proposition 7.9.6 (The spectral criterion for cone stability).2C4122B9D586 The cone $C^*(L)$ is stable in Definition 7.9.3 if and only if

\[ \lambda _0(L)\geq -\frac{(n-2)^2}{4}. \]

This is [ChMin], Proposition 26.1, attributed there to Simons.

Proof

Write $a=(n-2)/2$, $t=\log r$, and $v(t,p)=r^ a\phi (r,p)$. The cone metric and the same integration by parts as in Lemma 7.9.4 give the exact identity

\[ Q_ C(\phi ,\phi )=\int _{\mathbb R}\int _ L \left(|\partial _ tv|^2+|\nabla _ Lv|^2+(a^2-|A_ L|^2)v^2\right)dA_ Ldt. \]

If $\lambda _0\geq -a^2$, the angular part plus $a^2v^2$ has nonnegative integral at each fixed $t$, proving stability. Conversely, if a smooth $\psi $ has Rayleigh quotient below $-a^2$, choose $v(t,p)=\chi (t/T)\psi (p)$, with nonzero smooth compactly supported $\chi $. After division by $\int \chi (t/T)^2dt$, the positive time-derivative term is $T^{-2}\int |\chi '|^2/\int \chi ^2$ times $\int _ L\psi ^2$. For large $T$ the total form is negative. This contradicts stability and proves necessity without assuming that the Rayleigh infimum is attained. $\square$

Example 7.9.7 (Product-sphere links and the dimension-seven threshold).BD8080CF7EC4 Let $p,q\geq 1$, set $n=p+q+1$, and put

\[ L=\mathbb S^ p\left(\sqrt{\frac p{p+q}}\right) \times \mathbb S^ q\left(\sqrt{\frac q{p+q}}\right)\subset \mathbb S^ n. \]

Writing its radii as $a,b$, a unit normal is $\nu (x,y)=(bx/a,-ay/b)$. Its principal curvatures are $b/a$ with multiplicity $p$ and $-a/b$ with multiplicity $q$. Hence $H_ L=0$ and $|A_ L|^2=p+q=n-1$. The Rayleigh quotient is minimized by constants, so $\lambda _0=-(n-1)$. Proposition 7.9.6 shows that its cone is stable away from zero exactly when $n-1\leq (n-2)^2/4$, or $n\geq 7$ in this family. This is a stability calculation, not a classification of which product cones minimize area. For equal factors with $p=q\geq 3$, Theorem 4.5.8 proves the stronger finite-perimeter minimizing property. Compare the cone examples in [ChMin], Sections 13, 17, and 26.

Theorem 7.9.8 (Simons identity for a minimal spherical hypersurface).8B46D4CA80AE Let $L^ d\subset \mathbb S^{d+1}$ be a smooth two-sided minimal hypersurface of the unit sphere. Its scalar second fundamental form satisfies

\[ \Delta _ L h=(d-|A|^2)h,\qquad \frac12\Delta _ L|A|^2=|\nabla A|^2+d|A|^2-|A|^4. \]

Here $\Delta _ Lh$ is the rough covariant Laplacian. These are the constant-curvature, minimal specializations of [HP99], Corollary 2.2, p. 49; the corresponding norm inequality is [ChMin], Lemma 26.5.

Proof

The Gauss and Codazzi equations follow by decomposing the ambient covariant derivative into its tangential and normal parts. In the unit sphere they give $K_ L(e_ i,e_ k)=1+\kappa _ i\kappa _ k$ in a principal frame and $\nabla _ kh_{ij}=\nabla _ ih_{kj}$. The latter holds because the normal component of the ambient curvature on three tangent vectors is zero. Tracing Codazzi and using $H=0$ gives $\sum _ k\nabla _ kh_{kj}=0$.

At a chosen point use a geodesic orthonormal frame diagonalizing $h$. Commutation on a covariant two-tensor gives

\[ \Delta h_{ij}=\sum _ k\bigl ((\nabla _ k\nabla _ i-\nabla _ i\nabla _ k)h\bigr )_{kj}, \]
\[ \bigl ((\nabla _ k\nabla _ i-\nabla _ i\nabla _ k)h\bigr )(e_ k,e_ j) =-h(R^ L(e_ k,e_ i)e_ k,e_ j)-h(e_ k,R^ L(e_ k,e_ i)e_ j). \]

The diagonal component is therefore

\[ \Delta h_{ii}=\sum _ k(1+\kappa _ i\kappa _ k)(\kappa _ i-\kappa _ k) =d\kappa _ i-\kappa _ i|A|^2, \]

since $\sum _ k\kappa _ k=0$. The off-diagonal components are zero at this point by the same Gauss equation in a principal frame. This proves the tensor identity. Take its inner product with $h$ and apply the product rule to $|A|^2$ to obtain the second identity. The curvature sign is the one fixed in Proposition 11.3.4. $\square$

Proposition 7.9.9 (An upper bound for the nonflat link Rayleigh value).40C07F2B543A For a smooth closed minimal link $L^{n-1}$ with $A_ L$ not identically zero,

\[ \lambda _0(L)\leq -(n-1). \]
Proof

Integrate the spherical Simons identity to get

\[ \int _ L|\nabla A|^2-\int _ L|A|^4 =-(n-1)\int _ L|A|^2. \]

The Lipschitz function $w=|A|$ satisfies $|\nabla w|\leq |\nabla A|$ almost everywhere. Use $w$ in the Rayleigh quotient by smooth approximation, for example $w_\varepsilon =\sqrt{|A|^2+\varepsilon ^2}$ and passage in $W^{1,2}$. Its denominator tends to the positive number $\int |A|^2$, and its numerator is no larger in the limit than the left side above. This proves the bound. The approximation avoids dividing by $|A|$ at its zero set. Compare [ChMin], the proof of Corollary 26.6, pp. 67–68. $\square$

Theorem 7.9.10 (Stable cones with smooth links are flat in dimensions at most six).0574E4F9C647 Let $2\leq n\leq 6$ and let $L^{n-1}\subset \mathbb S^ n$ be a smooth closed connected embedded two-sided minimal hypersurface. If $C^*(L)$ is stable away from its vertex, then $L$ is a great sphere and $C(L)$ is a hyperplane. This is the smooth-link version of the dimension bound in [ChMin], Corollary 26.6.

Proof

If $A_ L$ is not identically zero, the previous proposition and the stability criterion give

\[ -\frac{(n-2)^2}{4}\leq \lambda _0(L)\leq -(n-1), \]

which is impossible for $2\leq n\leq 6$. Hence $A_ L=0$. Its spherical unit normal has zero Euclidean derivative in every tangent direction, so it is constant on connected $L$. Thus $L$ lies in the equator orthogonal to that normal. It is open in that equator by equality of dimensions, and closed by compactness; hence it is the entire great sphere. The hypotheses include smoothness of the link. Extending this statement to cones with additional singularities requires a separate regularity or dimension-reduction argument. $\square$
