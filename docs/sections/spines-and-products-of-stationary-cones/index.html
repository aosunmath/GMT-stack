6.9 Spines and products of stationary cones84D0DFE4E7F0

84D0DFE4E7F0

Let $C$ be a nonzero stationary integral $m$-cone on $\mathbb R^ N$, where $1\leq m<N$, and write $\mu =\| C\| $ and $\Theta (y)=\Theta ^ m(\mu ,y)$. We develop the argument of [Minter], Lemma 1.5, pp. 13–15. A cone is invariant under dilations as a varifold, including the tangential Jacobian and the recorded planes.

Proposition 6.9.1 (Density under scaling and at infinity).135F8E55ADD3 For every $y\in \mathbb R^ N$ and $s>0$,

\[ \Theta (sy)=\Theta (y),\qquad \lim _{R\to \infty }\frac{\mu (B_ R(y))}{\omega _ mR^ m}=\Theta (0). \]

Consequently $\Theta (y)\leq \Theta (0)$ for every $y$.

Proof

Homogeneity gives $\mu (B_ r(sy))=s^ m\mu (B_{r/s}(y))$. Divide by $\omega _ mr^ m$ and let $r\downarrow 0$. For $R>|y|$, the inclusions

\[ B_{R-|y|}(0)\subset B_ R(y)\subset B_{R+|y|}(0) \]

and $\mu (B_ t(0))=\omega _ mt^ m\Theta (0)$ squeeze the ratio at $y$ between $\Theta (0)(1-|y|/R)^ m$ and $\Theta (0)(1+|y|/R)^ m$. Let $R\to \infty $. Stationary monotonicity, Theorem 6.1.5, bounds the density at $y$ by this limit. $\square$

Proposition 6.9.2 (Maximal density gives another cone vertex).53B7945861D9 For $y\in \mathbb R^ N$, $\Theta (y)=\Theta (0)$ if and only if $C$ is invariant under every homothety $h_{y,s}(x)=y+s(x-y)$, $s>0$.

Proof

If the densities agree, the nondecreasing mass ratio centered at $y$ has equal limits at zero and infinity by Proposition 6.9.1. It is therefore constant. Translate the origin to $y$ and apply Theorem 6.7.4; using reciprocal scale gives precisely the stated homotheties. Conversely these homotheties make every centered ratio at $y$ constant, so its zero and infinite scale limits coincide, again by Proposition 6.9.1. $\square$

Lemma 6.9.3 (Two vertices generate a line of translations).A06E3D122AB7 If a varifold is invariant under $h_{0,s}$ and $h_{y,s}$ for every $s>0$, then it is invariant under $\tau _{ty}(x)=x+ty$ for every real $t$.

Proof

The composition $h_{y,s}\circ h_{0,1/s}$ equals $x\mapsto x+(1-s)y$. Varifold pushforwards compose: the scalar tangential Jacobians multiply, and here $s^ m s^{-m}=1$. Thus every translation with $t<1$ preserves the varifold. The inverse of a preserving translation also preserves it, supplying all remaining values of $t$. This is an identity of full varifolds, not just their supports. $\square$

Proposition 6.9.4 (The spine is the linear set of maximal density).6C0B9465DCAB The spine from Definition 6.3.7 satisfies

\[ S(C)=\{ y:\Theta (y)=\Theta (0)\} . \]

It is a closed linear subspace of dimension at most $m$.

Proof

A preserving translation gives $\mu (B_ r(y))=\mu (B_ r(0))$, hence equality of densities. The reverse implication follows from Proposition 6.9.2 and Lemma 6.9.3. Preserving translations are closed under addition and inverses; the lemma gives all real multiples of any one of them. They form a linear subspace. Closedness also follows from continuity of translations against compactly supported continuous tests, with all supports in a common compact set when the translation vectors converge.

Write $d=\dim S(C)$. The mass $b=\mu (B_1(0))$ is positive, by conical homogeneity and nonzeroness. A lattice in $S(C)$ with separation three has at least $c_ dR^ d$ points in $B_ R(0)$ for large $R$ if $d>0$. The unit balls at these points are disjoint, each has mass $b$, and all lie in $B_{R+1}(0)$. Thus $c_ dbR^ d\leq b(R+1)^ m$, forcing $d\leq m$. The case $d=0$ needs no estimate. $\square$

Lemma 6.9.5 (Factoring a translation-invariant Radon measure).5E59E735DDCC Let $L$ be a $d$-dimensional linear subspace, $d\geq 1$. If a Radon measure $\mu $ on $L^\perp \times L$ is invariant under translations in $L$, there is a unique Radon measure $\nu $ on $L^\perp $ such that

\[ \mu =\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ d. \]

Here orthonormal coordinates fix the Euclidean volume measure on $L$.

Proof

For a bounded Borel set $A\subset L^\perp $, the measure $B\mapsto \mu (A\times B)$ is locally finite and translation invariant on $L$. Such a measure is a constant multiple of $\mathcal L^ d$: partition half-open cubes into equal subcubes, use translation invariance to give them equal mass, and then approximate arbitrary boxes by rational grids. A bounded piece of a coordinate hyperplane has zero mass, since arbitrarily many disjoint translates fit in a fixed bounded box; thus grid boundaries cause no ambiguity. Uniqueness of locally finite measures from boxes extends the equality to all Borel sets.

Let $Q$ be a half-open unit cube in $L$ and define $\nu (A)=\mu (A\times Q)$. This is countably additive and finite on bounded sets, and hence is a Radon measure in Euclidean space. The preceding argument gives $\mu (A\times B)=\nu (A)\mathcal L^ d(B)$ for bounded rectangles. Exhaustion and uniqueness of product measures prove the claim for all Borel sets, as well as uniqueness of $\nu $. $\square$

Lemma 6.9.6 (Spine directions lie in the recorded planes).CCCFFD8D2555 If $L\subset S(C)$, then $L\subset S$ for $C$-almost every $(x,S)$.

Proof

The constant mass ratio at zero and Corollary 6.6.5 give $x\in S$ almost everywhere. Each vector $e$ in a fixed basis of $L$ is another cone vertex by Proposition 6.9.4, so the same argument gives $x-e\in S$ almost everywhere. Intersect the finitely many full-measure sets and subtract to obtain $e\in S$ for every basis vector. $\square$

Proposition 6.9.7 (Integral product splitting along a proper spine subspace).8AEAAFC90D06 Suppose $L\subset S(C)$ and $1\leq d=\dim L<m$. There is a nonzero stationary integral $(m-d)$-cone $C_0$ on $L^\perp $ such that

\[ C=C_0\times |L|. \]

The product records the plane $T_ yC_0\oplus L$ and the multiplicity of $C_0$ at $(y,z)$. The rectifiable coarea input is [Sim18], Chapter 3, (2.8)–(2.9) and Remark 2.10, pp. 93–94; compare the conclusion of [Minter], Lemma 1.5.

Proof

Write $C=V(M,\theta )$. Lemmas 6.9.5 and 6.9.6 give $\mu =\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ d$ and $L\subset T_ xM$ almost everywhere. Thus the tangential Jacobian of the projection $p:M\to L$ is one. Weighted rectifiable coarea, Corollary 3.3.4, expresses $\mu $ as the integral over $z\in L$ of the measures

\[ \nu _ z=\theta (\, \cdot \, ,z)\mathcal H^{m-d}\llcorner \{ y:(y,z)\in M\} . \]

For almost every $z$, this is a locally finite integral rectifiable measure. Local finiteness and integrality follow on a countable compact exhaustion, with $\theta \geq 1$; exceptional sets of zero weighted measure meet almost every fiber in zero weighted measure. Coarea and the product identity give $\int _ B\nu _ z(A)\, dz=\nu (A)\mathcal L^ d(B)$. Apply this to a countable collection of bounded boxes generating the Borel sets of $L^\perp $. It follows that $\nu _ z=\nu $ as measures for almost every $z$.

Choose one such fiber and let $C_0$ be its integral rectifiable lift in $L^\perp $. The area formula on product charts gives a rectifiable product $C_0\times |L|$ with weight $\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ d$ and planes $T_ yC_0\oplus L$. Lemma 6.7.2 therefore identifies this product with $C$; no invariance of individual exceptional fibers was assumed.

For $Y\in C_ c^1(L^\perp ;L^\perp )$ choose $\chi \in C_ c^1(L)$ with $\int \chi =1$ and test with $X(y,z)=\chi (z)Y(y)$. Divergence in the $L$ directions is zero, and product integration gives $0=\delta C(X)=\delta C_0(Y)$. Finally homogeneity of $\mu $ gives $\nu (sA)=s^{m-d}\nu (A)$ by testing rectangles and scaling their $L$ factor. The dilated lift of $C_0$ has the same weight as $C_0$, so Lemma 6.7.2 proves conical invariance. Nonzeroness follows from the product identity. $\square$

Corollary 6.9.8 (A full-dimensional spine forces a multiple plane).5FC3B6942F0A If $\dim S(C)=m$, then $C=q|S(C)|$ for a positive integer $q$.

Proof

Set $L=S(C)$. Lemma 6.9.5 gives $\mu =\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ m$. Homogeneity now implies $\nu (sA)=\nu (A)$ for every $s>0$. The dyadic annuli $\{ 2^{-j-1}\leq |y|<2^{-j}\} $ all have equal $\nu $ mass; their disjoint union lies in a compact ball. Local finiteness forces that mass to be zero. Scaling and exhaustion show $\nu $ is supported at zero, so $\mu =q\mathcal H^ m\llcorner L$ for a finite $q>0$. Rectifiable lift uniqueness identifies $C=q|L|$, and integrality makes $q$ an integer. Equivalently the transverse factor is the zero-dimensional varifold $q\delta _0$. $\square$
