7.13 Power tests and Moser iteration7DA889D6DE36

7DA889D6DE36

We now prove the local-boundedness step used in elliptic regularity. Unless stated otherwise, $n\geq 2$, $A$ is measurable and symmetric, and $\lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I$ with $0<\lambda \leq \Lambda $. A weak subsolution of $\operatorname {div}(A Du)=0$ satisfies $\int A Du\cdot D\phi \leq 0$ for nonnegative compactly supported tests. There are no lower-order terms in this section. The source is [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Theorem 1 and its preparatory lemmas, printed pp. 199–205.

Theorem 7.13.1 (The Sobolev gain used in iteration).B8CE044300F3 For $n\geq 2$, put $\chi =n/(n-2)$ if $n>2$, and $\chi =2$ if $n=2$. There is $S_ n<\infty $ such that every $w\in H_0^1(B_2\subset \mathbb R^ n)$ satisfies

\[ \left(\int _{B_2}|w|^{2\chi }\right)^{1/\chi } \leq S_ n\int _{B_2}|Dw|^2. \]

See [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Lemma 2, pp. 200–201, and Corollary 1, p. 202. The choice $n/(n-2)$ is not used in dimension two.

Proof

Corollary 7.14.6, proved from the coordinate-projection Holder inequality and the first-power Sobolev estimate, gives the assertion with $R=2$. Its radius factor is fixed and can be included in $S_ n$. In dimension two the proof uses the finite-power inequality of Theorem 7.14.5 with $q=4$. $\square$

Lemma 7.13.2 (Positive parts preserve the subsolution inequality).B951D0F32E6B If $v\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ is a weak subsolution for the coefficients fixed above, then $(v-k)_+$ is a weak subsolution for every constant $k$. If $v$ is a weak solution, both $v_+$ and $(-v)_+$ are nonnegative weak subsolutions.

Proof

Choose smooth convex nondecreasing approximations $\beta _\varepsilon $ to the positive-part function, with $0\leq \beta '_\varepsilon \leq 1$ and $\beta ''_\varepsilon \geq 0$. Test the subsolution inequality with $\eta \beta '_\varepsilon (v-k)$ for $0\leq \eta \in C_ c^\infty (U)$. This is an admissible $H_0^1$ test; approximation by nonnegative smooth tests is justified because $A Dv$ is locally square integrable. The chain rule gives

\[ \int \beta '_\varepsilon (v-k)A Dv\cdot D\eta \leq -\int \eta \beta ''_\varepsilon (v-k)A Dv\cdot Dv\leq 0. \]

The Sobolev chain rule and the fact that $Dv=0$ almost everywhere on a level set give $\beta _\varepsilon (v-k)\to (v-k)_+$ in $H^1$ on compact subsets. Pass to the limit to get the required inequality. These elementary Sobolev composition and approximation facts are the functional-analytic inputs. For a solution, $-v$ is also a solution; apply the first assertion with $k=0$ to both signs. The constant shift is legitimate because the equation has no zero-order term. $\square$

Lemma 7.13.3 (Energy estimate for truncated powers).7066CAF042C4 Let $u\geq 0$ be an $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ weak subsolution, let $p\geq 2$, $L>0$, and set

\[ u_ L=\min \{ u,L\} ,\qquad v_ L=u\, u_ L^{p/2-1}. \]

For $p=2$ interpret $v_ L=u$. For every nonnegative smooth compactly supported cutoff $\eta $,

\[ \int |D(\eta v_ L)|^2 \leq C p^2(\Lambda /\lambda )^2\int v_ L^2|D\eta |^2, \]

where $C$ is an absolute constant independent of $p,L,u$.

Proof

For fixed $L$, the functions $s\mapsto s\min (s,L)^{p-2}$ and $s\mapsto s\min (s,L)^{p/2-1}$ are Lipschitz on $[0,\infty )$, with the $p=2$ case interpreted separately. Thus $\eta ^2u u_ L^{p-2}$ is an admissible nonnegative $H_0^1$ test, even though no boundedness of $u$ has been proved. The term obtained by differentiating $u_ L^{p-2}$ is nonnegative by ellipticity. Dropping it and using Young's inequality gives

\[ \int \eta ^2 u_ L^{p-2}|Du|^2 \leq 4(\Lambda /\lambda )^2\int u^2u_ L^{p-2}|D\eta |^2. \]

On $\{ u<L\} $ the derivative of $v_ L=u^{p/2}$ is $(p/2)u^{p/2-1}Du$, whereas on $\{ u>L\} $ it is $L^{p/2-1}Du$. Consequently

\[ |Dv_ L|^2\leq (p^2/4)u_ L^{p-2}|Du|^2 \]

almost everywhere. Combining this with $|D(\eta v_ L)|^2\leq 2\eta ^2|Dv_ L|^2+2v_ L^2|D\eta |^2$ proves the estimate. Truncation gives an $H^1$ function with bounded value, not a claim of bounded gradient; this distinction matters in the source's power-test calculation, [SimonPDE], pp. 202–203. $\square$

Lemma 7.13.4 (One step of integrability improvement).C8B26982BF2A Let $u\geq 0$ be a weak subsolution on $B_2$, and suppose $u\in L^ p(B_ s)$ for $p\geq 2$ and $1\leq r<s\leq 2$. With $\chi $ as in Theorem 7.13.1,

\[ \| u\| _{L^{p\chi }(B_ r)} \leq \left(\frac{C p^2}{(s-r)^2}\right)^{1/p}\| u\| _{L^ p(B_ s)}, \]

where $C$ depends only on $n$ and $\Lambda /\lambda $.

Proof

Choose $\eta =1$ on $B_ r$, supported in $B_ s$, with $|D\eta |\leq C/(s-r)$. Apply the Sobolev gain to $\eta v_ L$ and then Lemma 7.13.3. The right side is bounded by $Cp^2(s-r)^{-2}\int _{B_ s}u^ p$, because $v_ L^2=u^2u_ L^{p-2}\leq u^ p$. As $L\to \infty $, Fatou's lemma gives

\[ \left(\int _{B_ r}u^{p\chi }\right)^{1/\chi } \leq \frac{Cp^2}{(s-r)^2}\int _{B_ s}u^ p. \]

Taking the $p$th root proves the claim. In particular the higher integrability is a conclusion of the argument, not an assumption permitting an untruncated power test. $\square$

Theorem 7.13.5 (Moser local boundedness for nonnegative subsolutions).DB183F3A4CE7 Let $n\geq 2$, let $A$ be measurable and symmetric on $B_{2R}(a)$ with $\lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I$ for $0<\lambda \leq \Lambda $, and let $u\in H^1(B_{2R}(a))$ be a nonnegative weak subsolution of $\operatorname {div}(A Du)=0$. Then

\[ \operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ R(a)}u \leq C(n,\Lambda /\lambda )\left(R^{-n}\int _{B_{2R}(a)}u^2\right)^{1/2}. \]

This is the zero-lower-order, $p=2$ case of [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Theorem 1, p. 200.

Proof

Translation and dilation reduce to $a=0,R=1$ without changing the ellipticity ratio. Let $r_ j=1+2^{-j}$ and $p_ j=2\chi ^ j$. Starting from $u\in L^2(B_2)$, Lemma 7.13.4 successively gives

\[ \| u\| _{L^{p_{j+1}}(B_{r_{j+1}})} \leq \bigl (Cp_ j^2 2^{2j+2}\bigr )^{1/p_ j} \| u\| _{L^{p_ j}(B_{r_ j})}. \]

The logarithm of the product of these constants is bounded by a multiple of $\sum _ j(1+j)\chi ^{-j}$, which is finite. Thus $\| u\| _{L^{p_ j}(B_1)}\leq C\| u\| _{L^2(B_2)}$ for every $j$. If $u>A$ on a subset of $B_1$ of positive measure $m$, then $\| u\| _{L^{p_ j}(B_1)}\geq A m^{1/p_ j}$. Letting $j\to \infty $ bounds every such level $A$ by $C\| u\| _2$, and hence bounds the essential supremum. This does not assume $u\in L^\infty $ in advance. Scaling the volume integral back gives the factor $R^{-n/2}$, including in dimension two. $\square$

Proposition 7.13.6 (Local bounds from every positive integrability exponent).667B61D52CFD Under the hypotheses of Theorem 7.13.5, for every $p>0$,

\[ \operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ R(a)}u \leq C(n,\Lambda /\lambda ,p)\left(R^{-n}\int _{B_{2R}(a)}u^ p\right)^{1/p}. \]

The right side is allowed to be infinite. For $p<1$ no norm triangle inequality is needed. Compare [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Theorem 1.

Proof

Work on $B_2$ by scaling. For $p\geq 2$, Holder and Theorem 7.13.5 suffice. For $0<p<2$, put $P=(\int _{B_2}u^ p)^{1/p}$ and $M(r)=\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ r}u$. Applying the half-ball estimate on small balls centered in $B_ r$ gives, for $1\leq r<s\leq 3/2$,

\[ M(r)\leq C(s-r)^{-n/2}\| u\| _{L^2(B_ s)} \leq C(s-r)^{-n/2}M(s)^{1-p/2}P^{p/2}. \]

A finite covering of $B_ r$ by balls of radius comparable to $s-r$ justifies the first inequality with the same constant. Young's inequality then yields, for any fixed $0<\theta <1$,

\[ M(r)\leq \theta M(s)+C_{\theta ,p}(s-r)^{-n/p}P. \]

Choose $r_ j=1+(1-2^{-j})/2$ and $\theta <2^{-n/p}$. Iteration gives a convergent geometric series for the error terms, since $r_{j+1}-r_ j=2^{-j-2}$. The remainder is at most $\theta ^ j M(3/2)$ and tends to zero; $M(3/2)<\infty $ follows from the already proved local $L^2$ bound. Thus $M(1)\leq C_ pP$. Rescaling proves the result. The argument uses interpolation of integrals and a scalar iteration, not Banach-space properties for $p<1$. $\square$

Proposition 7.13.7 (Weak maximum principle with a Sobolev boundary condition).3E2A4AEFD01C Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ n$ be bounded, $n\geq 2$, and let $u\in H^1(\Omega )$ be a weak subsolution with the ellipticity assumptions above. If $(u-k)_+\in H_0^1(\Omega )$ for a constant $k$, then $u\leq k$ almost everywhere in $\Omega $. No regularity of the boundary of $\Omega $ is assumed; its boundary condition is expressed by this Sobolev membership.

Proof

Set $w=(u-k)_+$. It is an admissible nonnegative $H_0^1$ test, by approximation. The Sobolev chain rule gives $Dw=Du$ on $\{ u>k\} $ and zero elsewhere, so

\[ 0\geq \int A Du\cdot Dw=\int A Dw\cdot Dw\geq \lambda \int |Dw|^2. \]

Extend $w$ by zero to a ball containing $\Omega $. Its extension lies in $H_0^1$ of that ball. The scaled version of Theorem 7.13.1 now forces $w=0$, which is the assertion. Unlike a pointwise boundary statement, the assumed membership has meaning on any bounded open domain. $\square$

Corollary 7.13.8 (An integrable entire subsolution vanishes).D0D8708926B9 Let $n\geq 2$ and let $A$ be measurable and symmetric on all of $\mathbb R^ n$, with fixed global bounds $\lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I$. If $u\geq 0$ is a weak subsolution in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n)$ and $\int _{\mathbb R^ n}u^ p<\infty $ for some $p>0$, then $u=0$ almost everywhere. A signed entire weak solution with finite $\int |u|^ p$ also vanishes.

Proof

Proposition 7.13.6, on balls of arbitrarily large radius, bounds $\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ R}u$ by $C R^{-n/p}(\int _{\mathbb R^ n}u^ p)^{1/p}$, which tends to zero. Every fixed ball lies in these expanding balls, so $u$ vanishes on every ball. For a signed solution, its positive and negative parts are subsolutions by Lemma 7.13.2, and each has finite $p$ integral. Apply the first assertion to both. The global ellipticity bounds and the integrability hypothesis are essential parts of this conclusion. $\square$
