9.3 Bumpy metrics, multiplicity, and abundance0D93167A032E

0D93167A032E

In this section $M^{n+1}$ is closed. The width $\omega _ p$ always denotes the mod-two volume spectrum defined in Definition 9.1.2; it is not an arbitrary fixed homotopy-class width.

Definition 9.3.1 (Bumpy metrics).3AFB2285C181 A smooth Riemannian metric is bumpy if every closed immersed minimal hypersurface has trivial kernel for its normal Jacobi operator. This includes finite covers; checking only embedded hypersurfaces is a weaker condition. See [White17], Theorem 2.1, and [Zhou20], Introduction.

Theorem 9.3.2 (Generic bumpiness).826686D45D1C For a smooth closed manifold $M$, a Baire-generic smooth Riemannian metric has no closed immersed minimal submanifold with a nontrivial Jacobi field. Here generic means that the property holds on a residual set in the $C^\infty $ topology. This is [White17], Theorem 2.1 with the symmetry group taken to be trivial and $k=\infty $. The proof remains external.

Theorem 9.3.3 (Multiplicity-one realization of the volume spectrum).0BB7ED8003DE Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be a smooth closed connected orientable Riemannian manifold with $2\leq n\leq 6$ and a bumpy metric. For every positive integer $p$, there is a finite disjoint collection of smooth, connected, closed, embedded, two-sided minimal hypersurfaces $\Sigma _1,\ldots ,\Sigma _ q$ realizing

\[ \omega _ p(M,g)=\sum _{i=1}^ q\operatorname {Area}_ g(\Sigma _ i),\qquad \sum _{i=1}^ q\operatorname {index}(\Sigma _ i)\leq p. \]

Each component occurs with multiplicity one. We state an orientable specialization of [Zhou20], Theorem A and Theorem 5.2, retaining its mod-two volume-spectrum setting. This is an existence statement about a realization of $\omega _ p$, not a claim about every critical varifold in an arbitrary min-max class. The proof uses prescribed-mean-curvature approximation and remains external.

Theorem 9.3.4 (Infinitely many minimal hypersurfaces).DD08D48E52FD Every smooth closed connected Riemannian manifold $M^{n+1}$ with $2\leq n\leq 6$ contains infinitely many geometrically distinct smooth closed embedded minimal hypersurfaces. Neither bumpiness nor positive Ricci curvature is required. Source: [Song18], Theorem 1, in the supplied June 22, 2018 version. The statement concerns distinct hypersurfaces, not repeated integer multiples of one surface. Its proof remains external.

Theorem 9.3.5 (Equidistribution for generic metrics).5DAA3668FA8E Let $M^{n+1}$ be smooth, closed, and connected, with $2\leq n\leq 6$. For a $C^\infty $-generic metric $g$, there exists a sequence of smooth connected closed embedded minimal hypersurfaces $\Sigma _ j$ such that, for every $f\in C^\infty (M)$,

\[ \lim _{q\to \infty } \frac{\sum _{j=1}^ q\int _{\Sigma _ j}f\, dA_ g}{\sum _{j=1}^ q\operatorname {Area}_ g(\Sigma _ j)} =\frac{1}{\operatorname {Vol}_ g(M)}\int _ M f\, dV_ g. \]

This uses the total area of the first $q$ surfaces as the denominator, rather than the unweighted average of their separately normalized area measures. Source: [MNS19], Main Theorem, equation (1), p. 422. The theorem provides a suitable sequence; it does not assert equidistribution for every choice of width realizers. The proof remains external.
