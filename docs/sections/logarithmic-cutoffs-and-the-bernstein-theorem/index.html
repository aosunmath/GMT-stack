7.8 Logarithmic cutoffs and the Bernstein theorem1F07836348F7

1F07836348F7

The following proof of the two-dimensional Bernstein theorem separates its ingredients: stability, quadratic area growth, and cutoffs of arbitrarily small Dirichlet energy. Properness supplies compact support for cutoffs defined by ambient distance. We follow [ChMin], Corollary 18.2 and Section 25, pp. 51 and 62–64.

Definition 7.8.1 (Exhausting cutoffs with vanishing Dirichlet energy).428ADAED15DA A smooth manifold without boundary has the cutoff property used here if there are compactly supported locally Lipschitz functions $\eta _ j$ such that

\[ 0\leq \eta _ j\leq 1,\qquad \eta _ j\longrightarrow 1\text{ pointwise}, \qquad \int |\nabla \eta _ j|^2\longrightarrow 0. \]

Only this explicit analytic property is needed below. It is often expressed through capacity or parabolicity, but those broader equivalences are not assumed here. The logarithmic cutoffs in [ChMin], the proof of Theorem 25.1, provide a geometric example.

Proposition 7.8.2 (A nonnegative stability potential vanishes under small-energy cutoffs).58F6D1EE7150 Suppose a smooth manifold has the cutoff property in Definition 7.8.1, and let $q\geq 0$ be a continuous function satisfying

\[ \int q\phi ^2\leq \int |\nabla \phi |^2 \quad \text{for every }\phi \in C_ c^\infty . \]

Then $q=0$ everywhere. In particular, a two-sided stable minimal hypersurface in Euclidean space with this cutoff property is totally geodesic.

Proof

Compactly supported locally Lipschitz tests are admissible by approximation in $W^{1,2}$ on a fixed compact neighborhood of their support; $q$ is bounded there, so the potential integral also converges. Use $\eta _ j$ as a test and apply Fatou's lemma to the nonnegative functions $q\eta _ j^2$. This gives $\int q\leq \liminf _ j\int |\nabla \eta _ j|^2=0$. Continuity yields pointwise vanishing. For a Euclidean minimal hypersurface the stability potential is $q=|A|^2$. $\square$

Proposition 7.8.3 (Quadratic area growth gives logarithmic cutoffs).80D85FC586A0 Let $F:\Sigma ^2\to \mathbb R^ N$ be a proper smooth immersion of a surface without boundary. Suppose for some $a$ and $C<\infty $,

\[ \operatorname {Area}\bigl (F^{-1}(B_ R(a))\bigr )\leq CR^2 \quad (R\geq 1). \]

Area is computed in the induced metric, including multiplicity of the immersion. Then $\Sigma $ has the cutoff property of Definition 7.8.1.

Proof

Write $\rho =|F-a|$ and take $R=2^ k$, $k\geq 1$. Define $\eta _ R$ to be one for $\rho \leq R$, zero for $\rho \geq R^2$, and $\log (R^2/\rho )/\log R$ in between. Properness makes its support compact. Since $|\nabla _\Sigma \rho |\leq 1$, almost everywhere on the annulus,

\[ |\nabla \eta _ R|^2\leq \frac{1}{\rho ^2(\log R)^2}. \]

Decompose $R<\rho <R^2$ into the $k$ dyadic annuli $2^ j<\rho <2^{j+1}$, $j=k,\ldots ,2k-1$. On each, the integral of $\rho ^{-2}$ is at most $2^{-2j}C2^{2j+2}=4C$. Hence $\int |\nabla \eta _ R|^2\leq 4Ck/(k\log 2)^2\to 0$. The functions take values between zero and one and converge pointwise to one. This is the logarithmic cutoff argument in [ChMin], pp. 63–64, with the squared-gradient estimate explicit. $\square$

Theorem 7.8.4 (Stable Bernstein with quadratic area growth).A5EAD6B995B6 Let $F:\Sigma ^2\to \mathbb R^3$ be a nonempty complete connected proper smooth two-sided minimal immersion without boundary. If it is stable and its induced area satisfies the quadratic growth bound in Proposition 7.8.3, then its image is an affine plane.

Proof

The logarithmic cutoffs and Proposition 7.8.2 give $A=0$. The global unit normal therefore has zero derivative and is constant on connected $\Sigma $. The image lies in an affine plane and is open in that plane because $F$ is an immersion of the same dimension. Properness makes the image closed, so it is the entire plane. This is the stability-and-growth argument used for graphs in [ChMin], Theorem 25.1. The growth and properness hypotheses are part of this version; no classification of all complete stable immersions in higher dimensions is claimed. $\square$

Proposition 7.8.5 (Uniform area growth for an entire minimal graph).2D3AB8786897 If $u\in C^2(\mathbb R^ n)$ solves the scalar minimal surface equation, its graph $\Gamma $ satisfies, for every ambient center $a$ and radius $R>0$,

\[ \mathcal H^ n(\Gamma \cap B_ R(a)) \leq (n+1)\omega _{n+1}R^ n. \]

No global gradient bound is assumed. This gives an explicit constant in [ChMin], Corollary 18.2, p. 51.

Proof

The vector field $N(x,z)=(-Du(x),1)/\sqrt{1+|Du(x)|^2}$ is globally defined, has length one, and is divergence free. It is the outward normal to the subgraph $\Omega =\{ z<u(x)\} $. For almost every radius, the graph meets the sphere transversely, and the divergence theorem on the bounded region $\Omega \cap B_ R(a)$ gives

\[ 0=\mathcal H^ n(\Gamma \cap B_ R(a)) +\int _{\Omega \cap \partial B_ R(a)}N\cdot \nu _{B_ R}\, d\mathcal H^ n. \]

The absolute value of the second term is no larger than the area of the entire sphere, $(n+1)\omega _{n+1}R^ n$. The divergence theorem applies to this piecewise smooth boundary; its codimension-two corner has zero boundary area. Classical graph bootstrapping supplies smoothness when using Sard's theorem for the transverse radii. Approximation by good radii increasing to an arbitrary $R$ proves the estimate for every ball. This is also the flux form of the graph calibration in Proposition 7.2.13. $\square$

Theorem 7.8.6 (Bernstein's theorem for an entire graph over the plane).1087ACBCE404 Every $C^2$ solution $u:\mathbb R^2\to \mathbb R$ of the scalar minimal surface equation is affine.

Proof

Its graph is smooth by Proposition 7.2.7, two-sided and stable by Corollary 7.6.4, and has quadratic area growth by Proposition 7.8.5. An entire graph is proper: the inverse image of an ambient compact set is closed and has bounded horizontal coordinates. Its induced metric is complete, since it dominates the Euclidean metric on the base and is uniformly comparable to it on each compact subset. Theorem 7.8.4 makes the image an affine plane. A plane that is a graph over all of $\mathbb R^2$ is the graph of an affine function. This is the geometric proof of [ChMin], Theorem 25.1, pp. 62–64. $\square$

Example 7.8.7 (A stable graph need not be an entire graph).729C4A9152CC The nonflat Scherk graph in Example 7.2.14 is stable by its positive vertical Jacobi function. Its domain is a square, so it does not satisfy Bernstein's entire-domain hypothesis. It is also not proper as that graph in $\mathbb R^3$: along $x=y\uparrow \pi /2$ its height stays zero and its points approach a finite point outside the graph. Thus pulling back ambient distance cutoffs does not automatically produce compactly supported functions on this incomplete graph. No contradiction with Theorem 7.8.4 follows.
