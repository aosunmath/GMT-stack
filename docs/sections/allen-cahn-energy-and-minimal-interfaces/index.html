12.1 Allen-Cahn energy and minimal interfacesF710E3587F5F

F710E3587F5F

Allen–Cahn theory approaches geometric interfaces through functions. Chodosh's 2019 course notes, in the supplied revision dated June 29, 2023, give the basic analytic and variational setup [ChAC]. The energy below is a diffuse-interface energy; its min-max levels are not identified with cycle-space widths without a comparison theorem.

Definition 12.1.1 (The Allen-Cahn energy).E4C01FDF2BAC Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be a smooth closed manifold and $\varepsilon >0$. For $u\in H^1(M)\cap L^4(M)$ set

\[ E_\varepsilon (u)=\int _ M\left(\frac\varepsilon 2|\nabla u|^2+\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \right)dV_ g, \qquad W(s)=\frac14(1-s^2)^2. \]

We assign energy $+\infty $ outside this domain. The wells are $u=\pm 1$. The interface normalization is $c_ W=\int _{-1}^{1}\sqrt{2W(s)}\, ds=2\sqrt2/3$. Source: [ChAC], Definition 2.1 and Section 2.2. Any comparison with area must keep this normalization.

Proposition 12.1.2 (Equation and second variation of Allen-Cahn).6CA7B05E1A99 A smooth function is critical for $E_\varepsilon $ if and only if $\varepsilon ^2\Delta _ g u=W'(u)=u(u^2-1)$. At a smooth critical point its second variation in the direction $\phi $ is

\[ Q_\varepsilon (\phi ,\phi )=\int _ M\left(\varepsilon |\nabla \phi |^2+\varepsilon ^{-1}W''(u)\phi ^2\right)dV_ g. \]

Compare [ChAC], Section 2.1.

Proof

Differentiate the integrand at $u+t\phi $ once and integrate the gradient term by parts on the closed manifold. The first derivative is $\int _ M(-\varepsilon \Delta _ g u+\varepsilon ^{-1}W'(u))\phi $. Its vanishing for every smooth test function is exactly the equation. Differentiating a second time gives the displayed quadratic form. $\square$

Example 12.1.3 (A single transition layer).3C84C0DE867A For the potential in Definition 12.1.1, $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$ satisfies $q''=W'(q)$, $q(\pm \infty )=\pm 1$, and $q'=\sqrt{2W(q)}$. Thus $u(t)=q(t/\varepsilon )$ solves the one-dimensional equation, and its energy per unit transverse area is

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\left(\tfrac 12|q'|^2+W(q)\right)dt =\int _{-1}^{1}\sqrt{2W(s)}\, ds=c_ W. \]

The substitution $s=q(t)$ proves the identity and explains why diffuse-interface energies must be divided by $c_ W$ when comparing with area. See [ChAC], Section 2.2.

Remark 12.1.4 (Multiplicity, curvature, and index in dimension three).7FD866591D17 The supplied Chodosh–Mantoulidis paper [CM] is arXiv:1803.02716v2, with a manuscript date of May 16, 2018. Its Sections 3–5 develop curvature estimates, separation of transition sheets, and the relationship between multiplicity and Jacobi fields for solutions with controlled energy and Morse index. Theorem 1.7 explains why a smooth limiting component with multiplicity, or a one-sided component, forces a positive Jacobi field (on the two-sided cover when needed). For bumpy metrics on closed three-manifolds this obstruction gives two-sided multiplicity-one components. The full convergence and index theorems, including their analytic hypotheses, remain external dependencies here. We do not infer any equality of Allen–Cahn and Almgren–Pitts widths from this source discussion.
