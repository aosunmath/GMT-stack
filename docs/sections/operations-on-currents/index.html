5.3 Operations on currents4F98A2504D31

4F98A2504D31

Pushforwards, restrictions, and homotopies turn geometric constructions into identities with quantitative mass bounds. Throughout this section, $m$ is the current dimension and $N$ is the ambient Euclidean dimension. We retain the comass convention of Definition 5.1.3. The main source is [Sim18], Chapter 6, Section 2, pp. 159–169; its initial Euclidean norm on forms must be distinguished from comass for nonrectifiable currents.

Definition 5.3.1 (Support and normal currents).F9AA0927D6D3 The support $\operatorname {spt}T$ of a current in $U$ is the complement, relative to $U$, of the largest open set on which it vanishes on all test forms. A current is locally normal if $T$ and $\partial T$ have finite mass on every relatively compact open subset of $U$. It is normal with finite mass if $\mathbf M(T)+\mathbf M(\partial T)<\infty $. Normality is an analytic condition; it does not require integer multiplicities or rectifiability. Every locally integral current is locally normal. See [Sim18], Chapter 6, Sections 2–3, and [Fed69], Section 4.1.

Theorem 5.3.2 (Representation of currents with locally finite mass).E73216D55EF5 If $T$ has locally finite mass in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, there are a Radon measure $\| T\| $ and a measurable $m$-vector field $\xi $ such that

\[ T(\omega )=\int _ U\langle \omega ,\xi \rangle \, d\| T\| , \qquad |\xi |_{\mathrm{mass}}=1\quad \| T\| \text{-almost everywhere}, \qquad \mathbf M_ W(T)=\| T\| (W). \]

Here $|\cdot |_{\mathrm{mass}}$ is the norm on $m$-vectors dual to comass. The field $\xi $ need not be simple. The current extends uniquely to compactly supported continuous forms, and its integral representation also defines evaluation on bounded Borel forms with compact support. This is the vector-measure representation theorem, obtained from Riesz representation and polar decomposition. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 6, equations (2.8)–(2.10), with the norm convention changed by duality as explained in Remark 2.7. The representation theorem remains an external dependency.

Proposition 5.3.3 (Weak compactness from local mass bounds).AA1586653736 Suppose $T_ j$ are $m$-currents in $U$ and $\sup _ j\mathbf M_ W(T_ j)<\infty $ for every $W\Subset U$. A subsequence converges weakly to an $m$-current with locally finite mass. If also $\sup _ j\mathbf M_ W(\partial T_ j)<\infty $ for every such $W$, the limit is locally normal. This conclusion does not by itself preserve integer rectifiability; that is the additional content of Theorem 5.1.6. Compare [Sim18], Lemma 2.17, p. 162.

Proof

On an exhaustion of $U$ by relatively compact open sets, the coordinate measures in Theorem 5.3.2 have uniformly bounded total variation. Weak-star compactness of finite vector measures, the separability of continuous compactly supported test functions, and a diagonal choice give a subsequence converging on all compactly supported continuous forms. The limits on overlapping sets agree, hence define a current on $U$. Mass lower semicontinuity gives its local mass bound. Boundary commutes with weak convergence by Proposition 5.1.2; applying mass lower semicontinuity once more bounds the boundary mass. The functional-analytic weak-star compactness theorem is used here as an external input. $\square$

Definition 5.3.4 (Restriction and contraction by a differential).D80FDA4F9AB7 For a locally finite-mass current and a Borel set $E\subset U$, define

\[ (T\llcorner E)(\omega )=\int _ E\langle \omega ,\xi \rangle \, d\| T\| . \]

More generally $T\llcorner u$ inserts a locally bounded Borel scalar function $u$ into this integral. For smooth $u$ and $m\geq 1$, the $(m-1)$-current $T\llcorner du$ is defined by

\[ (T\llcorner du)(\eta )=T(du\wedge \eta ). \]

The order $du\wedge \eta $ fixes the sign used below. Restricting a normal current to an arbitrary Borel set need not give a normal current. See [Sim18], Chapter 6, equations (2.11)–(2.12) and Section 4.

Proposition 5.3.5 (Boundary of a smooth scalar restriction).C518F9F87741 For a locally normal $m$-current, $m\geq 1$, and smooth $u$,

\[ \partial (T\llcorner u)=(\partial T)\llcorner u-T\llcorner du. \]

In particular multiplication by a smooth compactly supported function produces a normal current of finite mass, with

\[ \mathbf M\bigl (\partial (T\llcorner u)\bigr ) \leq \int |u|\, d\| \partial T\| +\int |Du|\, d\| T\| . \]
Proof

For a test $(m-1)$-form $\eta $, expand $d(u\eta )=du\wedge \eta +u\, d\eta $ and apply $T$. This gives the identity. If $\| \eta \| _*\leq 1$, then $\| du\wedge \eta \| _*\leq |Du|$: on a unit simple $m$-vector this is the norm bound for contraction by $Du$. The mass estimates follow by integration and taking the supremum. Compare [Sim18], the computation on p. 170. $\square$

Definition 5.3.6 (Smooth pushforward and properness).6D60EECBFF25 Let $f:U\to V$ be smooth and proper on $\operatorname {spt}T$: for every compact $K\subset V$, the set $f^{-1}(K)\cap \operatorname {spt}T$ is compact in $U$. Define

\[ f_\# T(\omega )=T(\chi f^*\omega ), \]

where $\chi \in C_ c^\infty (U)$ equals one on a neighborhood of $\operatorname {spt}T\cap f^{-1}(\operatorname {spt}\omega )$. Different choices give the same value because their difference vanishes near the part of the current being tested. In particular compact support of $T$ guarantees properness. See [Sim18], equation (2.29), p. 164.

Proposition 5.3.7 (Pushforward, boundary, and composition).738450753968 For smooth maps with the required properness,

\[ \partial (f_\# T)=f_\# (\partial T),\qquad (g\circ f)_\# T=g_\# (f_\# T),\qquad \operatorname {spt}(f_\# T)\subset f(\operatorname {spt}T). \]

The image on the right is relatively closed under the properness hypothesis. It can be strictly larger than the support of the pushforward because of cancellation.

Proof

The first two identities follow from $d(f^*\eta )=f^*(d\eta )$ and $(g\circ f)^*=f^*g^*$. In the boundary calculation, derivatives of the auxiliary cutoff vanish near $\operatorname {spt}T\cap f^{-1}(\operatorname {spt}\eta )$, and the remaining terms vanish where the test form does. To prove the support inclusion, a test form supported off $f(\operatorname {spt}T)$ pulls back to zero near the support of $T$. Properness makes that image closed: a convergent sequence of image points lies in a compact target neighborhood, whose inverse image on the support is compact. See [Sim18], equation (2.31). $\square$

Proposition 5.3.8 (Mass bound for a pushforward).126C4418F9FD Let $T$ have locally finite mass and let $f$ be as in Definition 5.3.6. For open $W\subset V$,

\[ \mathbf M_ W(f_\# T)\leq \int _{f^{-1}(W)}\| Df(x)\| ^ m\, d\| T\| (x). \]

Thus $\mathbf M(f_\# T)\leq L^ m\mathbf M(T)$ if $\| Df\| \leq L$ on $\operatorname {spt}T$. For $m=0$, use $L^0=1$, including when $L=0$. The constant is one with the comass convention; compare [Sim18], Remark 2.30(1), which also discusses the other norm convention.

Proof

On an orthonormal $m$-frame, the pullback evaluates $\omega $ on its image under $Df$, whose simple-vector norm is at most $\| Df\| ^ m$. Hence $\| f^*\omega \| _*\leq \| Df\| ^ m\| \omega \| _*$. Integrate this bound against the polar representation of $T$ and take the supremum over test forms supported in $W$. The image-vector estimate is dual to the mass norm, so it does not assume that the polar field of a general current is simple. $\square$

Theorem 5.3.9 (Lipschitz pushforwards of integral currents).C03BD7F60BF1 For a locally integral current $T$ and a locally Lipschitz map $f$ proper on its support, the pushforward is well defined and locally integral. It commutes with boundary. If $f$ is globally $L$-Lipschitz, then

\[ \mathbf M(f_\# T)\leq L^ m\mathbf M(T),\qquad \mathbf M(\partial f_\# T)\leq L^{m-1}\mathbf M(\partial T)\quad (m\geq 1). \]

For $T=\llbracket M,\theta ,\tau \rrbracket $, its action is the integral of the test form against $\Lambda ^ m D^ Mf(\tau )\theta $ on $M$, where $D^ Mf$ is the approximate tangential derivative. The signed multiplicities of overlapping images are added before taking mass. Sources: [Sim18], Lemma 2.36, pp. 166–167, and Lemma 3.12 with Remark 3.13, pp. 174–175. The Lipschitz extension and integer-rectifiability arguments remain external; they are not a consequence of differentiability at ambient Lebesgue-almost every point alone.

Example 5.3.10 (Projection can cancel two sheets).BD37354CBD90 Let $I_+$ and $I_-$ be the horizontal segments $[0,1]\times \{ 1\} $ and $[0,1]\times \{ -1\} $, both oriented toward increasing first coordinate. Set $T=\llbracket I_+\rrbracket -\llbracket I_-\rrbracket $, and let $p(x,y)=x$. Then $\mathbf M(T)=2$ while $p_\# T=0$: both segment integrals become the same integral over $[0,1]$ with opposite signs. The associated unoriented varifold has pushforward of mass $2$ instead. This illustrates the signed-multiplicity distinction in [Sim18], Remark 3.13.

Definition 5.3.11 (The interval product).1CD110260D8E For a compactly supported finite-mass $m$-current $T$ in $\mathbb R^ N$, write a smooth $(m+1)$-form on $\mathbb R\times \mathbb R^ N$ as $dt\wedge \alpha (t,x)+\beta (t,x)$, with $\alpha $ and $\beta $ containing only spatial differentials. Define

\[ (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times T)(dt\wedge \alpha +\beta ) =\int _0^1 T(\alpha (t,\cdot ))\, dt. \]

The interval direction comes first in the orientation. The product has mass at most $\mathbf M(T)$ and support in $[0,1]\times \operatorname {spt}T$. For an integer rectifiable $T$, the product is integer rectifiable by the product representation of its oriented tangent planes. See [Sim18], equations (2.19)–(2.25) and Remark 3.2(2).

Proposition 5.3.12 (Boundary of the interval product).35BE43CA75FC For a compactly supported normal current $T$, let $i_ t(x)=(t,x)$. Then

\[ \partial (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times T) =(i_1)_\# T-(i_0)_\# T-\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times \partial T. \]

In particular the interval product of an integral current is integral. See [Sim18], equation (2.26), p. 164.

Proof

Write a test $m$-form as $dt\wedge \gamma +\delta $. The component of its exterior derivative containing $dt$ is $dt\wedge (\partial _ t\delta -d_ x\gamma )$. Integrating gives $T(\delta (1,\cdot ))-T(\delta (0,\cdot ))-\int _0^1\partial T(\gamma (t,\cdot ))\, dt$, which is the stated formula. For dimension zero the term containing $\gamma $ is absent. The mass bounds for the product and its boundary give normality; the integer rectifiable product description gives integrality when $T$ is integral. $\square$

Proposition 5.3.13 (The homotopy formula).1820DA232B40 Let $T$ be a compactly supported normal current, and let $h:[0,1]\times \mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R^ Q$ be smooth near $[0,1]\times \operatorname {spt}T$. Put $f=h(0,\cdot )$ and $g=h(1,\cdot )$. Then

\[ g_\# T-f_\# T =\partial h_\# (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times T) +h_\# (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times \partial T). \]

For maps defined only near this compact set, cutoffs localize the construction. See [Sim18], equation (2.32), p. 166.

Proof

Apply $h_\# $ to Proposition 5.3.12, commute pushforward with boundary, and use $h\circ i_0=f$, $h\circ i_1=g$. Moving the product-boundary term to the other side yields the formula. Both terms matter when $T$ has nonzero boundary. $\square$

Definition 5.3.14 (A flat norm for real currents).0F1A75F2601B For a compactly supported $m$-current in $\mathbb R^ N$, define

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(T)=\inf _{T=R+\partial S} \bigl (\mathbf M(R)+\mathbf M(S)\bigr ), \]

where $R$ and $S$ are compactly supported real currents of finite mass of dimensions $m$ and $m+1$. The value is allowed to be infinite. This choice of competitors is different from the integer polyhedral competitors in Definition 5.2.2; for a polyhedral integer chain it gives a value no larger than that earlier infimum. No equality of these two infima is asserted here. Compare the flat topology in [Fed69], Section 4.1, and the homotopy estimates in [Sim18], Section 2.

Proposition 5.3.15 (Flat control of test forms and boundaries).F92644CBD3BD For every compactly supported smooth $m$-form $\omega $,

\[ |T(\omega )|\leq \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(T) \max \{ \| \omega \| _{*,\infty },\| d\omega \| _{*,\infty }\} . \]

Consequently $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(T)=0$ implies $T=0$, flat convergence implies weak convergence, and $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(\partial T)\leq \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(T)$. On the vector space where it is finite, $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}$ is a norm.

Proof

For $T=R+\partial S$, write $T(\omega )=R(\omega )+S(d\omega )$ and use the two mass bounds. Take the infimum. If the infimum is zero, all test evaluations vanish. For the boundary estimate use $\partial T=\partial R$ as a decomposition with zero residual and filling $R$. Triangle inequality follows by adding decompositions; scaling decompositions gives absolute homogeneity. These arguments also explain why the test-form topology is weaker. $\square$

Proposition 5.3.16 (Nondegeneracy of the integer polyhedral flat metric).AB58565BA69A The flat norm in Definition 5.2.2 is symmetric, subadditive, and zero only for the zero chain. Thus its metric completion does not identify distinct polyhedral chains.

Proof

Reversing the signs of a residual and a filling proves symmetry; adding two decompositions and using mass subadditivity proves the triangle inequality. For nondegeneracy, view each oriented polyhedron as its integration current. Stokes' formula identifies its polyhedral boundary with its current boundary. Thus every integer polyhedral decomposition is an admissible real-current decomposition, and $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(P)\leq \mathcal F(P)$. If $\mathcal F(P)=0$, Proposition 5.3.15 shows that its current is zero. A nonzero subdivided polyhedral chain has a face with nonzero coefficient. Choose a point in its relative interior away from the other faces and a test form supported in a sufficiently small ball, with positive evaluation on its orientation. Its integral against the chain is nonzero, a contradiction. For dimension zero use a test function supported near a point with nonzero coefficient. This proves the metric assertion without identifying the real-current and integer-polyhedral flat infima. $\square$

Proposition 5.3.17 (A quantitative affine homotopy estimate).1ECE4BB7EEE7 Let $m\geq 1$, let $T$ be compactly supported and normal, and let smooth maps $f,g:\mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R^ Q$ satisfy $\| Df\| ,\| Dg\| \leq L$ near $\operatorname {spt}T$ and $|f-g|\leq \delta $ on that support. Then

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(g_\# T-f_\# T) \leq \delta \bigl (L^ m\mathbf M(T)+L^{m-1}\mathbf M(\partial T)\bigr ). \]

For $m=1$, the second power is interpreted as one. This is the global finite-mass version of [Sim18], equation (2.34).

Proof

Use $h(t,x)=(1-t)f(x)+tg(x)$. The image of the interval direction is $g-f$, of norm at most $\delta $; each spatial differential has norm at most $L$. The product formula and the comass estimate therefore give

\[ \mathbf M\bigl (h_\# (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times T)\bigr ) \leq \delta L^ m\mathbf M(T), \]

and the same estimate with $\partial T$ gives $\delta L^{m-1}\mathbf M(\partial T)$. Insert these two currents as the filling and residual in the homotopy formula. For general polar vectors the estimate follows by duality with comass, just as in Proposition 5.3.8. $\square$

Definition 5.3.18 (The cone over a compactly supported current).55F0DD68BC15 For a compactly supported normal current $T$ in $\mathbb R^ N$ and $a\in \mathbb R^ N$, set

\[ C_ aT=h_\# (\llbracket [0,1]\rrbracket \times T), \qquad h(t,x)=a+t(x-a). \]

This definition also applies to zero-dimensional currents. Its support lies in the union of the segments from $a$ to $\operatorname {spt}T$. See [Sim18], equations (2.37)–(2.39), p. 167.

Proposition 5.3.19 (Cone identity and mass estimate).FE4C86C4A57D For $m\geq 1$ and $T$ as above,

\[ \partial C_ aT=T-C_ a(\partial T),\qquad \mathbf M(C_ aT)\leq \frac{R}{m+1}\mathbf M(T) \quad \text{if }\operatorname {spt}T\subset \overline B_ R(a). \]

If $T$ is integral, then so is $C_ aT$. In particular an integral cycle of positive dimension supported in a ball has an integral filling in the same ball, with the displayed mass bound.

Proof

In the homotopy formula, $g$ is the identity and $f$ is constant. A constant map sends every positive-dimensional current to zero, so the boundary identity follows. The differential sends the interval direction to $x-a$ and spatial $m$-vectors to $t^ m$ times themselves. Thus the mass is at most $\int _0^1 Rt^ m\mathbf M(T)\, dt=R\mathbf M(T)/(m+1)$. The interval product is integral when $T$ is, and its smooth pushforward remains integral by Theorem 5.3.9. Convexity of the ball controls the support. $\square$

Example 5.3.20 (The zero-dimensional augmentation condition).A3DF20FFB628 For $T=\sum _{i=1}^ k a_ i\delta _{p_ i}$ with integer coefficients, the cone is a sum of oriented segments and

\[ \partial C_ aT=T-\left(\sum _ i a_ i\right)\delta _ a. \]

Thus a zero-dimensional current has a compactly supported one-current filling when $\sum _ i a_ i=0$. This condition is also necessary: if $T=\partial S$, test against a compactly supported function identically one near $\operatorname {spt}S$ to get $T(1)=S(d1)=0$, with the cutoff understood. This explains why the positive-dimensional cone identity requires a separate statement in dimension zero.
