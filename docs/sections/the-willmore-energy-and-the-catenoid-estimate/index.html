10.1 The Willmore energy and the catenoid estimateC8139A8F0719

C8139A8F0719

The same general min-max machinery can be applied to very different geometric families. The energy bound and topological detection must be supplied anew for each application.

Definition 10.1.1 (Willmore energy and normalization).9E90E1906976 For a smooth immersed closed surface $\Sigma \subset S^3$ in the unit round sphere, set

\[ \mathcal W(\Sigma )=\int _\Sigma (1+H_{\mathrm{av}}^2)\, dA, \qquad H_{\mathrm{av}}=(k_1+k_2)/2. \]

For a surface in $\mathbb R^3$ the corresponding energy is $\int _\Sigma H_{\mathrm{av}}^2\, dA$. The subscript distinguishes averaged mean curvature from the trace convention in Definition 7.1.1. Source: [MNWill], Definition 1.2, p. 685. Definition 10.3.1 gives the vector convention, and Proposition 10.3.5 proves the spherical–Euclidean energy identity.

Theorem 10.1.2 (The Willmore bound for embedded positive-genus surfaces).1EB4A795BBF2 A smooth connected closed embedded surface $\Sigma \subset S^3$ of genus at least one satisfies $\mathcal W(\Sigma )\geq 2\pi ^2$. Equality holds precisely for conformal images of the Clifford torus $S^1(1/\sqrt2)\times S^1(1/\sqrt2)$. Source: [MNWill], Theorem A, p. 685. The proof is external. Its main bridge constructs a canonical family controlled by the Willmore energy and uses its topology to force a min-max lower bound; it is not a consequence of the Weyl asymptotic formula. Corollary 10.4.9 explains its extension to smooth orientable immersions using the multiplicity bound.

Remark 10.1.3 (What the catenoid estimate supplies).1D4901862AD8 For a closed two-sided unstable embedded minimal surface in a three-manifold, the first negative direction for area produces nearby sheets with a quadratic area decrease. Ketover–Marques–Neves [KMN], Section 2 and Theorem 2.4, control the cost of inserting and opening connecting necks, obtaining suitable families whose areas remain strictly below twice the central area. Their logarithmic neck estimate resolves the critical two-dimensional comparison. Theorem 10.6.3 computes the Euclidean leading constant, and Proposition 10.6.6 isolates the area comparison with an unstable pair of sheets. A complete formulation of the deformation and its topology is still an external dependency here. Applications must retain the particular sweepout class; the estimate is not a general theorem that all min-max limits have multiplicity one.

Theorem 10.1.4 (One-parameter width in positive Ricci curvature).7A5ED0EFD787 Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be smooth, closed, connected, and orientable, with $2\leq n\leq 6$ and $\operatorname {Ric}_ g>0$. Consider the classical Almgren–Pitts width of the integral fundamental class, using one-parameter integer-cycle sweepouts based at the zero cycle. This width is realized with multiplicity one by an orientable embedded minimal hypersurface of Morse index one. Source: [KMN], Theorem 1.5; proof external. This is a fixed fundamental-class width. The source's Remark 1.6 cautions that allowing periodic families in other components of the mod-two cycle space changes the problem.
