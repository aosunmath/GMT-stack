3.8 Weights and tangent measures62798D27DF95

62798D27DF95

An integrable weight can make a rectifiable surface measure locally finite even when its unweighted area is not. We prove the weighted tangent statement by exhausting the surface with subsets where the weight has a positive lower bound. This expands [Sim18], Chapter 3, Definition 1.7 and Theorem 1.9, printed pp. 90–92. Here $1\leq m<n$ and $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ is open. We write $\eta _{x,r}(y)=(y-x)/r$.

Proposition 3.8.1 (Multiplying a unit-density tangent by an integrable weight).D3D6B64F4764 Let $M\subset U$ be measurable, countably $m$-rectifiable and locally finite in $\mathcal H^ m$ measure. Put $\sigma =\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$. For $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\sigma )$, at $\sigma $-almost every $x$ and for every $\phi \in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}r^{-m}\int _ M\phi ((y-x)/r)f(y)\, d\mathcal H^ m(y) =f(x)\int _{T_ xM}\phi \, d\mathcal H^ m. \]

The weight may be signed. All integrals are local to $U$ for small enough $r$.

Proof

Choose a point where Theorem 3.6.6, Corollary 3.6.7, and the absolute-oscillation conclusion of Theorem 2.6.5 for $f$ all hold. Their simultaneous set has full $\sigma $ measure. For a test supported in $B_ R$, the difference between the scaled integral with $f(y)$ and that with the constant $f(x)$ has absolute value at most

\[ \| \phi \| _\infty r^{-m}\int _{B_{Rr}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|\, d\sigma (y). \]

The oscillation integral is $o(\sigma (B_{Rr}(x)))$, and the density limit gives $\sigma (B_{Rr}(x))=O(r^ m)$. Thus the error tends to zero. The remaining constant-weight integral converges by the tangent theorem. The same full-measure set works for every compactly supported test, since the oscillation and density estimates hold at all small radii. A countable compact exhaustion proves the local-domain version. $\square$

Definition 3.8.2 (A tangent plane with respect to a positive weight).8AE36515BAA5 Let $M\subset U$ be $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable, and let $\theta :M\to (0,\infty )$ be measurable and finite, with $\mu =\theta \mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$ locally finite. An $m$-plane $P$ is a weighted approximate tangent at $x\in M$ if

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}r^{-m}\int _ M\phi ((y-x)/r)\theta (y)\, d\mathcal H^ m(y) =\theta (x)\int _ P\phi \, d\mathcal H^ m \quad (\phi \in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)). \]

This is the convention of [Sim18], Definition 1.7, p. 91. Positivity and finiteness of $\theta (x)$ make the plane unique when it exists. The definition does not require locally finite unweighted area.

Lemma 3.8.3 (Positive weights give finite-area pieces and the same null sets).21236CD2BB9C Under Definition 3.8.2, there are increasing measurable sets $M_ j\subset M$ covering $M$, such that $M_ j$ is bounded and has finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, and $1/j\leq \theta \leq j$ on $M_ j$. Further, $\mu (A)=0$ for a measurable $A\subset M$ if and only if $\mathcal H^ m(A)=0$. Borel representatives can be chosen for $M$ and its restricted weighted measure.

Proof

Choose increasing compact sets $K_ j\subset U$ whose union is $U$, and put $M_ j=M\cap K_ j\cap \{ 1/j\leq \theta \leq j\} $. They increase and cover $M$ because the weight is positive and finite. The bound $\mathcal H^ m(M_ j)\leq j\mu (K_ j)<\infty $ proves finite area. For a measurable $A$, a zero Hausdorff measure gives a zero weighted integral; conversely $\mathcal H^ m(A\cap M_ j)\leq j\mu (A)$ and continuity from below show that a zero weighted integral forces $\mathcal H^ m(A)=0$.

Each $M_ j$ has a Borel hull with null Hausdorff difference, by the argument in Lemma 3.6.1. Their union is a Borel representative of $M$ modulo an $\mathcal H^ m$-null set. On this sigma-finite Hausdorff measure space, a measurable finite function has a Borel representative modulo a null set, by approximation with simple functions and Borel representatives of their level sets. Its weighted restriction is a locally finite Borel measure, hence Radon. Such changes do not change any integral or almost-everywhere assertion. $\square$

Lemma 3.8.4 (Recovering a tangent from a full-density restriction).173F37205E7D Let $\mu $ be Radon, $A$ Borel, $x\in A$, and suppose

\[ \frac{(\mu \llcorner A)(B_ r(x))}{\mu (B_ r(x))}\to 1, \qquad r^{-m}(\eta _{x,r})_\# (\mu \llcorner A) \rightharpoonup q\mathcal H^ m\llcorner P \]

for $q\in (0,\infty )$ and an $m$-plane $P$. Then $r^{-m}(\eta _{x,r})_\# \mu $ has the same limit, and

\[ \mu (B_ r(x)\setminus A)=o(r^ m). \]
Proof

The plane-tangent ball calculation gives $(\mu \llcorner A)(B_ r)/(\omega _ mr^ m)\to q$. Dividing by the first assumed ratio gives $\mu (B_ r)/(\omega _ mr^ m)\to q$. Subtracting the two expressions gives the stated small mass outside $A$. The difference of the two scaled integrals against a test supported in $B_ R$ is bounded by its supremum norm times $r^{-m}\mu (B_{Rr}(x)\setminus A)$, which tends to zero. Thus the unrestricted tangent is the same. This conclusion needs the positive finite density of the restricted tangent as well as relative density one. $\square$

Theorem 3.8.5 (Weighted tangents without locally finite unweighted area).97F1676D4605 Suppose $M$ is countably $m$-rectifiable and satisfies Definition 3.8.2. Then the weighted approximate tangent exists at $\mathcal H^ m$-almost every point of $M$, and

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ mr^ m}=\theta (x) \]

at almost every such point. This proves the forward direction of [Sim18], Theorem 1.9, p. 91.

Proof

Use the Borel representatives and pieces $M_ j$ in Lemma 3.8.3. Each piece is rectifiable with finite unweighted area. Proposition 3.8.1, applied to its restricted weight, gives the tangent $\theta (x)\mathcal H^ m\llcorner T_ xM_ j$ for $\mu _ j=\mu \llcorner M_ j$ at $\mathcal H^ m$-almost every $x\in M_ j$. By Theorem 2.6.5 for $\chi _{M_ j}$ with respect to $\mu $, one also has $\mu _ j(B_ r)/\mu (B_ r)\to 1$ at $\mu $-almost every point of $M_ j$. The null-set equivalence makes both conclusions simultaneous outside an $\mathcal H^ m$-null subset of $M_ j$. Lemma 3.8.4 transfers the tangent to $\mu $. A countable union of exceptional subsets covers the only exclusions in $M$. The ball-density assertion follows either from that lemma or by continuous approximation of the unit-ball indicator. Positivity of $\theta $ is used when transferring relative density to absolute scale $r^ m$. $\square$

Theorem 3.8.6 (Positive weighted plane tangents imply rectifiability).BC73C8D035CF Under Definition 3.8.2, suppose the weighted approximate tangent exists at $\mathcal H^ m$-almost every point of $M$. Then $M$ is countably $m$-rectifiable. Together with Theorem 3.8.5, this gives the weighted characterization in [Sim18], Theorem 1.9.

Proof

At a point with tangent $\theta (x)\mathcal H^ m\llcorner P_ x$, the continuous-cutoff and compact-cone arguments in Lemma 3.7.6 give positive lower density $\theta (x)\omega _ m$ for $\mu $ and zero normalized mass in $C_{1/4}(x,P_ x)\cap B_ r(x)$. Apply Theorem 3.7.5 to the full-measure set of such points, using the Radon measure from Lemma 3.8.3. The result is a countable graph cover; the omitted subset is Hausdorff-null by hypothesis. This proves rectifiability without asserting locally finite unweighted area. The positive density constant may vary with $x$, as allowed by the pointwise cone theorem. $\square$

Corollary 3.8.7 (The tangent plane is independent of a positive weight).EC2DBE512FEA Suppose a rectifiable $M$ carries two positive finite measurable weights $\theta ,\rho $ whose weighted measures are locally finite. Their weighted approximate tangent planes agree at $\mathcal H^ m$-almost every point of $M$, although their limiting plane measures have coefficients $\theta (x)$ and $\rho (x)$.

Proof

Exhaust $M$ by compactly located measurable pieces where both weights lie between $1/j$ and $j$. Each piece has finite unweighted area. On each piece Proposition 3.8.1 gives the same ordinary tangent plane for both weights, with their respective coefficients. Radon differentiation of the piece's indicator and Lemma 3.8.4 transfer each limit to its full weighted measure. Remove the countable union of exceptional Hausdorff-null subsets. The limiting planes coincide there because both were obtained from the same piece. This proof compares planes, not the unequal scalar multiplicities. $\square$
