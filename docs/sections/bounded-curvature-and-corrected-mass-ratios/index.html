6.8 Bounded curvature and corrected mass ratios07F762F17112

07F762F17112

Here $V$ is an $m$-varifold in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $1\leq m<N$, with weight $\mu $. Assume throughout that

\[ \delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot X\, d\mu ,\qquad |H|\leq \Lambda \quad \mu \text{-almost everywhere}, \]

for compactly supported $C^1$ fields, with $\Lambda \geq 0$. In particular the first variation has no singular boundary part. These are sufficient hypotheses for [Sim18], Chapter 8, (3.4)–(3.5), p. 241. No rectifiability is required unless stated.

Proposition 6.8.1 (Annular identity with mean-curvature error).11614439894F Let $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$ and $0<\sigma <\rho <R$. With $f_ a,T_ a$ as in Definition 6.6.1, define

\[ \gamma _{\sigma ,\rho }(r)=\begin{cases} \max \{ r,\sigma \} ^{-m}-\rho ^{-m},& r<\rho ,\\ 0,& r\geq \rho . \end{cases} \]

Then

\[ f_ a(\rho )-f_ a(\sigma ) =\int _{G_ m(B_\rho (a)\setminus B_\sigma (a))}T_ a\, dV +\frac1m\int \gamma _{\sigma ,\rho }(|x-a|)H(x)\cdot (x-a)\, d\mu . \]

In particular

\[ f_ a(\rho )-f_ a(\sigma )\geq -\Lambda \int _\sigma ^\rho f_ a(t)\, dt. \]
Proof

Use the cutoff and divergence computation in the proof of Proposition 6.6.3. First variation is now $-\int \gamma H\cdot (x-a)\, d\mu $, giving the stated sign after rearrangement. Approximate arbitrary radii from below through continuity radii as before; the bounded curvature and local finiteness justify dominated convergence for the extra term.

The layer-cake formula

\[ \gamma _{\sigma ,\rho }(r)=\int _\sigma ^\rho m t^{-m-1}\boldsymbol 1_{\{ r<t\} }\, dt \]

shows, by Tonelli, that the magnitude of a negative curvature contribution is at most

\[ \Lambda \int _\sigma ^\rho t^{-m-1} \int _{B_ t(a)}|x-a|\, d\mu \, dt \leq \Lambda \int _\sigma ^\rho f_ a(t)\, dt. \]

The radial-tilt term is nonnegative. No differentiability of the ball-mass function was used. $\square$

Lemma 6.8.2 (An integrating factor for a measure differential inequality).8BCD68460FB3 Let $f$ be a locally bounded, left-continuous function of locally bounded variation on an interval $I$. If for every $s<t$ in $I$,

\[ f(t)-f(s)\geq -\Lambda \int _ s^ t f(r)\, dr, \]

then $e^{\Lambda t}f(t)$ is nondecreasing.

Proof

Fix $t_0\in I$ and put $g(t)=f(t)+\Lambda \int _{t_0}^ t f(r)\, dr$. The hypothesis says $g$ is nondecreasing, so its distributional derivative is a nonnegative Radon measure. The product rule for a smooth factor and a BV function gives

\[ D(e^{\Lambda t}f)=e^{\Lambda t}(Df+\Lambda f\, dt) =e^{\Lambda t}Dg\geq 0. \]

For completeness this product rule follows by writing the distributional derivative against a compactly supported smooth test and expanding the derivative of its product with $e^{\Lambda t}$. A function with nonnegative distributional derivative has a nondecreasing representative, as is seen by mollification. Left continuity selects that representative at every interior point, including jumps, and proves the claimed pointwise conclusion. $\square$

Theorem 6.8.3 (Exponential monotonicity for bounded mean curvature).6871F1D8A0BA Let $V$ be an $m$-varifold in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $1\leq m<N$, with $\mu =\| V\| $. Suppose $\delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot X\, d\mu $ for all $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N)$ and $|H|\leq \Lambda $ almost everywhere, where $\Lambda \geq 0$. If $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$, then

\[ r\longmapsto e^{\Lambda r}\frac{\mu (B_ r(a))}{\omega _ m r^ m} \]

is nondecreasing for $0<r<R$. The density $\Theta ^ m(\mu ,a)$ exists, is finite and nonnegative, and $\mu (\{ a\} )=0$. This is the bounded-mean-curvature case of [Sim18], Chapter 8, (3.5), p. 241.

Proof

The function $r\mapsto \mu (B_ r(a))$ is nondecreasing and left-continuous. Therefore $f_ a$ is locally bounded and of locally bounded variation away from zero. Proposition 6.8.1 and Lemma 6.8.2 give exponential monotonicity. Its nonnegative ratios have a finite limit at zero, bounded above by any fixed-radius value. Since $e^{\Lambda r}\to 1$, that limit is the usual density after division by $\omega _ m$. The resulting small-ball bound $\mu (B_ r(a))\leq Cr^ m$ excludes atoms. $\square$

Corollary 6.8.4 (Density and mass lower bounds with bounded curvature).CECE32EC9E60 If $V$ is also integral, then for every $a\in \operatorname {spt}\mu $,

\[ \Theta ^ m(\mu ,a)\geq 1,\qquad \mu (B_ r(a))\geq e^{-\Lambda r}\omega _ m r^ m \]

whenever $\overline{B_ r(a)}\subset U$.

Proof

First density is upper semicontinuous in the center. Indeed, for $a_ j\to a$ and $0<\varepsilon <s$, the inclusion $B_{s-\varepsilon }(a_ j)\subset B_ s(a)$ and exponential monotonicity give

\[ \Theta ^ m(\mu ,a_ j)\leq e^{\Lambda (s-\varepsilon )}\frac{\mu (B_ s(a))}{\omega _ m(s-\varepsilon )^ m} \]

for large $j$. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, then $s\downarrow 0$. Integrality supplies density at least one almost everywhere. Choose such points approaching any support point; upper semicontinuity gives the same lower bound there. Finally compare the corrected ratio at $r$ with its limit at zero. This proof parallels Corollary 6.3.4 and specifies the curvature correction. $\square$

Example 6.8.5 (Why a disk is not a zero-curvature test case).FD22E871D957 A flat unit $m$-disk in an $m$-plane of $\mathbb R^ N$, viewed as a varifold on all of $\mathbb R^ N$, has a boundary contribution to first variation. It does not satisfy the hypotheses of this section with $H=0$.

Proof

The divergence theorem gives $\delta V(X)=\int _{\partial D}X\cdot \nu \, d\mathcal H^{m-1}$, where $\nu $ is the outward conormal; for $m=1$ this is the signed endpoint sum. This measure is singular with respect to the disk's $m$-dimensional weight. The centered ratio $\| V\| (B_ r)/r^ m$ equals $\omega _ m$ for $r\leq 1$ and $\omega _ m r^{-m}$ for $r>1$, so it decreases beyond the boundary. Vanishing classical curvature in the interior cannot remove that boundary term. $\square$
