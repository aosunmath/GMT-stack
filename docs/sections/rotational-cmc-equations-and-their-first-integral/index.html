11.10 Rotational CMC equations and their first integral2650A51A7E9F

2650A51A7E9F

The graph equation and conserved quantity are described in [Maz02], Section 2.1, p. 4. The rotational metric and curvature formulas are also in [BBM13], pp. 2–3. We use trace mean curvature with $A=D\nu $ and derive the signs and arclength equations directly.

Proposition 11.10.1 (Geometry of an arclength surface of revolution).321B1FEAB359 Let $(r(s),z(s))$ be a smooth plane curve on an interval, with $r>0$ and $(r')^2+(z')^2=1$. Write $r'=\cos \psi $, $z'=\sin \psi $ for a smooth angle lift. The immersion

\[ F(s,\theta )=(r(s)\cos \theta ,r(s)\sin \theta ,z(s)) \]

has metric $ds^2+r^2d\theta ^2$. Relative to $\nu =(z'\cos \theta ,z'\sin \theta ,-r')$, its principal curvatures are $\psi '$ and $z'/r$. Thus its trace mean curvature is $H=\psi '+\sin \psi /r$.

Proof

The coordinate derivatives are orthogonal, with lengths one and $r$. The indicated normal is orthogonal to both and has length one. Differentiation gives $D_ s\nu =\psi 'F_ s$ and $D_\theta \nu =(z'/r)F_\theta $. These are the two eigenvalue equations for the shape operator. A smooth angle lift exists on any interval because the tangent takes values in the circle; it may wind, so no fixed principal branch of the angle is required. $\square$

Proposition 11.10.2 (The rotational CMC system and local uniqueness).875DC79A9E8E A rotational arclength immersion has constant trace curvature $H$ precisely when

\[ r'=\cos \psi ,\qquad z'=\sin \psi ,\qquad \psi '=H-\frac{\sin \psi }{r}. \]

For any initial data with $r>0$, this system has a unique smooth local solution. Its first integral is

\[ c=r\sin \psi -\frac H2r^2. \]
Proof

The curvature formula gives the system. Its right side is smooth for $r>0$, so the ordinary local existence and uniqueness theorem applies. Differentiation along a solution yields

\[ c'=\cos \psi \sin \psi +r\cos \psi \left(H-\frac{\sin \psi }{r}\right)-Hr\cos \psi =0. \]

Thus $c$ is constant throughout the interval of existence. The ODE theorem is a background analytic input; this statement gives no continuation through $r=0$. $\square$

Proposition 11.10.3 (The cylindrical graph equation).B7727F541343 For a positive smooth function $r=r(z)$, the cylindrical graph $(r(z)\cos \theta ,r(z)\sin \theta ,z)$, oriented by its outward radial normal, has trace mean curvature

\[ H=\frac1{r\sqrt{1+r_ z^2}}-\frac{r_{zz}}{(1+r_ z^2)^{3/2}}. \]

For constant $H$ its equation and first integral are

\[ r_{zz}=\frac{1+r_ z^2}{r}-H(1+r_ z^2)^{3/2},\qquad c=\frac r{\sqrt{1+r_ z^2}}-\frac H2r^2. \]
Proof

Reparametrize the meridian by arclength increasing with $z$. Then $z_ s=(1+r_ z^2)^{-1/2}$, $r_ s=r_ z(1+r_ z^2)^{-1/2}$, and the tangent angle satisfies $\psi _ s=-r_{zz}(1+r_ z^2)^{-3/2}$. Substitute into Proposition 11.10.1 and solve for $r_{zz}$. The conserved quantity is the preceding first integral with $\sin \psi =z_ s$. At $H=1$, this is equation (1) in [Maz02], p. 4. The first integral used there is $-2c$. $\square$

Proposition 11.10.4 (The first integral from area minus volume).70AC5E706A80 For fixed endpoint radii of a cylindrical graph, critical points of area minus $H$ times enclosed volume satisfy the ODE in Proposition 11.10.3. The translation-invariant one-dimensional integrand is

\[ L(r,p)=2\pi r\sqrt{1+p^2}-H\pi r^2, \]

and its Beltrami quantity $L-pL_ p$ equals $2\pi c$.

Proof

The lateral area element is $r\sqrt{1+r_ z^2}\, d\theta \, dz$, and the volume between fixed axial planes is $\int \pi r^2dz$. Endpoint-fixed variations kill boundary terms. Direct differentiation gives

\[ L_ r=2\pi \sqrt{1+p^2}-2\pi Hr,\qquad L_ p=\frac{2\pi rp}{\sqrt{1+p^2}}. \]

The equation $(L_ p)'=L_ r$ simplifies to the stated graph ODE. Because $L$ has no explicit $z$ dependence, $(L-r_ zL_ p)'=r_ z(L_ r-(L_ p)')=0$. Evaluating $L-r_ zL_ p$ gives $2\pi [r/\sqrt{1+r_ z^2}-Hr^2/2]$. This is a stationarity calculation; it does not assert that a critical graph minimizes area under the volume constraint. $\square$

Lemma 11.10.5 (Factoring the positive-flux radius equation).739CD651E3E2 Fix $H>0$, $0<a<1/H$, $b=2/H-a$, and $c=Hab/2$. Then

\[ r^2-\left(\frac H2r^2+c\right)^2 =\frac{H^2}4(b^2-r^2)(r^2-a^2). \]

In an arclength solution with this flux and $z'>0$, the radius belongs to $[a,b]$ and satisfies

\[ (r')^2=\frac{H^2}{4r^2}(b^2-r^2)(r^2-a^2),\qquad z'=\frac H2r+\frac cr. \]
Proof

Use $a+b=2/H$ and $ab=2c/H$ to compare coefficients in the polynomial identity. The conserved quantity gives $z'=Hr/2+c/r>0$, and $r'^2+z'^2=1$ gives the displayed radius equation. Its left side is nonnegative and $r>0$, so the factorization forces $a\le r\le b$. The squared equation alone is not a uniqueness statement at a turning radius; the smooth second-order system in Proposition 11.10.2 retains that information. $\square$
