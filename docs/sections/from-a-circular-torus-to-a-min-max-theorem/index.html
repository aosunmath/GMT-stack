15.3 From a circular torus to a min-max theoremB44199ED186F

B44199ED186F

The sources here are the authors' papers: [LY82], the opening pages of [SimW93], and the introduction of [MNWill]. The entries distinguish the historical questions of identifying a candidate, proving existence of an optimizer, and determining the sharp value. They do not replace the mathematical statements and proof dependencies linked below.

Remark 15.3.1 (A family calculation suggested a global conjecture).E15742E4F29E Marques–Neves date Willmore's torus conjecture to 1965 in [MNWill], Section 1.1, p. 684. The circular torus with ratio $R/r=\sqrt2$ attains energy $2\pi ^2$, but a calculation over circular tori leaves all other immersions untreated. The same candidate is a stereographic image of the Clifford torus in $S^3$. Theorems 16.18.2 and 10.3.6, and Example 16.18.5, explain why this candidate is natural without assuming the eventual global theorem.

Remark 15.3.2 (Conformal area separated the multiple-point obstruction).07DE29ED0FC2 In their 1982 paper [LY82], Li–Yau introduced conformal volume and proved a multiplicity bound for the bending energy of an immersed surface; see the introduction and Theorem 6, p. 286. A double point forces energy at least $8\pi $, already above the conjectured torus minimum $2\pi ^2$. Thus the unresolved low-energy problem concerned embeddings. Theorem 10.4.4 gives a direct radial proof of that obstruction, while Corollary 10.4.9 shows precisely how it combines with the later embedded theorem. The two numerical thresholds serve different purposes.

Remark 15.3.3 (Existence and the exact minimum were separate achievements).F2980A8D4351 Simon's 1993 paper [SimW93], pp. 281–283, established a smooth embedded torus minimizing Willmore energy among embedded tori in each Euclidean ambient dimension at least three. This supplied an optimizer without identifying its sharp energy in three-space. The 2014 paper of Marques–Neves [MNWill], Theorem A, p. 685, determined the positive-genus bound in $S^3$ and its equality case through min-max theory. Their Corollary D, p. 687, realizes the Clifford torus through a five-parameter min-max family. The local square identities developed here illuminate Simon's estimates; neither his full existence proof nor the five-parameter min-max proof has been replaced by those local calculations.
