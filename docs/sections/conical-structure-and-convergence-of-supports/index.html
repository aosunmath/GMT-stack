6.7 Conical structure and convergence of supports7E1DC84EF076

7E1DC84EF076

This section supplies the homogeneity argument in tangent-cone construction and separates weak measure convergence from convergence of supports. The source for the cone argument is [Minter], Lemmas 1.1–1.2, pp. 11–12. Integral compactness remains the external input in Theorem 6.2.1.

Lemma 6.7.1 (Stationarity is closed under varifold convergence).30C4784016ED If stationary $m$-varifolds $V_ j$ converge weakly to $V$ in $U$, then $V$ is stationary in $U$.

Proof

For $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N)$ the function $(x,S)\mapsto \operatorname {div}_ S X(x)$ is continuous and compactly supported on the Grassmann bundle. Thus $\delta V(X)=\lim _ j\delta V_ j(X)=0$. This assertion alone does not guarantee that an arbitrary limit is integral; integrality uses Theorem 6.2.1 under its hypotheses. $\square$

Lemma 6.7.2 (The weight determines a rectifiable varifold).FE7578F66513 Two rectifiable $m$-varifolds with the same weight are equal as measures on the Grassmann bundle.

Proof

Write their weights as $\theta _ i\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M_ i$ with positive densities. Equality of the weights implies that $M_1$ and $M_2$ differ by an $\mathcal H^ m$-null set, after discarding null subsets. Their approximate tangent planes therefore agree almost everywhere by the rectifiable tangent characterization, Theorem 3.1.4. Their Radon–Nikodym densities also agree. Substitute into Definition 6.1.2 for every compactly supported continuous test function. The positivity of the densities and rectifiability are essential to this argument. $\square$

Proposition 6.7.3 (Radial tangency gives homogeneous weight).A324E62A87A7 Let $C$ be a stationary $m$-varifold on all of $\mathbb R^ N$, with $x\in S$ for $C$-almost every $(x,S)$. Its weight $\nu $ satisfies

\[ s^{-m}\int \phi (x/s)\, d\nu (x)=\int \phi (x)\, d\nu (x)\qquad (s>0) \]

for every $\phi \in C_ c^1(\mathbb R^ N)$. In particular $\nu (sA)=s^ m\nu (A)$ for every Borel set $A$.

Proof

Test stationarity with $X(x)=\phi (x)x$. Radial tangency gives $\int (m\phi +x\cdot D\phi )\, d\nu =0$. The derivative of $F(s)=s^{-m}\int \phi (x/s)\, d\nu $ is

\[ F'(s)=-s^{-m-1}\int \bigl (m\phi (x/s)+(x/s)\cdot D\phi (x/s)\bigr )\, d\nu =0, \]

using the same identity with the test function $x\mapsto \phi (x/s)$. Differentiation is justified by a common compact support and bounded derivatives when $s$ ranges in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty )$. Thus $F(s)=F(1)$. Equality on smooth tests identifies the two Radon measures and extends to Borel sets. $\square$

Theorem 6.7.4 (Constant mass ratio characterizes rectifiable stationary cones).21FBA0CA65BE For a stationary rectifiable $m$-varifold $C$ on $\mathbb R^ N$, the following are equivalent: $r^{-m}\| C\| (B_ r(0))$ is constant for all $r>0$; $x\in S$ for $C$-almost every $(x,S)$; and $(\eta _{0,s})_\# C=C$ for every $s>0$. Compare [Minter], Lemma 1.2, pp. 11–12.

Proof

The annular identity, Proposition 6.6.3, applied on countably many annuli exhausting $\mathbb R^ N\setminus \{ 0\} $, proves radial tangency from the constant ratio. The origin has zero weight by Corollary 6.6.4. Radial tangency gives the homogeneous weight identity by Proposition 6.7.3. The dilation has tangential Jacobian $s^{-m}$, so the weights of $C$ and $(\eta _{0,s})_\# C$ coincide. Both varifolds are rectifiable; Lemma 6.7.2 identifies their entire lifts. Finally dilation invariance gives $\| C\| (B_ r)=r^ m\| C\| (B_1)$ directly. Homogeneity of the weight alone would not justify identifying the Grassmannian distributions for a general nonrectifiable varifold. $\square$

Proposition 6.7.5 (Uniform mass lower bound at integral support points).46494077AB90 If $V$ is stationary and integral in $U$, $a\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| $, and $\overline{B_ r(a)}\subset U$, then

\[ \| V\| (B_ r(a))\geq \omega _ m r^ m. \]

In particular a nonzero stationary integral varifold on all of $\mathbb R^ N$ has infinite total mass.

Proof

Corollary 6.3.4 gives density at least one. Monotonicity bounds each normalized mass ratio below by its limit at zero. For an entire nonzero varifold choose one support point and let $r\to \infty $. $\square$

Proposition 6.7.6 (Local convergence of stationary integral supports).3F8B37A1F50E Suppose stationary integral $m$-varifolds $V_ j$ converge to a stationary integral varifold $V$ in $U$. For every compact $K\subset U$ and $\varepsilon >0$, for all sufficiently large $j$,

\[ \operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| \cap K\subset \{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,\operatorname {spt}\| V\| )<\varepsilon \} , \]
\[ \operatorname {spt}\| V\| \cap K\subset \{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,\operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| )<\varepsilon \} . \]

Distances are to the full supports in $U$, not their intersections with $K$; the distance to an empty support is infinite.

Proof

If the first inclusion fails, choose support points $a_ j\in K$ staying at least $\varepsilon $ from the limiting support, and pass to $a_ j\to a\in K$. Choose $r>0$ small enough that $\overline{B_{2r}(a)}\subset U$ misses that support. A nonnegative compactly supported cutoff equal to one on $B_ r(a)$ then has integrals against $\| V_ j\| $ tending to zero. But $B_{r/2}(a_ j)\subset B_ r(a)$ for large $j$, so Proposition 6.7.5 gives a fixed positive lower bound, a contradiction.

If the second inclusion fails, choose $a_ j\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| \cap K$ tending to $a$ while $B_\varepsilon (a_ j)$ misses $\operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| $. The relative support is closed, so $a$ belongs to it. A fixed sufficiently small ball about $a$ lies in these empty balls for large $j$. All compactly supported test integrals in that ball vanish in the limit, contradicting that $a$ is a support point. Only the first direction needs the uniform integral mass lower bound. $\square$
