16.15 Self-shrinkers in higher codimension67B67A857CA0

67B67A857CA0

These examples use the vector convention $\vec H=\operatorname {tr}B=\Delta X=-X^\perp /2$ for a smooth Euclidean immersion $X:M^ n\to \mathbb R^ N$, where $n\geq 1$. Thus the squared curvature is $|B|^2=\sum _{i,j}|B(e_ i,e_ j)|^2$. The primary models are [CL12], Section 5, pp. 8–9, rescaled from that paper's convention $\vec H=-X^\perp $. The scalar hypersurface formulas of Section 14.17 cannot simply be used for a higher-rank normal bundle.

Proposition 16.15.1 (Minimal submanifolds of a sphere give shrinkers).F8D9EB520CEE Let $X:M^ n\to S^{N-1}(R)\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $n\geq 1$ and $R>0$, be a smooth immersion. It is a Euclidean self-shrinker if and only if $R=\sqrt{2n}$ and it is minimal in that sphere. For this radius,

\[ |B^{\mathbb R^ N}|^2=|B^{S^{N-1}(R)}|^2+\frac12. \]
Proof

The sphere's vector second fundamental form in Euclidean space is $-(1/R^2)gX$. Decomposing the ambient derivative first relative to $M$ in the sphere and then relative to the sphere in Euclidean space gives

\[ B^{\mathbb R^ N}_{ij}=B^{S^{N-1}(R)}_{ij}-\frac{\delta _{ij}}{R^2}X, \qquad \vec H^{\mathbb R^ N}=\vec H^{S^{N-1}(R)}-\frac n{R^2}X. \]

The two normal summands are perpendicular. Also $X^\perp =X$, since $|X|$ is constant. The equation $\vec H^{\mathbb R^ N}=-X/2$ therefore says separately that the spherical mean curvature vanishes and $n/R^2=1/2$. Squaring the orthogonal decomposition of $B$ gives the norm identity, since the radial summand has squared norm $n/R^2=1/2$. $\square$

Example 16.15.2 (Products of round factors and a Euclidean factor).1069828FDBF6 Let $p\geq 1$, $d_1,\ldots ,d_ p\geq 1$ and $q\geq 0$ be integers, and put $m=\sum _ i d_ i$, $n=m+q$. The embedded product

\[ M=\prod _{i=1}^ p S^{d_ i}(\sqrt{2d_ i})\times \mathbb R^ q\subset \mathbb R^{n+p} \]

is a complete proper self-shrinker. Its curvature satisfies $|B|^2=p/2$ and $|\vec H|^2=m/2$. It has polynomial area growth, is compact exactly when $q=0$, and in that case is minimal in $S^{n+p-1}(\sqrt{2n})$.

Proof

For arbitrary radii $r_ i$, the mean-curvature vector has $i$th spherical block $-d_ iX_ i/r_ i^2$ and zero Euclidean block. The normal projection of $X=(X_1,\ldots ,X_ p,y)$ is $(X_1,\ldots ,X_ p,0)$. Thus the shrinker equation holds exactly when $r_ i^2=2d_ i$ for every $i$. The different radial normals are orthogonal, so

\[ |B|^2=\sum _ i\frac{d_ i}{r_ i^2}=p/2, \qquad |\vec H|^2=\sum _ i\frac{d_ i^2}{r_ i^2}=m/2. \]

A product of complete round metrics and a Euclidean metric is complete. Compactness of the spherical factors and boundedness of the Euclidean coordinate on ambient compact sets prove properness. The portion in an ambient ball is bounded by the product of the total spherical volumes and a radius-$R$ Euclidean ball volume, hence has growth at most $C(1+R)^ q$. When $q=0$, the squared ambient radius is $\sum _ i2d_ i=2n$ and Proposition 16.15.1 proves spherical minimality. Compare [CL12], Examples 5.1–5.2. $\square$

Proposition 16.15.3 (Gaussian area of the product models).A67A81338F21 Write $\sigma _ d=\mathcal H^ d(S^ d(1))$. The normalized Gaussian area of Example 16.15.2 is

\[ F_{0,1}(M)=\prod _{i=1}^ p\left[(4\pi )^{-d_ i/2}e^{-d_ i/2}\sigma _{d_ i}(2d_ i)^{d_ i/2}\right]. \]

It is independent of the dimension $q$ of the Euclidean factor.

Proof

The product area element factors, and $|X|^2=2\sum _ i d_ i+|y|^2$. Tonelli's theorem separates the positive Gaussian integral into the spherical factors and

\[ (4\pi )^{-q/2}\int _{\mathbb R^ q}e^{-|y|^2/4}\, dy=1. \]

The radius-$\sqrt{2d_ i}$ sphere has area $\sigma _{d_ i}(2d_ i)^{d_ i/2}$, on which the Gaussian exponential is the constant $e^{-d_ i/2}$. Multiplying gives the formula. The statement computes Gaussian area at center zero and scale one; it does not invoke a separate theorem identifying an entropy supremum over all centers and scales. $\square$

Example 16.15.4 (The Clifford torus shrinker in four dimensions).2E92C8C6AAA0 The map

\[ X(\theta ,\varphi )=(\sqrt2\cos \theta ,\sqrt2\sin \theta , \sqrt2\cos \varphi ,\sqrt2\sin \varphi ) \]

on $(\mathbb R/2\pi \mathbb Z)^2$ is a flat embedded torus in $\mathbb R^4$ with $|B|^2=1$, $|\vec H|^2=1$, area $8\pi ^2$ and $F_{0,1}=2\pi /e$. It is minimal in $S^3(2)$, where its squared second fundamental form is $1/2$.

Proof

The metric is $2(d\theta ^2+d\varphi ^2)$, so it is flat with area $8\pi ^2$. Apply Example 16.15.2 with two circle factors. Since $|X|^2=4$, direct substitution gives $F_{0,1}=(4\pi )^{-1}e^{-1}8\pi ^2=2\pi /e$. Proposition 16.15.1 subtracts $1/2$ from the Euclidean curvature norm to give the spherical value. This is a codimension-two shrinker and its Euclidean curvature exceeds the hypersurface gap threshold. $\square$

Proposition 16.15.5 (The compact mean-curvature threshold).5BF54675E5EE On every connected closed Euclidean self-shrinker $M^ n$, in any codimension,

\[ \frac1{\operatorname {Vol}(M)}\int _ M|\vec H|^2\, d\mu =\frac n2. \]

If $|\vec H|^2$ is everywhere at most $n/2$, everywhere at least $n/2$, or constant, then the image lies in $S^{N-1}(\sqrt{2n})$ and is minimal there. See [CL12], Proposition 5.2, for the source normalization.

Proof

The immersion identities give

\[ \Delta |X|^2=2n+2\langle X,\vec H\rangle =2n-4|\vec H|^2. \]

Integrating on a closed manifold gives the average identity. Each stated pointwise condition forces $|\vec H|^2=n/2$ everywhere, so $|X|^2$ is harmonic and hence constant on the connected compact manifold. It follows that $X^ T=\nabla |X|^2/2=0$. The shrinker equation now gives $|X|^2=4|\vec H|^2=2n$, and Proposition 16.15.1 gives spherical minimality. This conclusion does not say that an arbitrary-codimension example is a round hypersphere. $\square$

Proposition 16.15.6 (There is no closed Euclidean self-expander).3A94265F2B89 For $n\geq 1$, no nonempty closed immersed $n$-manifold in Euclidean space satisfies $\vec H=X^\perp /2$, in any codimension.

Proof

For an expander the same immersion calculation gives $\Delta |X|^2=2n+|X^\perp |^2>0$. Its integral on a closed manifold would be positive, whereas the divergence theorem makes that integral zero. This proves the contradiction and the observation in [CL12], Proposition 5.3, directly with the present normalization. $\square$

Lemma 16.15.7 (The Gaussian radial identity in any codimension).36F00EED1081 Let $X:M^ n\to \mathbb R^ N$ be a connected complete smooth boundaryless self-shrinker with polynomial area growth, counting area on the parameter manifold. With $\rho =(4\pi )^{-n/2}e^{-|X|^2/4}$,

\[ \int _ M |X|^2\rho \, d\mu =2n\int _ M\rho \, d\mu . \]

Neither a global unit normal nor hypersurface codimension is required.

Proof

The identities $\Delta X=\vec H=-X^\perp /2$ and $|DX|^2=n$ give $\Delta |X|^2=2n-|X^\perp |^2$. Since $\nabla |X|^2=2X^ T$, subtraction of the Gaussian drift gives

\[ \mathcal D|X|^2=2n-|X|^2,\qquad \mathcal D=\Delta -\tfrac 12\nabla _{X^ T} =\rho ^{-1}\operatorname {div}(\rho \nabla \, \cdot \, ). \]

Polynomial area growth implies integrability of $(1+|X|^2)\rho $ by exactly the annular estimate in Lemma 14.7.4; that estimate uses only area, not codimension. Take intrinsic compact cutoffs $\eta _ R$ equal to one on a radius-$R$ ball, zero outside radius $2R$, with $|\nabla \eta _ R|\leq 2/R$. Completeness supplies compact support. Weighted integration by parts gives

\[ \int \eta _ R(2n-|X|^2)\rho =-\int \langle \nabla \eta _ R,2X^ T\rangle \rho . \]

The right side has absolute value at most $(4/R)\int |X|\rho $, tending to zero. Dominated convergence gives the conclusion on the left. This explicitly extends the hypersurface computation instead of invoking a hypersurface statement for a higher-codimension immersion. $\square$

Proposition 16.15.8 (A complete mean-curvature bound forces spherical containment).97FDF140143F Under the hypotheses of Lemma 16.15.7, assume $|\vec H|^2\geq n/2$ everywhere. Then $|\vec H|^2=n/2$, $X^ T=0$, and the immersed image is a minimal submanifold of $S^{N-1}(\sqrt{2n})$. This is the rescaled assertion of [CL12], Proposition 5.1. It does not classify all minimal submanifolds of that sphere.

Proof

The shrinker equation gives the pointwise identity

\[ |X|^2=|X^ T|^2+4|\vec H|^2\geq 2n. \]

The preceding lemma says that its Gaussian average is exactly $2n$. The nonnegative continuous function $|X|^2-2n$ therefore has zero weighted integral and vanishes everywhere. Both nonnegative terms in $|X^ T|^2+4(|\vec H|^2-n/2)=0$ vanish. The fixed-radius criterion in Proposition 16.15.1 now gives spherical minimality. The Euclidean-factor models of Example 16.15.2, with $q>0$, have $|\vec H|^2=(n-q)/2<n/2$ and unbounded image, showing why that lower threshold cannot simply be omitted. $\square$
