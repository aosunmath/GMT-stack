15.1 Origins of geometric measure theory33FDA03D4698

33FDA03D4698

These historical notes connect the mathematical definitions with the problems and people that shaped them. Personal recollections are attributed to their author; they are not used as substitutes for theorem statements. The principal first-person sources are Fleming's 2015 retrospective [Fle15], his 2016 account of De Giorgi [Fle16], and his 2007 memoir [Fle07].

Remark 15.1.1 (1958–1960: compactness as a design principle).9EF132811524 Fleming recalls that he joined Brown in 1958 and worked with Federer during the academic year 1958–1959 on the paper Normal and integral currents, published in 1960. The objective was more specific than merely enlarging the class of smooth surfaces: generalized domains of integration had to retain enough geometric structure while admitting compactness for variational problems. This helps explain the simultaneous appearance of mass and boundary-mass bounds in the compactness theory. See [Fle15], Section 3, pp. 13–16, and [Fle07], Part I, Section 1. The corresponding definitions are in the currents chapter.

Remark 15.1.2 (De Giorgi's boundary without smoothness).E409AB8EC052 In his account of the 1950s, Fleming emphasizes De Giorgi's replacement of a smooth boundary by a reduced boundary carrying an approximate normal. A divergence theorem could then survive even when the topological boundary was unsuitable for classical differential geometry. The connection with distributional derivatives of characteristic functions brought the problem into the theory of functions of bounded variation. The modern formulation is Theorem 4.1.5; the historical account is [Fle16], Section 3, pp. 3–4. Fleming also stresses the influence of Caccoppoli's earlier work.

Remark 15.1.3 (1969: a dimension threshold that surprised analysts).AE10C8E9DF1A Fleming remembers being at Stanford in spring 1969 when news arrived that the higher-dimensional Bernstein assertion fails for entire minimal graphs over $\mathbb R^ m$ when $m\geq 8$. His recollection records Gilbarg's surprise that a PDE conclusion valid through seven variables could fail beyond that threshold. The geometric explanation passes through nonflat minimizing cones. The dimension here is the graph domain dimension, not the ambient dimension. See [Fle15], Section 7, p. 26, for both the anecdote and the chain from De Giorgi through Almgren and Simons. Compare the minimal graph equation in Proposition 7.2.1. Theorem 7.8.6 supplies the two-dimensional geometric proof, while Theorem 7.9.10 explains the dimension restriction for stable cones with smooth links.

Remark 15.1.4 (Different notions of a generalized surface).A7B67D7E0642 The history did not produce a single universal replacement for a smooth surface. Fleming describes the influence of de Rham's currents, Whitney's geometric integration theory, and Young's generalized surfaces, before discussing Almgren's varifolds. Orientation and boundary make currents suited to one family of questions; measures recording tangent planes make varifolds suited to another. Allard's 1972 paper [Allard72] developed first-variation, compactness, and regularity tools for the latter setting. See [Fle15], Sections 2 and 8. The modern compatibility theorem of White, Theorem 6.2.3, shows how these viewpoints can interact without identifying their topologies.
