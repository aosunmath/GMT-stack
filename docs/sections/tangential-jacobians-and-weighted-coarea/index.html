3.3 Tangential Jacobians and weighted coarea1BA039560294

1BA039560294

Integration along fibers is used both to cut currents and to select level sets of controlled perimeter. The relevant derivative on a rectifiable set is tangential; ambient differentiability at almost every ambient point does not by itself define a derivative on that set. We follow [Sim18], Chapter 3, Section 2, pp. 92–94.

Proposition 3.3.1 (Weighted Euclidean area formula).82E9244AC74A Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.2.2, let $h:V\to [0,\infty ]$ be measurable. Then

\[ \int _ V h(x)J_ mDf(x)\, dx =\int _{\mathbb R^ n}\sum _{x\in f^{-1}(y)}h(x)\, d\mathcal H^ m(y). \]

The sum means integration against counting measure, and both sides may be infinite. If $h$ is signed, the same identity holds when the left side with $|h|$ is finite.

Proof

For $h=\chi _ A$ this is the area formula. Additivity gives the identity for nonnegative simple functions; monotone convergence gives the general nonnegative case. Apply it to the positive and negative parts for the signed statement. This is the extension in [Sim18], Chapter 3, equation (2.5), specialized to a Euclidean domain. Integrability with the Jacobian, rather than unweighted integrability alone, is the relevant condition. $\square$

Definition 3.3.2 (Tangential differential and coarea Jacobian).72A60FF7DFA9 Let $M\subset U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ be countably $m$-rectifiable, $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable, and of locally finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure. Let $f:U\to \mathbb R^ k$ be locally Lipschitz, where $1\leq k<m$. On countably many $C^1$ manifold pieces covering $M$ up to a null set, differentiate the restrictions of $f$ in manifold charts. Their differentials agree on overlaps almost everywhere, defining

\[ d^ Mf_ x:T_ xM\to \mathbb R^ k,\qquad J^ M_ kf(x)=\sqrt{\det \bigl (d^ Mf_ x(d^ Mf_ x)^*\bigr )}. \]

Set the Jacobian to zero on the exceptional null set. If $k=1$, the tangential gradient represents the differential and $J^ M_1f=|\nabla ^ Mf|$. The existence of the manifold decomposition and the almost-everywhere independence are rectifiability results recorded in [Sim18], Chapter 3, Definition 2.1 and equations (2.2), (2.7); their proofs remain external here. At a point where the ambient differential exists, the tangential differential is its restriction to $T_ xM$.

Theorem 3.3.3 (Coarea on a rectifiable set).6D91F1CCF988 With $M,f,m,k$ as in Definition 3.3.2, for every $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable $A\subset M$,

\[ \int _ A J^ M_ k f\, d\mathcal H^ m =\int _{\mathbb R^ k}\mathcal H^{m-k}(A\cap f^{-1}(y))\, dy. \]

The fiber measure is measurable and the equality allows infinite values. For almost every $y$, the fiber $M\cap f^{-1}(y)$ is countably $(m-k)$-rectifiable and has locally finite $\mathcal H^{m-k}$ measure in $U$.

The coarea and rectifiability assertions are [Sim18], Chapter 3, equation (2.8) and Remark 2.10(2), pp. 93–94. Their proofs use manifold decomposition, the manifold coarea theorem, and approximation of Lipschitz maps; those arguments remain external. Local finiteness of the fibers follows from the formula on a countable compact exhaustion and local Lipschitz bounds, which bound $J^ M_ kf$ there.

Corollary 3.3.4 (Weighted rectifiable coarea).F478D1BCB783 For nonnegative measurable $g$ on $M$,

\[ \int _ M gJ^ M_ kf\, d\mathcal H^ m =\int _{\mathbb R^ k}\left(\int _{M\cap f^{-1}(y)}g\, d\mathcal H^{m-k}\right)dy. \]

The identity also holds for signed $g$ with $\int _ M|g|J^ M_ kf<\infty $. In particular, if $C=\{ x:J^ M_ kf(x)=0\} $, then $\mathcal H^{m-k}(C\cap f^{-1}(y))=0$ for almost every $y$; no assertion that $f(C)$ has measure zero is made.

Proof

Approximate a nonnegative function by increasing simple functions and apply Theorem 3.3.3 and monotone convergence on both sides. Positive and negative parts give the signed statement. Set $g=\chi _ C$ for the last assertion. This is [Sim18], Chapter 3, equation (2.9). $\square$

Proposition 3.3.5 (Selecting a radial slice of controlled area).579B93950659 Let $m\geq 2$, let $M$ satisfy the rectifiability and local-finiteness hypotheses above, and suppose $\overline{B_ b(a)}\subset U$, $0<a_0<b$. Then

\[ \int _{a_0}^ b\mathcal H^{m-1}(M\cap \partial B_ r(a))\, dr \leq \mathcal H^ m(M\cap (B_ b(a)\setminus \overline{B_{a_0}(a)})). \]

Consequently, for every $\varepsilon >0$ there is a radius $r\in (a_0,b)$, outside any prescribed Lebesgue-null exceptional set, such that the slice measure is at most the right side divided by $b-a_0$, plus $\varepsilon $.

Proof

Apply weighted coarea to the distance $\rho (x)=|x-a|$ with the annulus indicator. Its tangential gradient has norm at most one. The average bound implies the existence assertion; otherwise integrating the strict opposite inequality outside the exceptional set would contradict the bound. The exceptional-set clause lets this selection be combined with the almost-everywhere conclusions of the current slicing theorem, Theorem 5.4.2. $\square$

Example 3.3.6 (Projection recovers Fubini).33937AFF00CD On $M=\mathbb R^ m$, let $f(x_1,\ldots ,x_ m)=(x_1,\ldots ,x_ k)$. Then $d^ Mf(d^ Mf)^*=I_ k$ and $J^ M_ kf=1$. The fibers are affine copies of $\mathbb R^{m-k}$, so weighted coarea becomes Fubini–Tonelli. This is [Sim18], Chapter 2, Remark 7.4(2).

Example 3.3.7 (An ambient exceptional set can contain the whole surface).4C93F9F0300B For $M=\mathbb R^ m\times \{ 0\} \subset \mathbb R^{m+1}$ and $f(x,z)=|z|$, the ambient function is Lipschitz but is not differentiable at any point of $M$. Its restriction to $M$ is constant and its tangential gradient is zero everywhere. Thus assigning an arbitrary ambient derivative on the ambient Lebesgue-null exceptional set is not a definition of the tangential derivative. Differentiation in manifold charts is essential.
