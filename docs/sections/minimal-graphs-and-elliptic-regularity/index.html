7.2 Minimal graphs and elliptic regularity6777B79D5D8A

6777B79D5D8A

The graph equation is a first bridge from geometric variation to nonlinear elliptic PDE. Simon's supplied Lectures on PDE, last updated March 5, 2015, develops the analytic background and treats the minimal surface and mean curvature equations in Chapter 19 [SimonPDE].

Proposition 7.2.1 (The minimal graph equation).118E021473BF Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be open and $u\in C^2(U)$. The graph of $u$ is stationary for compactly supported vertical variations if and only if

\[ \operatorname {div}\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}}\right)=0. \]

Equivalently, $\bigl (\delta _{ij}-u_ i u_ j/(1+|Du|^2)\bigr )u_{ij}=0$, where repeated indices are summed. Compare [SimonPDE], Chapter 19, and [ChMin], Section 18.

Proof

For a relatively compact domain containing the support of a test function $\phi $, the graph area is $\int \sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$ by the area formula. Its derivative at $u+t\phi $ is $\int Du\cdot D\phi /\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$. Integration by parts gives the stated equation. Expanding the divergence gives the nondivergence expression. Since $u$ is $C^2$, the weak and pointwise expressions agree. $\square$

Remark 7.2.2 (Regularity techniques and their hypotheses).5BFC45FF0297 The analytic and geometric regularity inputs are developed separately below. Definitions 7.2.3 and 7.5.2 specify weak equations; Proposition 7.2.4 identifies the gradient bound that gives uniform ellipticity. Theorems 7.5.6 and 7.5.8 provide distinct estimates for measurable and Hölder coefficients. The graph comparison and calibration results then give geometric consequences.

For nonsmooth varifolds, Theorem 6.3.6 constructs blow-ups, Theorem 6.4.3 states the density and mean-curvature hypotheses for Allard regularity, and Theorem 7.6.6 gives a low-dimensional stable curvature estimate. Their sources are [SimonPDE], [ChMin], and [Minter]. These results do not assert unrestricted regularity for stationary varifolds; cylindrical-cone and general sheeting theories still require their own development.

Definition 7.2.3 (Weak minimal graphs).2BE9138D926B A function $u\in W^{1,\infty }_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ open, is a weak solution of the scalar minimal surface equation if

\[ \int _ U F(Du)\cdot D\phi =0\quad (\phi \in C_ c^\infty (U)), \qquad F(p)=\frac p{\sqrt{1+|p|^2}}. \]

The integrand is locally integrable, so the equation is defined without second derivatives. Here the graph has codimension one. See [Minter], Section 3.1, p. 48.

Proposition 7.2.4 (Ellipticity and the gradient bound).29B464EBCF55 For $F$ in Definition 7.2.3, write $W(p)=\sqrt{1+|p|^2}$. Then

\[ A_{ij}(p)=\partial _{p_ j}F_ i(p)=\frac{\delta _{ij}}{W(p)}-\frac{p_ i p_ j}{W(p)^3}. \]

Its eigenvalues are $W^{-1}$ on $p^\perp $ and $W^{-3}$ in the direction of $p$; at $p=0$ they are all one. Consequently, when $|p|\leq L$,

\[ (1+L^2)^{-3/2}|\xi |^2\leq A(p)\xi \cdot \xi \leq |\xi |^2. \]

The nondivergence coefficient matrix $a(p)=W(p)A(p)$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $(1+|p|^2)^{-1}$. These ellipticity constants deteriorate as the gradient grows. Compare [ChMin], Exercise 18.1.

Proof

Differentiate $p_ i(1+|p|^2)^{-1/2}$. Decompose $\xi $ into its components parallel and perpendicular to $p$. The displayed eigenvalues and bounds follow directly. $\square$

Proposition 7.2.5 (Linearization and differences of graph solutions).65270DE0F6E0 For a smooth $u$, the linearization of $u\mapsto \operatorname {div}F(Du)$ in direction $v$ is $\operatorname {div}(A(Du)Dv)$. If $u_1,u_2$ are smooth minimal graph solutions, their difference $w=u_2-u_1$ satisfies

\[ \operatorname {div}(\overline A Dw)=0,\qquad \overline A(x)=\int _0^1 A\bigl ((1-t)Du_1(x)+tDu_2(x)\bigr )\, dt. \]

If both gradients have norm at most $L$, then $\overline A$ has the bounds in Proposition 7.2.4. In particular each derivative $D_ k u$ of a smooth solution satisfies $\operatorname {div}(A(Du)D(D_ k u))=0$.

Proof

Differentiate $F(Du+tDv)$ under the divergence. For two solutions, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives $F(Du_2)-F(Du_1)=\overline A Dw$; take its divergence. The segment between the two gradients stays in the ball of radius $L$, so averaging preserves the ellipticity bounds. Differentiating the equation in $x_ k$ gives the last claim. This is the divergence-form counterpart of the linearization in [ChMin], Proposition 19.3. $\square$

Theorem 7.2.6 (Smoothness of Lipschitz weak minimal graphs).86FA24C083FB Every locally Lipschitz weak solution of the scalar minimal surface equation on an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ has a smooth representative in $U$. No smallness of its Lipschitz constant is required locally. This scalar result does not extend as stated to arbitrary higher-codimension minimal graph systems. The proof uses difference quotients, uniform ellipticity on compact subsets, De Giorgi–Nash–Moser regularity for derivatives, and Schauder bootstrapping. Sources: [Minter], Section 3.1, and the discussion following Theorem 2.5; [SimonPDE], Chapters 18–19. The weak-solution regularity proof remains external.

Proposition 7.2.7 (Bootstrapping a classical minimal graph).9843EEC0DB02 A $C^2$ solution of the minimal surface equation on $U$ belongs to $C^\infty (U)$.

Proof

On nested relatively compact balls the gradient is bounded, and the coefficients $a^{ij}(Du)$ are $C^1$, hence Hölder continuous for every exponent below one. Apply Theorem 7.5.8 to $a^{ij}(Du)D_{ij}u=0$ to obtain $u\in C^{2,\alpha }_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Differentiation gives

\[ a^{ij}(Du)D_{ij}(D_ k u)+ \bigl (\partial _{p_\ell }a^{ij}(Du)D_{ij}u\bigr )D_\ell (D_ k u)=0. \]

The lower-order coefficients are $C^{0,\alpha }$. Interior Schauder regularity gives one further derivative, with the differentiated equation justified by difference quotients if needed. Repeating on smaller balls proves the claim. This is the bootstrap in [ChMin], Lemma 19.1, pp. 51–52. $\square$

Proposition 7.2.8 (Strong comparison for minimal graphs).EA3CB199A4FD Let $U$ be connected, and let $u_1,u_2\in C^2(U)$ solve the minimal surface equation. If $u_1\leq u_2$ in $U$ and equality holds at one interior point, then $u_1=u_2$ throughout $U$.

Proof

The solutions are smooth by Proposition 7.2.7. Their nonnegative difference solves the locally uniformly elliptic equation in Proposition 7.2.5. On a sufficiently small ball about a zero, Harnack gives $\sup w\leq C\inf w=0$. Thus the zero set is open; it is also closed by continuity. Connectedness completes the proof. Compare [ChMin], Proposition 19.3. $\square$

Corollary 7.2.9 (Boundary comparison and uniqueness).AF64217A81F7 Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be a bounded connected domain. Suppose $u_1,u_2\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\overline U)$ are minimal graph solutions and $u_1\leq u_2$ on $\partial U$. Then $u_1\leq u_2$ in $U$. In particular a classical solution with prescribed continuous boundary values is unique, when it exists.

Proof

If $t=\max _{\overline U}(u_1-u_2)>0$, the maximum is attained in $U$. The functions $u_1$ and $u_2+t$ are ordered solutions with interior contact, so Proposition 7.2.8 makes them equal throughout $U$. Continuity at the boundary contradicts the boundary inequality. This is the sliding argument of [ChMin], Corollary 19.4. $\square$

Corollary 7.2.10 (Interior tangency principle).DBF134AFFBB2 Let two smooth embedded minimal hypersurfaces in a smooth Riemannian manifold touch at an interior point. If one lies locally on one side of the other, then their supports agree in a neighborhood of that point.

Proof

Use a common tangent plane and a coordinate chart to write both hypersurfaces as graphs. Their difference is nonnegative and solves the linear uniformly elliptic equation obtained by integrating the linearization of the local mean-curvature operator between the graphs. The coefficients are smooth and bounded on a smaller chart. The interior Harnack principle for this equation, including its bounded lower-order coefficients, forces the difference to vanish. See [ChMin], Section 19, and [SimonPDE], Chapter 18, Theorem 1. The hypothesis is interior one-sided contact; transverse crossings do not satisfy it. $\square$

Definition 7.2.11 (Calibrations).5CC331303E30 On an oriented Riemannian manifold, a calibration of degree $n$ is a smooth closed $n$-form $\omega $ with comass at most one at every point. An oriented immersed $n$-submanifold $\Sigma $ is calibrated when $\omega |_{T\Sigma }=dA_\Sigma $. The comass is the supremum of the absolute value on unit simple $n$-vectors, as in Definition 5.1.3. See [ChMin], Section 16.

Proposition 7.2.12 (A calibration proves area minimization).27EE3D841187 Let $\Sigma $ and $S$ be compact oriented smooth $n$-submanifolds with the same oriented boundary in an open set $U$, and suppose $S-\Sigma =\partial R$ for a compactly supported $(n+1)$-current $R$ in $U$. If a calibration $\omega $ on $U$ calibrates $\Sigma $, then $\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )\leq \operatorname {Area}(S)$.

Proof

Stokes' theorem and $d\omega =0$ give $\int _ S\omega -\int _\Sigma \omega =R(d\omega )=0$. A cutoff identically one near the compact supports justifies evaluating a possibly noncompactly supported form. The calibration identity gives $\int _\Sigma \omega =\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )$, while the comass bound gives $\int _ S\omega \leq \operatorname {Area}(S)$. Compare [ChMin], Theorem 16.2. Theorem 5.5.3 extends the comparison to finite-mass currents and records its equality condition. $\square$

Proposition 7.2.13 (Minimal graphs are calibrated in their cylinder).74F9A2FF880C If $u\in C^2(U)$ solves the minimal surface equation, define on $U\times \mathbb R$

\[ N(x,z)=\frac{(-Du(x),1)}{\sqrt{1+|Du(x)|^2}},\qquad \omega =\iota _ N(dx_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dx_ n\wedge dz). \]

Orient the graph so that $\omega $ restricts to its positive area form. Then $\omega $ calibrates it. Consequently compact portions of the graph minimize area against oriented competitors homologous to them by compactly supported currents inside $U\times \mathbb R$.

Proof

The normal field has length one, so the comass of $\omega $ is one. Also $d\omega =(\operatorname {div}N)\, dx_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dz=0$ by the graph equation and independence from $z$. On the graph, $N$ is its unit normal, giving the calibration identity. Apply Proposition 7.2.12. This is [ChMin], Example 16.3 and Theorem 18.1. The cylinder restriction is part of the conclusion. $\square$

Example 7.2.14 (A graph with unbounded slope at its domain boundary).62DB8C81E589 On $(-\pi /2,\pi /2)^2$, the function $u(x,y)=\log (\cos y)-\log (\cos x)$ is a minimal graph. Indeed $u_ x=\tan x$, $u_ y=-\tan y$, $u_{xx}=\sec ^2x$, $u_{yy}=-\sec ^2y$, and $u_{xy}=0$, so

\[ (1+u_ y^2)u_{xx}-2u_ xu_ yu_{xy}+(1+u_ x^2)u_{yy}=0. \]

Its slope is bounded on compact subsets but diverges near the square's boundary. Thus local ellipticity does not give a single uniform ellipticity constant on the entire domain. Compare [ChMin], Exercise 18.2.
