11.5 Constrained Jacobi fields and spectral tests61B167A3C472

61B167A3C472

A scalar constraint changes the equation defining the nullspace as well as the allowed test functions. We work on a smooth closed connected two-sided CMC boundary $\Sigma =\partial \Omega $ in a Riemannian manifold and put

\[ q=|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu ),\qquad J=\Delta _\Sigma +q,\qquad Q(f,g)=\int _\Sigma (\langle \nabla f,\nabla g\rangle -qfg)\, dA. \]

The source is [BdCE88], Section 2, pp. 125–129. The scalar mean curvature in that paper is the negative of our $H/n$; its normalized Ricci tensor is multiplied by $n$ in the potential, giving the usual Ricci tensor used here.

Definition 11.5.1 (The constrained Jacobi nullspace).B49559F552E6 Let $\mathcal Z=\{ f\in C^\infty (\Sigma ):\int _\Sigma f\, dA=0\} $. A constrained Jacobi speed is an $f\in \mathcal Z$ for which $Q(f,g)=0$ for every $g\in \mathcal Z$. An ordinary Jacobi speed instead satisfies $Jf=0$, with no mean condition built into that equation. This is the distinction in [BdCE88], Definition 2.8, Proposition 2.9 and Remark 2.11. Being a null vector of a bilinear form is stronger than merely satisfying $Q(f,f)=0$ when the form is indefinite.

Proposition 11.5.2 (The constrained Jacobi equation has a constant right-hand side).2927E9BDF88B For $f\in \mathcal Z$, the constrained Jacobi condition is equivalent to

\[ Jf=b\quad \hbox{on }\Sigma \]

for some real constant $b$. Moreover $b=\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )^{-1}\int _\Sigma qf\, dA$. This is [BdCE88], Proposition 2.9.

Proof

Integration by parts gives $Q(f,g)=-\int gJf$. If $Jf=b$, the latter expression vanishes for all $g$ of zero mean. Conversely let $b$ be the average of the smooth function $Jf$ and take $g=Jf-b\in \mathcal Z$. The nullspace condition gives $0=-\int (Jf-b)Jf=-\int (Jf-b)^2$, hence $Jf=b$ everywhere. Integrating the equation and using $\int \Delta f=0$ gives the formula for $b$. If $q$ is constant, then $b=0$ since $f$ has zero mean; for variable $q$ that deduction is unavailable. $\square$

Lemma 11.5.3 (Zero quadratic form and stability).758BCB624841 Suppose $Q\geq 0$ on $\mathcal Z$. For $f\in \mathcal Z$, the condition $Q(f,f)=0$ is then equivalent to the constrained Jacobi condition. Without stability, the implication from zero quadratic form need not hold.

Proof

For every $g\in \mathcal Z$ and $t\in \mathbb R$,

\[ 0\leq Q(f+tg,f+tg)=2tQ(f,g)+t^2Q(g,g) \]

when $Q(f,f)=0$. A nonzero linear coefficient would make the expression negative for a sufficiently small $t$ of one sign. Thus $Q(f,g)=0$ for every $g$. The converse follows by taking $g=f$. For an indefinite form, $B((a,b),(a,b))=a^2-b^2$ has $B((1,1),(1,1))=0$ but $B((1,1),(1,0))=1$. This algebraic example explains why the stability assumption cannot be removed from the inference. $\square$

Proposition 11.5.4 (Tangents to fixed-volume CMC families).8A682F667B82 Let $\Sigma _ t$ be a smooth family of closed CMC boundaries with constant enclosed volume, and write $H_{\Sigma _ t}=c(t)$. Its initial normal speed $f$ satisfies

\[ \int _{\Sigma _0}f\, dA=0,\qquad Jf=-c'(0). \]

It is therefore a constrained Jacobi speed. This identifies the geometric meaning of the constant in [BdCE88], equation (2.10).

Proof

Differentiate volume and use the transport formula to obtain zero mean. Proposition 11.3.4 gives the derivative of mean curvature for a normal variation as $-Jf$. A tangential component of velocity contributes the tangential derivative of $H_{\Sigma _0}$, which vanishes because it is constant. Thus $c'(0)=-Jf$ at every point, and Proposition 11.5.2 applies. This is a necessary condition for an actual CMC family; it does not assert that every constrained Jacobi field integrates to such a family. $\square$

Proposition 11.5.5 (Killing fields give zero-mean Jacobi speeds on boundaries).AABDDC64A629 Let $K$ be a smooth ambient Killing field defined on a neighborhood of the compact region $\Omega $. Then $f=\langle K,\nu \rangle $ on its CMC boundary satisfies $Jf=0$ and $\int _\Sigma f=0$. Hence it belongs to the constrained Jacobi nullspace. The function can be identically zero if $K$ is everywhere tangent to $\Sigma $. Compare [BdCE88], Proposition 2.12 and the following paragraph.

Proof

The local flow of $K$ consists of isometries, so it preserves the constant mean-curvature value of $\Sigma $. The normal-variation formula, with zero tangential derivative of $H$, gives $Jf=0$. Tracing the Killing equation gives $\operatorname {div}K=0$ in the ambient manifold. The divergence theorem on $\Omega $ therefore gives $\int _\Sigma f=0$. The boundary hypothesis matters for this last step; an arbitrary two-sided hypersurface need not bound an available region on which $K$ is defined. $\square$

Proposition 11.5.6 (The constant-potential stability criterion).DFACAFB09577 Suppose $q$ is constant on the closed connected hypersurface and let $\lambda _1>0$ be the first positive eigenvalue of $-\Delta _\Sigma $. Then

\[ \Sigma \text{ is volume-constrained stable}\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad q\leq \lambda _1. \]

If $q>0$ and an ambient Killing field has a nonzero normal speed on $\Sigma $, with the hypotheses of Proposition 11.5.5, this is equivalent to $q=\lambda _1$. This is the precise nontrivial-mode setting of [BdCE88], Proposition 2.13 and its preceding paragraph.

Proof

The Rayleigh characterization of the first eigenvalue gives $\int |\nabla f|^2\geq \lambda _1\int f^2$ for every zero-mean smooth $f$. Thus $q\leq \lambda _1$ implies nonnegativity of $Q$. If $q>\lambda _1$, a first eigenfunction gives $Q(f,f)=(\lambda _1-q)\int f^2<0$. Existence and smoothness of this eigenfunction use the compact Laplace spectral theorem. For a nonzero Killing normal speed, $Jf=0$ gives $-\Delta f=qf$, and $f$ has zero mean. Hence $q$ is a positive eigenvalue and $q\geq \lambda _1$. Combining the inequalities gives the last equivalence. Without such a mode, equality is not part of the general criterion. $\square$

Proposition 11.5.7 (Index and nullity for a constant potential).8A971915C319 Under the hypotheses of Proposition 11.5.6, let the eigenvalues of $-\Delta $ be $0=\lambda _0<\lambda _1\leq \lambda _2\leq \cdots $, counted with multiplicity. The constrained index is the number of $\lambda _ j<q$ with $j\geq 1$. Its constrained nullspace is the span of the eigenfunctions with $\lambda _ j=q$, $j\geq 1$. If $q>0$, the unrestricted index is the constrained index plus one.

Proof

Use the external compact spectral theorem to expand $f=\sum _ j a_ j\phi _ j$ in an orthonormal Laplace eigenbasis, with $\phi _0$ constant. The second-variation form is diagonal:

\[ Q(f,f)=\sum _ j(\lambda _ j-q)a_ j^2. \]

For smooth $f$ this follows from integration by parts and the spectral expansion of the Dirichlet energy. Zero mean removes exactly $a_0$. The negative eigenspace gives a negative subspace of the asserted dimension; any larger subspace intersects its orthogonal complement and hence contains a vector with nonnegative quadratic form. Proposition 11.5.2 gives $Jf=0$ in the constant-potential case, so the constrained nullspace consists exactly of the eigenmodes with eigenvalue $q$. Finally the constant mode is negative precisely when $q>0$. The discreteness and divergence of the Laplace spectrum ensure finiteness of these counts. $\square$
