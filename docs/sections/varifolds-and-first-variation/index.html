6.1 Varifolds and first variation555AA5C62B73

555AA5C62B73

A varifold records location and an unoriented tangent plane. This formulation places smooth and nonsmooth surfaces in a common space of Radon measures. We use [Sim18], Chapter 8, Sections 1–3.

Definition 6.1.1 (Varifold and weight).4CCDC6F2267C Let $1\leq m<n$. The Grassmannian $G(n,m)$ is the space of unoriented $m$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb {R}^ n$, with the topology induced by the orthogonal projection matrices. An $m$-varifold in $U$ is a nonnegative Radon measure on $G_ m(U)=U\times G(n,m)$. Its weight is the Radon measure on $U$ defined by

\[ \| V\| (A)=V(A\times G(n,m))\qquad (A\subset U\text{ Borel}). \]

Varifold convergence means weak convergence of these measures against $C_ c(G_ m(U))$. See [Sim18], Chapter 8, Section 1, pp. 235–236.

Definition 6.1.2 (Rectifiable and integral varifolds).46E46AD2EF79 Let $M\subset U$ be $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and countably $m$-rectifiable, with locally finite $\mathcal{H}^ m$ measure in $U$. Let $\theta :M\to (0,\infty )$ be measurable and satisfy $\int _{M\cap K}\theta \, d\mathcal{H}^ m<\infty $ for every compact $K\subset U$. The associated rectifiable varifold $V(M,\theta )$ is determined by

\[ \int \varphi (x,S)\, dV(M,\theta )(x,S) =\int _ M\varphi (x,T_ xM)\theta (x)\, d\mathcal{H}^ m(x). \]

It is integral if $\theta $ is integer-valued almost everywhere. Its weight is $\theta \mathcal{H}^ m\llcorner M$. The tangent planes exist almost everywhere by Theorem 3.1.4; changing the integrand on the exceptional null set has no effect. See [Sim18], Chapter 4, Section 1, and Chapter 8, p. 236.

Definition 6.1.3 (First variation, stationarity, and mean curvature).CBCB798B764C For an $m$-varifold $V$ and $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb {R}^ n)$, define

\[ \delta V(X)=\int _{G_ m(U)}\operatorname {div}_ S X(x)\, dV(x,S),\qquad \operatorname {div}_ S X=\sum _{i=1}^ m\langle D_{e_ i}X,e_ i\rangle , \]

where $(e_ i)$ is any orthonormal basis of $S$. The trace is independent of that basis. We call $V$ stationary in $U$ if $\delta V(X)=0$ for every such $X$.

It has locally bounded first variation if for each $W\Subset U$ there is $C_ W<\infty $ such that $|\delta V(X)|\leq C_ W\sup |X|$ whenever $X$ is supported in $W$. In the case when the first variation is represented entirely by a vector field $H\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\| V\| ;\mathbb {R}^ n)$,

\[ \delta V(X)=-\int _ U H\cdot X\, d\| V\| , \]

we call $H$ the generalized mean curvature. Locally bounded first variation alone may also have a singular boundary term; the last identity assumes that term is absent. See [Sim18], Chapter 8, equations (2.3)–(2.10), pp. 239–240.

Proposition 6.1.4 (An affine plane is stationary).B47686B92774 Let $P\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be an affine $m$-plane and let $\theta >0$ be constant. Then $V(P,\theta )$ is stationary in $\mathbb {R}^ n$.

Proof

Identify $P$ isometrically with $\mathbb {R}^ m$. The restriction of a compactly supported vector field has compact support on $P$, and its tangential divergence is the Euclidean divergence of its tangential component. The integral of each coordinate derivative is zero by the fundamental theorem of calculus and Fubini's theorem. Hence $\delta V(X)=\theta \int _ P\operatorname {div}_ P X\, d\mathcal{H}^ m=0$. $\square$

Theorem 6.1.5 (Stationary monotonicity).A08B64B5DD43 Let $V$ be a stationary $m$-varifold in $U$, let $a\in U$, and suppose $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$. Then

\[ r\longmapsto \frac{\| V\| (B_ r(a))}{\omega _ mr^ m} \quad \text{is nondecreasing for }0<r<R. \]

Consequently $\Theta ^ m(\| V\| ,a)$ exists and is finite and nonnegative. No rectifiability hypothesis is needed for this conclusion, and the density may be zero.

See [Sim18], Chapter 8, Section 3, equations (3.5)–(3.6), p. 241, with $\Lambda =0$.

Proof

Proposition 6.6.3, proved directly from radial first variation, expresses the difference of any two unnormalized mass ratios as the integral of a nonnegative radial-tilt function. Thus the ratio is nondecreasing. Its limit at zero exists and is nonnegative. Local finiteness bounds that limit above by the ratio at any fixed smaller radius, so it is finite. $\square$
