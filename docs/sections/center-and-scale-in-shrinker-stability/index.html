14.9 Center and scale in shrinker stability577C1FAE04B9

577C1FAE04B9

Allowing the Gaussian center and scale to vary changes the stability question. We keep this finite-dimensional adjustment explicit. The source is [CM12], Theorem 4.14, Lemma 4.23, and Sections 5–6 and 11.1. Write $[u]=\int _\Sigma u(4\pi )^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4}\, dA$ and use $L$ from Definition 14.8.2.

Proposition 14.9.1 (First variation with a moving Gaussian center and scale).9DB971431D60 Let $\Sigma _ s$ be a smooth compactly supported variation of a smooth two-sided properly immersed boundaryless hypersurface with polynomial area growth. Let the center and scale satisfy $c_0=0$, $t_0=1$, $c'_0=y$, $t'_0=h$, and let the initial normal speed be $f$. For

\[ F_{c,t}(\Sigma )=(4\pi t)^{-n/2}\int _\Sigma e^{-|x-c|^2/(4t)}\, dA \]

we have

\[ \left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_0 F_{c_ s,t_ s}(\Sigma _ s) =[f\Phi +h\alpha +\beta ],\qquad \alpha =\tfrac {|x|^2}{4}-\tfrac n2,\quad \beta =\tfrac {\langle x,y\rangle }{2}. \]

If $\Sigma $ is also a complete connected self-shrinker, this derivative is zero.

Proof

Differentiate the area factor and the logarithm of the Gaussian density. The shape terms give $f\Phi $ by Proposition 14.8.1; differentiating the scale gives $h\alpha $, and differentiating the center gives $\beta $. Outside the changing compact set, Gaussian moments from Lemma 14.7.4 dominate the parameter derivatives uniformly near $s=0$. At a shrinker, $\Phi =0$, $[\alpha ]=0$ and $[\beta ]=0$ by Proposition 14.7.6. This is the normalized first-variation formula underlying [CM12], Sections 3–4. $\square$

Theorem 14.9.2 (The simultaneous shape, center, and scale Hessian).85B891A44BBE Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a connected complete smooth properly immersed two-sided self-shrinker without boundary and with polynomial area growth. For a smooth compactly supported shape variation with initial normal speed $f$, and center/scale derivatives $y\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $h\in \mathbb R$ at $(c,t)=(0,1)$,

\[ \left.\frac{d^2}{ds^2}\right|_0 F_{c_ s,t_ s}(\Sigma _ s) =Q(f)+2h[fH]-h^2[H^2]+[f a_ y]-\tfrac 12[a_ y^2], \qquad a_ y=\langle y,\nu \rangle . \]

Here $[u]=\int _\Sigma u(4\pi )^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^2/4}\, dA$, $Q$ is Definition 14.8.2, and $h=t'_0$ is the derivative of the Gaussian time scale. This is [CM12], Theorem 4.14, pp. 777–778.

Proof

At a critical point the second derivative depends only on initial velocities: differences in accelerations are paired with the vanishing first variation. We may therefore use linear center and scale paths and a normal parametrization to compute. At $s=0$, differentiating the general versions of the three factors in Proposition 14.9.1 gives

\[ \Phi '=-Lf+hH+\tfrac 12a_ y,\quad \alpha '=fH-\beta -\tfrac h2(|x|^2-n),\quad \beta '=\tfrac 12fa_ y-\tfrac 12|y|^2-h\beta . \]

For example $\Phi _ s=H_ s-\langle x_ s-c_ s,\nu _ s\rangle /(2t_ s)$ and $\alpha _ s=|x_ s-c_ s|^2/(4t_ s^2)-n/(2t_ s)$. At the shrinker the logarithmic derivative of the weighted area element is $h\alpha +\beta $. The product rule consequently yields

\[ F''=\left[-fLf+2hfH+fa_ y-\tfrac {h^2}{2}(|x|^2-n) -2h\beta -\tfrac 12|y|^2+(h\alpha +\beta )^2\right]. \]

Now $[\beta ]=[\alpha \beta ]=0$ by Proposition 14.7.6. Proposition 14.7.7 gives $[\alpha ^2]=n[1]/2-[H^2]$, while $[|x|^2]=2n[1]$. Thus the quadratic scale terms reduce to $-h^2[H^2]$. Finally $[\beta ^2]=[|y^ T|^2]/2$, again by the moment identities, so the center terms reduce to $-[a_ y^2]/2$. All differentiations outside compact shape support involve only polynomial Gaussian moments, already integrable by the stated growth hypothesis. $\square$

Definition 14.9.3 (F-stability and its quantifiers).1D2681AB2F92 A shrinker satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 14.9.2 is $F$-stable if, for every $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$, there exist a center derivative $y$ and a scale derivative $h$ for which that second variation is nonnegative. It is $F$-unstable if some such $f$ makes the second variation negative for every $y,h$. This is [CM12], Section 4.3. Fixing $y=h=0$ instead gives the different question in Theorem 14.8.8. Entropy uses the supremum over all centers and positive scales, Definition 14.4.6; local $F$-stability does not by definition settle that global supremum problem. Theorem 14.12.7 supplies the entropy-decrease implication for an $F$-unstable shrinker without a Euclidean line factor.

Proposition 14.9.4 (Removing geometric modes on a closed shrinker).7D33754FC184 Let $\Sigma $ be a connected smooth closed two-sided immersed self-shrinker. Set $\mathcal T=\{ \langle y,\nu \rangle :y\in \mathbb R^{n+1}\} $. Every smooth $f$ has a unique orthogonal decomposition

\[ f=aH+b+g,\qquad b\in \mathcal T,\quad g\perp H,\quad g\perp \mathcal T \]

in Gaussian $L^2$. Its joint Hessian is

\[ Q(g)-(a-h)^2[H^2]-\tfrac 12[(b-a_ y)^2]. \]

Hence its maximum over $h,y$ is $Q(g)$, attained when $h=a$ and $a_ y=b$.

Proof

First $H$ cannot vanish identically: if it did, $\Delta |x|^2=2n$ on the closed manifold, contradicting its integral being zero. Corollary 14.8.7 makes $\mathbb RH$ orthogonal to the finite-dimensional space $\mathcal T$, giving the decomposition. Symmetry of $L$ and its equations $LH=H$, $Lb=b/2$ give

\[ Q(f)=-a^2[H^2]-\tfrac 12[b^2]+Q(g),\quad [fH]=a[H^2],\quad [fa_ y]=[ba_ y]. \]

Insert these in Theorem 14.9.2 and complete the two squares. By the definition of $\mathcal T$, the required vector $y$ exists; uniqueness of $y$ is unnecessary. No spectral assumption about a noncompact surface has entered. $\square$

Proposition 14.9.5 (The round sphere is F-stable).0948281F1984 The sphere $S^ n(\sqrt{2n})\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\geq 1$, is $F$-stable. For the remainder $g$ orthogonal to constants and translation modes,

\[ Q(g)\geq \frac1n[g^2]. \]

The estimate uses the classical spherical harmonic spectrum as an external spectral input, as in [CM12], Lemma 4.23.

Proof

Here $x^ T=0$, $|A|^2=1/2$, $H=\sqrt{n/2}$, and $L=\Delta +1$. The external spherical harmonic decomposition gives eigenvalues $k(k+n-1)/(2n)$ of $-\Delta $, $k=0,1,\ldots $. Constants are the $k=0$ space; the $k=1$ space consists of restrictions of linear functions, precisely the normal translation modes up to a constant factor. On their orthogonal complement the eigenvalues are at least $(n+1)/n$. Expansion in the orthogonal spherical harmonics therefore gives $[|\nabla g|^2]-[g^2]\geq [g^2]/n$. Proposition 14.9.4 supplies center and scale adjustments making the full Hessian nonnegative. The proof does not contradict fixed-center instability. $\square$

Theorem 14.9.6 (Sign-changing mean curvature gives closed F-instability).12B9846AA0AB If a connected smooth closed two-sided immersed self-shrinker has scalar mean curvature $H$ that changes sign, it is $F$-unstable. The argument uses compact elliptic spectral theory as an external input; it is the instability step in [CM12], Section 6, pp. 782–783.

Proof

The external principal-eigenfunction theorem for a smooth self-adjoint elliptic operator on a connected closed manifold says that the largest eigenvalue $\lambda _0$ of $L$ is simple and admits a strictly positive smooth eigenfunction $u$. This is Corollary 5.15 of [CM12], written with $Lu=\lambda _0u$ rather than $Lu=-\mu _1u$. Since $LH=H$ and $H$ changes sign, $H$ cannot be the principal eigenfunction; simplicity gives $\lambda _0>1$. Weighted symmetry then makes $u$ orthogonal to $H$ and every translation mode, whose eigenvalue is $1/2$. The joint Hessian for $f=u$ is

\[ -\lambda _0[u^2]-h^2[H^2]-\tfrac 12[a_ y^2]<0 \]

for every $h,y$. Compactness makes $u$ an admissible compactly supported variation. This proves instability, without using or claiming the separate classification of mean-convex shrinkers. $\square$

Theorem 14.9.7 (Nontrivial round cylinders are F-unstable).C6F03108FD3A For $1\leq k<n$, the self-shrinker $S^ k(\sqrt{2k})\times \mathbb R^{n-k}$ is $F$-unstable for compactly supported normal variations. This is the cylinder case of [CM12], Section 11.1, pp. 816–817. In particular the roles of cylinders in entropy and singularity theory cannot be summarized as $F$-stability under Definition 14.9.3.

Proof

Write $z\in \mathbb R^{n-k}$ for the axial coordinate. The cylinder has $H=\sqrt{k/2}$, $|A|^2=1/2$, and $Lz_1=z_1/2$. Choose smooth radial cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ R(z)\leq 1$, equal to one for $|z|\leq R$, zero for $|z|\geq 2R$, and with $|\nabla \eta _ R|\leq C/R$. Set $f_ R=\eta _ Rz_1$. This has compact support because the spherical factor is compact. Its oddness in $z_1$ gives $[f_ R]=0$ exactly. The normal depends only on the spherical factor, and its integral on that sphere is zero, so $[f_ Ra_ y]=0$ for every $y$.

The Gaussian product measure has all polynomial moments. Therefore $f_ R\to z_1$ and $\nabla f_ R\to \nabla z_1$ in Gaussian $L^2$: the cutoff errors are supported in Gaussian tails and $|z_1\nabla \eta _ R|\leq C|z|/R$. Since $|A|^2+1/2=1$,

\[ Q(f_ R)\longrightarrow [|\nabla z_1|^2-z_1^2] =-\tfrac 12[z_1^2]<0. \]

The last equality follows from the one-dimensional Gaussian identity $[z_1^2]=2[1]$, obtained by integrating the derivative of $z_1e^{-z_1^2/4}$ on the real line. Choose one large $R$ with $Q(f_ R)<0$. Its joint Hessian is $Q(f_ R)-h^2[H^2]-[a_ y^2]/2$, strictly negative for every center and scale derivative. Exact oddness makes this conclusion uniform in $h$, without passing a nonuniform limit through an optimization. $\square$
