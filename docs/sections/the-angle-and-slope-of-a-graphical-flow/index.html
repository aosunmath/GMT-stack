14.18 The angle and slope of a graphical flow05F805A9EA8C

05F805A9EA8C

Ecker–Huisken's gradient estimates begin with the reciprocal vertical angle; see [EH89], Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.2, printed p. 458. We derive the local identities first and apply the maximum principle on a compact periodic graph. This avoids any implicit maximum-principle assumption at spatial infinity. The flat formulas below also hold for curves, so here $n\geq 1$.

Definition 14.18.1 (Periodic graphs and normal material time).D5F2CC03369E Let $\Lambda \subset \mathbb R^ n$ be a full-rank lattice, $\mathbb T^ n=\mathbb R^ n/\Lambda $ its flat torus, and let $u:\mathbb T^ n\times I\to \mathbb R$ be smooth. A periodic graphical mean curvature flow is the graph of $u$ in the flat product $\mathbb T^ n\times \mathbb R$, with $u$ satisfying Proposition 14.3.13. Equivalently, its lift is a periodic entire graph in Euclidean space. With $e$ the upward parallel unit vector, set

\[ \nu =\frac{(-Du,1)}{v},\qquad v=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2},\qquad w=\langle \nu ,e\rangle =v^{-1}>0. \]

We use $A=D\nu $ and $\vec H=-H\nu $. The symbol $\partial _ t^\perp $ denotes differentiation in a parametrization with velocity $-H\nu $, and $\mathcal P=\partial _ t^\perp -\Delta _{M_ t}$. It is not automatically the derivative at a fixed graph coordinate. This is the angle convention of [EH89], Section 3.

Proposition 14.18.2 (Angle, reciprocal angle and their powers).7D398B6399E3 On any oriented smooth flat-ambient flow with $w=\langle \nu ,e\rangle >0$ and $v=w^{-1}$,

\[ \mathcal Pw=|A|^2w,\qquad \mathcal Pv=-|A|^2v-2v^{-1}|\nabla v|^2. \]

For every real $p$,

\[ \mathcal P(v^ p)=-p|A|^2v^ p-p(p+1)v^{p-2}|\nabla v|^2, \qquad \mathcal P\log v=-|A|^2-v^{-2}|\nabla v|^2. \]

These are pointwise identities wherever the angle is positive, independently of compactness. The reciprocal-angle equation is [EH89], Lemma 3.1.

Proof

Proposition 11.8.3 already proves $\Delta _ M\nu =\nabla H-|A|^2\nu $. Proposition 14.3.2 gives $\partial _ t^\perp \nu =\nabla H$. Subtract and pair with the parallel vector $e$ to obtain the equation for $w$. These local identities also hold on a flat quotient and in dimension one. The scalar chain rule for $\mathcal P$ is $\mathcal P\Phi (w)=\Phi '(w)\mathcal Pw-\Phi ''(w)|\nabla w|^2$. Take $\Phi (w)=w^{-1}$ and use $|\nabla w|^2=v^{-4}|\nabla v|^2$. This proves the equation for $v$. Apply the same rule to $v^ p$; the two gradient coefficients combine to $-2p-p(p-1)=-p(p+1)$. Taking $\Phi (v)=\log v$ gives the last formula directly. No sign restriction on $p$ is needed because $v$ is positive. $\square$

Lemma 14.18.3 (The drift introduced by graph coordinates).3B25B7CBF648 For the parametrization $F(x,t)=(x,u(x,t))$, its velocity decomposes as

\[ F_ t=-H\nu +T,\qquad T=u_ t e^ T=-Hv e^ T, \]

where $e^ T=e-w\nu $. If $f$ is a geometric scalar on the moving graph, then

\[ \partial _ t^{\rm graph}f=\partial _ t^\perp f+\langle T,\nabla f\rangle . \]

Thus an equation involving $\mathcal P+b\cdot \nabla $ becomes, in graph coordinates, the equation with operator $\partial _ t^{\rm graph}-\Delta +(b-T)\cdot \nabla $.

Proof

The graph equation says $u_ t=-Hv$. Since $e=w\nu +e^ T$, its velocity $u_ te$ has normal part $u_ tw\nu =-H\nu $ and the asserted tangential part. Removing that tangential velocity by a time-dependent parameter change gives a normal parametrization locally in time. The ordinary chain rule for a scalar under this parameter change gives the displayed derivative identity. On any compact smooth time slab of a periodic graph, $T$ and all coefficients are smooth and bounded. Hence the tangential term is a legitimate drift in the compact maximum principle, and vanishes at a spatial extremum of $f$. This explains why an angle calculation in normal time can be used for a graph-coordinate estimate. $\square$

Proposition 14.18.4 (Slope cannot grow on a periodic graph).87959CB5E839 For a smooth periodic graphical flow on $[s,T)$,

\[ 1\leq v(x,t)\leq V_ s:=\max _{M_ s}v\quad (s\leq t<T). \]

Consequently $|Du(x,t)|\leq \max |Du(\cdot ,s)|$, and the graphical equation has spatial ellipticity between $V_ s^{-2}$ and $1$ throughout this interval. This is the periodic version of [EH89], Corollary 3.2; it does not assert global existence.

Proof

The reciprocal-angle equation has $\mathcal Pv\leq 0$. By Lemma 14.18.3, its graph-coordinate form has only an additional smooth tangential drift. On any compact smooth slab $[s,t_1]\subset [s,T)$, the maximum principle therefore gives $\max v(t)\leq \max v(s)$. One can obtain the weak principle directly by applying the first interior maximum test to $v-V_ s-\eta (t-s)$ and then letting $\eta \downarrow 0$. Compactness supplies a maximum; there is no lateral boundary. The lower bound follows from the graph formula. The gradient bound and ellipticity are immediate from $v^2=1+|Du|^2$ and Proposition 14.3.13. $\square$

Lemma 14.18.5 (The gradient of the reciprocal angle).525F397210CA Pointwise on a graphical hypersurface,

\[ \nabla w=A(e^ T),\qquad \nabla v=-v^2 A(e^ T),\qquad |\nabla v|\leq v\sqrt{v^2-1}\, |A|. \]

In particular $v^{-1}|\nabla v|\leq v|A|$. These estimates control the drift in the weighted curvature equation when slope and curvature are bounded, as in [EH89], Corollary 4.2.

Proof

For a tangent vector $X$, differentiate $w=\langle \nu ,e\rangle $ to obtain $Xw=\langle AX,e^ T\rangle $. Self-adjointness of $A$ identifies its gradient. Differentiate $v=w^{-1}$ for the second formula. Finally $|e^ T|^2=1-w^2=1-v^{-2}$ and the operator norm of $A$ is at most its tensor norm. Hence $|\nabla v|\leq v^2|A|\sqrt{1-v^{-2}}$, which is the stated bound, including at points where $v=1$ or $A=0$. $\square$
