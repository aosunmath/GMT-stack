5.10 Geometric slices and their boundary identityED01D488E588

ED01D488E588

We separate the geometric level set, the distributional boundary, and the argument identifying them outside one null set. This supplies the boundary-identification argument used in Theorem 5.4.2. The remaining geometric background is rectifiable coarea, the countable $C^1$ decomposition, and its almost-everywhere fiber tangent assertion, as in [Sim18], Chapter 6, Lemmas 4.1 and 4.5, pp. 180–183.

Lemma 5.10.1 (Orientation of a codimension-one fiber).BD53F1D000B3 Let $P$ be an oriented $m$-plane, $m\geq 1$, with unit simple orientation $\tau $, and let $v\in P$ be a unit vector. Define $\tau \mathbin {\lrcorner }v$ by

\[ \langle \tau \mathbin {\lrcorner }v,\omega \rangle =\langle \tau ,v^\flat \wedge \omega \rangle . \]

It is the unit simple orientation of $P\cap v^\perp $ characterized by $v\wedge (\tau \mathbin {\lrcorner }v)=\tau $. When $m=1$, it is the signed unit zero-vector. This is the linear algebra in [Sim18], equation (4.3).

Proof

For $m\geq 2$, choose an oriented orthonormal basis $(v,e_2,\ldots ,e_ m)$ of $P$. Then $\tau =v\wedge e_2\wedge \cdots \wedge e_ m$, and evaluation on basis forms shows that contraction is $e_2\wedge \cdots \wedge e_ m$. For $m=1$, write $\tau =\sigma v$ with $\sigma \in \{ 1,-1\} $. The contraction is the scalar $\sigma \in \Lambda ^0P$, which gives the same identity and unit norm. These formulas give simplicity, norm one, the tangent plane, and the asserted orientation identity. $\square$

Definition 5.10.2 (The geometric level current).E6A04ADFDFCA Let $T=\llbracket M,\theta ,\tau \rrbracket $ be a locally integer rectifiable $m$-current of locally finite mass in an open set $U$, $m\geq 1$, and let $f:U\to \mathbb R$ be locally Lipschitz. Write

\[ M_ t=\{ x\in M:f(x)=t,\ |\nabla ^ Mf(x)|>0\} ,\qquad S_ t=\llbracket M_ t,\theta ,\tau \mathbin {\lrcorner } (\nabla ^ Mf/|\nabla ^ Mf|)\rrbracket . \]

This is defined at the almost-everywhere levels where the fiber is rectifiable, its tangent plane is $T_ xM\cap \ker d^ Mf_ x$, and its weighted mass is locally finite. Values on the exceptional levels are not specified by this definition. See [Sim18], Lemma 4.1 and Definition 4.4.

Proposition 5.10.3 (Mass and integration of geometric slices).39945FA00011 The geometric level currents exist for almost every $t$. For every Borel $E\subset U$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\| S_ t\| (E)\, dt =\int _ E|\nabla ^ Mf|\, d\| T\| . \]

For a smooth compactly supported $(m-1)$-form $\omega $ and $\psi \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\psi (t)S_ t(\omega )\, dt =\int _ M\psi (f)\langle \tau ,d^ Mf\wedge \omega \rangle \, d\| T\| . \]

These are the mass and signed coarea identities underlying [Sim18], Lemma 4.5(1).

Proof

For $m\geq 2$, use weighted rectifiable coarea, Corollary 3.3.4. For $m=1$, use Proposition 5.9.1. The almost-everywhere tangent-plane assertion is the fiber part of rectifiable coarea, recorded in [Sim18], Lemma 4.1; it remains an external geometric input. Lemma 5.10.1 makes the prescribed orientation unit and simple. The multiplicity is the positive integer $\theta $, so the mass measure is exactly $\theta \mathcal H^{m-1}\llcorner M_ t$. Coarea with weight $\theta \chi _ E$ proves the mass identity, including infinite values. With $E$ in a compact exhaustion, local Lipschitz bounds and local mass finiteness show that the slice has locally finite mass for almost every $t$, simultaneously on all compact subsets.

For the signed identity, insert the weight

\[ \theta \psi (f)\left\langle \tau \mathbin {\lrcorner } \frac{\nabla ^ Mf}{|\nabla ^ Mf|},\omega \right\rangle \]

on the noncritical part and set it to zero elsewhere. Its absolute value after multiplication by $|\nabla ^ Mf|$ is integrable on the compact support of $\omega $. Signed coarea and the contraction identity therefore give the formula. Critical points contribute zero almost everywhere in the level, so this convention causes no missing term. $\square$

Lemma 5.10.4 (An integrated identity for the distributional slice).4D3D3D80B610 Assume also that $T$ is locally normal, and put $B_ t=\partial (T\llcorner \{ f<t\} )-(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f<t\} $. For every test $(m-1)$-form $\omega $ and $\psi \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\psi (t)B_ t(\omega )\, dt =\int _{\mathbb R}\psi (t)S_ t(\omega )\, dt. \]

The left-hand side exists without assuming finite mass of $B_ t$. This is an integrated version of the approximation argument in [Sim18], Lemma 4.5(2).

Proof

Set $Q(s)=\int _ s^\infty \psi (t)\, dt$. Fubini, applied to the locally finite measures representing $T$ and $\partial T$ on the test support, gives

\[ \int \psi (t)B_ t(\omega )\, dt =(T\llcorner Q(f))(d\omega ) -((\partial T)\llcorner Q(f))(\omega ). \]

The composition $Q(f)$ is locally Lipschitz. Proposition 5.9.5 identifies this difference with $-(T\llcorner d^ M(Q(f)))(\omega )$. Since $Q'=-\psi $, Lemma 5.9.3 changes the negative sign to a positive one and gives

\[ \int _ M\psi (f)\langle \tau ,d^ Mf\wedge \omega \rangle \, d\| T\| . \]

Proposition 5.10.3 identifies this with the required integral of $S_ t$. The two scalar functions of $t$ are measurable and locally integrable: the first is bounded by the local masses times the $C^1$ norm of $\omega $, and the second is locally integrable by coarea. $\square$

Proposition 5.10.5 (A single exceptional null set for all test forms).38E741B69B67 Under the preceding assumptions there is a Lebesgue-null set $N\subset \mathbb R$ such that

\[ B_ t=S_ t\quad \text{as currents for every }t\notin N. \]

Thus equality is not merely an almost-everywhere assertion with a different exceptional set for each test form. This expands the countable-density step in [Sim18], p. 183.

Proof

For a fixed test form $\omega $, Lemma 5.10.4 says that the locally integrable function $B_ t(\omega )-S_ t(\omega )$ has zero pairing with every smooth compactly supported scalar function. Lemma 3.4.4 makes it zero almost everywhere.

Choose countable $C^1$-dense test families on a relatively compact exhaustion of $U$ using Lemma 5.8.1. The approximants have a common slightly larger compact support. Remove the null exceptional set for each member of all these countable families, together with the set where $S_ t$ fails to have locally finite mass.

For each remaining $t$, a test supported in a compact $K$ satisfies

\[ |B_ t(\omega )|\leq \| T\| (K)\| d\omega \| _{*,\infty } +\| \partial T\| (K)\| \omega \| _{*,\infty }, \qquad |S_ t(\omega )|\leq \| S_ t\| (K)\| \omega \| _{*,\infty }. \]

Use the slightly larger compact set for the same estimates on approximating forms. $C^1$ convergence therefore extends equality from the chosen countable family to every test. The removed set is countable union of null sets, proving the claim. The $C^1$ topology is necessary here because the distributional expression for $B_ t$ contains $d\omega $ before its finite-mass representation is established. $\square$

Proposition 5.10.6 (Integral restrictions and integral slice boundaries).8D661A656C0A If $T$ is locally integral, then for almost every $t$ both $T\llcorner \{ f<t\} $ and $S_ t$ are locally integral. For $m\geq 2$,

\[ \partial S_ t=-S_ t^{\partial T}, \]

where $S_ t^{\partial T}$ denotes the geometric slice constructed from $\partial T$. For $m=1$ the boundary of the zero-current $S_ t$ is zero. See [Sim18], Lemma 4.5(2)–(3).

Proof

Apply Proposition 5.10.5 to $T$. If $m\geq 2$, also apply it to $\partial T$, which is locally integer rectifiable and locally normal because $\partial ^2T=0$. Intersect the two full-measure sets of good levels. Taking a distributional boundary in the identity for $S_ t$ gives

\[ \partial S_ t=-\partial ((\partial T)\llcorner \{ f<t\} ) =-S_ t^{\partial T}. \]

The latter is integer rectifiable with locally finite mass. Thus $S_ t$ and its boundary are integer rectifiable and locally finite, which is precisely local integrality. In dimension one its boundary vanishes by convention, giving the same conclusion directly. The restriction $T\llcorner \{ f<t\} $ is integer rectifiable, and

\[ \partial (T\llcorner \{ f<t\} )=(\partial T)\llcorner \{ f<t\} +S_ t \]

has the same properties and locally finite mass. Hence the restriction is locally integral. No compactness theorem is needed for these deductions. $\square$
