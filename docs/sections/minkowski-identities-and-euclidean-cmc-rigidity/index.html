11.4 Minkowski identities and Euclidean CMC rigidity3063D1CCB1C7

3063D1CCB1C7

The volume constraint admits a particularly useful test function built from the support function. We derive the integral identities and then identify an exactly volume-preserving path producing that test. The rigidity theorem is due to Barbosa and do Carmo; [Wen91], pp. 375–379, gives an elementary parallel-surface proof. Our convention is $A=D\nu $ and $H=\operatorname {tr}A$, so an outward sphere has $H=n/R$. Wente's scalar mean curvature is $-H/n$ in this notation.

Definition 11.4.1 (Support function and tangential position).752F687F8B39 For a smooth oriented hypersurface $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, with position vector $x$ relative to a fixed origin and unit normal $\nu $, its support function and tangential position field are

\[ u=\langle x,\nu \rangle ,\qquad x^ T=x-u\nu . \]

Neither $u$ nor $x^ T$ requires star-shapedness; $u$ may change sign. The variation used in [Wen91], pp. 377–378, combines normal displacement with dilation and thus involves $u$.

Lemma 11.4.2 (Tangential position and the first Minkowski identity).D97C63B72CB5 For the notation of Definition 11.4.1,

\[ \nabla _\Sigma x^ T=I-uA,\qquad \operatorname {div}_\Sigma x^ T=n-Hu. \]

If $\Sigma $ is compact without boundary, then $\int _\Sigma Hu\, dA=n\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )$. This is the integral identity underlying [Wen91], equation (10) and its appendix.

Proof

For a tangent vector $v$, differentiate $x-u\nu $ in Euclidean space and project tangentially. The derivative of $x$ is $v$, the term $v(u)\nu $ has zero tangential projection, and the remaining term is $-uAv$. Taking the trace gives the divergence formula. The divergence theorem on the closed hypersurface gives the integral identity. No sign condition on $u$ or on the principal curvatures enters this argument. $\square$

Lemma 11.4.3 (The first Newton tensor has zero divergence).56A52E4D2856 On a Euclidean hypersurface put $P_1=HI-A$. Then

\[ \operatorname {div}_\Sigma P_1=0,\qquad \operatorname {tr}P_1=(n-1)H,\qquad \operatorname {tr}(P_1A)=H^2-|A|^2. \]

Here $H$ need not be constant.

Proof

The Euclidean Codazzi equation $\nabla _ i h_{jk}=\nabla _ j h_{ik}$ follows by commuting ambient derivatives of the normal and using zero ambient curvature. Contracting the first and third indices gives $\nabla ^ i h_{ij}=\nabla _ jH$. Thus the divergences of $HI$ and $A$ cancel. The trace formulas follow by expanding $P_1$ in an orthonormal principal frame. These are local differential identities, independent of stability or compactness. $\square$

Proposition 11.4.4 (The second Minkowski identity).70CDEF2521D0 Every smooth closed oriented Euclidean hypersurface satisfies

\[ \int _\Sigma u(H^2-|A|^2)\, dA=(n-1)\int _\Sigma H\, dA. \]

In particular, if $H$ is constant and $a=\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )$, then

\[ \int _\Sigma u|A|^2\, dA=Ha. \]

This supplies a divergence-form route to the curvature defect in [Wen91], equation (11).

Proof

Use Lemma 11.4.3 and Lemma 11.4.2 to compute

\[ \operatorname {div}_\Sigma (P_1x^ T) =\operatorname {tr}(P_1\nabla x^ T) =(n-1)H-u(H^2-|A|^2). \]

Its integral vanishes. When $H$ is constant, the first Minkowski identity gives $H\int u=na$. Therefore $H^2\int u-(n-1)Ha=Ha$, which is the claimed second formula. This deduction does not divide by $H$. $\square$

Proposition 11.4.5 (The support function solves an inhomogeneous Jacobi equation).DA3F5A1EA115 For a Euclidean hypersurface, let $J=\Delta _\Sigma +|A|^2$. Then

\[ Ju=H+\langle \nabla H,x^ T\rangle . \]

Thus $Ju=H$ when $H$ is constant. Compare the dilation component of the variation in [Wen91], p. 378.

Proof

Differentiating $u=\langle x,\nu \rangle $ gives $\nabla u=Ax^ T$. Taking its divergence, contracting Codazzi as above, and using $\nabla x^ T=I-uA$ yields

\[ \Delta u=\langle \nabla H,x^ T\rangle +\operatorname {tr}(A(I-uA)) =\langle \nabla H,x^ T\rangle +H-u|A|^2. \]

Move the last term to the left. In the CMC case this also agrees with the normal-variation formula: under dilation the initial normal speed is $u$, while the derivative of $H$ is $-H$. $\square$

Proposition 11.4.6 (CMC area-volume balance).631069D9A13E Let $\Sigma =\partial \Omega \subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a nonempty smooth compact boundary of a bounded region of positive volume, with outward normal and constant $H$. Then

\[ H(n+1)\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega )=n\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma ). \]

Consequently $H>0$. This is [Wen91], equation (10), in the outward, unaveraged convention.

Proof

The ambient divergence theorem applied to $x$ gives $\int _\Sigma u=(n+1)\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega )$. Insert this into the first Minkowski identity. Positivity of volume and area gives the sign of $H$. More generally, the first Minkowski identity alone rules out any nonempty closed Euclidean minimal hypersurface, without needing an enclosed region. $\square$

Proposition 11.4.7 (A zero-mean test measures the full umbilicity defect).7077458D4E54 Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth closed Euclidean CMC hypersurface. Define

\[ f=1-\frac Hn u,\qquad D=|A|^2-\frac{H^2}{n}. \]

Then $\int _\Sigma f=0$, $Jf=D$, and the Euclidean second-variation form satisfies

\[ Q(f,f)=-\int _\Sigma D\, dA. \]

The function $f$ is the normal speed identified in [Wen91], p. 378.

Proof

The first Minkowski identity gives the zero mean. Since $J1=|A|^2$ and $Ju=H$, the Jacobi equation follows. The first and second Minkowski identities also give

\[ \int uD=Ha-\frac{H^2}{n}\int u=0. \]

Integrating by parts in the second-variation formula of Proposition 11.3.5, we obtain $Q(f,f)=-\int fJf=-\int D+(H/n)\int uD=-\int D$. Pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives $D\geq 0$, with equality exactly when $A=(H/n)I$. All integrals use the induced area measure. $\square$

Proposition 11.4.8 (Parallel displacement followed by volume correction).ECC2BC2CD9E4 Under the hypotheses of Proposition 11.4.6, the test in Proposition 11.4.7 is realized by an exactly volume-preserving family

\[ y_ t=s(t)(x+t\nu ),\qquad s(t)=\left(\frac{\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega )}{\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega _ t)}\right)^{1/(n+1)}, \]

where $\partial \Omega _ t=\{ x+t\nu :x\in \Sigma \} $ for sufficiently small $|t|$. This is the family in [Wen91], Section II, with signs translated to our convention.

Proof

The tubular neighborhood theorem for the compact embedded boundary makes $x+t\nu $ embedded for small $|t|$ and gives a smoothly varying bounded region $\Omega _ t$. In a principal frame the differential is $I+tA$, so its area element is $\prod _ i(1+t\kappa _ i)dA$. For sufficiently small $t$ these factors are positive, and its outward normal remains $\nu $. The volume transport formula gives $V'(0)=a$, where $V(t)=\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega _ t)$ and $a=\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )$. Dilation by the positive factor $s(t)$ multiplies volume by $s(t)^{n+1}$, so the corrected volume is exactly $V(0)$. Also

\[ s'(0)=-\frac{a}{(n+1)V(0)}=-\frac Hn. \]

Thus $y'_0=\nu -(H/n)x$ has normal part $f\nu $. Its tangential part is harmless for the second variation at a CMC critical point, as established in Proposition 11.3.5. Consequently the second derivative of area along this explicit path is $-\int D$. $\square$

Theorem 11.4.9 (Euclidean volume-stability rigidity).82E696DEA5C5 Let $n\geq 2$ and let $\Sigma =\partial \Omega \subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth connected compact embedded CMC boundary. It is stable under the total-volume constraint if and only if it is a round sphere. This is the embedded-boundary case of the Barbosa–do Carmo theorem, stated and proved by a parallel-family argument in [Wen91], pp. 375–379.

Proof

If $\Sigma $ is volume-constrained stable, Proposition 11.4.7 gives $0\leq Q(f,f)=-\int D$. Since $D\geq 0$ is continuous, $D=0$ everywhere. Hence $A=(H/n)I$. Proposition 11.4.6 gives $H>0$. For every tangent vector $v$,

\[ D_ v\left(x-\frac nH\nu \right)=v-\frac nH Av=0. \]

Connectedness gives a fixed center $b=x-(n/H)\nu $. Thus $|x-b|=n/H$, and $\Sigma $ lies in the corresponding sphere. Its inclusion is locally open in that sphere because the tangent spaces have the same dimension; its compact image is closed. The sphere is connected, so the image is the whole sphere. Conversely Proposition 11.3.8 proves the required nonnegativity for every zero-mean speed on a round sphere. Proposition 11.3.2 identifies this condition with stability along exact volume-preserving variations. No star-shapedness or pointwise convexity is assumed in the argument. $\square$
