13.4 Weiss and Monneau monotonicity8A7934261852

8A7934261852

Monotonicity identifies the homogeneity of blow-ups and removes subsequence ambiguity at singular points. Throughout this section $u$ is a normalized obstacle solution as in Definition 13.3.4, the chosen free-boundary point has been translated to zero, and $\overline{B_ R}\subset U$. We follow [Fig18], Section 8, pp. 17–19. Derivative identities are stated almost everywhere in the radius, with local absolute continuity supplying monotonicity at every radius.

Definition 13.4.1 (The Weiss energy).E7030A133549 For $0<r<R$, define

\[ W(r,u)=r^{-n-2}\int _{B_ r}(|Du|^2+2u)\, dx -2r^{-n-3}\int _{\partial B_ r}u^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}. \]

The coefficient $2$ in the bulk term corresponds to the equation $\Delta u=1$ on positivity, and the boundary coefficient corresponds to quadratic scaling. Direct change of variables gives $W(r,u)=W(1,u_{0,r})$. This is [Fig18], Proposition 8.2.

Theorem 13.4.2 (Weiss monotonicity and its exact derivative).F5304A899D2C Let $u$ be a normalized obstacle solution with $0\in \Gamma $ and $\overline{B_ R}\subset U$. The function $r\mapsto W(r,u)$ is locally absolutely continuous and

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(r,u) =\frac{2}{r^{n+2}}\int _{\partial B_ r} \left(\partial _\nu u-\frac{2u}{r}\right)^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1} \geq 0 \]

for almost every $r$. It has a finite limit $W(0+,u)$ as $r\downarrow 0$.

Proof

Write $u_ r(x)=r^{-2}u(rx)$. On a compact interval of positive radii, the $C^{1,1}$ bounds justify differentiation under the fixed-ball integrals and integration by parts for almost every radius. In particular $W(1,u_ r)$ is absolutely continuous and

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(1,u_ r) =2\int _{B_1}(1-\Delta u_ r)\partial _ ru_ r +2\int _{\partial B_1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)\partial _ ru_ r. \]

The interior integrand vanishes: where $u_ r>0$ the first factor is zero, while on contact both $u_ r$ and $Du_ r$ vanish and $\partial _ ru_ r=r^{-1}(x\cdot Du_ r-2u_ r)=0$. On $\partial B_1$ this last identity becomes $r^{-1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)$. Hence

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(1,u_ r)=\frac2r\int _{\partial B_1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)^2. \]

Scaling the surface integral gives the displayed formula. Quadratic upper growth and $|Du(x)|\leq L|x|$ bound both terms in $W$ uniformly at small radii, so its monotone limit is finite. This is the computation in [Fig18], Proposition 8.2, with its regularity justification and scaling factors explicit. $\square$

Corollary 13.4.3 (Equality in Weiss monotonicity).A3D7C291127B For $u$ as in Theorem 13.4.2, the Weiss energy is constant on an interval $(a,b)\subset (0,R)$ if and only if

\[ x\cdot Du(x)=2u(x)\quad (a<|x|<b). \]

Equivalently, along every ray the quotient $u(r\omega )/r^2$ is constant while $a<r<b$. Constancy on $(0,b)$ means that $u$ is homogeneous of degree two in $B_ b$.

Proof

If the energy is constant, the nonnegative derivative formula makes $x\cdot Du-2u$ vanish on almost every sphere in the annulus. Coarea and continuity make it vanish everywhere there. Along a ray, $\frac d{dr}(r^{-2}u(r\omega ))=r^{-3}(r\omega \cdot Du-2u)$, which proves the homogeneity assertion. Conversely that identity makes the derivative of $W$ zero almost everywhere; absolute continuity makes $W$ constant. This gives the equality case of Theorem 13.4.2. $\square$

Proposition 13.4.4 (Every obstacle blow-up is quadratic homogeneous).55AC4AED0949 Every blow-up $u_0$ in Theorem 13.3.11 satisfies

\[ u_0(tx)=t^2u_0(x)\quad (t>0),\qquad W(s,u_0)=W(0+,u)\quad (s>0). \]
Proof

If $u_{r_ j}\to u_0$ in $C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$, then for any fixed $s>0$ the bulk and boundary integrals converge, giving $W(s,u_0)=\lim _ jW(s,u_{r_ j})=\lim _ jW(sr_ j,u)=W(0+,u)$. The limit is a normalized obstacle solution, so the equality case on every interval of radii proves global degree-two homogeneity. This uses no classification of homogeneous solutions. $\square$

Proposition 13.4.5 (Weiss energies of the two model families).D998F7CCB961 For the quadratic polynomials $p_ A$ and half-parabolas $h_ e$,

\[ W(r,p_ A)=\frac{\omega _ n}{2(n+2)},\qquad W(r,h_ e)=\frac{\omega _ n}{4(n+2)} \quad (r>0). \]

Thus the limiting Weiss energy distinguishes regular and singular points once the dichotomy theorem is applied.

Proof

Both models are degree-two homogeneous and satisfy $u\Delta u=u$ almost everywhere. Integration by parts on $B_1$ therefore gives $\int _{B_1}|Du|^2=2\int _{\partial B_1}u^2-\int _{B_1}u$, so $W(1,u)=\int _{B_1}u$. Rotational symmetry gives $\int _{B_1}x_ i^2=\omega _ n/(n+2)$. Taking the trace of $A$ gives the first value; restriction to a half-ball gives the second. Scaling gives all radii, and $C^1$ blow-up convergence gives the corresponding limiting energies. The common quadratic value is the constant used in [Fig18], Proposition 8.2. $\square$

Definition 13.4.6 (The Monneau distance to a quadratic model).8FCA50C0A333 For $p=p_ A$ as in Example 13.3.7, set

\[ M(r,u,p)=r^{-n-3}\int _{\partial B_ r}(u-p)^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1} =\int _{\partial B_1}(u_ r-p)^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}. \]

This is a spherical $L^2$ distance after quadratic rescaling, not an integral over the unit ball. Its monotonicity below requires a singular center. See [Fig18], Lemma 8.4.

Theorem 13.4.7 (Monneau monotonicity at a singular point).308C31A953C7 Let $u$ be a normalized obstacle solution with zero singular and $\overline{B_ R}\subset U$. For every quadratic model $p=p_ A$, the function $M(r,u,p)$ is locally absolutely continuous and nondecreasing. More precisely, with $w=u-p$,

\[ M'(r)=\frac2r\bigl (W(r,u)-W(r,p)\bigr ) +\frac{2}{r^{n+3}}\int _{B_ r}p\chi _{\{ u=0\} }\, dx \geq 0 \]

for almost every $r\in (0,R)$.

Proof

Existence of one quadratic blow-up and Proposition 13.4.5 imply $W(0+,u)=W(r,p)=\omega _ n/(2(n+2))$. Thus the first term in the claimed derivative is nonnegative by Weiss monotonicity.

Because $\Delta p=1$ and $x\cdot Dp=2p$, expansion of the energy and integration by parts cancel the cross terms, leaving

\[ W(r,u)-W(r,p)=r^{-n-2}\int _{B_ r}|Dw|^2 -2r^{-n-3}\int _{\partial B_ r}w^2. \]

Also $\Delta w=-\chi _{\{ u=0\} }$ and $w=-p$ on contact, so $w\Delta w=p\chi _{\{ u=0\} }\geq 0$. Another integration by parts yields

\[ r^{-n-3}\int _{\partial B_ r}w(x\cdot Dw-2w) =W(r,u)-W(r,p)+r^{-n-2}\int _{B_ r}w\Delta w. \]

Differentiating the fixed-sphere formula for $M$ gives $M'(r)=2r^{-n-4}\int _{\partial B_ r}w(x\cdot Dw-2w)$. Combining the last two identities proves the formula and its sign. Local absolute continuity follows from $C^{1,1}$ regularity on compact annuli. This is the argument of [Fig18], Lemma 8.4, with the nonnegative terms retained. $\square$

Theorem 13.4.8 (Uniqueness and continuity of singular blow-ups).3BC351E89BC9 At each singular point $x_0$ of a normalized obstacle solution there is one quadratic model $p_{x_0}$ such that

\[ u_{x_0,r}\longrightarrow p_{x_0}\quad \text{in }C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n) \quad \text{as }r\downarrow 0. \]

Its matrix depends continuously on $x_0$ within the singular set, and uniformly continuously on compact subsets of that set.

Proof

Translate $x_0$ to zero and choose a sequence with quadratic limit $p$. Then $M(r_ j,u,p)\to 0$. Since this distance is nonnegative and nondecreasing with the radius, its limit at zero is zero. Every other blow-up $q$ consequently agrees with $p$ on $\partial B_1$ in $L^2$, hence everywhere on that sphere by continuity. Both are homogeneous of degree two by Proposition 13.4.4, so they agree throughout space. Compactness and uniqueness of every subsequential limit give convergence along all radii.

For continuity, take singular points $x_ j\to x_0$ and choose a fixed small $\rho >0$ for which all the relevant balls remain in $U$. Apply Monneau monotonicity at $x_ j$ with the comparison polynomial $p_{x_0}$. Letting the smaller radius go to zero gives

\[ \int _{\partial B_1}|p_{x_ j}-p_{x_0}|^2 \leq \int _{\partial B_1} \left|\rho ^{-2}u(x_ j+\rho x)-p_{x_0}(x)\right|^2. \]

First send $j$ to infinity using continuity of $u$, then send $\rho $ to zero using the just-proved convergence at $x_0$. The resulting spherical $L^2$ distance tends to zero. On the finite-dimensional space of quadratic forms this is a norm, so the matrices converge. Continuity on a compact subset is uniform. This proves the uniqueness and continuity assertions of [Fig18], Theorem 8.1, using spherical integrals throughout. $\square$

Definition 13.4.9 (Singular strata of the classical obstacle problem).E0118BF86411 Write $p_{x_0}(x)=\frac12x\cdot A_{x_0}x$ for the unique singular blow-up and define

\[ \Sigma _ k=\{ x_0\in \Sigma :\dim \ker A_{x_0}=k\} , \qquad k=0,\ldots ,n-1. \]

The trace-one condition excludes the zero matrix. The kernel is the contact set of the blow-up; its dimension defines the stratum, not the dimension of the full ambient free boundary. See [Fig18], Section 9, p. 19.

Theorem 13.4.10 (Classical stratification of singular obstacle points).9A0B5D86CDB6 For a normalized obstacle solution and each $k=0,\ldots ,n-1$, the stratum $\Sigma _ k$ is locally contained in a $k$-dimensional $C^1$ submanifold. In particular each point of $\Sigma _0$ is isolated within $\Sigma _0$. Containment does not assert that the whole stratum is itself a manifold.

This is [Fig18], Theorem 9.1, p. 19, attributed there to Caffarelli. The extension and implicit-function arguments establishing manifold containment remain external. The uniqueness theorem alone does not provide this geometric conclusion.
