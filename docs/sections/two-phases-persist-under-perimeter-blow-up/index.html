4.10 Two phases persist under perimeter blow-up90C626A5512D

90C626A5512D

A compactness limit can lose an entire phase unless both phases have quantitative volume. We derive the needed bounds from the already proved first-power Sobolev inequality. This expands [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, printed p. 99, and avoids using the De Giorgi structure theorem in its own blow-up proof. Here $n\geq 2$.

Proposition 4.10.1 (The first-power inequality for compactly supported BV functions).5D94D6D39FA5 If $u\in BV(\mathbb R^ n)$ has compact support, then

\[ \| u\| _{L^{n/(n-1)}(\mathbb R^ n)}\leq |Du|(\mathbb R^ n). \]

The constant is the convenient nonsharp constant in Theorem 7.14.2. This is the BV version of the Sobolev input in [Sim18], p. 99.

Proof

The smooth mollifications with a nonnegative kernel from Lemma 4.2.3 have compact support, converge to $u$ in $L^1$ and have gradient integral at most $|Du|(\mathbb R^ n)$. Apply Theorem 7.14.2 to each mollification. Choose a sequence tending to $u$ almost everywhere and apply Fatou to the power $n/(n-1)$. Taking the appropriate root proves the assertion, including the conclusion that this higher-power norm is finite. $\square$

Corollary 4.10.2 (A cutoff Sobolev estimate for a phase).620C34C97991 If $E$ has locally finite perimeter in $U$ and $0\leq \phi \in C_ c^1(U)$, then

\[ \left(\int _ E\phi ^{n/(n-1)}\, dx\right)^{(n-1)/n} \leq \int \phi \, d|D\chi _ E|+\int _ E|D\phi |\, dx. \]

No bound on the total volume of $E$ is required. This is the cutoff inequality before equation (10) in [Sim18], p. 99.

Proof

By Proposition 4.2.2, $\phi \chi _ E$, extended by zero, is compactly supported and belongs to $BV(\mathbb R^ n)$. Its variation is at most the sum on the right. Proposition 4.10.1 applies, and $|\phi \chi _ E|^{n/(n-1)}=\phi ^{n/(n-1)}\chi _ E$. This proves the claim using only the distributional product rule. $\square$

Proposition 4.10.3 (A radial volume-perimeter inequality).A8AC237B1A7E If $B_ R(x)\Subset U$ and $V(r)=|E\cap B_ r(x)|$, then for almost every $0<r<R$,

\[ V(r)^{(n-1)/n}\leq |D\chi _ E|(B_ r(x))+V'(r). \]

The same inequality holds for $W(r)=|B_ r(x)\setminus E|$ with the same perimeter measure. This is the radial cutoff passage on printed p. 99 of [Sim18].

Proof

Use the smooth radial functions $\phi _\delta $ constructed in Lemma 4.9.4 in Corollary 4.10.2. Their values lie between zero and one and tend to the indicator of the closed ball. Its sphere is volume-null, so dominated convergence makes the left side tend to $V(r)^{(n-1)/n}$. At radii whose spheres have zero perimeter measure, the first term on the right tends to the open-ball perimeter. At Lebesgue points of $V'$, the second tends to $V'(r)$ by the weighted radial differentiation in that lemma. Both conditions hold almost everywhere. Apply the same argument to the complement for the final assertion. $\square$

Lemma 4.10.4 (A perimeter-support point sees positive volume of both phases).863266B2B394 If $x\in \operatorname {supp}|D\chi _ E|$ and $B_ r(x)\Subset U$, then

\[ |E\cap B_ r(x)|>0,\qquad |B_ r(x)\setminus E|>0. \]

This is a qualitative support assertion; it does not yet give a uniform density bound.

Proof

If the first volume were zero, $\chi _ E=0$ almost everywhere on the ball. Its distributional derivative would vanish there by testing the defining integral, contradicting membership in the support. If the second volume were zero, use $\chi _ E=1$ and the fact that the integral of the divergence of a compactly supported field is zero. In both cases the vector derivative and hence its total variation vanish on the ball, which is impossible. $\square$

Lemma 4.10.5 (Integrating a positive radial differential inequality).CC9DD078E7CD Suppose $V:[0,R)\to [0,\infty )$ is absolutely continuous on compact subintervals, $V(0)=0$, $V(r)>0$ for $r>0$, and

\[ V(r)^{(n-1)/n}\leq C V'(r)\quad \text{for almost every }r\in (0,R), \]

where $C>0$. Then $V(r)\geq (r/(nC))^ n$ for every $0<r<R$.

Proof

The assumed inequality makes $V'\geq 0$ almost everywhere, so $V$ is nondecreasing. On $[\delta ,r]$ with $0<\delta <r<R$, it is bounded away from zero. The absolutely continuous chain rule therefore gives

\[ \frac{d}{ds}V(s)^{1/n}=\frac1n V(s)^{-(n-1)/n}V'(s)\geq \frac{1}{nC} \]

almost everywhere on that interval. Integrate, and let $\delta \downarrow 0$. Absolute continuity at zero gives $V(\delta )\to 0$; alternatively the nonnegative term can be discarded before taking this limit. Raising the resulting root inequality to the power $n$ proves the claim at every radius. $\square$

Theorem 4.10.6 (Two-sided phase volume at a reduced-boundary point).C37DA08B0091 If $x\in \partial ^*E$, then for some $r_0>0$ and all $0<r<r_0$,

\[ |E\cap B_ r(x)|\geq c_ n r^ n,\qquad |B_ r(x)\setminus E|\geq c_ n r^ n, \qquad c_ n=(3n)^{-n}. \]

The constant is not sharp. These are the two estimates (10)–(11) in [Sim18], p. 99; the limiting density $1/2$ requires the subsequent blow-up argument.

Proof

Use Proposition 4.9.5 and Proposition 4.10.3 to obtain $V^{(n-1)/n}\leq \mu (B_ r)+V'\leq 3V'$ for almost every small radius. Lemma 4.10.4 supplies positivity at every radius, and Lemma 4.9.3 supplies absolute continuity and $V(0)=0$. Apply Lemma 4.10.5 with $C=3$. The argument for $W$ uses the other flux bound and gives the same constant. The intersection of the full-measure sets of good radii suffices for the differential inequalities; their integrated conclusions hold for every small radius. $\square$
