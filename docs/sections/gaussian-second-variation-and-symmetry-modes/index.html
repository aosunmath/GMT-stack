14.8 Gaussian second variation and symmetry modesA2DC3F2E48E4

A2DC3F2E48E4

The shrinker equation is the Euler–Lagrange equation of Gaussian area, but its Hessian differs from the ordinary minimal-surface Jacobi form. We use the scalar convention $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $ and $\Phi =H-\langle x,\nu \rangle /2$. Thus $\Phi =0$ is Definition 14.2.2. Sources are [CM12], Sections 4–5, and [CCMS20], Section 3.1. The notation $[u]=\int u\rho \, dA$ and $\mathcal D$ is that of Definition 14.7.1.

Proposition 14.8.1 (Compact variations of noncompact Gaussian area).E8BEBA69F77A Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth two-sided boundaryless immersed hypersurface with $F(\Sigma )=[1]<\infty $. For a smooth variation supported on a compact subset of its parameter manifold, with initial normal velocity $f\nu $,

\[ F'(0)=[f\Phi ]. \]

Consequently it is critical for all such variations exactly when it is a self-shrinker.

Proof

The changing part of the integral is compact. There $dA'=fH\, dA$ and $\rho '=-(f/2)\langle x,\nu \rangle \rho $, by Proposition 14.3.2. The product rule proves the formula. Tests with arbitrary compactly supported $f$ imply $\Phi =0$ by continuity. No boundary term at infinity is taken; the integrand is unchanged there. This extends Proposition 14.2.4 to the stated noncompact setting. $\square$

Definition 14.8.2 (The Gaussian Jacobi operator).9EB35742B3AF For a smooth two-sided self-shrinker, define

\[ Lf=\mathcal Df+(|A|^2+\tfrac 12)f, \qquad Q(f,g)=[\langle \nabla f,\nabla g\rangle -(|A|^2+\tfrac 12)fg] \]

for compactly supported smooth functions. Write $Q(f)=Q(f,f)$. The sign convention is that a negative value of $Q$ decreases centered Gaussian area to second order. See [CM12], (4.13), and [CCMS20], (3.1). The drift operator $\mathcal D$ and the Jacobi operator $L$ are different operators.

Proposition 14.8.3 (Linearizing the shrinker equation).3AED5B2D47FB For a normal variation $f\nu $ of a smooth two-sided hypersurface in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$,

\[ \Phi '= -\left(\Delta -\tfrac 12\langle x^ T,\nabla \cdot \rangle +|A|^2+\tfrac 12\right)f. \]

At a self-shrinker this is $-Lf$. A tangential velocity $Y$ adds $Y(\Phi )$, which vanishes at a self-shrinker.

Proof

Proposition 7.6.2 gives $H'=-\Delta f-|A|^2f$. Since $\nu '=-\nabla f$,

\[ (\langle x,\nu \rangle )'=f-\langle x^ T,\nabla f\rangle . \]

Subtract half this identity from the first one. Tangential variations differentiate the scalar function by reparametrization. This also explains the signs in [CM12], (4.10). $\square$

Theorem 14.8.4 (Second variation with fixed center and scale).81208FDD5A9C Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth two-sided boundaryless immersed self-shrinker with finite Gaussian area. For every smooth compactly supported variation with initial normal velocity $f\nu $,

\[ F''(0)=-[fLf]=Q(f). \]

Moreover $-[fLg]=Q(f,g)=-[gLf]$ for compactly supported smooth $f,g$. This is the fixed-center case of [CM12], Theorem 4.14, and [CCMS20], Section 3.1.

Proof

Differentiate Proposition 14.8.1. At a shrinker the factors multiplying $\Phi $ vanish, including any acceleration contribution, so the derivative is $[f\Phi ']=-[fLf]$. Proposition 14.8.3 gives the operator; Lemma 14.7.2 gives $Q$ and its bilinear symmetry. This is formal self-adjointness on compact tests; it does not specify a self-adjoint domain or a discrete spectrum on an arbitrary noncompact surface. $\square$

Proposition 14.8.5 (Translation and dilation modes).B2A315CA544B On a smooth two-sided self-shrinker, for each constant vector $v$ put $a_ v=\langle v,\nu \rangle $. Then

\[ La_ v=\tfrac 12a_ v,\qquad LH=H. \]

These pointwise identities do not require compactness. A mode is an eigenfunction only when it is nonzero and belongs to the domain under consideration. Compare [CM12], Theorem 5.2.

Proof

Translate the immersion to $x+sv$. Its scalar curvature and normal are unchanged, so its residual is $\Phi _ s=-s a_ v/2$. The normal velocity is $a_ v$; its tangential component contributes zero because $\Phi =0$. Linearization therefore yields $-La_ v=-a_ v/2$.

For the dilation $x_ s=(1+s)x$, scalar curvature is $H/(1+s)$ and the normal is unchanged. Thus $\Phi _ s=H((1+s)^{-1}-(1+s))$, with derivative $-2H$. The normal velocity is $\langle x,\nu \rangle =2H$. Linearization gives $-L(2H)=-2H$. These arguments are local calculations and do not assert convergence of noncompact integrals. $\square$

Proposition 14.8.6 (Rotations give Jacobi fields).3E301015DE84 If $K$ is a skew-symmetric linear map of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $\Sigma $ is a smooth two-sided self-shrinker, then

\[ L\langle Kx,\nu \rangle =0. \]
Proof

The orthogonal maps $e^{sK}$ preserve both mean curvature and the radial Gaussian weight. Thus $e^{sK}\Sigma $ has identically zero shrinker residual for every $s$. Its normal velocity is $\langle Kx,\nu \rangle $. Proposition 14.8.3 gives the conclusion. If the rotation preserves $\Sigma $, this function can be identically zero. This construction gives an integrable symmetry field; it does not say that every element of the kernel integrates to a family of shrinkers. $\square$

Corollary 14.8.7 (Orthogonality of the geometric modes on a closed shrinker).21CF7F379644 On a smooth closed two-sided self-shrinker,

\[ [H\langle v,\nu \rangle ]=0 \]

for every constant vector $v$. Nonzero rotation modes are likewise orthogonal to $H$ and all translation modes.

Proof

All smooth functions are compactly supported on a closed manifold. The bilinear symmetry in Theorem 14.8.4 gives $[uLw]=[wLu]$. Apply the pointwise equations of Propositions 14.8.5 and 14.8.6. Distinct eigenvalues $1,1/2,0$ force the respective inner products to be zero. $\square$

Theorem 14.8.8 (Every finite-Gaussian-area proper shrinker is unstable at fixed center).64BCB21D3A98 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a nonempty smooth complete properly immersed two-sided self-shrinker without boundary and with $F(\Sigma )<\infty $. There is $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$ with $Q(f)<0$. Thus no such shrinker is stable for Gaussian area with its center and scale fixed. This explains the distinction preceding [CM12], Section 4.3.

Proof

Choose smooth radial cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ R\leq 1$ equal to one on $B_ R$, zero outside $B_{2R}$, with $|\nabla _\Sigma \eta _ R|\leq C/R$. They are smooth at zero by being constant there, and properness makes their support compact on the parameter manifold. Dropping the nonpositive curvature term gives

\[ Q(\eta _ R)\leq \frac{C^2}{R^2}[1]-\tfrac 12[\eta _ R^2]. \]

Dominated convergence yields $[\eta _ R^2]\to [1]>0$. Hence the right side is negative for large $R$. This does not require a global integral bound for $|A|^2$: its contribution is finite on each compact support and only improves the inequality. The conclusion concerns fixed center and scale, unlike Definition 14.9.3. $\square$
