14.21 Maximum principles and entire graphical flowsE2D89828C9A3

E2D89828C9A3

The periodic estimates avoid spatial infinity. We now give an explicit maximum principle sufficient for entire graphs with controlled growth, then identify the additional existence input from [EH89], Theorem 4.6. Smoothness on a noncompact time slice alone is not a global bound.

Lemma 14.21.1 (Normal material heat equals the graph nondivergence operator).DBBE715C49A2 For an entire graphical mean curvature flow, let

\[ a^{ij}=\delta _{ij}-\frac{u_ i u_ j}{1+|Du|^2}. \]

For any geometric scalar $f$ written in graph coordinates,

\[ (\partial _ t^\perp -\Delta _ M)f =\partial _ t^{\rm graph}f-a^{ij}\partial _ i\partial _ j f. \]

The cancellation uses the graph equation and does not assert equality of the two time derivatives separately.

Proof

The graph metric has Christoffel symbols $\Gamma ^ k_{ij}=u_{ij}u_ k/v^2$, as follows by projecting $F_{ij}=u_{ij}e$ onto the tangent frame. Thus $\Delta _ M f=a^{ij}f_{ij}-a^{ij}u_{ij}u_ kf_ k/v^2$. The graph equation gives $a^{ij}u_{ij}=u_ t$. Meanwhile the tangential velocity in Lemma 14.18.3 has coordinate components $T^ k=u_ tu_ k/v^2$, since the projection of $e$ has components $u_ k/v^2$. Subtract the Laplacian from $\partial _ t^\perp f=\partial _ t^{\rm graph}f-T^ kf_ k$. The two first-order terms cancel. All statements are local, so the same calculation holds on a torus as well. $\square$

Lemma 14.21.2 (A maximum principle with an explicit polynomial growth condition).2C31DF28D132 On $\mathbb R^ n\times [s,T]$, let $a(x,t)$ be symmetric with $0\leq a\leq A_0 I$, and let $|b(x,t)|\leq B_0$. Suppose $f$ is continuous up to $t=s$, twice differentiable in space and once in time for $t>s$, and satisfies

\[ f_ t-a^{ij}f_{ij}-b^ if_ i\leq 0,\qquad f(x,s)\leq M, \qquad f(x,t)\leq C_0(1+|x|^2)^ m \]

for some finite $A_0,B_0,C_0,m\geq 0$, uniformly on the slab. Then $f\leq M$ throughout. Strict uniform ellipticity is not needed for this upper bound.

Proof

Choose $q>\max \{ m,1\} $ and put $\psi =(1+|x|^2)^ q$. Direct differentiation gives $|D\psi |\leq q\psi $ and $|D^2\psi |\leq 4q^2\psi $ in operator norm. For $K=1+4nA_0q^2+B_0q$, the function $B(x,t)=e^{K(t-s)}\psi (x)$ satisfies $B_ t-a^{ij}B_{ij}-b^ iB_ i\geq B>0$. For each $\eta >0$, the function $f-M-\eta B$ is negative initially and tends to minus infinity at spatial infinity uniformly in time, because $q>m$. If it were positive somewhere, it would have a positive maximum on a compact space-time cylinder, at a time later than $s$. At that point its spatial gradient is zero, its Hessian is nonpositive and its time derivative is nonnegative (one-sided at the final time). Positive semidefiniteness of $a$ would make its parabolic operator nonnegative. Its strict subsolution inequality makes the same operator negative, a contradiction. Thus $f\leq M+\eta B$; let $\eta \downarrow 0$ at each fixed point. This supplies an explicit alternative to the weighted noncompact maximum principle in [EH89], Corollary 1.1, under the growth hypotheses stated here. $\square$

Proposition 14.21.3 (Slope preservation for an entire graph with controlled growth).1D248E4A03EF Let $u$ be a smooth entire graphical flow on $[s,T]$. Suppose $v(x,t)=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$ has at most polynomial growth in $|x|$, uniformly on this slab, and $V_ s=\sup _ xv(x,s)<\infty $. Then $v(x,t)\leq V_ s$ throughout. No a priori curvature bound is needed for this conclusion.

Proof

Proposition 14.18.2 and Lemma 14.21.1 give $v_ t-a^{ij}v_{ij}=-|A|^2v-2v^{-1}|\nabla v|^2\leq 0$. The coefficient matrix always satisfies $0<a\leq I$, independently of whether a global slope bound has yet been proved. Apply Lemma 14.21.2 with zero drift and the assumed polynomial upper growth. This proves the bound without presuming a maximum is attained on the entire graph. It is a version of [EH89], Corollary 3.2 with an explicit sufficient growth class. $\square$

Theorem 14.21.4 (Entire-graph curvature smoothing with slab bounds).CA23D8CDDE36 Let $u$ be a smooth entire graphical flow on $[s,T]$, and assume $v$ and $|A|$ are bounded on this entire space-time slab. With $V_ s=\sup _ xv(x,s)$, for $s<t\leq T$ one has

\[ \sup _ x v(x,t)^2|A(x,t)|^2\leq \sup _ xv(x,s)^2|A(x,s)|^2, \]
\[ \bigl (1+2(t-s)|A(x,t)|^2\bigr )v(x,t)^2\leq V_ s^2, \qquad |A(x,t)|^2\leq \frac{V_ s^2-1}{2(t-s)}. \]

This states the bounds of [EH89], Corollary 4.2 and the base case of Proposition 4.4, in a class where the maximum principle above applies directly.

Proof

Use the exact inequalities for $P=v^2|A|^2$ and $Z=v^2+2(t-s)P$ from Section 14.19. Lemma 14.21.1 writes their operator as $\partial _ t-a^{ij}\partial _{ij}+2v^{-1}(\nabla v)^ k\partial _ k$. The Euclidean coordinate norm of a tangent vector is no greater than its graph-metric norm, since $g\geq I$. Therefore Lemma 14.18.5 bounds the coordinate drift by $2v|A|$, uniformly on the slab. Both $P$ and $Z$ are bounded there. Apply Lemma 14.21.2 with exponent zero, choosing the drift sign to match this operator. Their starting suprema are the two right-hand sides displayed above. The final curvature bound follows from $v\geq 1$, exactly as in the periodic case. The assumed slab bound is used to justify the maximum principle; it is not silently supplied by smoothness on a noncompact domain. $\square$

Corollary 14.21.5 (A bounded-slope ancient entire graph is a plane under slab curvature control).54D00AF5AF92 Let $u:\mathbb R^ n\times (-\infty ,T)\to \mathbb R$ be a smooth graphical flow with one uniform bound for $v$ over all space and all times. Assume additionally that $|A|$ is bounded on each closed finite time slab. Then $u(x,t)=p\cdot x+c$ for fixed $p,c$. In particular no nonstationary vertical translator satisfying these hypotheses exists.

Proof

Fix $t<T$. Theorem 14.21.4 applies from every earlier $s$, and its curvature numerator is bounded independently of $s$. Let $s\to -\infty $ to conclude $A(\cdot ,t)=0$. Lemma 14.20.1 gives $D^2u=0$, so $u(x,t)=p(t)\cdot x+c(t)$. The graph equation now has zero right-hand side for every $x$, forcing $p'(t)=0$ and $c'(t)=0$. A vertical translator would have $u=u_0+at$; hence its speed $a$ is zero. This is a parabolic rigidity deduction with the stated global bounds, not a claim about arbitrary local or strip graphs. $\square$

Proposition 14.21.6 (Uniqueness for entire flows with bounded slope and curvature).A8788BD1ACF4 Let $u_1,u_2$ be smooth entire graphical flows on $[0,T]$ with the same initial function. Suppose their slopes and curvatures are bounded on the whole slab. Then $u_1=u_2$ on this slab. The same statement holds on every finite slab of two all-time flows in this class.

Proof

Let $w=u_2-u_1$. Subtracting the two graphical equations, as in Proposition 14.3.14, gives

\[ w_ t=a^{ij}(Du_2)w_{ij}+b^ kw_ k,\qquad b^ k=\int _0^1\partial _{p_ k}a^{ij}(Du_1+\theta Dw)\, d\theta \, (u_1)_{ij}. \]

The slopes bound every interpolating gradient, and Lemma 14.20.1 bounds $D^2u_1$ by its curvature and slope bounds. Hence $b$ is uniformly bounded. Also $|w(x,t)|\leq C(1+|x|)$ on the slab: each gradient is bounded, and the heights at $x=0$ are bounded by continuity on the compact time interval. Apply Lemma 14.21.2 to $w$ and $-w$, both with zero initial value. This gives both inequalities and therefore equality. No global bound for the heights themselves is imposed. $\square$

Theorem 14.21.7 (Ecker–Huisken existence for globally Lipschitz entire graphs).FC44FD2231EF Let $u_0:\mathbb R^ n\to \mathbb R$ be globally Lipschitz, $n\geq 1$, with $\sqrt{1+|Du_0|^2}\leq V_0$ almost everywhere. There is a graphical mean curvature flow for all $t>0$, smooth at positive time, taking initial value $u_0$ by locally uniform convergence as $t\downarrow 0$. It satisfies

\[ v\leq V_0,\qquad t^{m+1}|\nabla ^ m A|^2\leq C(m,n,V_0) \quad (m=0,1,2,\ldots ). \]

If the initial graph is smooth with bounded covariant curvature derivatives of every order, the construction is smooth up to $t=0$ and has bounded curvature and its derivatives on each finite time slab. This is [EH89], Theorem 4.6, printed p. 464, together with Corollary 3.2 and Propositions 4.3–4.4. The parabolic existence, higher-derivative estimates and approximation arguments remain external. No existence theorem for arbitrary merely locally Lipschitz data without a global slope bound is asserted from this source.

Corollary 14.21.8 (Smooth periodic initial graphs exist globally and converge).EFBA67013512 Every smooth function on a flat torus is the initial value of a unique all-time smooth periodic graphical mean curvature flow. This solution has the convergence, area decay and total dissipation proved in Section 14.20. Its existence uses the external theorem above; those estimates have local proofs in the project.

Proof

Lift the initial function periodically to Euclidean space. Compactness of the torus bounds its slope and all derivatives, hence all covariant curvature derivatives of the graph. Theorem 14.21.7 gives an entire flow smooth up to time zero with bounded slope and curvature on every finite slab. Translating this solution by a lattice vector in the base gives another solution with the same initial value and bounds. Proposition 14.21.6 makes the two solutions equal. Thus the entire flow remains periodic and descends to the torus. The same uniqueness argument, or its compact maximum-principle version, gives uniqueness of the periodic solution. It is all-time smooth, so every hypothesis of the convergence and dissipation results is now satisfied. This use of a cited existence theorem is distinct from proving that theorem from the curvature estimate alone. $\square$
