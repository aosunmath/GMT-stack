12.11 Compactness at a fixed phase-field parameter383398F166B3

383398F166B3

Here $(M^ n,g)$ is a smooth nonempty closed Riemannian manifold, $n\geq 1$, and $\varepsilon >0$ is fixed. We develop the compactness calculation suggested in [ChAC], Exercise 4.4, p. 14. The residual retains the correct $\varepsilon ^2$ factor. The needed Sobolev compactness is proved in Section 7.18; elliptic regularity remains an analytic input identified in the proofs. The resulting compactness statement alone is not a mountain-pass existence theorem.

Proposition 12.11.1 (An energy identity for every closed critical point).1605E7AF7DC3 A smooth solution of $\varepsilon ^2\Delta _ g u=u^3-u$ satisfies

\[ \varepsilon ^2\int _ M|\nabla u|^2=\int _ M(u^2-u^4),\qquad E_\varepsilon (u;M)=\frac{\operatorname {Vol}(M)-\int _ Mu^4}{4\varepsilon }. \]

In particular $0\leq E_\varepsilon (u;M)\leq \operatorname {Vol}(M)/(4\varepsilon )$ and $\int _ M|\nabla u|^2\leq \operatorname {Vol}(M)/(4\varepsilon ^2)$. Equality in the energy upper bound occurs precisely for $u=0$.

Proof

Multiply the equation by $u$ and integrate by parts on the closed manifold. This gives the first identity. Substitute its gradient term into $E_\varepsilon =\varepsilon \int |\nabla u|^2/2+(4\varepsilon )^{-1}\int (1-2u^2+u^4)$; the $u^2$ terms cancel and give the second. Nonnegativity follows from the original energy definition. Proposition 12.2.3 gives $|u|\leq 1$, and $u^2-u^4\leq 1/4$, proving the gradient bound. The energy upper bound follows from $u^4\geq 0$, and equality forces $u=0$ by continuity. The identities concern critical points, not arbitrary functions with the same pointwise bound. $\square$

Theorem 12.11.2 (Strong compactness for a bounded residual sequence).A1F06EA746A2 Let $u_ j\in H^1(M)$ satisfy $|u_ j|\leq 1$ almost everywhere and $E_\varepsilon (u_ j;M)\leq C$. Suppose that

\[ r_ j=-\varepsilon ^2\Delta _ g u_ j+W'(u_ j)\longrightarrow 0 \quad \text{in }H^{-1}(M), \]

where $H^{-1}$ is the dual of $H^1(M)$ with its full norm. Then a subsequence converges strongly in $H^1$ and in every finite $L^ p$ to a smooth critical point $u$, with $|u|\leq 1$ and $E_\varepsilon (u_ j;M)\to E_\varepsilon (u;M)$. This develops the bounded-range compactness mechanism in [ChAC], Exercise 4.4, with the residual normalized to the displayed $\varepsilon $-equation. For this same residual, convergence in $L^2$ implies the $H^{-1}$ convergence assumed here.

Proof

The pointwise bound controls the $L^2$ norms, and the energy controls the gradients because $\varepsilon $ is fixed. Weak $H^1$ compactness and Theorem 7.18.2 supply a subsequence with $u_ j\rightharpoonup u$ in $H^1$ and $u_ j\to u$ strongly in $L^2$ and almost everywhere. The limit lies in $[-1,1]$. Since $W'$ is Lipschitz on this interval, $W'(u_ j)\to W'(u)$ in $L^2$. Passing to the equation against smooth tests shows that $u$ is a weak solution, and density gives the equation against every $H^1$ test.

Subtract the limit equation and test with $u_ j-u$:

\[ \varepsilon ^2\int |\nabla (u_ j-u)|^2 =\langle r_ j,u_ j-u\rangle -\int (W'(u_ j)-W'(u))(u_ j-u). \]

The dual pairing tends to zero because the tests stay bounded in $H^1$, and the second term tends to zero by strong $L^2$ convergence. Thus convergence is strong in $H^1$. Boundedness by one upgrades it to every finite $L^ p$: for $p\geq 2$ use $|u_ j-u|^ p\leq 2^{p-2}|u_ j-u|^2$, and for smaller $p$ use finite volume. The potential $W$ is Lipschitz on $[-1,1]$, so its integrals converge; strong gradient convergence then gives energy convergence.

Finally a bounded weak solution of this smooth semilinear equation is smooth by interior elliptic $W^{2,p}$ estimates followed by Sobolev embedding and bootstrapping in finitely many coordinate charts. These elliptic regularity inputs are external here. There is no restriction $n\leq 4$: the assumed uniform range bound supplies the integrability of the nonlinearity even when $H^1$ alone would not embed into $L^4$. $\square$

Corollary 12.11.3 (The entire critical set is compact for fixed epsilon).AC5DCB969E47 For a fixed $\varepsilon >0$ and fixed smooth closed $(M,g)$, every sequence of smooth Allen–Cahn critical points has a subsequence converging in $C^ k(M)$ for every finite $k$ to a smooth critical point. The constants in these compactness estimates may depend on $\varepsilon $.

Proof

The maximum bound and Proposition 12.11.1 supply all assumptions of Theorem 12.11.2, with residual zero. For the stronger convergence, the equation has uniformly bounded right-hand side after division by the fixed $\varepsilon ^2$. Global estimates obtained from the finitely many local elliptic charts give uniform $W^{2,p}$ bounds for every finite $p$. Choose $p>n$ to control $C^{1,\alpha }$, and bootstrap the smooth polynomial nonlinearity to uniform bounds of all derivative orders. These are the same external elliptic estimates used in the theorem. Arzela–Ascoli and a diagonal subsequence give every $C^ k$ convergence. The limit equation follows either from this convergence or from the preceding weak argument. These estimates do not assert compactness as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. $\square$

Proposition 12.11.4 (Index cannot increase in a bounded-range strong limit).30298CFE6EFF Fix $\varepsilon >0$. If $|u_ j|,|u|\leq 1$ almost everywhere and $u_ j\to u$ in $L^2(M)$, define the index of each quadratic form

\[ Q_{\varepsilon ,u}(\phi )=\int _ M \bigl (\varepsilon |\nabla \phi |^2+\varepsilon ^{-1}(3u^2-1)\phi ^2\bigr ) \]

as the supremum of dimensions of negative definite subspaces of smooth tests. Then $\operatorname {index}(Q_{\varepsilon ,u}) \leq \liminf _ j\operatorname {index}(Q_{\varepsilon ,u_ j})$. In particular a fixed upper bound for the indices of the critical points passes to the limit in Theorem 12.11.2.

Proof

Take any finite-dimensional negative subspace for the limit form and a smooth basis $\phi _1,\ldots ,\phi _ m$. Since $|u_ j^2-u^2|\leq 2|u_ j-u|$, the potential terms in every matrix entry $Q_{\varepsilon ,u_ j}(\phi _ r,\phi _ s)$ converge to those of the limit form by $L^2$ convergence and boundedness of the fixed tests. The negative definite limit matrix is bounded above by $-\delta $ times the Euclidean norm squared on its coefficient vectors for some $\delta >0$, using compactness of the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^ m$. Entrywise convergence preserves negative definiteness for all large $j$. Therefore the approximating indices are eventually at least $m$. Taking all possible finite $m$ proves the claim, including the case of an infinite limit index. No convergence of eigenfunction bases is assumed. $\square$

Proposition 12.11.5 (A sharp necessary parameter range for nonconstant solutions).8EFAD482DAF6 Assume $M$ is connected, and let

\[ \lambda _1=\inf _{\substack {v\in H^1(M),\ \int _ Mv=0\\ v\ne 0}} \frac{\int _ M|\nabla v|^2}{\int _ Mv^2}>0. \]

If $\varepsilon ^2\lambda _1\geq 1$, every smooth Allen–Cahn critical point is a constant in $\{ -1,0,1\} $. On a circle this threshold is sharp, as the periodic examples exist for every $\varepsilon ^2\lambda _1<1$.

Proof

Corollary 7.18.3 gives positivity of $\lambda _1$ and the Poincare inequality.

Let $\bar u$ be the average of a critical point and set $v=u-\bar u$. Testing its equation with $v$ gives

\[ \varepsilon ^2\int |\nabla u|^2-\int v^2 +\int (u^3-\bar u^3)(u-\bar u)=0. \]

The first two terms together are nonnegative by $\varepsilon ^2\lambda _1\geq 1$ and Poincare. The final integrand is nonnegative because the cubic is strictly increasing, and vanishes only when $u=\bar u$. It follows that $u$ is constant, including in the equality case of the threshold. The equation then forces one of the three stated values.

On the circle of length $\ell $, $\lambda _1=(2\pi /\ell )^2$, from the Fourier modes. Example 16.21.6 supplies a one-oscillation solution exactly for $\varepsilon <\ell /(2\pi )$, proving sharpness on this model. No sufficiency claim for a general manifold is made. $\square$
