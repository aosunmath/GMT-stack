14.26 Local gradient bounds from height oscillationC3D084C93309

C3D084C93309

An ambient-ball cutoff controls angle only while the surface remains inside that ambient ball. A height-dependent cutoff instead gives a gradient estimate on a fixed base ball. We develop the maximum-principle argument of [EH91], Theorem 2.3, printed pp. 551–553, with explicit, nonoptimal constants. The flow is assumed smooth on the relevant closed cylinder; this section proves an estimate for such a flow, not the boundary-value existence or approximation theorem.

Lemma 14.26.1 (The height-dependent cutoff at a large-angle maximum).15ECD7CEFB78 Work on the unit base ball, suppose the graph height $h<0$ throughout a finite time slab, and let $\beta \geq 1$. On the positive set of

\[ \varphi =1-|x|^2+\frac{h}{2\beta } \]

one has $\mathcal P\varphi \leq 2n$. At a point with $v\geq 4+16\beta $, one also has

\[ |\nabla \varphi |^2\geq \frac1{16\beta ^2}. \]

Here $\mathcal P=\partial _ t^\perp -\Delta $ and gradients are intrinsic to the moving graph.

Proof

The ambient height satisfies $\mathcal Ph=0$. Lemma 14.23.1 gives $\mathcal P(1-|x|^2)=2\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1})\leq 2n$. Write $e$ for the upward vertical unit vector and $X=(x,h)$. The intrinsic height gradient is $e^ T$, so $|\nabla h|^2=1-v^{-2}$. Since the ambient gradient of $|x|^2$ is the horizontal vector $2(x,0)$,

\[ \langle \nabla h,\nabla |x|^2\rangle =-2v^{-1}\langle \nu ,(x,0)\rangle , \qquad |\langle \nabla h,\nabla |x|^2\rangle |\leq 2/v \]

in the unit base ball. Consequently

\[ |\nabla \varphi |^2 \geq \frac{1-v^{-2}}{4\beta ^2}-\frac2{\beta v}. \]

For $v\geq 4+16\beta $ the first term is at least $3/(16\beta ^2)$ and the subtracted term at most $1/(8\beta ^2)$. This proves the estimate. Dropping the nonnegative term $|\nabla |x|^2|^2$ is legitimate; no estimate of the Euclidean height gradient by one is being made. $\square$

Theorem 14.26.2 (An explicit local height-to-gradient estimate).1439A7E413CC Let a graphical flow be smooth on a neighborhood of $\overline B_ R(y)\times [0,T]$, $R>0$. Define

\[ V_0=\sup _{B_ R(y)}\sqrt{1+|Du(x,0)|^2},\quad M=\sup _{B_ R(y)\times [0,T]}u,\quad K=M-\inf _{0\leq t\leq T}u(y,t). \]

Then $K\geq 0$ and, for every $0\leq t\leq T$,

\[ v(y,t)\leq 42V_0\exp \left((32n+1)\max \{ 1,K^2/R^2\} \right). \]

This is an explicit version of [EH91], Theorem 2.3. The constants here are sufficient constants, not sharp ones. Only the initial slope in the base ball and a height oscillation over the cylinder enter the estimate.

Proof

Translate and parabolically rescale to $y=0$, $R=1$, scaling height by $R^{-1}$ as well. First subtract $M+\epsilon $ from the rescaled height, so that the resulting $h$ is strictly negative throughout the cylinder. Set

\[ \beta =\max \{ 1,K+\epsilon \} ,\qquad \lambda =64n\beta ^2,\qquad \eta =(e^{\lambda \varphi }-1)_+, \quad \varphi =1-|x|^2+h/(2\beta ). \]

The positive support of $\eta $ stays strictly inside the base ball, uniformly over the finite slab. At the center $\varphi (0,t)\geq 1/2$, whereas everywhere $\varphi <1$.

Consider a positive maximum of $v\eta $ over space and all times up to a chosen time. If it exceeds the initial supremum, it occurs at positive time and inside the positive support. There $\nabla (v\eta )=0$. The angle evolution from Proposition 14.18.2 and the product rule give

\[ 0\leq \mathcal P(v\eta ) =v\mathcal P\eta -|A|^2v\eta \leq v\mathcal P\eta . \]

The two gradient terms cancel at the maximum. The tangential coordinate drift also vanishes there. Since $\eta =e^{\lambda \varphi }-1$ in this positive region, it follows that

\[ \mathcal P\varphi \geq \lambda |\nabla \varphi |^2. \]

If $v\geq 4+16\beta $, Lemma 14.26.1 would give $2n\geq 4n$, a contradiction. Thus at such a maximum $v<4+16\beta \leq 20\beta $. Since $\eta \leq e^\lambda $, the complete maximum estimate is

\[ v\eta \leq e^\lambda \max \{ V_0,20\beta \} \leq e^\lambda (V_0+20\beta ). \]

At the center $\eta \geq e^{\lambda /2}-1\geq \tfrac 12 e^{\lambda /2}$, because $\lambda \geq 64$. It follows that

\[ v(0,t)\leq 2(V_0+20\beta )e^{32n\beta ^2} \leq 42V_0e^{(32n+1)\beta ^2}, \]

using $V_0\geq 1$, $\beta \geq 1$ and $\beta \leq e^{\beta ^2}$. Let $\epsilon \downarrow 0$ and restore the scale. In the rescaled problem $K$ is the original oscillation divided by $R$, giving the stated formula. Only the positive region of $\eta $ was differentiated, and the lateral zero set cannot carry a positive maximum. $\square$

Proposition 14.26.3 (Local a priori slope and curvature from a larger initial ball).3286EE2A5DA1 Let an entire graphical flow be smooth on $[0,T]$. Fix $r>0$ and $R$ with $R^2>16r^2+2nT$. Suppose $|u(x,0)|\leq M$ on $B_ R(y)$ and $v(x,0)\leq V_0$ on $B_{3r}(y)$. Define

\[ V=42V_0\exp \left((32n+1) \max \{ 1,4(M+R)^2/r^2\} \right). \]

Then $v\leq V$ on $B_{2r}(y)\times [0,T]$, and

\[ \sup _{B_ r(y)}|A(\cdot ,t)|^2 \leq C(n)V^4(t^{-1}+r^{-2})\qquad (0<t\leq T). \]

The input is entirely local initial height and slope data. The height bound may be improved by subtracting a constant before applying the assertion.

Proof

Proposition 14.25.2 gives $|u|\leq M+R$ on $B_{4r}(y)\times [0,T]$. For any center $z\in B_{2r}(y)$, apply Theorem 14.26.2 to $B_ r(z)$, which is contained in $B_{3r}(y)$. Its initial slope supremum is at most $V_0$, and its height oscillation $K$ is at most $2(M+R)$. Thus its center slope is bounded by $V$, uniformly in $z$ and time. Next apply Theorem 14.23.5 with base radius $2r$ and interior ratio $1/2$. Absorb its fixed ratio factor in $C(n)$. Smoothness on finite compact cylinders justifies each maximum argument. Passing this estimate to merely locally Lipschitz initial data still requires the approximation/existence input in Theorem 14.25.5; it is not inferred by differentiating that initial graph. $\square$
