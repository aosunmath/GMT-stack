14.1 Smooth mean curvature flow1CA6D6DA3A2D

1CA6D6DA3A2D

Mean curvature flow is the evolution associated with the first variation of area. This introductory section fixes the sign and supplies a basic model. The subsequent sections develop evolution equations, comparison, Gaussian monotonicity, rescaling, and Brakke flow. Smooth identities and weak inequalities are stated separately.

Definition 14.1.1 (Smooth mean curvature flow).3FFB3EED5409 A smooth family of immersions $F:\Sigma ^ n\times [0,T)\to M^{n+1}$ evolves by mean curvature flow if

\[ (\partial _ tF)^\perp =\vec H. \]

Tangential velocity only changes the parametrization. With the convention of Definition 7.1.1, a normal parametrization satisfies $\partial _ tF=-H\nu $. The vector $\vec H$ is the trace mean curvature vector, so there is no factor $1/n$ in this equation. The smooth first-variation background is [ChMin], Section 7, and [MM], Section 2.

Proposition 14.1.2 (Area dissipation along a smooth closed flow).C8E6BF7840BF For a smooth mean curvature flow of a compact hypersurface without boundary,

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma _ t) =-\int _{\Sigma _ t}|\vec H|^2\, dA\leq 0. \]
Proof

Apply Proposition 7.1.2 at each time with the variation velocity $\partial _ tF$. Its normal component is $\vec H$, and its tangential component has zero inner product with $\vec H$. The first variation is therefore the negative integral of $|\vec H|^2$. $\square$

Example 14.1.3 (Shrinking round spheres).2E074E821F62 For a round $S^ n_{R(t)}\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ with outward normal, $H=n/R(t)$, so the flow equation gives $R'=-n/R$. Consequently

\[ R(t)^2=R(0)^2-2nt,\qquad 0\leq t<R(0)^2/(2n). \]

The sphere remains smooth on this interval and its radius tends to zero at its endpoint. This calculation checks the sign and time scaling of the convention; it makes no assertion about continuing an arbitrary flow through a singularity.

Remark 14.1.4 (Planned weak flows and related variational problems).D1D70B3B2945 Theorem 14.3.9 supplies the smooth existence and continuation statement, Proposition 14.3.8 gives avoidance, and Theorem 14.4.3 proves Gaussian monotonicity for a closed smooth flow. Definition 14.6.1 and Theorem 14.6.8 introduce one weak category and its compactness theorem. The distinction between weak flow and unit-regular flow is illustrated in Example 14.6.11. Tangent-flow extraction, level-set comparison, and finer singularity theory still require further development. Related variational branches include Plateau problems, free-boundary minimal surfaces, capillarity, and prescribed mean curvature.
