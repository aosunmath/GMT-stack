11.3 Volume constraints and CMC stabilityBAC2E4164020

BAC2E4164020

Stationarity and stability depend on which variations are allowed. We distinguish critical points of area minus a prescribed multiple of volume from critical points of area on a fixed-volume class. The second-variation form is the same at a CMC hypersurface, but its domain of test functions is different. Sources are [ZZCMC], equations (1.2)–(1.3) and Definition 2.1, and the smooth variation calculations in [HP99] and [ChMin], Section 23.

Definition 11.3.1 (Infinitesimal volume-preserving variations).69AD8C3A2E1D Let $\Omega $ be a smooth compact region in a Riemannian manifold, with nonempty smooth boundary $\Sigma $ and outward normal $\nu $. A normal speed $f\in C^\infty (\Sigma )$ preserves volume to first order when

\[ \int _\Sigma f\, dA=0. \]

An exactly volume-preserving variation instead satisfies $\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega _ t)=\operatorname {Vol}(\Omega )$ for all sufficiently small $t$. The integral condition is its linearization by Proposition 11.1.2. Unless stated otherwise, we constrain the total volume of the region, not a separate volume for every boundary component. Compare [ZZCMC], Introduction, with its unrestricted $\mathcal A^ c$ variations.

Proposition 11.3.2 (Realizing a zero-mean speed with an exact volume constraint).35ECF4C2F5DD Every smooth $f$ with $\int _\Sigma f=0$ is the initial normal speed of a smooth exactly volume-preserving variation of $\Omega $.

Proof

Use the normal tubular neighborhood of the compact embedded boundary. For small $(t,s)$, let $\Omega _{t,s}$ have boundary the normal graph of $tf+s$ over $\Sigma $. Its volume is a smooth function $V(t,s)$, with

\[ \partial _ tV(0,0)=\int _\Sigma f=0,\qquad \partial _ sV(0,0)=\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )>0. \]

The implicit function theorem gives a smooth $s(t)$ with $s(0)=0$ and $V(t,s(t))=V(0,0)$. Differentiating gives $s'(0)=0$, so the initial speed is $f$. Tubular coordinates and the inverse function theorem ensure that these small normal graphs bound the corresponding smoothly varied regions. This argument uses only the first-volume-variation formula. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.3 (Fixed-volume stationarity characterizes constant mean curvature).AC0FE2421EEA A smooth compact boundary $\Sigma =\partial \Omega $ is stationary for area under all exactly volume-preserving variations if and only if its outward scalar mean curvature is one constant on all of $\Sigma $.

Proof

By the preceding proposition, stationarity gives $\int _\Sigma Hf=0$ for every smooth zero-mean function. Set $\overline H=\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma )^{-1}\int _\Sigma H$ and choose $f=H-\overline H$. Then $0=\int H(H-\overline H)=\int (H-\overline H)^2$, so $H=\overline H$ everywhere. Conversely constant $H=c$ gives $\delta A=c\int f=0$ along every volume-preserving variation. This also shows why imposing only the total volume gives the same constant across different components: the admissible speeds allow exchange of volume between them. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.4 (Riemannian normal variation of mean curvature).2DBE30F27E50 Let a smooth two-sided hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold vary with normal velocity $f\nu $ at the time under consideration. With $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $,

\[ \partial _ tH=-\Delta _\Sigma f-(|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu ))f. \]

Minimality is not required. We use $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla _ X\nabla _ YZ-\nabla _ Y\nabla _ XZ-\nabla _{[X,Y]}Z$, so a sectional curvature is $\langle R(e_ i,\nu )\nu ,e_ i\rangle $. Compare [HP99], Theorem 3.2, p. 52, after replacing its inward speed by $-f$.

Proof

As in the Euclidean calculation, $\dot g_{ij}=2fh_{ij}$ and the covariant time derivative of the normal is $-\nabla f$. At a point with an orthonormal geodesic tangent frame, commute the ambient covariant derivatives in $h_{ij}=\langle \nabla _ i\nu ,e_ j\rangle $. This gives

\[ \dot h_{ij}=-\nabla _ i\nabla _ jf+fh_ i{}^ kh_{kj} -f\langle R(e_ i,\nu )\nu ,e_ j\rangle . \]

Indeed the commutator is $R(f\nu ,e_ i)\nu $, while differentiating $e_ j$ contributes $f\langle \nabla _ i\nu ,\nabla _ j\nu \rangle $. Tracing the displayed identity gives $-\Delta f+f|A|^2-f\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )$. Differentiating the inverse metric contributes $-2f|A|^2$, yielding the claim. This derives the formula independently of the second variation. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.5 (Second variation of area minus volume).0C2679767FF6 If a smooth compact boundary has $H=c$, the second derivative of $\mathcal A^ c=A-cV$ along a variation with initial normal speed $f$ is

\[ Q(f,f)=\int _\Sigma \left(|\nabla f|^2- (|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu ))f^2\right)dA. \]

Along an exactly volume-preserving variation, this is also the second derivative of area. The formula is [ZZCMC], equation (1.3), p. 6.

Proof

Differentiate $\delta \mathcal A^ c=\int (H-c)f\, dA$. At the critical hypersurface, the derivatives of the speed and the area element are multiplied by $H-c=0$. The remaining term is $\int f\dot H$. Proposition 11.3.4 and integration by parts give $Q(f,f)$. A tangential component of the initial velocity only differentiates the identically zero function $H-c$, so the result depends on the normal speed. If volume is constant along the variation, the second derivatives of $A$ and $A-cV$ coincide. $\square$

Definition 11.3.6 (Strong and volume-constrained CMC stability).067C762778A0 A closed CMC boundary is strongly stable if $Q(f,f)\geq 0$ for every smooth $f$. It is volume-constrained stable, also called weakly stable in this setting, if the inequality holds for all $f$ with $\int _\Sigma f=0$. The latter condition is equivalent to nonnegative second variation along exactly volume-preserving paths by Proposition 11.3.2. The stable $c$-hypersurfaces of [ZZCMC], Definition 2.1, use unrestricted compactly supported tests on their open domain, hence the strong notion there. Their local curvature estimates cannot be applied by silently replacing strong stability by a global volume constraint.

Proposition 11.3.7 (One scalar constraint changes index by at most one).93851119AB24 Let $\operatorname {ind}(Q)$ be the maximal dimension of a negative-definite subspace of smooth functions on a closed CMC boundary, and let $\operatorname {ind}_0(Q)$ use only zero-mean functions. Then

\[ \operatorname {ind}_0(Q)\leq \operatorname {ind}(Q) \leq \operatorname {ind}_0(Q)+1. \]

In particular volume-constrained stability implies unrestricted index at most one. The converse implication from index one to volume-constrained stability does not follow from this dimension estimate.

Proof

Every negative subspace in the constrained space is also negative in the full space. Conversely a finite-dimensional negative subspace $E$ intersects the kernel of the linear functional $f\mapsto \int f$ in dimension at least $\dim E-1$. The quadratic form remains negative on that intersection. Taking the supremum over $E$ proves the bounds; the argument also applies to unbounded index. This is linear algebra for the second-variation form, not an assertion about which eigenfunction has nonzero mean. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.8 (The sharp Poincare inequality on a round sphere).4AF6DFFAC5BC On the round sphere $S_ R^ n$, $n\geq 2$, every smooth $f$ of zero mean satisfies

\[ \int _{S_ R^ n}|\nabla f|^2\, dA\geq \frac n{R^2}\int _{S_ R^ n}f^2\, dA. \]

The constant is sharp, since restrictions of ambient linear coordinate functions attain equality.

Proof

The spectral theorem for the Laplace operator on a compact connected manifold gives a positive first eigenvalue $\lambda _1$ and a smooth zero-mean eigenfunction $u$, with $-\Delta u=\lambda _1u$. Integrate Bochner's identity and use $\operatorname {Ric}_{S_ R^ n}=(n-1)R^{-2}g$ and $|\nabla ^2u|^2\geq (\Delta u)^2/n$:

\[ 0=\int \left(|\nabla ^2u|^2-\lambda _1|\nabla u|^2 +\frac{n-1}{R^2}|\nabla u|^2\right) \geq (n-1)\left(\frac1{R^2}-\frac{\lambda _1}{n}\right) \int |\nabla u|^2. \]

Hence $\lambda _1\geq n/R^2$. Directly differentiating the coordinate functions on the sphere gives $\Delta x_ i=-nx_ i/R^2$, so equality is attained. The Rayleigh characterization of $\lambda _1$ gives the inequality for every zero-mean $f$. The compact spectral theorem and Bochner identity are the external analytic and differential-geometric inputs to this calculation. $\square$

Example 11.3.9 (A round sphere is weakly stable but strongly unstable).62447F464A27 The boundary of a Euclidean ball of radius $R$ has $H=n/R$, $|A|^2=n/R^2$, and

\[ Q(f,f)=\int _{S_ R^ n}\left(|\nabla f|^2-\frac n{R^2}f^2\right)dA. \]

For $n\geq 2$, Proposition 11.3.8 proves volume-constrained stability. The constant function gives $Q(1,1)=-nR^{-2}\operatorname {Area}(S_ R^ n)<0$, so the sphere is not strongly stable and its unrestricted index is exactly one by Proposition 11.3.7. Coordinate-function speeds have zero quadratic form; they are the normal speeds of translations. This elementary model distinguishes the two notions of stability used in CMC problems.

Proposition 11.3.10 (Strong instability under nonnegative Ricci curvature).6BAA97140F05 In an ambient manifold with $\operatorname {Ric}_ M\geq 0$, every nonempty closed two-sided CMC hypersurface with $c\ne 0$ is unstable for unrestricted $\mathcal A^ c$ variations.

Proof

The pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives $|A|^2\geq H^2/n=c^2/n$. Therefore $Q(1,1)=-\int (|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu ))<0$. The constant speed changes volume, so it is not a permitted test for volume-constrained stability. This is the CMC counterpart of Corollary 7.1.5. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.11 (Volume stability forces connectedness when the potential is positive).9C857ED1F719 Suppose a smooth compact CMC boundary is volume-constrained stable and $q=|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )>0$ on every component. Then it is connected. In particular this applies to a volume-constrained stable boundary with $c\ne 0$ in nonnegative Ricci curvature.

Proof

If there are two components $\Sigma _1,\Sigma _2$, choose a smooth function constant $a\ne 0$ on the first, constant $b$ on the second, and zero on all others, with $a\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma _1)+b\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma _2)=0$. It has zero mean and zero gradient. Positivity of $q$ gives $Q(f,f)=-\int qf^2<0$, contradicting constrained stability. The last assertion follows from $q\geq c^2/n$. The test transfers volume between components and is admissible for a total-volume constraint. $\square$

Definition 11.3.12 (Prescribed-mean-curvature energy).0874A98CACF0 For a smooth ambient function $h$ and a smooth compact region $\Omega $, put

\[ \mathcal A^ h(\Omega )=\operatorname {Area}(\partial \Omega )-\int _\Omega h\, dV. \]

The function $h$ is fixed in the ambient manifold as the region varies. The finite-perimeter local version was used in Proposition 4.3.5. Constant $h=c$ gives $\mathcal A^ c$; variable $h$ leads to a prescribed-mean-curvature equation, not generally to a fixed-volume constraint. Compare the constant case in [ZZCMC], equation (1.1).

Proposition 11.3.13 (First and second variation for prescribed mean curvature).2F067F1DC90E For the outward normal convention,

\[ \delta \mathcal A^ h(f\nu )=\int _\Sigma (H-h)f\, dA. \]

At a critical boundary $H=h|_\Sigma $, the second variation is

\[ Q_ h(f,f)=\int _\Sigma \left(|\nabla f|^2- (|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )+\partial _\nu h)f^2\right)dA. \]

The normal derivative of the ambient prescribed function is part of the potential.

Proof

The transport formula for the bulk integral gives $\frac d{dt}\int _{\Omega _ t}h=\int _{\Sigma _ t}hf$. Subtract this from the first area variation. At $H-h=0$, differentiation leaves $\int f(\dot H-f\partial _\nu h)$. Substitute Proposition 11.3.4 and integrate $-f\Delta f$ by parts. A tangential initial velocity differentiates $H-h=0$ along the hypersurface and contributes zero. The argument concerns a smooth critical boundary and supplies no regularity theorem for nonsmooth minimizers. $\square$

Proposition 11.3.14 (The CMC graph equation and oriented contact).8D4734A778BF For a graph $z=u(x)$ with upward normal, the equation $H=c$ is

\[ \operatorname {div}\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}}\right)=-c. \]

If two $C^2$ solutions with the same constant $c$ on a connected domain are ordered and have interior contact, they agree throughout the domain. In particular two smooth CMC hypersurfaces with the same mean-curvature constant and agreeing normals at a one-sided interior contact point agree locally. For the last assertion the ambient manifold may be Riemannian. Compare [ZZCMC], Lemma 2.7(i), p. 7.

Proof

The graph formula for $H$ follows from the normal convention in Proposition 14.3.13. Subtracting the two equations cancels $-c$, so the difference satisfies the divergence-form linear equation in Proposition 7.2.5, with locally uniformly elliptic coefficients. Harnack applied to the nonnegative difference gives the same open-and-closed argument as for minimal graphs. In Riemannian coordinates, the difference equation has smooth bounded lower-order terms on a smaller common graph chart; the local Harnack principle gives equality near the contact point. Agreement of the normals ensures the same signed equation. Oppositely oriented touching sheets are not covered by this conclusion. $\square$
