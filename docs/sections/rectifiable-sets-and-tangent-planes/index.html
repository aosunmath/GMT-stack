3.1 Rectifiable sets and tangent planes4F8F7AEE0F1B

4F8F7AEE0F1B

Lipschitz parametrizations are differentiable almost everywhere. Their images therefore provide the basic class of sets on which tangent-plane geometry survives. We follow [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 1, and Chapter 3, Section 1.

Definition 3.1.1 (Countable rectifiability).F69B7B515F3F An $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable set $M\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ is countably $m$-rectifiable if there are Lipschitz maps $f_ i:\mathbb {R}^ m\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ such that

\[ \mathcal{H}^ m\left(M\setminus \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }f_ i(\mathbb {R}^ m)\right)=0. \]

Local finiteness of $\mathcal{H}^ m\llcorner M$ is an additional hypothesis when needed, not part of this definition. See [Sim18], Chapter 3, Definition 1.1, p. 85; compare [WhiCho14], Section 7, pp. 33–34. In Federer's notation this is countable $(\mathcal{H}^ m,m)$ rectifiability; his “countably $m$ rectifiable” requires an exact countable union. See [Fed69], Section 3.2.14, pp. 251–252. The null exceptional set must be retained when translating between the conventions.

Theorem 3.1.2 (Rademacher).27C55CB3CC94 Let $V\subset \mathbb {R}^ m$ be open and let $f:V\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ be locally Lipschitz. Then for $\mathcal{L}^ m$-almost every $x\in V$ there is a linear map $Df(x):\mathbb {R}^ m\to \mathbb {R}^ n$ such that

\[ \lim _{h\to 0}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)-Df(x)h|}{|h|}=0. \]

See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 1.4, pp. 44–46, for the scalar theorem and the proof attributed there to Morrey. The local proof ingredients follow in this chapter.

Proof

First suppose the target is scalar and work in a neighborhood with Lipschitz constant $L$. Choose a countable dense family $v_ k$ in $S^{m-1}$. For each fixed $k$, Lemma 3.4.5 gives a full-measure set on which $D_{v_ k}f=v_ k\cdot g$, where $g$ consists of the coordinate derivatives. Intersect these sets and the full-measure set where all coordinate derivatives exist. Countability preserves full measure. At every point of this intersection, Lemma 3.4.6 proves differentiability with derivative $g$. A countable collection of Lipschitz neighborhoods covers $V$, giving the scalar result throughout $V$. Apply this result to the finitely many coordinate functions of a vector-valued map, intersect their full-measure sets, and combine the component error estimates in the Euclidean norm. This proves the stated theorem. The one-dimensional fundamental theorem for absolutely continuous functions remains the background input used in Lemma 3.4.3. $\square$

Definition 3.1.3 (Approximate tangent plane).455A7C72F775 Let $M\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable with locally finite $\mathcal{H}^ m$ measure. An $m$-dimensional linear subspace $P$ is the approximate tangent plane to $M$ at $x$ if

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}r^{-m}\int _ M\varphi \left(\frac{y-x}{r}\right)\, d\mathcal{H}^ m(y) =\int _ P\varphi \, d\mathcal{H}^ m \quad \text{for every }\varphi \in C_ c(\mathbb {R}^ n). \]

When it exists, it is unique and is denoted by $T_ xM$. This is the measure-limit convention in [Sim18], Chapter 3, Definition 1.4, pp. 86–87; it includes unit density. Uniqueness follows because the limiting measures have supports equal to their respective planes.

Theorem 3.1.4 (Tangent planes on rectifiable sets).07FB5BA849C0 Let $M\subset \mathbb {R}^ n$ be $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and satisfy $\mathcal{H}^ m(M\cap K)<\infty $ for every compact $K$. Then $M$ is countably $m$-rectifiable if and only if $T_ xM$ in Definition 3.1.3 exists at $\mathcal{H}^ m$-almost every $x\in M$. In particular, a countably $m$-rectifiable set with this local finiteness property satisfies

\[ \Theta ^ m(\mathcal{H}^ m\llcorner M,x)=1 \quad \text{for }\mathcal{H}^ m\text{-almost every }x\in M. \]

The proof is supplied below from the separately developed decomposition, differentiation and cone arguments. See [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 1.6 and Remark 1.5(3), p. 87. The density conclusion follows from the tangent-measure limit by approximating the indicator of the unit ball with continuous test functions. Local finiteness is essential to this formulation.

Proof

For $1\leq m<n$, Theorem 3.6.6 gives the forward implication and Theorem 3.7.7 gives the converse. Corollary 3.6.7 gives the density assertion. The forward argument uses the local proof of the $C^1$ decomposition, which retains Whitney extension and the Euclidean area formula as external inputs. It also uses Radon differentiation from Besicovitch selection. The converse follows from pointwise density and cone decay through uniform-scale graph pieces; it does not assume a quantitative separation between different pieces.

For $m=n$, every measurable subset of $\mathbb R^ n$ is countably $n$-rectifiable under Definition 3.1.1, since the identity map covers the whole space. Lebesgue differentiation gives density one at almost every point of $M$. At such a point, the scaled missing volume in each fixed ball tends to zero, so compactly supported test integrals converge to those of $\mathcal L^ n=\mathcal H^ n\llcorner \mathbb R^ n$. This proves the tangent and density assertions. If the zero-dimensional case is included, local finiteness of $\mathcal H^0\llcorner M$ makes $M$ finite in every bounded ball, hence countable with each point isolated. Near each of its points the measure is a single unit atom, whose dilations have tangent $\mathcal H^0\llcorner \{ 0\} $ and density one with $\omega _0=1$. Thus the endpoint cases require no cone argument. $\square$
