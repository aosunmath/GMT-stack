12.4 Recovery layers and variational limits4E08B346AEDF

4E08B346AEDF

The lower bound becomes sharp by placing the one-dimensional profile along an interface. We develop the construction behind [ChAC], Proposition 3.6, first for smooth interfaces and then for compactly contained finite-perimeter sets. The general relative-boundary approximation is recorded separately as an external input. Throughout, $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ is bounded and open and $n\geq 2$. We write $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$ for the profile of Example 12.1.3.

Proposition 12.4.1 (A flat layer and its limiting energy measure).FB823D886F0F Let $e\in \mathbb S^{n-1}$, $b\in \mathbb R$, and $u_\varepsilon (x)=q((x\cdot e-b)/\varepsilon )$ on $\mathbb R^ n$, where $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$. This is a smooth Allen–Cahn solution, and locally

\[ u_\varepsilon \to 2\chi _{\{ x\cdot e>b\} }-1,\qquad \mu _\varepsilon ^{u_\varepsilon }\rightharpoonup c_ W\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner \{ x\cdot e=b\} . \]

The phase convergence is in every finite $L^ p$ on bounded sets.

Proof

The chain rule and $q''=W'(q)$ give the equation. The phase convergence follows pointwise off the hyperplane and then by bounded convergence. Write $x=y+(b+\varepsilon t)e$ with $y\in e^\perp $. Equipartition gives energy density $\varepsilon ^{-1}(q'(t))^2$. For a continuous compactly supported test function $\zeta $, Fubini gives

\[ \int \zeta \, d\mu _\varepsilon =\int _{e^\perp }\int _{\mathbb R} \zeta (y+(b+\varepsilon t)e)(q'(t))^2\, dt\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}(y). \]

Its integrand is dominated by an integrable function using the bounded transverse projection of the support and $\int (q')^2=c_ W$. Dominated convergence proves the measure limit. The fixed translation belongs inside the scaled argument: $q((x\cdot e-b)/\varepsilon )$ keeps the interface at $x\cdot e=b$. This clarifies the rescaling in [ChAC], Section 2.3.1. $\square$

Lemma 12.4.2 (Tubular coordinates near a smooth boundary).F8A91A8CB4B3 Let $E$ be open with compact $C^2$ boundary $\Sigma =\partial E\Subset U$. For some $\rho >0$, the map $F(y,t)=y+t\nu (y)$, $y\in \Sigma $, $|t|<\rho $, is one-to-one onto a tubular neighborhood, where $\nu $ is outward. Its volume element is $J(y,t)d\mathcal H^{n-1}(y)dt$, with

\[ J(y,t)=\det (I+tD_\Sigma \nu ),\qquad J(y,0)=1, \]

and $J>0$ and $J\to 1$ uniformly as $t\to 0$. The signed distance $d$, positive in $E$, satisfies $d(F(y,t))=-t$, $|Dd|=1$, and $\Delta d|_\Sigma =-H$ for $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $.

Proof

At $t=0$, the derivative of $F$ maps tangent directions identically and the $t$ direction to $\nu $, so the inverse function theorem gives local coordinates. Compactness supplies a uniform radius. Global injectivity for a smaller radius follows by contradiction: two colliding normal segments with lengths tending to zero would have base points tending to the same boundary point, contradicting local injectivity. Nearest-point minimization shows these normal coordinates give the signed distance. Tangential differentiation of $F$ yields $I+tD_\Sigma \nu $, and its normal derivative is $\nu $; this gives the Jacobian. Positivity and uniform convergence follow by reducing $\rho $. Since $Dd=-\nu $ on the boundary and has zero normal derivative along normal segments, its divergence there is $-\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $. These are the coordinate facts used in [ChAC], p. 10. $\square$

Theorem 12.4.3 (Recovery along a smooth compact interface).A3FF775127BE Suppose $E\Subset U$ is open with $C^2$ boundary. For nonempty $E$, let $d$ be its signed distance, positive inside, and put $u_\varepsilon =q(d/\varepsilon )$ with $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$; for empty $E$, put $u_\varepsilon =-1$. Then $u_\varepsilon \in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U)$, $|u_\varepsilon |\leq 1$, and

\[ u_\varepsilon \to u_ E\text{ in every finite }L^ p(U),\qquad E_\varepsilon (u_\varepsilon ;U)\to c_ WP(E;U). \]

Moreover the energy measures converge to $c_ W\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner \partial E$ on compactly supported continuous tests.

Proof

Signed distance is Lipschitz with constant one, so the composition is Sobolev and bounded. It converges to the two phases off the measure-zero boundary. In the tube of Lemma 12.4.2, $|Dd|=1$ and the energy is

\[ \int _\Sigma \int _{-\rho /\varepsilon }^{\rho /\varepsilon } (q'(s))^2J(y,-\varepsilon s)\, ds\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}(y). \]

The bounded Jacobian and integrability of $(q')^2$ allow dominated convergence, giving $c_ W\mathcal H^{n-1}(\Sigma )$. Outside the tube, $|d|\geq \rho $. Both $(q'(d/\varepsilon ))^2$ and $W(q(d/\varepsilon ))$ are bounded by $Ce^{-c\rho /\varepsilon }$; the gradient bound $|Dd|\leq 1$ therefore makes the remaining energy at most $C|U|\varepsilon ^{-1}e^{-c\rho /\varepsilon }\to 0$. Insert a test function in the same calculation to obtain the measure limit. Bounded convergence proves all the stated phase convergences. This is the smooth-interface construction in [ChAC], Proposition 3.6, with the tail estimate retained. Empty $E$ is handled by $u_\varepsilon \equiv -1$. $\square$

Proposition 12.4.4 (The equation error of a curved recovery layer).F1ECC254AE30 In the tubular neighborhood of the preceding theorem,

\[ \varepsilon ^2\Delta u_\varepsilon -W'(u_\varepsilon ) =\varepsilon q'(d/\varepsilon )\Delta d. \]

On the interface this equals $-\varepsilon q'(0)H$. Thus a recovery sequence need not consist of critical points; even when $H=0$ on the central interface, the displayed error need not vanish away from it.

Proof

Differentiate the composition twice to get $\varepsilon ^2\Delta u_\varepsilon =q''(d/\varepsilon )|Dd|^2+\varepsilon q'(d/\varepsilon )\Delta d$. Use $|Dd|=1$, $q''=W'(q)$, and the boundary value of $\Delta d$ from Lemma 12.4.2. This explains the distinction between recovery competitors in [ChAC], Section 3.1, and constructed solutions in Section 4. $\square$

Theorem 12.4.5 (Recovery for a compactly contained finite-perimeter set).B5A69F7987FD Let $E$ agree almost everywhere with a subset of a compact set $K\Subset U$, and assume $P(E;\mathbb R^ n)<\infty $. There are $u_\varepsilon \in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U)$, $|u_\varepsilon |\leq 1$, such that

\[ u_\varepsilon \to u_ E\text{ in }L^1(U),\qquad E_\varepsilon (u_\varepsilon ;U)\to c_ WP(E;U). \]
Proof

Use the mollification and regular-level construction in Theorem 4.4.11. Since the original set is supported in $K$, the approximating sets $E_ k$ lie in shrinking neighborhoods of $K$; hence $E_ k\Subset U$ for all sufficiently large $k$. Their volumes converge in symmetric difference and their total perimeters converge to $P(E;\mathbb R^ n)=P(E;U)$.

For each $k$, Theorem 12.4.3 supplies a family $v_{k,\varepsilon }$. Choose numbers $\eta _ k\downarrow 0$ so that for $0<\varepsilon <\eta _ k$ its $L^1$ error from $u_{E_ k}$ and its energy error from $c_ WP(E_ k;U)$ are each at most $1/k$. Choose $k(\varepsilon )\to \infty $ with $\varepsilon <\eta _{k(\varepsilon )}$ and set $u_\varepsilon =v_{k(\varepsilon ),\varepsilon }$. The triangle inequality, the factor-two phase distance, and convergence of the perimeters prove the result. The empty approximation is assigned the constant minus-one family. This proves the interior case of [ChAC], Proposition 3.6 from the BV approximation already available here. $\square$

Definition 12.4.6 (Gamma convergence in the phase topology).7F23C83BEC45 Functionals $F_\varepsilon :L^1(U)\to [0,\infty ]$ Gamma-converge to $F_0$ if (i) every sequence $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1$ satisfies $F_0(u)\leq \liminf _ jF_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j)$, and (ii) for each $u$ there is a family $u_\varepsilon \to u$ in $L^1$ with $\limsup _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}F_\varepsilon (u_\varepsilon )\leq F_0(u)$. The second family is called a recovery sequence. This is the variational convergence described in [ChAC], Remark 3.7; it concerns energy values and minimization, not convergence of all critical points.

Theorem 12.4.7 (Relative recovery without prescribed boundary values).9F5A13E8887B Let $U$ be bounded with Lipschitz boundary, and let $E\subset U$ have finite perimeter relative to $U$. There exists a recovery family with $|u_\varepsilon |\leq 1$,

\[ u_\varepsilon \in H^1(U)\cap L^4(U),\quad u_\varepsilon \to 2\chi _ E-1\text{ in }L^1(U),\quad E_\varepsilon (u_\varepsilon ;U)\to c_ WP(E;U). \]

This is [ChAC], Proposition 3.6, stated on a Lipschitz domain to specify a standard relative approximation setting. The approximation of interfaces meeting $\partial U$, followed by the diagonal recovery construction, remains an external proof dependency. No trace is fixed, and no boundary energy is included in this statement.

Corollary 12.4.8 (The unconstrained perimeter Gamma limit).E301EA40DFEE On a bounded Lipschitz domain, the energies of Definition 12.2.1 Gamma-converge in $L^1(U)$ to

\[ F_0(u)= \begin{cases} c_ WP(E;U),& u=2\chi _ E-1\text{ a.e.},\ E\text{ of finite perimeter},\\ +\infty ,& \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]
Proof

Theorem 12.3.4 is the lower-bound condition. For finite $F_0$, Theorem 12.4.7 is the upper-bound condition. If $F_0(u)=+\infty $, the upper-bound condition is vacuous, and the constant family $u_\varepsilon =u$ in the extended functional domain suffices. This proves the assertion using the explicit external recovery input; it is not a proof of that approximation theorem. Fixed traces or volume constraints would define different admissible problems and require their own recovery arguments. $\square$

Proposition 12.4.9 (Limits of minimizers with a fixed local radius).D0597DBED3DE Let $U$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain and $\delta >0$. Suppose $\varepsilon _ j\downarrow 0$, $E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\leq C$, and

\[ E_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\leq E_{\varepsilon _ j}(v;U) \quad \text{whenever }\| v-u_ j\| _{L^1(U)}<\delta . \]

After a subsequence, $u_ j\to u_ E$ in $L^1(U)$, the energies converge to $c_ WP(E;U)$, and

\[ P(E;U)\leq P(F;U)\quad \text{if }|E\triangle F|<\delta /2. \]

Only non-strict local minimality is concluded, even if the approximating minima were strict. The radius $\delta $ is independent of $j$.

Proof

Compactness gives the phase limit. For a finite-perimeter competitor $F$ with $2|E\triangle F|<\delta $, choose its recovery family $v_ j$ from Theorem 12.4.7. Then $\| v_ j-u_ j\| _1\to \| u_ F-u_ E\| _1=2|E\triangle F|<\delta $, so for all large $j$ the comparison is admissible. There is a fixed positive margin below $\delta $. The lower bound for $u_ j$, the minimizing inequality, and recovery for $F$ yield $c_ WP(E;U)\leq \liminf _ jE_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\leq \limsup _ jE_{\varepsilon _ j}(u_ j;U)\leq c_ WP(F;U)$. Taking $F=E$ proves energy convergence; arbitrary $F$ proves local perimeter minimality. Infinite-perimeter competitors impose no restriction. This is the argument of [ChAC], Proposition 3.10, with the phase/indicator distance and strict comparison margin explicit, in the unconstrained Euclidean-domain setting. $\square$
