16.13 Weighted Jacobi fields on grim reapers and planes284EDC167E4D

284EDC167E4D

The grim-reaper family was constructed in Proposition 16.3.5. Here its arclength coordinate exposes the weighted stability operator and a distinction between strict positivity on compact tests and a positive spectral gap. The weight is the translator weight of [HIMW19], Proposition 3.2, with upward speed one.

Proposition 16.13.1 (Global arclength coordinates for the unit grim reaper).DA07A2F7D6E1 The curve $y=-\log \cos x$, $|x|<\pi /2$, has the global arclength parametrization

\[ X(s)=(\arctan (\sinh s),\log \cosh s),\qquad s\in \mathbb R. \]

With upward normal $\nu =(-\tanh s,\operatorname {sech}s)$, its scalar curvature is $H=-\operatorname {sech}s$. For velocity $v=e_2$, its weight and Jacobi operator are

\[ \rho _ v=\cosh s,\qquad J_ v=\frac{d^2}{ds^2}+\tanh s\frac d{ds}+\operatorname {sech}^2s. \]
Proof

The identity $\cos (\arctan (\sinh s))=\operatorname {sech}s$ places the point on the graph. Its derivative is $(\operatorname {sech}s,\tanh s)$ and has length one; the first coordinate increases onto $(-\pi /2,\pi /2)$. Differentiating the normal gives $\nu '=-\operatorname {sech}s\, X'$, proving the signed curvature. The height is $h=\log \cosh s$, whose derivative is $\tanh s$, and $|A|^2=\operatorname {sech}^2s$. Definition 14.14.1 gives the displayed operator. $\square$

Proposition 16.13.2 (The two translation fields and the weighted square-integrable kernel).8C7B738A7944 All smooth real solutions of $J_ vu=0$ on the complete unit grim reaper are

\[ u(s)=a\operatorname {sech}s+b\tanh s. \]

Both basis functions are bounded, but the weighted square-integrable kernel is exactly the span of $w=\operatorname {sech}s$, with

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}w^2\rho _ v\, ds=\pi . \]
Proof

The two functions, up to sign, are the normal components of vertical and horizontal translations. Proposition 14.14.4 therefore makes them solutions; substitution in the displayed operator also verifies this directly. At $s=0$ their initial data are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, so they form a basis of the two-dimensional solution space of the smooth second-order ODE. Their boundedness is immediate. If $b\ne 0$, the function has a nonzero limit at either infinite end while $\rho _ v=\cosh s$ grows exponentially, so it is not weighted square-integrable. For $w$, the integrand is $\operatorname {sech}s$. Since $(\arctan (\sinh s))'=\operatorname {sech}s$, its integral is $\pi $. $\square$

Proposition 16.13.3 (Strict compact stability with zero spectral bottom).7B9E16310EBB For every nonzero $\phi \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$ the grim-reaper form satisfies

\[ Q_ v(\phi )=\int _{\mathbb R}\operatorname {sech}s\, |(\phi \cosh s)'|^2ds>0. \]

Nevertheless its variational spectral bottom is

\[ \inf _{0\ne \phi \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)} \frac{Q_ v(\phi )}{\int \phi ^2\cosh s\, ds}=0. \]
Proof

Insert $w=\operatorname {sech}s$ in Proposition 14.14.6 to get the formula. Equality would make $\phi \cosh s$ constant; compact support forces that constant to be zero. To compute the infimum, choose a fixed smooth cutoff $\chi $ equal to one on $[-1,1]$, supported in $[-2,2]$, and put $\phi _ R=w\chi (s/R)$. Then

\[ Q_ v(\phi _ R)=R^{-2}\int \operatorname {sech}s\, |\chi '(s/R)|^2ds \le R^{-2}\| \chi '\| _\infty ^2\pi , \]

while $\int \phi _ R^2\rho _ v\to \pi $ by dominated convergence. Stability gives the opposite bound zero. The null function $w$ in the weighted square-integrable kernel is not compactly supported, so there is no contradiction with strict positivity on compact tests. $\square$

Proposition 16.13.4 (A one-dimensional Schrödinger form).752E1E7B14D3 The substitution $\psi =\sqrt{\cosh s}\, \phi $ preserves the weighted $L^2$ norm and transforms the grim-reaper quadratic form into

\[ Q_ v(\phi )=\int _{\mathbb R}\left[|\psi '|^2+ \left(\frac14-\frac34\operatorname {sech}^2s\right)\psi ^2\right]ds \]

for compactly supported smooth real tests. The weighted null field $\operatorname {sech}s$ becomes $\operatorname {sech}^{1/2}s$.

Proof

Writing $h=\log \cosh s$ gives $\phi =e^{-h/2}\psi $. Thus the weighted derivative energy is $\int (\psi '-h'\psi /2)^2$. Integrate the cross term by parts; its contribution is $h''\psi ^2/2$. The resulting potential for the full quadratic form is $h''/2+(h')^2/4-|A|^2$. Substituting $h''=\operatorname {sech}^2s$, $(h')^2=1-\operatorname {sech}^2s$ and $|A|^2=\operatorname {sech}^2s$ gives the formula. The norm identity and the transformed null field follow immediately from the substitution. No complete spectral resolution is asserted. $\square$

Proposition 16.13.5 (Grim cylinders have a different square-integrable kernel).01561F6C5365 Let $M$ be the product of the complete unit grim reaper with $\mathbb R^ k$, $k\ge 1$, translating in its grim-reaper plane. It is weighted stable and has variational spectral bottom zero, but it has no nonzero smooth Jacobi field in $L^2(\rho _ vdA)$.

Proof

The metric is $ds^2+|dy|^2$, the weight is $\cosh s$, and the operator is the one in Proposition 16.13.1 plus $\Delta _ y$. The function $w(s,y)=\operatorname {sech}s$ is a positive Jacobi field, proving stability. It is not weighted square-integrable because its squared integral in $s$ is $\pi $ and the Euclidean factor has infinite volume. Lemma 14.14.10 therefore gives the zero kernel.

For the spectral bottom, multiply the cutoff tests of Proposition 16.13.3 by $\eta (y/R)$, with nonzero smooth compactly supported $\eta $ on $\mathbb R^ k$. Separation of variables makes the Rayleigh quotient the sum of the grim-reaper quotient and $R^{-2}\int |\nabla \eta |^2/\int \eta ^2$. Both tend to zero. This example has zero infimum without a nonzero weighted square-integrable null field; it does not require an assertion about the entire spectrum. $\square$

Example 16.13.6 (A translating plane has a positive weighted gradient gap).84BE9F6FEFFB Let $P\cong \mathbb R^ n$ be an affine hyperplane and let $0\ne v$ be tangent to it. The image is stationary under translation by $v$, but its translator-weighted form has variational spectral bottom $|v|^2/4$. Its induced Euclidean metric is complete and its induced conformal translator metric is incomplete.

Proof

Since $A=0$, the Jacobi form equals the weighted gradient energy. For $\psi =e^{\langle x,v\rangle /2}\phi $, integration of the compactly supported cross term gives

\[ Q_ v(\phi )=\int _ P\left(|\nabla \psi |^2+\frac{|v|^2}{4}\psi ^2\right)dA, \qquad \int _ P\phi ^2\rho _ v\, dA=\int _ P\psi ^2dA. \]

The lower bound is immediate. Taking $\psi _ R(x)=\eta ((x-p)/R)$ for a fixed nonzero compactly supported smooth $\eta $ makes its gradient Rayleigh quotient tend to zero, proving sharpness. Euclidean completeness is that of an affine plane. The ray $p-tv/|v|$ stays in $P$ and has finite conformal length by Proposition 14.15.1, proving the final assertion. A tangential velocity changes this weighted functional even though it does not change the plane's image under the flow. $\square$
