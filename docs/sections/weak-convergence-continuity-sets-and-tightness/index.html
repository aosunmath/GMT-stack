2.4 Weak convergence, continuity sets, and tightness5BDE435DF2F3

5BDE435DF2F3

We now supply the compactness argument used throughout GMT and explain when weak convergence controls masses. All measures are nonnegative Radon measures on an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ unless otherwise specified. The main source is [Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 5, especially Theorem 5.15, pp. 38–39. The countable testing argument below uses the Euclidean setting explicitly. Examples 16.1.3 and 16.1.4 explain why the qualifications are needed.

Lemma 2.4.1 (A countable family of compactly supported tests).EBB2048864FA There are compact sets $K_ m\subset \operatorname {int}K_{m+1}\subset U$ exhausting $U$, and a countable family $\mathcal D\subset C_ c(U)$, such that every $f\in C_ c(U)$ supported in $K_ m$ is a uniform limit of members of $\mathcal D$ supported in $K_{m+1}$.

Proof

One may take $K_ m=\{ x\in U:|x|\leq m,\ \operatorname {dist}(x,\mathbb R^ n\setminus U)\geq 1/m\} $, with distance to the empty set interpreted as infinity. These are compact, lie in the interior of the next set, and their interiors cover $U$. Choose fixed cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ m\leq 1$, equal to one on $K_ m$, with support in $\operatorname {int}K_{m+1}$, using Lemma 2.3.2.

There is a countable family $\mathcal P$ of compactly supported continuous functions on $\mathbb R^ n$ dense for the uniform norm in $C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$: use piecewise multilinear interpolation on dyadic grids, with finitely many nonzero rational vertex values. Uniform continuity controls interpolation error on sufficiently small cubes; approximation of the finitely many vertex values by rationals adds arbitrarily small uniform error. A function in $C_ c(U)$ extends continuously by zero to $\mathbb R^ n$. If its support is in $K_ m$, uniform approximants $q_ j\in \mathcal P$ give $\eta _ mq_ j\to \eta _ mf=f$, with all supports in $K_{m+1}$. The countable union $\mathcal D=\bigcup _ m\eta _ m\mathcal P$ has the asserted property. Empty initial compact sets cause no difficulty. $\square$

Lemma 2.4.2 (Diagonal extraction of a positive limit functional).B9EF3BFBC186 Suppose $\sup _ j\mu _ j(K)<\infty $ for every compact $K\subset U$. There are a subsequence $\mu _{j_ k}$ and a positive linear functional $L:C_ c(U)\to \mathbb R$ with

\[ \int f\, d\mu _{j_ k}\longrightarrow L(f)\qquad (f\in C_ c(U)). \]
Proof

Enumerate the family $\mathcal D$ from Lemma 2.4.1. For each of its functions the integrals form a bounded real sequence, because its support is compact. Successive subsequences and the diagonal construction give simultaneous convergence for every member of $\mathcal D$. For arbitrary $f$ supported in $K_ m$, approximate it uniformly by $g\in \mathcal D$ supported in $K_{m+1}$. If $M_ m=\sup _ j\mu _ j(K_{m+1})$, then

\[ \left|\int f\, d\mu _{j_ k}-\int f\, d\mu _{j_\ell }\right| \leq 2M_ m\| f-g\| _\infty + \left|\int g\, d\mu _{j_ k}-\int g\, d\mu _{j_\ell }\right|. \]

First make the uniform error small and then take $k,\ell $ large. Thus the integrals of $f$ are Cauchy and define a finite limit $L(f)$. Limits preserve real linear combinations and nonnegativity. This proves the assertion. Sequential extraction here is justified by the countable tests, not merely by a compactness assertion in a possibly nonmetrizable dual space. $\square$

Proposition 2.4.3 (Upper semicontinuity on compact sets).5E1115A68860 If $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \mu $ in $U$, then

\[ \limsup _ j\mu _ j(K)\leq \mu (K) \]

for every compact $K\subset U$.

Proof

By outer regularity and local finiteness, choose an open neighborhood $O$ of $K$ with compact closure in $U$ and $\mu (O\setminus K)<\varepsilon $. A cutoff $0\leq \eta \leq 1$ supported in $O$ and equal to one on $K$ gives

\[ \limsup _ j\mu _ j(K)\leq \lim _ j\int \eta \, d\mu _ j =\int \eta \, d\mu \leq \mu (O)\leq \mu (K)+\varepsilon . \]

Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Compactness both permits the cutoff and makes all these integrals finite. Compare the open-set inequality in Proposition 2.1.4. $\square$

Corollary 2.4.4 (Convergence of masses on continuity sets).DDCA164F3208 If $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \mu $ in $U$ and $E\subset U$ is Borel with compact closure in $U$ and $\mu (\partial E)=0$, then $\mu _ j(E)\to \mu (E)$. In particular, for a fixed center $x\in U$, this holds for $E=B_ r(x)$ at all but countably many radii with $\overline B_ r(x)\subset U$.

Proof

Proposition 2.1.4 and Proposition 2.4.3 give

\[ \mu (\operatorname {int}E)\leq \liminf _ j\mu _ j(E) \leq \limsup _ j\mu _ j(E)\leq \mu (\overline E). \]

The boundary hypothesis makes both endpoints equal to $\mu (E)$. For the ball statement, distinct centered spheres are disjoint. Inside any fixed compact ball of finite measure, only finitely many of them can have mass at least $1/k$ for a fixed positive integer $k$. Taking the union over $k$ and over a countable exhaustion of the admissible radii gives at most countably many exceptional spheres. $\square$

Definition 2.4.5 (Uniform tightness and bounded-continuous testing).9FBD8CC6ACC8 A sequence of finite Radon measures $\mu _ j$ on $U$ is uniformly tight if, for every $\varepsilon >0$, some compact $K\subset U$ satisfies $\sup _ j\mu _ j(U\setminus K)<\varepsilon $. Convergence against all $f\in C_ b(U)$, the bounded continuous functions, is called narrow convergence here. It is stronger than the compact-test convergence in Definition 2.1.2; the two agree on uniformly tight families with uniformly bounded total mass, as proved next.

Theorem 2.4.6 (Tightness upgrades compact tests to bounded tests).51E7B8F93AB4 Suppose $\mu _ j\rightharpoonup \mu $ in $U$, $\sup _ j\mu _ j(U)<\infty $, and the sequence is uniformly tight. Then $\mu $ is finite and $\mu _ j$ converge narrowly to $\mu $. In particular $\mu _ j(U)\to \mu (U)$; if all $\mu _ j$ are probability measures, so is $\mu $. The compact-test convention is that of [Sim18], Theorem 5.15; the additional tightness hypothesis here controls the tails, by the argument below.

Proof

The open-set lower-semicontinuity inequality gives $\mu (U)\leq \liminf _ j\mu _ j(U)<\infty $. Given $\varepsilon >0$, tightness supplies a compact set controlling all $\mu _ j$ tails; inner regularity of the finite measure $\mu $ supplies another compact set controlling its tail. Their union $K$ controls both. Choose $0\leq \eta \leq 1$ compactly supported and equal to one on $K$. For $f\in C_ b(U)$, the test $\eta f$ is compactly supported, while

\[ \left|\int (1-\eta )f\, d\mu _ j\right| +\left|\int (1-\eta )f\, d\mu \right|\leq 2\varepsilon \| f\| _\infty . \]

Take the limit in the compactly supported part and then let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. The constant test $f=1$ gives convergence of total masses. In contrast, the escaping measures in Example 16.1.3 are not uniformly tight. $\square$

Corollary 2.4.7 (Sequential compactness for tight probability measures).E6622953C8DE Every uniformly tight sequence of Radon probability measures on $U$ has a narrowly convergent subsequence whose limit is a Radon probability measure. Conversely, a narrowly convergent sequence of Radon probability measures is uniformly tight.

Proof

The local bounds required by Theorem 2.1.3 hold with upper bound one. Extract a compact-test limit and apply Theorem 2.4.6.

Conversely, suppose $\mu _ j$ converge narrowly to the probability measure $\mu $. Choose compact $K$ with $\mu (K)>1-\varepsilon $ and a cutoff $0\leq \eta \leq 1$ equal to one on $K$, with compact support $L$. For all sufficiently large $j$, $\int \eta \, d\mu _ j>1-2\varepsilon $, so $\mu _ j(U\setminus L)<2\varepsilon $. Each of the finitely many remaining probability measures has a compact set carrying all but $2\varepsilon $ of its mass by inner regularity. Adding those sets to $L$ gives a single compact set for the whole sequence. Letting the initial $\varepsilon $ be arbitrarily small proves tightness. $\square$

Proposition 2.4.8 (Smooth densities approximate any Radon measure locally).6D1B7D98305E Let $\mu $ be a Radon measure on $\mathbb R^ n$ and $0\leq \rho \in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$ with $\int \rho =1$. For $\varepsilon >0$ set

\[ f_\varepsilon (x)=\int \varepsilon ^{-n}\rho ((x-y)/\varepsilon )\, d\mu (y), \qquad d\mu _\varepsilon =f_\varepsilon \, dx. \]

Then $f_\varepsilon $ is nonnegative and smooth, $\mu _\varepsilon $ is Radon, and $\mu _\varepsilon \rightharpoonup \mu $ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Finite total mass of $\mu $ is not required.

Proof

On a fixed compact set of $x$ values, every contributing $y$ lies in a fixed larger compact set. Local finiteness bounds the integrals of every derivative of the smooth kernel there, justifying differentiation under the integral and proving smoothness. For $\varphi \in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$, Fubini and a change of variables give

\[ \int \varphi \, d\mu _\varepsilon =\int \left(\int \varphi (y+\varepsilon z)\rho (z)\, dz\right)d\mu (y). \]

The inner function converges uniformly to $\varphi (y)$ by uniform continuity. For $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$, all these functions have support in the compact one-neighborhood of $\operatorname {spt}\varphi $, whose $\mu $ mass is finite. This proves convergence. Smooth locally bounded densities define Radon measures. The conclusion generalizes the concentration example and the diffuse Cantor approximations in Examples 16.1.2 and 16.1.12. $\square$

Proposition 2.4.9 (Finite atomic measures also approximate locally).EDC3E29B3DCC Every Radon measure $\mu $ on $\mathbb R^ n$ is a compact-test limit of finite nonnegative atomic measures. Consequently compact-test convergence alone preserves neither absolute continuity nor atomicity.

Proof

Partition the half-open cube $[-j,j)^ n$ into finitely many half-open cubes $Q$ of side $1/j$, and choose each cube center $x_ Q$. Define $\nu _ j=\sum _ Q\mu (Q)\delta _{x_ Q}$; local finiteness makes this a finite measure. For a test $\varphi $ supported in a compact $K$, sufficiently large outer cubes contain the compact one-neighborhood of $K$. Cubes contributing either to $\varphi (x_ Q)$ or to its $\mu $ integral lie in that neighborhood once $\sqrt n/j<1$. Thus

\[ \left|\int \varphi \, d\nu _ j-\int \varphi \, d\mu \right| \leq \mu (K_1)\, \omega _\varphi (\sqrt n/j), \]

where $K_1=\{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,K)\leq 1\} $ and $\omega _\varphi $ is a modulus of continuity. This tends to zero. Taking Lebesgue measure as the limit gives non-atomic limits of atomic measures; Proposition 2.4.8 gives smooth-density approximations of singular limits. $\square$
