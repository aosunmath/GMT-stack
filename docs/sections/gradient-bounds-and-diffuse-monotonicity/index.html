12.5 Gradient bounds and diffuse monotonicity1DC232E7975B

1DC232E7975B

The energy inequality for arbitrary competitors in Lemma 12.2.8 differs from the pointwise gradient estimate for entire solutions. We now prove the latter and derive the exact energy-ratio identity. The source is [ChAC], Appendix B, Problem 1, pp. 27–28. We use $W(s)=(1-s^2)^2/4$ and initially set $\varepsilon =1$.

Lemma 12.5.1 (Uniform estimates for bounded entire solutions).43AE37D12C79 If $u\in C^2(\mathbb R^ n)$, $|u|\leq 1$, and $\Delta u=W'(u)$, then $u$ is smooth. For every integer $k\geq 1$, $\sup _{\mathbb R^ n}|D^ ku|\leq C(n,k)$. Translates of any such sequence of solutions have a subsequence converging smoothly on compact sets to another solution with values in $[-1,1]$.

Proof

On each ball of radius two, both $u$ and $W'(u)$ are uniformly bounded. The interior Laplacian estimate of Theorem 7.5.9, with $p>n$, gives a uniform $W^{2,p}$ bound on a smaller ball. Sobolev embedding gives a uniform $C^{1,\alpha }$ bound for some $\alpha >0$. The polynomial right-hand side then has controlled Hölder derivatives, and Theorem 7.5.8 and its higher-order versions bootstrap to every order on smaller balls. Translation invariance makes the constants independent of the center. Arzelà–Ascoli and a diagonal exhaustion give the asserted compactness. For $n=1$ the same estimates follow directly from $u''=W'(u)$ and boundedness on intervals. This is the elliptic input in [ChAC], Exercise 5.6 and Problem 1(d); the underlying elliptic estimates remain the previously recorded external inputs. $\square$

Lemma 12.5.2 (A bounded function has arbitrarily small gradient somewhere).4E4A8609F931 If $v\in C^2(\mathbb R^ n)$ is bounded, then $\inf _{\mathbb R^ n}|Dv|=0$.

Proof

Otherwise $|Dv|\geq a>0$. The vector field $X=Dv/|Dv|$ is continuously differentiable and bounded. Its integral curves exist for all forward time: bounded speed prevents escape to infinity in finite time, and local ODE continuation applies on compact sets. Along a curve $\gamma $, $\frac d{dt}v(\gamma (t))=|Dv(\gamma (t))|\geq a$, contradicting boundedness. This proves the elementary step in [ChAC], Problem 1(a). $\square$

Lemma 12.5.3 (The differential inequality for the gradient defect).21D87AED7971 For a smooth solution of $\Delta u=W'(u)$, put $P=|Du|^2/2-W(u)$. On the open set $\{ |Du|>0\} $,

\[ \Delta P-\frac{2W'(u)}{|Du|^2}Du\cdot DP \geq \frac{|DP|^2}{|Du|^2}\geq 0. \]
Proof

Differentiation and the Euclidean Bochner identity give $\Delta P=|D^2u|^2-(W'(u))^2$ and $DP=D^2u\, Du-W'(u)Du$. Hence

\[ |D^2u|^2\geq \frac{|D^2u\, Du|^2}{|Du|^2} =\frac{|DP|^2+2W'(u)Du\cdot DP}{|Du|^2}+(W'(u))^2. \]

Subtract the last square. This is [ChAC], Problem 1(b), with $P$ equal to one-half of the defect used there. The drift coefficients are locally bounded where the gradient is nonzero. $\square$

Theorem 12.5.4 (Modica's pointwise gradient estimate).667E950C291A Every $C^2$ entire solution $u:\mathbb R^ n\to [-1,1]$ of $\Delta u=W'(u)$ satisfies

\[ \frac12|Du|^2\leq W(u). \]
Proof

By Lemma 12.5.1, $S=\sup P$ is finite. Suppose $S>0$, choose $x_ j$ with $P(x_ j)\to S$, and pass to a smooth local limit $v$ of $u(x+x_ j)$. Then $P_ v\leq S$ everywhere and $P_ v(0)=S$. In particular $Dv(0)\ne 0$.

On the connected component $O$ of $\{ |Dv|>0\} $ containing zero, Lemma 12.5.3 and the strong maximum principle make $P_ v$ constant, equal to $S$. The principle applies locally to the elliptic operator with the displayed drift and no zero-order term; connectedness propagates constancy through $O$. On this component $|Dv|^2=2(W(v)+S)\geq 2S$. A finite boundary point of $O$ would retain this lower bound by continuity and would still lie in the same open regular component, a contradiction. Thus $O$ is both open and closed in $\mathbb R^ n$, so it is all of $\mathbb R^ n$. This contradicts Lemma 12.5.2. Therefore $S\leq 0$. This supplies the attained and unattained supremum arguments requested in [ChAC], Problem 1(c)–(d). $\square$

Corollary 12.5.5 (Nonpositive discrepancy at arbitrary diffuse scale).4EB15D8DC6CD If $u:\mathbb R^ n\to [-1,1]$ solves $\varepsilon ^2\Delta u=W'(u)$, then

\[ \frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2-\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon \leq 0. \]

Thus the discrepancy measure of Definition 12.2.10 is nonpositive.

Proof

Apply Theorem 12.5.4 to $v(y)=u(\varepsilon y)$ and divide the resulting inequality by $\varepsilon $. The entire-domain hypothesis is retained by this rescaling. $\square$

Example 12.5.6 (A local solution need not satisfy Modica's bound).46CB1D1432FA The initial-value problem $u''=u^3-u$, $u(0)=0$, $u'(0)=2$ has a smooth solution on a neighborhood of zero, with $|u|<1$ after restricting the interval. At zero its defect is $P(0)=2-1/4=7/4>0$. Thus boundedness on a small domain and the Euler equation alone do not imply Theorem 12.5.4. This also distinguishes the pointwise estimate from the energy inequality, which remains valid for this function.

Theorem 12.5.7 (Rigidity in the pointwise gradient estimate).F85802FCEEC5 Under the hypotheses of Theorem 12.5.4, equality at one point holds if and only if either $u\equiv \pm 1$ or

\[ u(x)=\tanh \left(\frac{e\cdot x-b}{\sqrt2}\right) \quad \text{for some }e\in \mathbb S^{n-1},\ b\in \mathbb R. \]
Proof

By the ordered contact principle of Lemma 12.7.2, a nonconstant solution has $-1<u<1$ everywhere. At an equality point $P=0$, $W(u)>0$ and hence $Du\ne 0$. The strong maximum principle used above makes $P=0$ on that regular component. A finite boundary point of the component would have $Du=0$ and $P=0$, forcing $W(u)=0$, impossible for this nonconstant solution. Thus $P=0$ throughout space.

Let $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$ and $f=q^{-1}(u)$. Since $q'>0$ and $(q')^2=2W(q)$, the equality gives $|Df|=1$. The equation for $u=q(f)$ gives $q'\Delta f=0$, so $f$ is harmonic. Bochner's identity now gives $0=\Delta |Df|^2=2|D^2f|^2$. Therefore $f=e\cdot x-b$ with $|e|=1$. Conversely the displayed layers and constant wells satisfy equality directly. This proves [ChAC], Problem 1(e), including the constant-well alternatives. $\square$

Definition 12.5.8 (The diffuse stress-energy tensor).D34F2C0DB289 For a smooth function on a Euclidean open set, put

\[ e_\varepsilon =\frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2+\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon , \qquad T_{ij}=e_\varepsilon \delta _{ij}-\varepsilon D_ i uD_ j u. \]

This tensor records the response of the energy to changes of the independent variable. We use $(\operatorname {div}T)_ j=\sum _ iD_ iT_{ij}$ and $T:DX=\sum _{i,j}T_{ij}D_ iX_ j$. It provides the integration-by-parts identity behind [ChAC], Problem 1(f).

Proposition 12.5.9 (Stress-energy conservation).A8022BB0F3F8 For a smooth solution of $\varepsilon ^2\Delta u=W'(u)$ on $U$, the tensor in Definition 12.5.8 satisfies

\[ \operatorname {div}T=0,\qquad \int _ U T:DX=0\quad (X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ n)). \]
Proof

Differentiate the two terms in $T$. The mixed Hessian terms cancel and leave $(\operatorname {div}T)_ j=(\varepsilon ^{-1}W'(u)-\varepsilon \Delta u)D_ j u=0$. Integration by parts gives the test-field identity. Compact support removes the boundary term. The conclusion uses the Euler equation, not just an energy bound. $\square$

Theorem 12.5.10 (The exact diffuse energy-ratio identity).E77581190B89 Let $u$ be a smooth Allen–Cahn solution with parameter $\varepsilon >0$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{B_ R(a)}$. For $0<r<R$, write $E(r)=E_\varepsilon (u;B_ r(a))$. Then

\[ \frac d{dr}\bigl (r^{1-n}E(r)\bigr ) =r^{-n}\int _{B_ r(a)}\left(\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon -\frac\varepsilon 2|Du|^2\right)dx +\varepsilon r^{1-n}\int _{\partial B_ r(a)}(\partial _\nu u)^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}. \]
Proof

Translate $a$ to zero. Conservation of $T$ implies $\operatorname {div}(Tx)=\operatorname {tr}T=ne_\varepsilon -\varepsilon |Du|^2$. On the sphere, $x=r\nu $, so $(Tx)\cdot \nu =r(e_\varepsilon -\varepsilon (\partial _\nu u)^2)$. The divergence theorem and $E'(r)=\int _{\partial B_ r}e_\varepsilon $ give

\[ rE'(r)=(n-2)E(r)+2\int _{B_ r}\frac{W(u)}\varepsilon +\varepsilon r\int _{\partial B_ r}(\partial _\nu u)^2. \]

Differentiate $r^{1-n}E(r)$ and combine terms to obtain the formula. Smoothness justifies it for every $r>0$ under consideration. This derives [ChAC], Problem 1(f), with the bulk term equal to minus the discrepancy density in Definition 12.2.10; no extra factor two occurs. $\square$

Corollary 12.5.11 (Monotonicity and area-order energy growth).52022BB1B0F4 For an entire solution $u:\mathbb R^ n\to [-1,1]$ with fixed $\varepsilon >0$, the function $r^{1-n}E_\varepsilon (u;B_ r(a))$ is nondecreasing for every center $a$. In particular, for $R\geq r>0$,

\[ E_\varepsilon (u;B_ R(a))\geq (R/r)^{n-1}E_\varepsilon (u;B_ r(a)). \]
Proof

Corollary 12.5.5 makes the bulk term in Theorem 12.5.10 nonnegative; the boundary term is a square. Integrate the derivative inequality. This is a diffuse counterpart of stationary area-ratio monotonicity, with an additional discrepancy term. $\square$

Corollary 12.5.12 (Finite total energy in dimension at least two).4D27FAB15F2F If $n\geq 2$ and an entire solution $u:\mathbb R^ n\to [-1,1]$ has finite total energy $\int _{\mathbb R^ n}(\varepsilon |Du|^2/2+W(u)/\varepsilon )dx$ for one fixed $\varepsilon >0$, then $u\equiv +1$ or $u\equiv -1$.

Proof

If any ball had positive energy, the preceding growth bound would make the total energy infinite as $R\to \infty $, since $n-1>0$. Hence every ball has zero energy, and Proposition 12.2.4 gives a constant well. The dimension restriction matters: the one-dimensional heteroclinics have finite energy by Theorem 12.2.6. $\square$

Example 12.5.13 (Energy ratios of a flat transition layer).55D0C917348E Let $n\geq 2$ and $u(x)=q(x_ n/\varepsilon )$, with $q(t)=\tanh (t/\sqrt2)$ and fixed $\varepsilon >0$. Then

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}r^{1-n}E_\varepsilon (u;B_ r)=0, \qquad \lim _{r\to \infty }r^{1-n}E_\varepsilon (u;B_ r)=c_ W\omega _{n-1}. \]

The first limit follows because the smooth density is bounded near zero and the ball volume is of order $r^ n$. Slicing in $x_ n=t$ writes the ratio as

\[ \omega _{n-1}\int _{-r}^{r}\varepsilon ^{-1}(q'(t/\varepsilon ))^2 (1-t^2/r^2)^{(n-1)/2}\, dt. \]

Dominated convergence and the total profile energy $c_ W$ give the second limit. The ratio therefore is not constant at the diffuse scale, even for a flat layer. This computes [ChAC], Problem 1(g).
