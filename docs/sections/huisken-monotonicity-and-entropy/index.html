14.4 Huisken monotonicity and entropyA375AE5FEE73

A375AE5FEE73

The Gaussian integral measures area near a spacetime point at the parabolic scale. Its dissipation identifies self-similar models, while taking the supremum over centers and scales gives entropy. We first prove the identities for smooth closed Euclidean flows, so every integration by parts has no boundary term. Noncompact and weak formulations need additional hypotheses and are stated separately. The main source is [Hui90], Section 3, with the normalization converted to that of Definition 14.2.2.

Proposition 14.4.1 (Transport of a test function along a closed flow).C7F71E4E8F61 Let $\Sigma _ t$ be a smooth closed mean curvature flow in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, and let $\phi (x,t)$ be smooth in an ambient neighborhood of its spacetime track. Then

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\int _{\Sigma _ t}\phi \, d\mu _ t =\int _{\Sigma _ t}\left(\partial _ t\phi +\nabla \phi \cdot \vec H-\phi |\vec H|^2\right)d\mu _ t. \]

Here $\partial _ t\phi $ is the ambient partial derivative, with the spatial point held fixed.

Proof

Choose a normal parametrization. The chain rule gives $\frac{d}{dt}\phi (F(p,t),t)=\partial _ t\phi +\nabla \phi \cdot \vec H$, and Proposition 14.3.2 gives $\partial _ t d\mu =-|\vec H|^2d\mu $. Adding the two contributions proves the formula. A tangential velocity adds a tangential divergence, whose integral vanishes on a closed hypersurface. Compare [WhiMCF15], Sections 6 and 9. $\square$

Definition 14.4.2 (Backward Gaussian kernel).E0C369653A50 For a spacetime center $(x_0,t_0)$ and $t<t_0$, define

\[ \rho _{x_0,t_0}(x,t)=(4\pi (t_0-t))^{-n/2} \exp \left(-\frac{|x-x_0|^2}{4(t_0-t)}\right). \]

The normalization uses the dimension $n$ of the evolving hypersurface, not the ambient dimension $n+1$. In particular its integral over an affine $n$-plane through $x_0$ is one. See [Hui90], p. 288.

Theorem 14.4.3 (Huisken's monotonicity identity for closed smooth flows).09F92AB39467 For a smooth closed Euclidean mean curvature flow and a fixed $(x_0,t_0)$, at every flow time $t<t_0$,

\[ \frac{d}{dt}\int _{\Sigma _ t}\rho _{x_0,t_0}\, d\mu _ t =-\int _{\Sigma _ t}\left|\vec H+ \frac{(x-x_0)^\perp }{2(t_0-t)}\right|^2\rho _{x_0,t_0}\, d\mu _ t. \]

This is [Hui90], Theorem 3.1, pp. 288–289. The square uses the vector mean curvature, so the sign agrees with $\vec H=-H\nu $.

Proof

Write $y=x-x_0$, $\tau =t_0-t$, and $\rho =\rho _{x_0,t_0}$. Direct differentiation gives

\[ \partial _ t\rho =\rho \left(\frac n{2\tau }-\frac{|y|^2}{4\tau ^2}\right), \qquad \nabla \rho =-\frac{y}{2\tau }\rho . \]

The integrand in Proposition 14.4.1 is consequently

\[ \rho \left(\frac n{2\tau }-\frac{|y|^2}{4\tau ^2} -\frac{\langle y,\vec H\rangle }{2\tau }-|\vec H|^2\right). \]

Since $\operatorname {div}_{\Sigma _ t}y^ T=n+\langle y,\vec H\rangle $, the product rule gives

\[ \operatorname {div}_{\Sigma _ t}\left(\rho \frac{y^ T}{2\tau }\right) =\rho \left(\frac n{2\tau }+\frac{\langle y,\vec H\rangle }{2\tau } -\frac{|y^ T|^2}{4\tau ^2}\right). \]

Subtract this divergence from the previous integrand. The remaining expression is $-\rho |\vec H+y^\perp /(2\tau )|^2$. The divergence integrates to zero because the time slice is compact and without boundary. $\square$

Corollary 14.4.4 (Gaussian density at an endpoint).EDEB2CF33691 Suppose a smooth closed flow is defined on $[a,t_0)$. For every $x_0\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ the finite limit

\[ \Theta (\mathcal M,x_0,t_0)= \lim _{t\uparrow t_0}\int _{\Sigma _ t}\rho _{x_0,t_0}\, d\mu _ t \]

exists and is nonnegative. Equivalently, the Gaussian ratio at scale $r$ is the integral on $\Sigma _{t_0-r^2}$ with variance $r^2$, and these ratios are nondecreasing as $r$ increases within the available time interval.

Proof

Theorem 14.4.3 makes the integral nonincreasing in time and bounded below by zero. At any fixed earlier time it is finite because the time slice is compact. This yields a finite one-sided limit even when the flow is singular at $t_0$. Replacing $t$ by $t_0-r^2$ reverses the order of the parameter. This is the smooth-flow density construction used in [WhiMCF15], Section 10.2. $\square$

Proposition 14.4.5 (Gaussian density at a smooth embedded point).EB49673FA9F0 If a closed embedded flow is smooth through time $t_0$ and $x_0\in \Sigma _{t_0}$, then $\Theta (\mathcal M,x_0,t_0)=1$. If $x_0\notin \Sigma _{t_0}$, the density is zero. These assertions concern a single embedded sheet with unit multiplicity.

Proof

Near a point on $\Sigma _{t_0}$, use a smooth graphical representation over its tangent plane. At time $t_0-r^2$, dilating by $r^{-1}$ about $x_0$ makes the graph converge smoothly on each bounded set to that plane: the spatial graph error is quadratic and the time displacement is $O(r^2)$. Its Gaussian integral converges to the plane integral, one. On the fixed graphical neighborhood the rescaled area elements are uniformly bounded and the Gaussian tails are uniformly integrable. Outside that neighborhood a fixed positive distance from $x_0$ gives an exponentially small term, since the total areas stay bounded near $t_0$. If $x_0$ is off the final slice, compactness and smooth dependence in time give a positive distance from all nearby slices, so the entire integral tends to zero. Compare [WhiMCF15], Section 10.2. $\square$

Definition 14.4.6 (Gaussian area at arbitrary center and scale, and entropy).19F04507562E For a Radon measure $\mu $ on $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, put

\[ F_{x_0,s}(\mu )=(4\pi s)^{-n/2}\int e^{-|x-x_0|^2/(4s)}\, d\mu (x), \qquad s>0, \]
\[ \lambda (\mu )=\sup _{x_0\in \mathbb R^{n+1},\ s>0}F_{x_0,s}(\mu ). \]

The value may be infinite. For a hypersurface, use its area measure; for an integral varifold, use its weight measure, including multiplicity. The functional in Proposition 14.2.4 is $F_{0,1}$. Sources: [WhiMCF15], Section 10, and [CCMS20], Section 2.

Proposition 14.4.7 (Entropy is invariant under translations and dilations).C2244E09A64A Let $D(x)=b+ax$, $a>0$, and define the geometric dilation of an $n$-dimensional measure by $\mu '=a^ nD_\# \mu $. Then

\[ F_{x_0,s}(\mu ')=F_{(x_0-b)/a,s/a^2}(\mu ), \qquad \lambda (\mu ')=\lambda (\mu ). \]

Thus the measure scaling includes the tangential area factor, as in Definition 6.3.1.

Proof

Substitute $x=b+ay$ into the defining integral. The factor $a^ n$ combines with $(4\pi s)^{-n/2}$ to give $(4\pi s/a^2)^{-n/2}$. The change $(x_0,s)\mapsto ((x_0-b)/a,s/a^2)$ is a bijection on the set of centers and positive scales, so taking suprema proves the entropy identity. $\square$

Proposition 14.4.8 (Entropy is nonincreasing along a closed smooth flow).01983F7EAFC3 If $t_1<t_2$ are smooth times of a closed Euclidean mean curvature flow, then

\[ \lambda (\Sigma _{t_2})\leq \lambda (\Sigma _{t_1}). \]

The supremum in the entropy definition need not be attained for this argument.

Proof

Fix $x_0$ and $s>0$ and apply Theorem 14.4.3 with terminal time $t_2+s$. It gives

\[ F_{x_0,s}(\Sigma _{t_2})\leq F_{x_0,s+t_2-t_1}(\Sigma _{t_1})\leq \lambda (\Sigma _{t_1}). \]

Take the supremum over $x_0,s$. This is the entropy consequence discussed in [WhiMCF15], Section 10. $\square$

Proposition 14.4.9 (Entropy and uniform Euclidean area growth).9867B85F190C If $\lambda (\mu )\leq \Lambda $, then for every $x\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $r>0$,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))\leq e^{1/4}(4\pi )^{n/2}\Lambda r^ n. \]

Conversely, if $\mu (B_ r(x))\leq Kr^ n$ for every center and radius, then $\lambda (\mu )\leq C(n)K$. The uniformity in the center is part of the hypothesis.

Proof

In $B_ r(x)$ the exponential in $F_{x,r^2}$ is at least $e^{-1/4}$, proving the first inequality. Conversely fix a scale $s=r^2$. Bound the contribution of $B_ r(x)$ by $(4\pi )^{-n/2}K$. On the annulus $2^ jr\leq |y-x|<2^{j+1}r$, the exponential is at most $e^{-4^ j/4}$ and the mass is at most $K(2^{j+1}r)^ n$. Thus the whole integral is bounded by

\[ (4\pi )^{-n/2}K\left(1+\sum _{j=0}^\infty 2^{(j+1)n}e^{-4^ j/4}\right). \]

The sum converges independently of $x,r$. Compare the bounded-area-ratio class used in [WhiMCF15], Section 10.1. $\square$

Example 14.4.10 (A plane records its multiplicity in entropy).73E4373C1387 For an affine $n$-plane $P$ with integer multiplicity $q\geq 1$,

\[ F_{x_0,s}(q\mathcal H^ n\llcorner P) =q\exp \left(-\frac{\operatorname {dist}(x_0,P)^2}{4s}\right), \qquad \lambda (q|P|)=q. \]

To check this, split $x-x_0$ into orthogonal tangential and normal components and integrate the tangential Gaussian. The supremum is attained by choosing $x_0\in P$. Small support excess therefore does not make the entropy close to one when multiplicity is large; compare Example 6.4.6.
