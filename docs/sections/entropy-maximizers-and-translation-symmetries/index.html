14.11 Entropy maximizers and translation symmetries7A32DDA20884

7A32DDA20884

A self-shrinker's normalized Gaussian area is its entropy. To prove this on a noncompact surface, we keep the Gaussian integration and equality cases explicit. Our reference is [CM12], Lemma 7.10 and equations (7.14)–(7.27), pp. 785–788. The shrinker normalization is $\vec H=-x^\perp /2$. We write $[u]_{c,t}=\int _\Sigma u\rho _{c,t}\, dA$.

Lemma 14.11.1 (Shifted weighted divergence identities).733F0C797F3D Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth complete properly immersed boundaryless self-shrinker with polynomial area growth. For any $c,y\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $t>0$,

\[ [\langle (x-c)^ T,y^ T\rangle ]_{c,t} =-t[\langle x^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle ]_{c,t}, \]
\[ [\langle (x-c)^ T,x^ T\rangle ]_{c,t} =t[2n-|x^\perp |^2]_{c,t}. \]
Proof

The shifted drift $\mathcal D_{c,t}=\Delta -\langle (x-c)^ T,\nabla \cdot \rangle /(2t)$ is $\rho _{c,t}^{-1}\operatorname {div}_\Sigma (\rho _{c,t}\nabla \cdot )$. Since $\Delta x=-x^\perp /2$,

\[ 2\mathcal D_{c,t}\langle x,y\rangle =-\langle x^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle -\frac{\langle (x-c)^ T,y^ T\rangle }{t}, \]
\[ \mathcal D_{c,t}|x|^2 =2n-|x^\perp |^2-\frac{\langle (x-c)^ T,x^ T\rangle }{t}. \]

The integral of each drift derivative is zero. Indeed, test its weighted divergence with compact radial cutoffs. The error is bounded by a polynomial times a Gaussian on the receding annulus, and tends to zero by Lemma 14.7.4; properness makes these cutoffs compact. Integrating the displayed identities proves the claims. This is [CM12], (7.18)–(7.19). $\square$

Proposition 14.11.2 (Gaussian area decreases along parabolic parameter paths).6CD90D8527E9 Let $\Sigma $ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 14.11.1. Fix $y\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $a\in \mathbb R$, set $t_ s=1+as^2$, and put $g(s)=F_{sy,t_ s}(\Sigma )$ whenever $t_ s>0$. Then for $s\geq 0$,

\[ g'(s)=-\frac{s}{2t_ s^2} \bigl [|(asx+y)^\perp |^2\bigr ]_{sy,t_ s}\leq 0. \]
Proof

Write $z=x-sy$ and suppress the weighted subscript. The preceding identities imply

\[ \left[\frac{\langle z^ T,y^ T\rangle }{t_ s}\right] =-[\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle +s|y^\perp |^2], \]
\[ \left[\frac{|z^ T|^2}{t_ s}\right] =[2n-|z^\perp |^2-s\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle ]. \]

For the second equation substitute $x=z+sy$ into the second shifted identity and use the first. Differentiation under the Gaussian integral is justified by polynomial growth on any compact interval with $t_ s>0$. It gives

\[ g' =\frac{t_ s'}{4t_ s} \left[\frac{|z^ T|^2}{t_ s}+\frac{|z^\perp |^2}{t_ s}-2n\right] +\frac12\left[\frac{\langle z^ T,y^ T\rangle }{t_ s} +\frac{\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle }{t_ s}\right]. \]

Insert the two identities and use $t_ s'=2as$ and $t_ s^{-1}-1=-as^2/t_ s$. Completing the square yields $g'=-s[|as z^\perp +t_ s y^\perp |^2]/(2t_ s^2)$. Since $as z+t_ s y=asx+y$, this is the stated formula. It is [CM12], (7.27), with the square simplified. $\square$

Theorem 14.11.3 (A shrinker attains its entropy at its normalized parameters).700C3DB366A4 For a smooth complete properly immersed boundaryless self-shrinker $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ with polynomial area growth,

\[ F_{c,t}(\Sigma )\leq F_{0,1}(\Sigma ),\qquad \lambda (\Sigma )=F_{0,1}(\Sigma )<\infty \]

for all $c$ and $t>0$. Consequently

\[ \operatorname {Area}(\Sigma \cap B_ r(c)) \leq e^{1/4}(4\pi )^{n/2}F_{0,1}(\Sigma )r^ n. \]

This is the maximum statement proved in [CM12], Section 7.2.

Proof

In Proposition 14.11.2, choose $y=c$ and $a=t-1$. Along $0\leq s\leq 1$, $t_ s=(1-s^2)+s^2t$ is positive. Hence $F_{c,t}=g(1)\leq g(0)=F_{0,1}$. The latter is finite by Lemma 14.7.4. Take the supremum to get equality with entropy. Proposition 14.4.9 gives the uniform ball-area bound. Thus the initially assumed polynomial exponent need not equal $n$; the shrinker equation improves it to the displayed bound. $\square$

Proposition 14.11.4 (Constant tangent vectors and product splitting).7CEAAB5D3070 For a connected complete properly embedded smooth hypersurface $\Sigma \subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, define

\[ E=\{ v\in \mathbb R^{n+1}:v\in T_ x\Sigma \text{ for every }x\in \Sigma \} . \]

This is a linear subspace. The surface is invariant under translation by $E$ and splits orthogonally as

\[ \Sigma =(\Sigma \cap E^\perp )\times E. \]

In particular, a nonzero constant vector is tangent everywhere exactly when the surface splits off the corresponding Euclidean line.

Proof

The intersection of the linear spaces $T_ x\Sigma $ is linear. A constant tangent vector $v$ induces a smooth bounded vector field on the complete induced manifold. Its integral curves exist for all real time: on every bounded time interval their lengths are bounded, so completeness and local ODE existence prevent a finite endpoint. Their ambient derivatives equal $v$, hence they are $x+sv$. This proves translation invariance in each direction of $E$.

Translate $x\in \Sigma $ by $-\pi _ Ex$ to obtain $x-\pi _ Ex\in \Sigma \cap E^\perp $. The converse inclusion follows by translation invariance. The restriction of $\pi _ E$ to $\Sigma $ is a submersion because $E\subset T_ x\Sigma $; its zero fiber is a smooth submanifold. The map from this fiber times $E$ to $\Sigma $ by addition is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse $(\pi _{E^\perp },\pi _ E)$, and orthogonality makes the induced metric a product. No shrinker equation is needed for this lemma. $\square$

Lemma 14.11.5 (A smooth proper minimal shrinker is a plane).A18B950AE5BE If a connected complete properly embedded smooth boundaryless self-shrinker has $\vec H=0$ everywhere, it is a linear hyperplane through the origin.

Proof

The shrinker equation gives $x^\perp =0$, so the position field is tangent. Its integral curve through $p$ is $e^ sp$. It exists for every finite positive or negative time: its speed on any finite interval is bounded, and completeness supplies the continuation argument used in Proposition 14.11.4. Thus $rp\in \Sigma $ for all $r>0$. Properness makes the embedded image closed; letting $r\downarrow 0$ gives $0\in \Sigma $.

Smoothness at zero implies that normalized secants approaching zero approach its tangent plane. Applying this to $rp$ shows $p\in T_0\Sigma $ for every $p\in \Sigma $. The inclusion of $\Sigma $ in the $n$-plane $T_0\Sigma $ is locally open by full rank. It is closed by properness and nonempty, so connectedness of the plane makes it the entire plane. This supplies the smooth-cone step used in [CM12], p. 786. $\square$

Proposition 14.11.6 (All maximizing parameters of an embedded shrinker).8338D14CFD9B Let $\Sigma $ be a connected complete smooth properly embedded boundaryless self-shrinker with polynomial area growth, and let $E$ be the constant tangent subspace of Proposition 14.11.4. If $\Sigma $ is not a plane, then

\[ F_{c,t}(\Sigma )=\lambda (\Sigma )\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad t=1\text{ and }c\in E. \]

For a linear hyperplane $P$, equality holds precisely when $c\in P$, with any $t>0$.

Proof

Use the path in Theorem 14.11.3. Equality at both endpoints forces its nonincreasing smooth function to be constant. For every $0<s<1$, the integral square in Proposition 14.11.2 is zero. The weight is positive and the integrand continuous, so $((t-1)sx+c)^\perp =0$ at every point. Subtract this identity at two different $s$. If $t\ne 1$, it gives $x^\perp =0$, and Lemma 14.11.5 forces a plane. Otherwise $c^\perp =0$, which is $c\in E$. Conversely, if $t=1$ and $c\in E$, the square vanishes all along the path and gives equality. The plane statement follows from Example 14.4.10. This identifies the degeneracy excluded in [CM12], Lemma 7.10. $\square$

Proposition 14.11.7 (A quadratic parameter deficit without line splitting).218C6C0F43AF Let $\Sigma $ be a connected complete smooth properly embedded two-sided boundaryless self-shrinker with polynomial area growth and with $E=\{ 0\} $. There are $\gamma ,r>0$ such that

\[ F_{c,t}(\Sigma )\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-\gamma \bigl (|c|^2+(t-1)^2\bigr ) \]

whenever $|c|^2+(t-1)^2\leq r^2$ and $t>0$.

Proof

The first parameter derivatives vanish by Proposition 14.9.1. With the shape fixed, Theorem 14.9.2 gives the Hessian on $(y,h)$ as

\[ -h^2\int H^2\rho _{0,1}\, dA-\tfrac 12\int |y^\perp |^2\rho _{0,1}\, dA. \]

Since $E=0$, every nonzero $y$ gives a positive second integral. Also $H$ is not identically zero: Lemma 14.11.5 would give a plane, whose tangent subspace has dimension $n\geq 1$. The Hessian is therefore negative definite. Compactness of the unit sphere in the finite-dimensional parameter space gives a uniform negative bound. Gaussian domination gives continuity of second derivatives near $(0,1)$, so Taylor's theorem proves the quadratic estimate after decreasing $r$. Compare [CM12], (7.12). $\square$

Theorem 14.11.8 (A uniform entropy gap away from the normalized parameters).93F994D6C9F0 Let $\Sigma $ satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition 14.11.7. For every $\varepsilon >0$ there is $\delta >0$ such that

\[ \sup _{|c|+|\log t|\geq \varepsilon }F_{c,t}(\Sigma ) \leq \lambda (\Sigma )-\delta . \]

This is the uniform, rather than merely pointwise, conclusion of [CM12], Lemma 7.10. In particular $\lambda (\Sigma )>1$.

Proof

Choose a closed Euclidean parameter ball of radius $r_0>0$ about $(0,1)$, small enough to lie in the quadratic-deficit neighborhood and in $\{ |c|+|\log t|<\varepsilon \} $. Its boundary has deficit at least $\gamma r_0^2$. For any target outside $\{ |c|+|\log t|<\varepsilon \} $, the continuous path $(sc,1+(t-1)s^2)$ starts at the center and ends outside this ball. It crosses the boundary at some $s_0\in (0,1]$. Its scale remains positive, and Proposition 14.11.2 makes the Gaussian area nonincreasing after that crossing. Thus every target has deficit at least $\delta =\gamma r_0^2$.

Choose a point $p\in \Sigma $ and let $t\downarrow 0$. The parameters $(p,t)$ eventually satisfy the excluded-neighborhood condition. Proposition 14.10.7 gives $F_{p,t}\to 1$, so $1\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-\delta $. This proves strict entropy greater than one without a separate classification theorem. $\square$
