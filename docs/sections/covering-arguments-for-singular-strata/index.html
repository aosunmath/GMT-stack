6.10 Covering arguments for singular strataA56526554CB7

A56526554CB7

Let $V$ be a stationary integral $m$-varifold in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $1\leq m<N$. Write $\Theta (x)=\Theta ^ m(\| V\| ,x)$ and use the strata $\mathcal S^ j(V)$ of Definition 6.3.9. The argument below expands [Minter], Theorem 1.7 and its proof, pp. 17–19 and 22–24. It uses every possible tangent cone through the definition of the strata, but never assumes tangent-cone uniqueness.

Definition 6.10.1 (One-sided approximation by planes).90B2A5CC707F A set $A\subset \mathbb R^ N$ has the $(j,\delta ,r_0)$ approximation property if for every $a\in A$ and $0<r\leq r_0$ there is a $j$-dimensional linear subspace $L=L(a,r)$ such that

\[ A\cap B_ r(a)\subset \{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,a+L)<\delta r\} . \]

Here $0\leq j<N$ and $\delta ,r_0>0$. The plane may depend on both center and scale; no converse approximation of the plane by $A$ is required. Compare [Minter], p. 19.

Lemma 6.10.2 (Refining a cover near a plane).F04616F157C3 There is $C_ N\geq 1$ such that, if $0<\delta <1/16$ and $A$ has the $(j,\delta ,r_0)$ approximation property, each $A\cap B_ r(a)$, $a\in A$, $r\leq r_0$, can be covered by at most $C_ N\delta ^{-j}$ balls of radius $8\delta r$ whose centers belong to $A\cap B_ r(a)$.

Proof

Project orthogonally onto the approximating plane $a+L$. The projected points lie in its $j$-ball of radius $r$. A cubical grid covers this ball by at most $C_ N\delta ^{-j}$ cells of diameter at most $\delta r$. For each cell whose inverse image meets $A\cap B_ r(a)$, select one point of that intersection. Two points over the same cell have projected distance at most $\delta r$ and normal components of length less than $\delta r$ each; their distance is less than $3\delta r$. The balls of radius $8\delta r$ at the selected points therefore cover the set. If $j=0$, the projection is a single point and one such ball suffices. Centers remain in $A$, which allows iteration of the approximation hypothesis. $\square$

Proposition 6.10.3 (Small one-sided approximation bounds Hausdorff dimension).EBCF2E3FB28D For every $\beta >0$ there is $\delta _*(N,j,\beta )>0$ such that any set with the $(j,\delta ,r_0)$ approximation property, $0<\delta <\delta _*$, has

\[ \mathcal H^{j+\beta }(A)=0. \]

No measurability or closedness assumption on $A$ is needed for this outer-measure conclusion.

Proof

Set $s=j+\beta $ and $\vartheta =8\delta $. Choose $\delta _*<1/16$ so small that $C_ N\delta ^{-j}\vartheta ^ s<1/2$ whenever $0<\delta <\delta _*$. This is possible because the expression is $C_ N8^ s\delta ^\beta $. First suppose $A$ is bounded. Total boundedness supplies a finite cover by balls of a fixed radius $r<r_0$ with centers in $A$. Let the sum of their diameter-to-the-$s$ powers be $T$.

Apply Lemma 6.10.2 to every ball and iterate. Each new center still lies in $A$ and each radius is smaller than $r_0$. At the $k$th stage all radii are $\vartheta ^ kr$ and the sum of diameter-to-the-$s$ powers is at most $2^{-k}T$. These are arbitrarily fine covers with cost tending to zero, so $\mathcal H^ s(A)=0$, regardless of its normalization constant. For unbounded $A$, apply the argument to $A\cap B_ ell(0)$, which inherits the same property, and take a countable union. $\square$

Lemma 6.10.4 (Nearly maximal density concentrates near a spine).B01C74EE7C25 For $a\in \mathcal S^ j(V)$ and $\delta >0$, there is $\varepsilon >0$ with $\overline{B_{2\varepsilon }(a)}\subset U$ such that for each $0<r\leq \varepsilon $ some $j$-plane $L=L(a,r)$ satisfies

\[ \{ y\in B_ r(a):\Theta (y)\geq \Theta (a)-\varepsilon \} \subset \{ y:\operatorname {dist}(y,a+L)<\delta r\} . \]
Proof

If the assertion fails, choose $\varepsilon _ i\downarrow 0$ and $r_ i\leq \varepsilon _ i$ for which no $j$-plane works. Theorem 6.3.6 gives a subsequence of $V_{a,r_ i}$ converging locally to a tangent cone $C$. Its spine has dimension at most $j$ by the definition of $\mathcal S^ j$. Choose a $j$-plane $L$ containing this spine. Failure for this particular plane gives points $z_ i\in B_1(0)$ with

\[ \Theta ^ m(\| V_{a,r_ i}\| ,z_ i)\geq \Theta (a)-\varepsilon _ i, \qquad \operatorname {dist}(z_ i,L)\geq \delta . \]

Pass to $z_ i\to z\in \overline{B_1(0)}$. Density upper semicontinuity, Proposition 6.3.3, gives $\Theta ^ m(\| C\| ,z)\geq \Theta (a)=\Theta ^ m(\| C\| ,0)$. Proposition 6.9.4 forces $z\in S(C)\subset L$, contradicting the distance bound. Convergence is on all compact subsets of $\mathbb R^ N$, so a limit with $|z|=1$ causes no boundary problem. $\square$

Proposition 6.10.5 (Decomposing a stratum by scale and density).BD5246D88700 For every fixed $\delta >0$, $\mathcal S^ j(V)$ is a countable union of sets, each having the $(j,\delta ,1/i)$ approximation property for some positive integer $i$.

Proof

Let $E_ i$ consist of points $a\in \mathcal S^ j(V)$ for which the conclusion of Lemma 6.10.4 holds with tolerance and scale $\varepsilon =1/i$, and $\overline{B_{2/i}(a)}\subset U$. Every stratum point belongs to some $E_ i$: decreasing the tolerance and allowed scale preserves the conclusion. Subdivide into

\[ E_{i,q}=\{ a\in E_ i:(q-1)/i<\Theta (a)\leq q/i\} ,\qquad q=1,2,\ldots . \]

Densities are finite and at least one at support points, so these sets cover the stratum. If $a,y\in E_{i,q}$, then $\Theta (y)>\Theta (a)-1/i$. Thus the concentration conclusion at $a$, for any $r\leq 1/i$, applies to every $y\in E_{i,q}\cap B_ r(a)$. This is precisely the desired approximation property. $\square$

Corollary 6.10.6 (The zero stratum is countable).A98914D9EBD1 The set $\mathcal S^0(V)$ is at most countable.

Proof

Use Proposition 6.10.5 with a fixed $0<\delta <1$. A piece with the $(0,\delta ,r_0)$ property has no distinct points $a,b$ with $d=|a-b|<r_0$: choose $d<r\leq r_0$ also satisfying $\delta r<d$. The property at $a$ would give $b\in B_{\delta r}(a)$, a contradiction. Each piece is therefore $r_0$-separated and has finitely many points in each bounded region. It is countable, as is the union of pieces. No local finiteness of the entire zero stratum is asserted. $\square$

Definition 6.10.7 (Flat singular points).8E5306B184C0 A singular point of $V$ is called flat if at least one tangent cone there is a positive-integer multiple of an $m$-plane. The point is still required to be singular according to Definition 6.3.5; a regular multiple plane is not a flat singular point. This terminology follows [Minter], p. 20.

Corollary 6.10.8 (Flat singular points form the top stratum difference).3C81F5ADA4BE The flat singular points are exactly $\mathcal S^ m(V)\setminus \mathcal S^{m-1}(V)$. At each such point the density is an integer $q\geq 2$.

Proof

Failure of membership in $\mathcal S^{m-1}$ means some tangent cone has spine dimension $m$, since Proposition 6.9.4 bounds it above by $m$. Corollary 6.9.8 identifies that cone as $q|P|$. Conversely a multiple plane has spine dimension $m$. The tangent-cone mass formula, Theorem 6.3.6, identifies the density at the original point as $q$. If $q=1$, Corollary 6.4.4 makes the point regular, a contradiction. $\square$

Corollary 6.10.9 (The singular set below density two).5CC0A191C3F4 One has

\[ \dim _{\mathcal H}\{ a\in \operatorname {sing}V:\Theta (a)<2\} \leq m-1. \]

For $m=1$ this set is at most countable.

Proof

Corollary 6.10.8 excludes the top stratum difference under the density bound. The set is therefore contained in $\mathcal S^{m-1}(V)$. Apply Theorem 6.3.10; for $m=1$ use the stronger conclusion of Corollary 6.10.6. This does not assert regularity merely from density less than two. $\square$
