4.2 BV approximation and compactness0D4FA44A41FD

0D4FA44A41FD

We now develop the analytic compactness behind finite-perimeter minimization. Here $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ is open, and $Du$ denotes the distributional vector measure of Definition 4.1.1. The basic reference is [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 2, pp. 52–56. The proofs below spell out the mollification and compactness steps needed in the direct method.

Proposition 4.2.1 (Lower semicontinuity of total variation).41DD93923E24 If $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, then for every open $W\subset U$,

\[ |Du|(W)\leq \liminf _ j|Du_ j|(W), \]

using the variational definition even when its value is infinite. In particular, uniform local variation bounds force $u\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$.

Proof

For $X\in C_ c^1(W;\mathbb R^ n)$ with $|X|\leq 1$, local $L^1$ convergence gives $\int u\operatorname {div}X=\lim _ j\int u_ j\operatorname {div}X\leq \liminf _ j|Du_ j|(W)$. Take the supremum over $X$. This is the general-function form of Proposition 4.1.3, and the lower-semicontinuity step in [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 2.6. $\square$

Proposition 4.2.2 (Multiplication by a smooth cutoff).770E0596E45F If $u\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ and $\eta \in C_ c^1(U)$, then the function $\eta u$, extended by zero outside $U$, belongs to $BV(\mathbb R^ n)$ and

\[ D(\eta u)=\eta Du+uD\eta \, \mathcal L^ n, \qquad |D(\eta u)|(\mathbb R^ n) \leq \int |\eta |\, d|Du|+\int |u||D\eta |\, dx. \]

The compact support of the cutoff prevents a boundary measure from appearing at $\partial U$.

Proof

For a compactly supported test field $X$, use $\eta \operatorname {div}X=\operatorname {div}(\eta X)-D\eta \cdot X$. The defining identity for $Du$ then gives the asserted vector measure. Its total variation is bounded by the sum of the total variations of the two terms. Both are finite on the compact support, and $\eta u$ is integrable. Compare the product estimate in [Sim18], Chapter 2, the proof of Lemma 2.8. $\square$

Lemma 4.2.3 (Mollification and the variation bound).33DD11902347 Let $u\in BV(\mathbb R^ n)$ and let $\rho \in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$ be nonnegative with integral one. Put $\rho _\varepsilon (x)=\varepsilon ^{-n}\rho (x/\varepsilon )$ and $u_\varepsilon =\rho _\varepsilon *u$. Then $u_\varepsilon \to u$ in $L^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ and

\[ Du_\varepsilon (x)=\int \rho _\varepsilon (x-y)\, dDu(y), \qquad |Du_\varepsilon (x)|\leq \int \rho _\varepsilon (x-y)\, d|Du|(y). \]

In particular, $\int |Du_\varepsilon |\leq |Du|(\mathbb R^ n)$, and for a Borel set $A$, $\int _ A|Du_\varepsilon |\leq |Du|(A_\varepsilon )$, where $A_\varepsilon =\{ y:\operatorname {dist}(y,A)<\varepsilon \} $. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Lemma 2.5.

Proof

Convolution is an approximate identity on $L^1$, giving the first convergence. Distributional differentiation commutes with convolution: test against a compactly supported smooth function, use Fubini, and then apply the defining integration-by-parts identity for $Du$. The triangle inequality for integration against a vector measure gives the pointwise bound. Integrate it and use Fubini again. The kernel has total integral one, and it vanishes when $|x-y|\geq \varepsilon $, which proves the two integral bounds. $\square$

Definition 4.2.4 (Strict convergence in BV).D0AFF720A2E2 A sequence $u_ j\in BV(U)$ converges strictly to $u\in BV(U)$ if

\[ \| u_ j-u\| _{L^1(U)}\to 0, \qquad |Du_ j|(U)\to |Du|(U). \]

This does not mean $|D(u_ j-u)|(U)\to 0$. In particular, convergence of the total variations is different from convergence in the full BV norm. The terminology describes the approximation property in [Sim18], Chapter 2, Lemma 2.5; the local Radon-measure version there requires localization.

Corollary 4.2.5 (Global mollification is strict approximation).C86A997AF569 The mollifications in Lemma 4.2.3 converge strictly to $u$ in $BV(\mathbb R^ n)$.

Proof

The lemma gives the $L^1$ convergence and the upper bound $\limsup \int |Du_\varepsilon |\leq |Du|(\mathbb R^ n)$. Proposition 4.2.1 gives the reverse lower bound. Both together yield convergence of the total variations. On a general domain, arbitrary zero extension can create a boundary jump; the whole-space hypothesis is part of this statement. $\square$

Proposition 4.2.6 (Translation estimate for BV functions).D525EB5675E7 For $u\in BV(\mathbb R^ n)$ and $h\in \mathbb R^ n$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R^ n}|u(x+h)-u(x)|\, dx \leq |h|\, |Du|(\mathbb R^ n). \]

Consequently, with the mollifier from Lemma 4.2.3,

\[ \| u_\varepsilon -u\| _{L^1}\leq \varepsilon \left(\int |z|\rho (z)\, dz\right)|Du|(\mathbb R^ n). \]
Proof

For the smooth function $u_\delta $, the fundamental theorem of calculus along $x+th$, followed by Fubini, gives

\[ \int |u_\delta (x+h)-u_\delta (x)|\, dx \leq |h|\int _0^1\int |Du_\delta (x+th)|\, dx\, dt \leq |h||Du|(\mathbb R^ n). \]

Pass to the limit $\delta \downarrow 0$ in $L^1$. Average the translation estimate against $\rho _\varepsilon (h)$ to get the second inequality. This supplies a quantitative form of the smoothing step in [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 2.6. $\square$

Theorem 4.2.7 (Local compactness of BV functions).C33FE0726B69 Suppose $u_ j\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ and for every relatively compact open $W\subset U$,

\[ \sup _ j\left(\| u_ j\| _{L^1(W)}+|Du_ j|(W)\right)<\infty . \]

Then a subsequence converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ to a function $u\in BV_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. The lower-semicontinuity conclusion of Proposition 4.2.1 holds on every open subset. This is [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 2.6, pp. 54–55.

Proof

First suppose the functions are globally BV, supported in one compact set $K$, and have uniformly bounded $L^1$ norm and total variation. For fixed $\varepsilon >0$, their convolutions with $\rho _\varepsilon $ have common compact support in $K+\overline{B_\varepsilon }$ and uniform bounds for their values and first derivatives, obtained from the $L^1$ bound and the suprema of $\rho _\varepsilon $ and $D\rho _\varepsilon $. Arzelà–Ascoli therefore makes these convolutions precompact in $L^1$.

Proposition 4.2.6 bounds the $L^1$ distance from each function to its convolution by $C\varepsilon $, uniformly in $j$. For each prescribed tolerance, the convolutions have a finite $L^1$ net, which becomes a finite net for the original functions with twice the error after choosing $\varepsilon $ small. Hence the original sequence is precompact in $L^1$.

For the local statement, choose an exhaustion $W_ k\Subset U$ and cutoffs $\eta _ k\in C_ c^1(U)$ equal to one on $W_ k$. Proposition 4.2.2 gives uniform whole-space BV bounds for $\eta _ ku_ j$, with a common compact support for each $k$. Extract subsequences successively and then take a diagonal subsequence. The limits agree on overlaps because they are limits in $L^1$, and therefore define $u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$. Proposition 4.2.1 gives local finite variation and the asserted bounds. $\square$

Corollary 4.2.8 (Compactness of finite-perimeter sets).8CF44F9AEC71 Let $E_ j$ be measurable sets with uniformly bounded perimeter on every relatively compact open subset of $U$. Then a subsequence satisfies $\chi _{E_ j}\to \chi _ E$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ for a set $E$ of locally finite perimeter. If all $E_ j$ lie almost everywhere in one compact set $K\subset \mathbb R^ n$ and have uniformly bounded total perimeter, the convergence is in $L^1(\mathbb R^ n)$, the limit also lies in $K$ almost everywhere, and the volumes converge.

Proof

On a relatively compact $W$, the characteristic functions have $L^1$ norm at most $\mathcal L^ n(W)$, so Theorem 4.2.7 applies. A further subsequence converges almost everywhere on an exhaustion; since every value is zero or one, its limit is a characteristic function. Proposition 4.1.3 gives the perimeter bound. With common compact support, use the whole-space compactness argument in the theorem. Global $L^1$ convergence implies convergence of the integrals and hence of the volumes. $\square$

Proposition 4.2.9 (Weak convergence of the derivative measures).CC9026172A99 If $u_ j\to u$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$ and their variations are uniformly bounded on relatively compact open sets, then $Du_ j\rightharpoonup Du$ as vector Radon measures locally in $U$. Convergence of the variation measures $|Du_ j|$ to $|Du|$ is not asserted.

Proof

Testing with a smooth compactly supported vector field $X$ gives $\int X\cdot dDu_ j=-\int u_ j\operatorname {div}X\to -\int u\operatorname {div}X$. A continuous compactly supported field can be approximated uniformly by smooth ones supported in a fixed relatively compact neighborhood. The variation bound controls the error uniformly in $j$, proving weak convergence against every continuous test field. This is the distributional-measure argument of [Sim18], Chapter 2, Section 2. $\square$

Example 4.2.10 (Variation control without function control is insufficient).BD09D0B6392E On a bounded nonempty open set, the constants $u_ j=j$ have $|Du_ j|=0$, but no subsequence converges in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The local $L^1$ hypothesis in Theorem 4.2.7 cannot be omitted. For characteristic functions this hypothesis is automatic locally, which explains the simpler assumptions in Corollary 4.2.8.

Example 4.2.11 (Strict convergence need not be BV-norm convergence).3214264E8308 In one dimension let $u=\chi _{(0,1)}$ and let $u_\varepsilon $ be its nonnegative smooth mollifications. Then $u_\varepsilon \to u$ strictly by Corollary 4.2.5, and $|Du|(\mathbb R)=2$. But $Du=\delta _0-\delta _1$ is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, whereas $Du_\varepsilon $ is absolutely continuous. Hence these derivative measures are mutually singular and

\[ |D(u_\varepsilon -u)|(\mathbb R)=\int |Du_\varepsilon |\, dx+2\longrightarrow 4. \]

Thus the stronger BV norm does not tend to zero. This example also distinguishes weak convergence of vector measures from convergence of their total variation norms.
