15.2 From one minimal hypersurface to many1912BF12699D

1912BF12699D

The following milestones refer to particular results and editions. Dates of supplied preprints are distinguished from dates of journal publication.

Remark 15.2.1 (Existence, regularity, and the meaning of dimension).035643999D57 Pitts' 1981 monograph [Pitts81] made discrete min-max and almost-minimizing constructions into a systematic existence theory. In the smooth closed setting, its original hypersurface range is $2\leq n\leq 5$, or ambient dimension $3\leq n+1\leq 6$. Schoen–Simon's stable regularity theory [SS81] supplies the extension used in the modern ambient-dimension-seven statement. This is why the project records Pitts' original range separately from later realization theorems. Song's introduction [Song18] gives a concise historical account of this distinction.

Remark 15.2.2 (The volume spectrum and a sequence of geometric questions).C5CCD5D00E73 The Weyl law [LMN] relates high-parameter widths to ambient volume. The next questions are different: whether there are infinitely many distinct hypersurfaces, whether they spread through the manifold, and whether a width is realized without multiplicity. The supplied sources record Song's 2018 preprint [Song18] on infinitely many hypersurfaces for arbitrary closed metrics in dimensions three through seven; the 2019 article of Marques–Neves–Song [MNS19] on equidistribution for generic metrics; and Zhou's 2020 article [Zhou20] on multiplicity one for the volume spectrum of a bumpy metric. Theorems 9.3.4, 9.3.5, and 9.3.3 state these conclusions separately, with their distinct assumptions.
