14.20 Convergence and area decay for periodic graphsB6C8CF57EDAB

B6C8CF57EDAB

The curvature estimate from [EH89], Proposition 4.4, gives a useful compact-domain consequence. Throughout this section, the flow is assumed smooth for every $t\geq 0$ on a fixed flat torus $\mathbb T^ n$. We prove its convergence, including quantitative area decay. These conclusions do not replace a proof of global existence.

Lemma 14.20.1 (Converting graph curvature to Euclidean Hessian bounds).DC18B9C427CF For a smooth graph with $v=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$, its metric and scalar second fundamental form satisfy

\[ g_{ij}=\delta _{ij}+u_ i u_ j,\qquad h_{ij}=-v^{-1}u_{ij},\qquad v^{-6}|D^2u|^2\leq |A|^2\leq v^{-2}|D^2u|^2. \]

The Hessian norm on the outer terms is Euclidean. In particular $|D^2u|\leq v^3|A|$.

Proof

The metric follows by differentiating the graph map. Differentiating $\langle \nu ,F_ j\rangle =0$ gives $h_{ij}=-\langle \nu ,F_{ij}\rangle =-u_{ij}/v$. In a Euclidean orthonormal basis diagonalizing $g^{-1}$, its eigenvalues lie between $v^{-2}$ and one. The squared tensor norm is $v^{-2}\sum _{i,j}\gamma _ i\gamma _ j u_{ij}^2$, where $\gamma _ i$ are these eigenvalues. Bound each product between $v^{-4}$ and one to obtain both inequalities. This proves the comparison without treating the coordinate Hessian as an intrinsic curvature tensor. $\square$

Lemma 14.20.2 (Periodicity controls slope and oscillation by the Hessian).DC81EF33765D Let $d$ be the diameter of the flat torus. For $u\in C^2(\mathbb T^ n)$,

\[ \| Du\| _\infty \leq d\| D^2u\| _\infty ,\qquad \max u-\min u\leq d\| Du\| _\infty \leq d^2\| D^2u\| _\infty . \]

Here the global parallel frame identifies tangent vectors at different points.

Proof

Every component of $Du$ has zero integral on the torus, by periodic integration by parts against a constant parallel vector. For points $x,y$, integrate the derivative of this vector-valued gradient along a shortest torus geodesic to get $|Du(x)-Du(y)|\leq d\| D^2u\| _\infty $. Average in $y$ and use the zero vector average to obtain the first estimate. Integrate $Du$ along a shortest geodesic between a maximum and a minimum for the second. A constant nonzero gradient on the Euclidean lift would not be periodic, which is precisely the obstruction removed by the averaging step. $\square$

Proposition 14.20.3 (Height intervals are nested).120F6F89BEF2 For a smooth periodic graphical flow, $\max _ xu(x,t)$ is nonincreasing and $\min _ xu(x,t)$ is nondecreasing. Hence its height remains in the initial interval at every smooth time.

Proof

At a spatial maximum, the graph equation has $Du=0$ and gives $u_ t=\Delta u\leq 0$. For a fully time-dependent argument, apply the first-interior-maximum test to $u-\max u(\cdot ,s)-\eta (t-s)$ on a compact time slab, using the positive definite graph coefficient matrix, and send $\eta \downarrow 0$. Apply the same reasoning to $-u$. Starting at any time $s$ proves nesting, not only a bound by the initial interval. This is the compact analogue of the height estimates in [EH89], Section 2. $\square$

Theorem 14.20.4 (Conditional convergence to one horizontal slice).033EF6B85768 Let a periodic graphical mean curvature flow be smooth for all $t\geq 0$, and put $V_0=\max \sqrt{1+|Du(\cdot ,0)|^2}$. There is a constant $c_\infty $ between its initial minimum and maximum such that

\[ \| u(\cdot ,t)-c_\infty \| _\infty + \| Du(\cdot ,t)\| _\infty +\| D^2u(\cdot ,t)\| _\infty \leq C t^{-1/2}\quad (t>0). \]

The constant depends only on $V_0$ and the flat torus. This is a consequence of the slope-based curvature estimate of [EH89], Proposition 4.4, with periodicity used to control the height oscillation.

Proof

Proposition 14.18.4 bounds $v$ by $V_0$. Theorem 14.19.4 and Lemma 14.20.1 give

\[ \| D^2u(\cdot ,t)\| _\infty \leq V_0^3\sqrt{\frac{V_0^2-1}{2t}}. \]

Lemma 14.20.2 gives the same rate for the gradient and oscillation. The nested closed height intervals in Proposition 14.20.3 are nonempty, bounded, and have length tending to zero. Their intersection is therefore one number $c_\infty $. At each time the distance of any height to this number is at most that interval's length. Combining these estimates proves the assertion. If $V_0=1$, the solution is already a constant and the estimates have zero right-hand side. The proof needs no conservation of the ordinary spatial average. $\square$

Proposition 14.20.5 (Graphical flow is an area gradient flow with variable mobility).87BC139616A5 For a smooth periodic function define its graph area $\mathcal A(u)=\int _{\mathbb T^ n}\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}\, dx$. Its first variation and the graphical flow equation are

\[ D\mathcal A(u)[\phi ]=\int H\phi \, dx, \qquad u_ t=-vH. \]

Thus the flow is the negative gradient for the positive inner product $\langle \phi ,\eta \rangle _ u=\int \phi \eta /v\, dx$. Along it,

\[ \frac d{dt}\mathcal A(u(t))=-\int H^2v\, dx =-\int u_ t^2/v\, dx. \]
Proof

Differentiate under the area integral and integrate by parts on the torus: $D\mathcal A(u)[\phi ]=\int Du\cdot D\phi /v=-\int \operatorname {div}(Du/v)\phi =\int H\phi $. The gradient with respect to the stated inner product is $vH$. Its negative is exactly the graphical equation. Substitution of $u_ t=-vH$ in the first variation proves the dissipation identity; it agrees with Proposition 14.1.2 because $d\mu =v\, dx$. The variable factor $v$ explains why an ordinary unweighted spatial mean is not automatically conserved. $\square$

Lemma 14.20.6 (An area-excess dissipation inequality).3D1A546B94E7 Let $u$ be any smooth function on the flat torus with $1\leq v\leq V$, and set

\[ \mathcal F(u)=\mathcal A(u)-\operatorname {Vol}(\mathbb T^ n),\qquad \lambda _1=\inf _{\substack {f\in H^1,\ \int f=0\\ f\ne 0}} \frac{\int |Df|^2}{\int f^2}>0. \]

Then

\[ \frac1{V+1}\int |Du|^2\leq \mathcal F(u)\leq \frac12\int |Du|^2, \qquad \int H^2v\, dx\geq \frac{2\lambda _1}{V^2}\mathcal F(u). \]
Proof

Use $v-1=|Du|^2/(v+1)$ to obtain the two-sided area comparison. If $Du=0$, the final estimate is immediate. Otherwise integration by parts gives

\[ \int H(u-\bar u)\, dx=\int \frac{|Du|^2}{v}\, dx \geq V^{-1}\int |Du|^2. \]

Cauchy–Schwarz and Corollary 7.18.3 bound its square above by $\lambda _1^{-1}\int H^2\int |Du|^2$. Cancel the positive gradient integral to get $\int H^2\geq \lambda _1V^{-2}\int |Du|^2$. Since $v\geq 1$ and $\int |Du|^2\geq 2\mathcal F$, this proves the dissipation bound. No critical-point assumption was made for this inequality. $\square$

Theorem 14.20.7 (Exponential decay of periodic graph area and gradient energy).DFA20B22D3F3 Under Theorem 14.20.4, put $\gamma =2\lambda _1/V_0^2$. Then

\[ \mathcal F(u(t))\leq \mathcal F(u(0))e^{-\gamma t},\qquad \int |Du(\cdot ,t)|^2\leq (V_0+1)\mathcal F(u(0))e^{-\gamma t}. \]

The curvature estimate of [EH89] supplies the slope-controlled setting; the quantitative energy decay follows here from the graph variation and the torus Poincare inequality.

Proof

The slope bound permits $V=V_0$ in Lemma 14.20.6 at every time. Proposition 14.20.5 gives $\mathcal F'(t)\leq -\gamma \mathcal F(t)$. Multiplication by $e^{\gamma t}$ and integration prove the first inequality. The lower area comparison from the lemma yields the second. Both inequalities hold on every existing smooth time interval; the all-time hypothesis is needed only when taking a large-time limit. $\square$

Lemma 14.20.8 (A Lipschitz-to-uniform interpolation estimate on the torus).0DA0D08FCC08 There is a constant $C$ depending only on the flat torus such that every vector-valued Lipschitz function $f$ with Lipschitz constant at most $L\geq 0$ satisfies

\[ \| f\| _\infty \leq C\left(\| f\| _2+ L^{n/(n+2)}\| f\| _2^{2/(n+2)}\right). \]

The Euclidean norm is used in the target; $C$ does not depend on its dimension.

Proof

Choose a fixed $r_0>0$ smaller than the injectivity radius, so every ball of radius $r\leq r_0$ has volume $\omega _ nr^ n$. Let $M=\max |f|$, attained at $x_0$. If $M=0$ there is nothing to prove. If $L=0$, the function is constant and the first term suffices. Otherwise set $r=\min \{ r_0,M/(2L)\} $. The triangle inequality and the Lipschitz bound give $|f|\geq M/2$ on $B_ r(x_0)$. Hence $\| f\| _2^2\geq (\omega _ n/4)M^2r^ n$. If $r=r_0$, this bounds $M$ by a fixed multiple of $\| f\| _2$. In the other case it gives $M^{n+2}\leq C L^ n\| f\| _2^2$. Taking the appropriate root in each case proves the claim. Only the norm triangle inequality was used in the target, so the dimension of that target does not enter the constant. $\square$

Corollary 14.20.9 (Exponential convergence of height and slope).A9521FCD1263 In the setting of Theorem 14.20.4, with the same $c_\infty $ and $\gamma $, there is $C$ depending on the initial slope, area excess and torus such that

\[ \| u(\cdot ,t)-c_\infty \| _\infty +\| Du(\cdot ,t)\| _\infty \leq C e^{-\gamma t/(n+2)}\qquad (t\geq 1). \]

The argument here gives exponential convergence in $C^1$ and the preceding polynomial bound in $C^2$; it does not assert uniform estimates of all derivatives without further parabolic input.

Proof

The Hessian estimate in the proof of Theorem 14.20.4 gives one uniform bound $L$ for the Lipschitz constant of $Du$ for $t\geq 1$. Theorem 14.20.7 gives $\| Du\| _2\leq C e^{-\gamma t/2}$. Apply Lemma 14.20.8 to this vector-valued gradient. Its two terms decay at rates $\gamma /2$ and $\gamma /(n+2)$, respectively, so the slower rate bounds their sum. The height interval contains $c_\infty $, and its length is at most $d\| Du\| _\infty $ by Lemma 14.20.2. This gives the height estimate. When the initial area excess is zero, the solution is a constant and the conclusion is immediate. $\square$

Corollary 14.20.10 (The total curvature dissipation equals the initial area excess).D29C250B9C03 For an all-time smooth periodic graphical flow,

\[ \int _0^\infty \int _{M_ t}H^2\, d\mu _ t\, dt =\mathcal A(u(0))-\operatorname {Vol}(\mathbb T^ n). \]

More generally, the remaining dissipation after a time $s$ is exactly $\mathcal F(u(s))$ and is bounded by $\mathcal F(u(0))e^{-\gamma s}$.

Proof

Integrate Proposition 14.20.5 from $s$ to a finite $T$. The right-hand side is $\mathcal A(u(s))-\mathcal A(u(T))$. Theorem 14.20.7 implies that the latter area tends to the base torus volume as $T\to \infty $. Monotone convergence for the nonnegative time integrand gives the exact identity. The exponential bound is the same theorem evaluated at $s$. This is a finite total dissipation statement, not a pointwise estimate for $H$ at every late time. $\square$
