11.6 Geodesic spheres and spherical CMC rigidity7A9645C55242

7A9645C55242

The scalar constraint makes geodesic spheres stable even when their unrestricted second variation has a negative direction. We develop this explicitly in space forms and prove the spherical rigidity argument of [BdCE88], Section 3, pp. 129–131. All hypersurfaces here have dimension $n\geq 2$. We use unaveraged $H=\operatorname {tr}(D\nu )$ and the stability convention in Definition 11.3.6.

Proposition 11.6.1 (Geometry of a space-form geodesic sphere).7ED8B4A1932C In the simply connected complete space form of sectional curvature $c$, let $\Sigma _ r$ be a geodesic sphere of radius $r>0$, with $r<\pi /\sqrt c$ when $c>0$. Set

\[ s_ c(r)=\begin{cases} \sin (\sqrt c\, r)/\sqrt c,& c>0,\\ r,& c=0,\\ \sinh (\sqrt{-c}\, r)/\sqrt{-c},& c<0.\end{cases} \]

With the normal pointing away from the center, its induced metric is $s_ c(r)^2g_{S^ n}$ and

\[ A=\frac{s_ c'}{s_ c}I,\qquad H=n\frac{s_ c'}{s_ c},\qquad q=|A|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )=\frac n{s_ c^2}. \]

These formulas make explicit the quantities in [BdCE88], Lemma 3.4.

Proof

A perpendicular Jacobi field along a unit radial geodesic with initial data $Y(0)=0$, $Y'(0)=v$ satisfies $Y''+cY=0$ after parallel transport. Thus $Y(r)=s_ c(r)v$, giving the polar metric $dr^2+s_ c(r)^2g_{S^ n}$ before the antipode in the positive-curvature case. Taking one half of the radial derivative of the tangential metric gives $A=(s_ c'/s_ c)I$. Also $\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )=nc$. The identity $(s_ c')^2+cs_ c^2=1$ follows either from the displayed formulas or by differentiating it and using $s_ c''+cs_ c=0$ and the initial data. Therefore $q=n((s_ c'/s_ c)^2+c)=n/s_ c^2$. $\square$

Theorem 11.6.2 (Stability and unrestricted index of geodesic spheres).CC7BA2BA9BC5 For $n\geq 2$, every geodesic sphere in Proposition 11.6.1 is stable for the enclosed-volume constraint and has unrestricted index exactly one. This is the stability assertion of [BdCE88], Lemma 3.4, together with its index consequence.

Proof

The induced sphere has radius $s_ c(r)$, so Proposition 11.3.8 gives first positive eigenvalue $n/s_ c(r)^2=q$. Proposition 11.5.6 proves constrained stability. The constant function gives $Q(1,1)=-q\operatorname {Area}(\Sigma _ r)<0$. Proposition 11.3.7 then bounds the unrestricted index above by one, proving equality. The exact-volume realization proposition identifies the mean-zero calculation with the geometric variation problem. $\square$

Proposition 11.6.3 (Nullity of a geodesic sphere).4DBCEFBB183C The constrained Jacobi nullspace of $\Sigma _ r$ has dimension $n+1$ and consists of the first spherical harmonics for its round induced metric. The unrestricted Jacobi nullspace is the same space.

Proof

The potential is the positive constant $q=n/s_ c(r)^2$. By Proposition 11.5.7, the nullspace is the Laplace eigenspace with that eigenvalue. The standard spherical harmonic decomposition, used here as an external spectral input, gives eigenvalues $\ell (\ell +n-1)/s_ c(r)^2$, $\ell =0,1,\ldots $, with the $\ell =1$ eigenspace consisting of restrictions of linear functions on the intrinsic round-sphere model. It has dimension $n+1$ and zero mean. The constant mode has eigenvalue zero, so it adds no Jacobi fields in the unrestricted space. This computation concerns infinitesimal nullity and does not assert uniqueness of a nonlinear CMC deformation. $\square$

Lemma 11.6.4 (Position and normal equations in the unit sphere).1562412D4BBC Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset S^{n+1}\subset \mathbb R^{n+2}$ be a smooth two-sided CMC hypersurface with position $x$ and unit normal $\nu $ tangent to $S^{n+1}$. Then

\[ \Delta x=-nx-H\nu ,\qquad \Delta \nu =-H x-|A|^2\nu . \]

For $g_ v=\langle x,v\rangle $, $f_ v=\langle \nu ,v\rangle $ and constant $v\in \mathbb R^{n+2}$, these imply

\[ Jg_ v=|A|^2g_ v-Hf_ v,\qquad Jf_ v=nf_ v-Hg_ v, \qquad J=\Delta +|A|^2+n. \]

Compare [BdCE88], Lemma 3.3, after converting its mean-curvature convention.

Proof

Choose an orthonormal tangent frame geodesic at the point in question. In the ambient Euclidean space,

\[ D_{e_ i}e_ j=\nabla ^\Sigma _{e_ i}e_ j-h_{ij}\nu -\delta _{ij}x, \qquad D_{e_ i}\nu =\sum _ jh_{ij}e_ j. \]

Tracing the first formula gives $\Delta x$. Differentiating the second and tracing gives $\Delta \nu =\nabla H-|A|^2\nu -Hx$, using Codazzi in constant curvature. Since $H$ is constant, the gradient vanishes. Pairing the vector equations with $v$ and adding the potential $|A|^2+n$ gives the claimed Jacobi formulas. The ambient position and hypersurface normal are perpendicular, both of unit length. $\square$

Proposition 11.6.5 (Spherical coordinate tests and the summed defect).70DBFEAC8FA7 For a closed hypersurface in Lemma 11.6.4, put $D=|A|^2-H^2/n$ and

\[ u_ v=g_ v+\frac Hn f_ v. \]

Then $\int _\Sigma u_ v=0$ and $Ju_ v=Dg_ v$. If $v_1,\ldots ,v_{n+2}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb R^{n+2}$, then

\[ \sum _{i=1}^{n+2}Q(u_{v_ i},u_{v_ i})=-\int _\Sigma D\, dA. \]

This is the spherical test family in [BdCE88], proof of Theorem 1.2, pp. 130–131.

Proof

The position equation gives $u_ v=-\Delta g_ v/n$, hence zero integral. The two Jacobi equations give

\[ Ju_ v=(|A|^2-H^2/n)g_ v+(-H+H)f_ v=Dg_ v. \]

Therefore $Q(u_ v,u_ v)=-\int D(g_ v^2+(H/n)f_ vg_ v)$. Summing over the basis uses $\sum _ i g_{v_ i}^2=|x|^2=1$ and $\sum _ i f_{v_ i}g_{v_ i}=\langle \nu ,x\rangle =0$. These pointwise identities give the integral formula. Each summand is an admissible volume-constrained test, whether or not it vanishes identically. $\square$

Theorem 11.6.6 (Volume-stable CMC boundaries in a round sphere).9679240B53E6 For $n\geq 2$, a smooth closed connected embedded two-sided CMC boundary in the unit sphere $S^{n+1}$ is volume-constrained stable if and only if it is a geodesic sphere, including an equator. This is the positive-curvature case of [BdCE88], Theorem 1.2.

Proof

Set $D=|A|^2-H^2/n$. Under stability, every summand in Proposition 11.6.5 is nonnegative, whereas their sum is $-\int D\leq 0$. It follows that $D=0$ everywhere, hence $A=\kappa I$ with the constant $\kappa =H/n$. In $\mathbb R^{n+2}$,

\[ D_ v(\nu -\kappa x)=Av-\kappa v=0 \]

for every tangent vector $v$. Connectedness therefore gives a constant vector $a=\nu -\kappa x$, with $|a|^2=1+\kappa ^2$ and $\langle x,a\rangle =-\kappa $. The intersection of the unit sphere with this affine hyperplane is a connected geodesic sphere: its offset has absolute value $|\kappa |/\sqrt{1+\kappa ^2}<1$. The hypersurface image is open in that intersection by equality of tangent dimensions and closed by compactness, hence equals it. The converse is Theorem 11.6.2. When $\kappa =0$, the hyperplane passes through the origin and the geodesic sphere is an equator. Uniform rescaling gives the corresponding result for every positive ambient constant curvature. $\square$

Example 11.6.7 (An equator separates two notions of minimal stability).8B1EF7479D38 An equator $S^ n\subset S^{n+1}$ is minimal, has $A=0$, and is stable for the enclosed-volume constraint but unstable for unrestricted area variations. Its unrestricted index is one. This example is emphasized in [BdCE88], Remark 3.5.

Proof

At geodesic radius $\pi /2$ the space-form formula gives $H=0$ and $q=n$ because the ambient normal Ricci curvature is $n$. Thus $Q(f,f)=\int (|\nabla f|^2-nf^2)$. The sharp spherical Poincare inequality makes this nonnegative for zero-mean $f$, while $Q(1,1)<0$. The index statement follows as above. In particular the minimal equation $H=0$ does not remove the distinction between testing all normal speeds and testing only those that preserve enclosed volume. $\square$

Example 11.6.8 (Product tori have many volume-preserving unstable directions).B95481624243 Let $p,q\geq 1$, $n=p+q$, $r,s>0$, and $r^2+s^2=1$. The product

\[ \Sigma =S^ p(r)\times S^ q(s)\subset S^{n+1} \]

is a closed embedded CMC hypersurface. Its constrained index is at least $n+2$, and its unrestricted index is at least $n+3$. This supplies explicit unstable tests consistent with Theorem 11.6.6.

Proof

At $(x,y)$ take $\nu =((s/r)x,-(r/s)y)$. It is unit and perpendicular both to the position and to the product tangent space. Differentiating gives principal curvatures $s/r$ in the $p$ first-factor directions and $-r/s$ in the $q$ other directions. Hence $H=ps/r-qr/s$ is constant and

\[ |A|^2+n=\frac p{r^2}+\frac q{s^2}. \]

Each of the $p+1$ first-factor coordinate functions has zero mean and Laplace eigenvalue $p/r^2$, so its quadratic form is $-(q/s^2)\int f^2$. Each of the $q+1$ second-factor coordinates has eigenvalue $q/s^2$ and quadratic form $-(p/r^2)\int f^2$. Coordinate functions in this combined space are mutually orthogonal, and the second-variation form is diagonal on it, including when the two eigenvalues coincide. Thus they span a negative-definite mean-zero space of dimension $p+q+2=n+2$. The constant function is orthogonal to it and gives another negative direction. The coordinate eigenvalue formula follows directly by tracing the Euclidean second fundamental form of each round factor; no full product spectral decomposition is needed. $\square$
