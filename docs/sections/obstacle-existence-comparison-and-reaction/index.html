13.2 Obstacle existence, comparison, and reactionC54B567B0A07

C54B567B0A07

We use the admissible set and the sign convention of Definition 13.1.1: the energy is $\mathcal E_ f(v)=\frac12\int _ U|Dv|^2-\int _ Ufv$, with a lower obstacle. The direct method and one-sided variations already yield existence, comparison, and a reaction measure. The zero-forcing version is developed in [Fig18], Sections 2–3, pp. 2–5; the proofs below retain an $L^2$ forcing term.

Theorem 13.2.1 (Existence and uniqueness for the scalar obstacle problem).A38AB8D7E124 Under the hypotheses of Definition 13.1.1, there is a unique minimizer $u\in K$ of $\mathcal E_ f$. Uniqueness is as an $H^1$ function, hence up to almost-everywhere equality. No regularity of its free boundary is part of this assertion.

Proof

Fix $v_0\in K$ and write $v=v_0+w$, $w\in H^1_0(U)$. Poincare and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[ \mathcal E_ f(v)\geq \tfrac 14\| Dw\| _2^2-C, \]

where $C$ depends on $v_0,f,U$. Thus a minimizing sequence is bounded in $H^1$. Pass to a weakly convergent subsequence. The affine space $g+H^1_0(U)$ is weakly closed. The obstacle constraint is also weakly closed: for every nonnegative $\eta \in L^2(U)$, the inequalities $\int (v_ j-\psi )\eta \geq 0$ pass to the limit and imply $v\geq \psi $ almost everywhere. The squared gradient norm is weakly lower semicontinuous and the forcing term is weakly continuous, so the limit minimizes.

If $u_1,u_2$ minimize, their midpoint is admissible. The parallelogram identity shows that its energy is the average energy minus $\frac18\| D(u_1-u_2)\| _2^2$. Thus the gradients agree. Since $u_1-u_2\in H^1_0(U)$, Poincare gives equality of the functions. This extends the proof of [Fig18], Proposition 2.1, to the stated forcing class. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.2 (The energy gap controls distance to the minimizer).96ECD731A7B8 For the minimizer $u$ and any $v\in K$,

\[ \mathcal E_ f(v)-\mathcal E_ f(u)\geq \frac12\| D(v-u)\| _{L^2(U)}^2. \]

In particular every minimizing sequence converges strongly to $u$ in $H^1(U)$.

Proof

Expand the quadratic energy as in Proposition 13.1.2. Its linear part is nonnegative by the variational inequality, leaving the displayed bound. Poincare controls the $L^2$ norm of $v-u$, since the boundary traces agree. Thus convergence of energies to the minimum implies strong convergence without taking a further subsequence. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.3 (Stability with respect to forcing).7F60F782DF82 Keep $U,g,\psi $ fixed and let $u_ i$ minimize with forcing $f_ i\in L^2(U)$. Then

\[ \| D(u_1-u_2)\| _2\leq \| f_1-f_2\| _{H^{-1}(U)}, \]

where the dual norm uses $\| D\phi \| _2$ on $H^1_0(U)$.

Proof

Use $u_2$ as the competitor for $u_1$ and $u_1$ for $u_2$ in the two variational inequalities. After addition and rearrangement,

\[ \| D(u_1-u_2)\| _2^2\leq \int _ U(f_1-f_2)(u_1-u_2). \]

The difference belongs to $H^1_0(U)$. Apply the dual-norm bound and divide by its gradient norm when this norm is nonzero. The zero case is immediate. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.4 (Comparison for ordered data).72D49D22E1E5 On the same bounded Lipschitz domain, suppose $\psi _1\leq \psi _2$ almost everywhere, the boundary traces satisfy $\operatorname {Tr}g_1\leq \operatorname {Tr}g_2$, and $f_1\leq f_2$ almost everywhere. Assume both admissible classes are nonempty. Then their minimizers satisfy $u_1\leq u_2$ almost everywhere.

Proof

Sobolev truncation and the ordered traces give $w=(u_1-u_2)^+\in H^1_0(U)$. The functions $u_1-w=\min \{ u_1,u_2\} $ and $u_2+w=\max \{ u_1,u_2\} $ are admissible in the first and second problems respectively. The obstacle ordering is needed for the first assertion. Their variational inequalities imply

\[ \int _ U|Dw|^2 =\int _ U(Du_1-Du_2)\cdot Dw \leq \int _ U(f_1-f_2)w\leq 0. \]

Poincare gives $w=0$. The Sobolev positive-part chain rule and compatibility of traces with Lipschitz truncation are the functional-analytic inputs, with background in [SimonPDE], Chapters 5–9. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.5 (The nonnegative reaction measure).0005FEBA4E50 For a minimizer $u$, the distribution

\[ \lambda =-\Delta u-f \]

is a nonnegative Radon measure in $U$. In particular $-\Delta u\geq f$ distributionally. The assertion is local finiteness on compact subsets, not necessarily finite mass on all of $U$.

Proof

If $\phi \in C_ c^\infty (U)$ is nonnegative, $u+t\phi $ is admissible for $t\geq 0$. The variational inequality gives $\langle \lambda ,\phi \rangle =\int Du\cdot D\phi -\int f\phi \geq 0$. A positive distribution has order zero locally: for test functions supported in a compact $K$, choose a smooth nonnegative cutoff $\eta $ equal to one near $K$. Positivity applied to $\| \phi \| _\infty \eta \pm \phi $ bounds $|\langle \lambda ,\phi \rangle |$ by $\| \phi \| _\infty \langle \lambda ,\eta \rangle $. Extension to continuous test functions and the Riesz representation theorem give a nonnegative Radon measure. This is the forced version of [Fig18], Proposition 3.1. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.6 (The equation away from contact).4D161C1BC5E0 Suppose additionally that $u$ and $\psi $ have continuous representatives. On the open set $O=\{ u>\psi \} $,

\[ -\Delta u=f, \]

and the reaction measure vanishes. Consequently its support in $U$ is contained in the contact set $\{ u=\psi \} $.

Proof

A smooth test function compactly supported in $O$ has support on which $u-\psi $ has a positive minimum. Thus both $u+t\phi $ and $u-t\phi $ are admissible for sufficiently small $t>0$. Their variational inequalities give equality in the weak equation. Since the reaction vanishes on every such test function, it vanishes as a measure on $O$. The contact set is relatively closed by continuity. Compare [Fig18], Corollary 3.4. Without a continuous representative, a statement involving pointwise contact sets requires additional care. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.7 (The minimizer is the least admissible supersolution).EE25177AE75F If $v\in K$ satisfies

\[ \int _ U Dv\cdot D\phi \geq \int _ U f\phi \quad \text{for every nonnegative }\phi \in H^1_0(U), \]

then the minimizer $u$ satisfies $u\leq v$ almost everywhere.

Proof

The variational inequality with $u+\phi $ shows that $u$ itself is an admissible supersolution. Set $w=(u-v)^+\in H^1_0(U)$. The competitor $\min \{ u,v\} =u-w$ lies in $K$, so the variational inequality gives $\int Du\cdot Dw\leq \int fw$. The supersolution inequality gives the reverse bound for $Dv$. Subtraction yields $\int |Dw|^2\leq 0$, and Poincare gives the result. This is an order characterization within the stated common boundary class, not an assertion about arbitrary boundary data. $\square$

Proposition 13.2.8 (Subtracting the obstacle and fixing the forcing sign).5294DE479829 Assume $\psi \in C^2(U)$ and put $v=u-\psi $. Locally the new obstacle is zero, and differences of energies between competitors with the same boundary values are those of

\[ \mathcal J_ a(v)=\int \left(\tfrac 12|Dv|^2+av\right), \qquad a=-f-\Delta \psi . \]

On the noncontact region, whenever Proposition 13.2.6 applies, $\Delta v=a$. The normalized model $a=1$ therefore has a positive linear term in its energy.

Proof

Expand $|D(\psi +v)|^2$ and compare two competitors whose difference $w$ lies in $H^1_0$ on a relatively compact subdomain. The cross term is $\int D\psi \cdot Dw=-\int (\Delta \psi )w$. Together with $-\int fw$ this gives $\int aw$. The remaining terms depending only on $\psi $ cancel. The equation follows by subtracting $\Delta \psi $ from $\Delta u=-f$. This is the sign convention behind [Fig18], Section 5; that source begins with $f=0$ and chooses $-\Delta \psi =1$. $\square$
