14.6 Brakke flows and loss of massD4075D89C98E

D4075D89C98E

A weak flow records time-dependent area measures and an inequality expressing dissipation. It may lose mass abruptly. We use an integrated, boundaryless formulation, stating the integrability and time-interval requirements explicitly. This makes the difference from the smooth identity in Proposition 14.4.1 visible. Sources are [WhiMCF15], Section 9, and [CCMS20], Sections 2.4–2.6.

Definition 14.6.1 (Integral Brakke flow without boundary).E7CE50A27004 Let $U\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be open and $I$ an interval. An integral Brakke flow in the formulation used here is a weakly measurable family of Radon measures $(\mu _ t)_{t\in I}$ with the following properties. For almost every $t$, $\mu _ t=\| V_ t\| $ for an integral $n$-varifold, and its first variation has no singular part:

\[ \delta V_ t(X)=-\int \vec H_ t\cdot X\, d\mu _ t, \qquad \vec H_ t\perp T_ xV_ t\quad \mu _ t\text{-almost everywhere}. \]

On each compact $K\subset U$ and compact interval $[a,b]\subset I$ we require uniform local mass bounds and

\[ \sup _{t\in [a,b]}\mu _ t(K)<\infty , \qquad \int _ a^ b\int _ K|\vec H_ t|^2\, d\mu _ t\, dt<\infty . \]

For every such $[a,b]$ and every nonnegative $\phi \in C_ c^1(U\times [a,b])$,

\[ \mu _ b(\phi (\cdot ,b))-\mu _ a(\phi (\cdot ,a)) \leq \int _ a^ b\int \left(\partial _ t\phi +\nabla \phi \cdot \vec H_ t -\phi |\vec H_ t|^2\right)d\mu _ t\, dt. \]

Here $\mu _ t(f)=\int f\, d\mu _ t$. The integral inequality is imposed on every subinterval, not only at almost every pair of endpoints. The mass and curvature bounds are listed as part of the working formulation; compare [WhiMCF15], Definition 9.1 and Corollary 9.4–Theorem 9.5. Prescribed boundary terms are not included in this definition.

Proposition 14.6.2 (A smooth closed flow is a Brakke flow).8F7B94661BFD The area measures of a smooth closed embedded mean curvature flow satisfy Definition 14.6.1. Its integrated inequality holds with equality.

Proof

Each smooth time slice gives a multiplicity-one integral varifold, whose first variation is $-\int \vec H\cdot X$. Smoothness on the compact parameter manifold and a compact time interval gives both local mass and curvature bounds. Integrate Proposition 14.4.1 from $a$ to $b$. The same argument is local for test functions supported away from a boundary. $\square$

Example 14.6.3 (A plane that suddenly disappears).DE579F937CAC Let $P$ be an affine $n$-plane, let $q\geq 1$ be an integer, and fix a time $t_*$. Set $\mu _ t=q\mathcal H^ n\llcorner P$ for $t<t_*$ and $\mu _ t=0$ for $t\geq t_*$. This is an integral Brakke flow. To verify the inequality, note that $\vec H=0$ whenever the measure is nonzero. On an interval crossing $t_*$, the time integral of $\partial _ t\phi $ is

\[ q\int _ P\phi (x,t_*)\, d\mathcal H^ n-q\int _ P\phi (x,a)\, d\mathcal H^ n, \]

while the difference of the endpoint measures is just the negative second term. Nonnegativity of $\phi $ gives the required inequality. On intervals not crossing $t_*$ it is an equality. Thus the Brakke inequality alone permits disappearance even though curvature was zero. Compare [WhiMCF15], Remark 9.2.

Proposition 14.6.4 (A shrinking spatial cutoff).B62A11A57299 Suppose $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$ and $s\leq t$ are flow times. Put

\[ q(x,t)=\bigl (R^2-|x-a|^2-2n(t-s)\bigr )_+, \qquad \phi (x,t)=q(x,t)^3. \]

Then $t\mapsto \mu _ t(\phi (\cdot ,t))$ is nonincreasing for $t\geq s$. In particular, for $0<r<R$ and $0\leq t-s\leq (R^2-r^2)/(4n)$,

\[ \mu _ t(B_ r(a))\leq \left(\frac{2R^2}{R^2-r^2}\right)^3\mu _ s(B_ R(a)). \]

This is a cubic-cutoff version of the local mass estimate in [WhiMCF15], Theorem 9.3.

Proof

The cube of the positive part is $C^2$, with compact spatial support in $U$ for $t\geq s$. On the region where $q>0$, and for any $n$-plane $S$,

\[ \partial _ t\phi =-6nq^2,\qquad \operatorname {tr}_ S D^2\phi =6q|\nabla ^ Sq|^2-6nq^2. \]

Thus $\partial _ t\phi -\operatorname {tr}_ SD^2\phi \leq 0$, also across $q=0$. The first-variation identity with $X=\nabla \phi $ gives $\int \nabla \phi \cdot \vec H_ t\, d\mu _ t=-\int \operatorname {tr}_{T_ xV_ t}D^2\phi \, d\mu _ t$ at almost every time. Insert this in the integrated Brakke inequality and discard $-\phi |\vec H|^2$. This proves monotonicity. On $B_ r(a)$ at the stated times, $q\geq (R^2-r^2)/2$, whereas initially $\phi \leq R^6$. These two bounds give the displayed mass estimate. $\square$

Proposition 14.6.5 (Local curvature energy from the Brakke inequality).420AE237E649 Let $\eta \in C_ c^1(U)$ and $[a,b]\subset I$. Then

\[ \int _ a^ b\int \eta ^2|\vec H_ t|^2\, d\mu _ t\, dt \leq 2\mu _ a(\eta ^2)-2\mu _ b(\eta ^2) +4\int _ a^ b\int |D\eta |^2\, d\mu _ t\, dt. \]

The last integral is controlled by local mass bounds on the support of $\eta $. This is the energy estimate of [WhiMCF15], Theorem 9.5, written with a squared cutoff.

Proof

Use the time-independent test function $\phi =\eta ^2$. Young's inequality gives $2\eta \nabla \eta \cdot \vec H\leq \frac12\eta ^2|\vec H|^2+2|\nabla \eta |^2$. Move the remaining negative curvature term to the other side of the Brakke inequality and multiply by two. In particular a cutoff equal to one on a compact set bounds the spacetime curvature energy there. $\square$

Proposition 14.6.6 (One-sided limits of localized mass).A93E660E56E8 For $\eta \in C_ c^1(U)$ and a compact time interval $[a,b]\subset I$, there is $C<\infty $ such that

\[ t\longmapsto \mu _ t(\eta ^2)-Ct \]

is nonincreasing on $[a,b]$. Consequently its left and right limits exist at interior times, and

\[ \lim _{s\uparrow t}\mu _ s(\eta ^2)\geq \mu _ t(\eta ^2) \geq \lim _{s\downarrow t}\mu _ s(\eta ^2). \]

These inequalities allow downward jumps. They are not continuity in time. Compare [WhiMCF15], Theorems 9.6–9.7.

Proof

Complete the square in the time-independent Brakke integrand:

\[ -\eta ^2|\vec H|^2+2\eta \nabla \eta \cdot \vec H =-|\eta \vec H-\nabla \eta |^2+|\nabla \eta |^2\leq |\nabla \eta |^2. \]

Let $M$ bound $\mu _ t(\operatorname {spt}\eta )$ on the interval, and take $C=M\| D\eta \| _\infty ^2$. The integrated inequality between arbitrary times gives the monotonicity. The one-sided conclusions follow from elementary properties of a monotone real function. $\square$

Definition 14.6.7 (Convergence of integral Brakke flows).70B180C0E800 We say $\mathcal M_ j=(\mu ^ j_ t)$ converges to $\mathcal M=(\mu _ t)$ on a common space-time domain if $\mu ^ j_ t\rightharpoonup \mu _ t$ as Radon measures at every time, and for almost every time one can pass to a further subsequence, possibly depending on the time, for which the associated integral varifolds converge to the varifold of the limiting flow. This is the convention of [CCMS20], Section 2.6. The time-dependent subsequence in the second clause is not a single subsequence with varifold convergence at every time.

Theorem 14.6.8 (Compactness of integral Brakke flows).30AF4D42548B Let $\mathcal M_ j$ be integral Brakke flows on $U\times [a,b]$. Suppose for every compact $K\subset U$,

\[ \sup _ j\sup _{t\in [a,b]}\mu _ t^ j(K)<\infty . \]

Then a subsequence converges to an integral Brakke flow in the sense of Definition 14.6.7. Source: [WhiMCF15], Theorem 9.8, pp. 23–24; compare [CCMS20], Section 2.6. The proof combines the localized mass monotonicity, curvature energy bounds, varifold compactness, and lower semicontinuity of the Brakke functional. That proof remains external. This is weak compactness and does not assert smooth convergence.

Theorem 14.6.9 (Gaussian monotonicity for Brakke flows).7B8AE5E95CE9 Let $\mathcal M$ be an integral Brakke flow on $\mathbb R^{n+1}\times I$. Assume that on each compact time interval its measures have a uniform entropy bound. For $[a,b]\subset I$ and any $t_0>b$,

\[ \int \rho _{x_0,t_0}(x,b)\, d\mu _ b- \int \rho _{x_0,t_0}(x,a)\, d\mu _ a \leq -\int _ a^ b\int \left|\vec H_ t+ \frac{(x-x_0)^\perp }{2(t_0-t)}\right|^2\rho _{x_0,t_0}\, d\mu _ t\, dt. \]

The Gaussian ratios and density are then defined as in Corollary 14.4.4. Entropy is nonincreasing. The entropy bound supplies spatial tail control for using a Gaussian in place of compactly supported test functions; compare Proposition 14.4.9. Sources: [CCMS20], Section 2.5, and [WhiMCF15], Section 10.1. The cutoff and weak lower-semicontinuity argument remains external; equality is not claimed.

Definition 14.6.10 (Unit-regular Brakke flows).9EB9C4E36965 An integral Brakke flow is unit-regular if every interior spacetime point with Gaussian density one has an open spacetime neighborhood on which the flow is a smooth embedded multiplicity-one mean curvature flow. This is a two-sided neighborhood in time. It is an additional property, not part of Definition 14.6.1. See [CCMS20], Section 2.6.

Example 14.6.11 (Density one does not prevent a mass drop).97C8E1218C7D Take the disappearing plane of Example 14.6.3 with $q=1$. At $(x,t_*)$ with $x\in P$, every backward Gaussian ratio is one, so the Gaussian density is one. Nevertheless every two-sided spacetime neighborhood meets the vanished part of the flow. Thus this flow is not unit-regular. A backward regularity statement and a two-sided regularity statement are different at a mass-drop time; compare [WhiMCF15], the discussion after Theorem 11.2.

Definition 14.6.12 (Cyclic integral Brakke flows).262B8C72DD55 An integral Brakke flow is cyclic modulo two if at almost every time its associated rectifiable mod-two flat chain satisfies

\[ \partial [V_ t]=0. \]

Here $[V_ t]$ reduces the integer multiplicity modulo two, as in Definition 6.2.2. This property concerns a flat-chain boundary and does not assert that the varifold is orientable or that the flow is unit-regular. See [CCMS20], Section 2.6, and [White09].
