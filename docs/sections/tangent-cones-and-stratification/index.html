6.3 Tangent cones and stratification54A78170AF99

54A78170AF99

A blow-up records the geometry visible at smaller and smaller scales. Compactness provides limits, monotonicity forces them to be cones, and stratification measures their translational symmetries. Throughout this section $V$ is a stationary integral $n$-varifold in an open $U\subset \mathbb R^{n+k}$, where $n,k\geq 1$.

Definition 6.3.1 (Dilations of a varifold).CC955C20A984 For $a\in U$ and $r>0$, set $\eta _{a,r}(x)=(x-a)/r$. The dilated varifold $V_{a,r}=(\eta _{a,r})_\# V$ is defined on $\eta _{a,r}(U)$ by

\[ \int \varphi (y,S)\, dV_{a,r}(y,S) =r^{-n}\int \varphi ((x-a)/r,S)\, dV(x,S). \]

The factor $r^{-n}$ is the tangential Jacobian of the dilation. This is not the unweighted pushforward of the measure on the Grassmann bundle. See [Minter], Section 1, p. 11.

Proposition 6.3.2 (Mass and stationarity under dilation).989EAE3823D1 For every ball with $B_{rR}(a)\subset U$,

\[ \| V_{a,r}\| (B_ R(0))=r^{-n}\| V\| (B_{rR}(a)). \]

Moreover $V_{a,r}$ is stationary and integral, and $\Theta ^ n(\| V_{a,r}\| ,0)=\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)$.

Proof

The mass identity follows by testing the definition with approximations to the indicator of $B_ R$. If $Y$ is a compactly supported vector field in the dilated domain, put $X(x)=Y((x-a)/r)$. Then $\delta V_{a,r}(Y)=r^{1-n}\delta V(X)=0$. Rectifiable sets and integer multiplicities are preserved by the dilation. Divide the mass identity by $\omega _ n R^ n$ and send $R$ to zero to obtain the density identity. $\square$

Proposition 6.3.3 (Upper semicontinuity of stationary density).04D4F1210FAB If stationary integral varifolds $V_ j$ converge to $V$ in $U$ and $a_ j\to a\in U$, then

\[ \limsup _ j\Theta ^ n(\| V_ j\| ,a_ j)\leq \Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a). \]

In particular the density of a fixed stationary integral varifold is upper semicontinuous in the center. Compare [Minter], Lemma 0.6.

Proof

Fix $s>0$ with $\overline{B_ s(a)}\subset U$ and $\| V\| (\partial B_ s(a))=0$. For $0<\varepsilon <s$ and all large $j$, $B_{s-\varepsilon }(a_ j)\subset B_ s(a)$. Monotonicity bounds the density at $a_ j$ by $\| V_ j\| (B_ s(a))/(\omega _ n(s-\varepsilon )^ n)$. At the chosen continuity radius the numerator converges to $\| V\| (B_ s(a))$. Take the upper limit, then let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, and finally let $s\downarrow 0$ through continuity radii. Only countably many radii can fail the boundary-mass condition. $\square$

Corollary 6.3.4 (Density at every support point).3A22ED5AAEBA For every $a\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| $, $\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)\geq 1$.

Proof

Integrality gives positive integer density at $\| V\| $-almost every point. Every ball about a support point has positive weight, so choose such points tending to $a$. Proposition 6.3.3, applied to the constant sequence $V$, gives the lower bound. This conclusion uses both integrality and stationarity. $\square$

Definition 6.3.5 (Regular and singular points).9EC8D9CF963C A support point $a$ is regular if in some neighborhood $V=q|\Sigma |$, where $\Sigma $ is a smooth embedded $n$-submanifold without boundary there and $q$ is a positive integer. The remaining support points form $\operatorname {sing}V$; the regular set is $\operatorname {reg}V$. Locally constant multiplicity is included in regularity, so a multiple plane is regular. Transverse crossings are singular for this definition. For stationary integral varifolds this agrees with the smooth-support definition using the constancy theorem; see [Minter], Lemma 0.7 and Definitions 0.8–0.9.

Theorem 6.3.6 (Existence and structure of tangent cones).4897B19C7602 Let $a\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| $. For every sequence $r_ j\downarrow 0$, a subsequence of $V_{a,r_ j}$ converges locally as varifolds on $\mathbb R^{n+k}$ to a nonzero stationary integral $n$-varifold $C$. The limit is a cone:

\[ (\eta _{0,s})_\# C=C\quad (s>0),\qquad \| C\| (B_ R(0))=\omega _ n R^ n\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)\quad (R>0). \]

Such limits are called tangent cones; their set is denoted $\operatorname {Tan}(V,a)$. No uniqueness is asserted. Sources: [Minter], Section 1, Lemmas 1.1–1.2 and Definition 1.3. Compactness supplies a limit; the equality case of stationary monotonicity supplies its conical structure. That structural argument remains external.

Definition 6.3.7 (The spine of a stationary cone).19F77FBF48A6 For a nonzero stationary integral cone $C$ in $\mathbb R^{n+k}$, its spine is the set of translation symmetries

\[ S(C)=\{ y\in \mathbb R^{n+k}:(\eta _{y,1})_\# C=C\} . \]

Translation acts on the entire varifold, including multiplicity. See [Minter], Definition 1.4.

Theorem 6.3.8 (Spine structure and splitting).2C1BC96081EA The spine of a nonzero stationary integral $n$-cone is a linear subspace of dimension $d\leq n$, and

\[ S(C)=\{ y:\Theta ^ n(\| C\| ,y)=\Theta ^ n(\| C\| ,0)\} . \]

The vertex has maximal density. After an orthogonal change of coordinates, $C=C_0\times \mathbb R^ d$, where $C_0$ is a stationary integral $(n-d)$-cone; for $d=n$, $C_0$ is a positive integer multiple of the origin. Source: [Minter], Lemma 1.5. The proof remains external.

Definition 6.3.9 (Singular strata).AA22661A81D0 For $0\leq j\leq n$, define

\[ \mathcal S^ j(V)=\{ a\in \operatorname {sing}V: \dim S(C)\leq j\text{ for every }C\in \operatorname {Tan}(V,a)\} . \]

Thus $\mathcal S^0\subset \cdots \subset \mathcal S^ n=\operatorname {sing}V$. The quantifier is over every tangent cone, without assuming tangent-cone uniqueness. A point with a planar tangent cone of higher multiplicity can lie in the top stratum. See [Minter], Definition 1.6.

Theorem 6.3.10 (Dimension bound for the strata).20B7E5509A93 For every stationary integral $n$-varifold and $0\leq j\leq n$,

\[ \dim _{\mathcal H}\mathcal S^ j(V)\leq j. \]

This is the qualitative stratification theorem stated in [Minter], Theorem 1.7, p. 17. Its dimension-reduction and covering proof remains external. The estimate alone does not remove the top stratum or give the codimension-seven bound available under stronger minimizing or stability hypotheses.

Example 6.3.11 (Crossing planes remain stationary).BFB7F867B030 Let $P_1,P_2$ be distinct $n$-dimensional hyperplanes in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ through the origin. The varifold $|P_1|+|P_2|$ is stationary by linearity and Proposition 6.1.4. Its singular set and spine are $P_1\cap P_2$, of dimension $n-1$. The density is two on the intersection and one elsewhere on the support. Thus stationarity and integrality allow a large singular set. Compare [Minter], Sections 0–1.
