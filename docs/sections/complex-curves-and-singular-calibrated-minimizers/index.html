5.6 Complex curves and singular calibrated minimizers46B5EC17B55A

46B5EC17B55A

Complex curves supply explicit mass-minimizing currents with singularities in higher codimension. We prove the complex-dimension-one calibration directly, following [ChMin], Example 16.5, p. 46, and develop the cusp mentioned in [Minter], p. 20. No higher-dimensional Wirtinger inequality or general classification of singular complex varieties is used.

Lemma 5.6.1 (The Euclidean complex two-form has comass one).7DF03FA4481E On $\mathbb C^ k=\mathbb R^{2k}$ with its standard complex structure $J$, let

\[ \omega =\sum _{j=1}^ k dx_ j\wedge dy_ j. \]

Then $d\omega =0$, $\| \omega \| _*=1$, and for an orthonormal pair $(v,w)$,

\[ \omega (v,w)=1\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad w=Jv. \]

Thus equality selects complex lines with their complex orientation.

Proof

The coefficients are constant, so the form is closed. Direct calculation gives $\omega (v,w)=\langle Jv,w\rangle $. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality bounds its absolute value by one; the value one occurs at $(v,Jv)$, since $J$ is orthogonal and $v\perp Jv$. Equality with positive sign in Cauchy–Schwarz is exactly $w=Jv$. This is the two-dimensional calculation in [ChMin], Example 16.5. $\square$

Proposition 5.6.2 (Area and orientation of a holomorphic parametrization).CFFF3E4138B6 Let $F:D\subset \mathbb C\to \mathbb C^ k$ be holomorphic, and write the domain coordinate as $z=s+it$. Then

\[ F_ t=JF_ s,\qquad J_2DF=|F'(z)|^2, \qquad F^*\omega =|F'(z)|^2\, ds\wedge dt. \]

At every point with $F'\ne 0$, the oriented tangent plane is a positively oriented complex line.

Proof

The Cauchy–Riemann equations give the first identity componentwise. The two derivative vectors are orthogonal and have equal length $|F'|$, so their Gram determinant is $|F'|^4$. The area Jacobian is its square root. Lemma 5.6.1 gives $\omega (F_ s,F_ t)=|F_ s|^2$. At a critical point both sides vanish, so the formulas remain valid there. $\square$

Proposition 5.6.3 (Holomorphic disks give calibrated integral currents).BDA9EE059317 Let $D\subset \mathbb C$ be a bounded domain with smooth boundary, and suppose $F$ is holomorphic on a neighborhood of $\overline D$. Orient $D$ by $ds\wedge dt$ and put $T=F_\# \llbracket D\rrbracket $. Then $T$ is a compactly supported integral current,

\[ \partial T=F_\# \llbracket \partial D\rrbracket , \qquad \mathbf M(T)=T(\omega )=\int _ D|F'|^2\, ds\, dt. \]

It minimizes mass among compactly supported finite-mass real two-currents in $\mathbb C^ k$ with the same boundary. The formula counts multiplicity if $F$ is not injective.

Proof

The map is smooth and Lipschitz on a neighborhood of the compact closure; Theorem 5.3.9 gives integrality and the boundary formula. The pushforward representation gives $\mathbf M(T)\leq \int _ DJ_2DF$. Proposition 5.6.2 gives $T(\omega )=\int _ DJ_2DF$, whereas the comass bound gives $T(\omega )\leq \mathbf M(T)$. Equality follows, showing in particular that orientation cancellation cannot reduce the mass. Corollary 5.5.4 gives the minimizing property. Compactly supported cutoff forms justify each evaluation. $\square$

Example 5.6.4 (The complex cusp as an integral minimizer).94391B08C348 For $D=\{ z\in \mathbb C:|z|<1\} $ and $F(z)=(z^2,z^3)$, the current $T=F_\# \llbracket D\rrbracket $ is calibrated and mass minimizing with its prescribed outer boundary. The map is injective, is an immersion away from zero, and has area Jacobian

\[ J_2DF(z)=4|z|^2+9|z|^4. \]

Its image satisfies $w^2=v^3$ in coordinates $(v,w)\in \mathbb C^2$. The origin is not asserted to be a smooth complex submanifold point.

Proof

If two nonzero parameters have the same image, their ratio has both square and cube equal to one, so the parameters agree. Zero has only the zero preimage. The derivative $(2z,3z^2)$ vanishes exactly at zero. Its squared norm gives the displayed Jacobian by Proposition 5.6.2; Proposition 5.6.3 supplies integrality and minimization. The boundary is the smooth embedded outer curve $F(\partial D)$, since the derivative is nonzero there. The algebraic equation follows by substitution. Compare the cusp example of [Minter], p. 20; the parameter convention here interchanges the source's two coordinates. $\square$

Proposition 5.6.5 (Exact density of the calibrated cusp).213862B49C97 Let $V$ be the rectifiable varifold associated to the cusp current in Example 5.6.4. For $0<r<\sqrt2$, let $R\in (0,1)$ solve $R^4+R^6=r^2$. Then

\[ \| V\| (B_ r(0))=2\pi R^4+3\pi R^6, \qquad \frac{\| V\| (B_ r(0))}{\pi r^2} =\frac{2+3R^2}{1+R^2}. \]

In particular $\Theta ^2(\| V\| ,0)=2$.

Proof

The norm squared of $F(z)$ is $|z|^4+|z|^6$, strictly increasing in $|z|$. Thus the preimage of the ambient ball is the parameter disk of radius $R$. Injectivity and the area formula give

\[ \| V\| (B_ r(0))=2\pi \int _0^ R(4t^2+9t^4)t\, dt =2\pi R^4+3\pi R^6. \]

Divide by $\pi (R^4+R^6)$ and let $R\downarrow 0$. The critical point contributes no area atom. $\square$

Proposition 5.6.6 (The cusp has a unique double-plane tangent).C6197AF07C02 For the cusp varifold $V$, the full family of dilations $V_{0,r}$ converges as $r\downarrow 0$ to $2|P|$, where $P=\mathbb C\times \{ 0\} \subset \mathbb C^2$.

Proof

In the dilation integral make the substitution $z=\sqrt r\, \zeta $. The position is then $(\zeta ^2,\sqrt r\, \zeta ^3)$, the plane away from zero is the complex span of $(2\zeta ,3\sqrt r\, \zeta ^2)$, and the rescaled area factor is

\[ 4|\zeta |^2+9r|\zeta |^4. \]

For a continuous Grassmann-bundle test supported over $B_ A(0)$, the contributing parameters satisfy $|\zeta |\leq \sqrt A$, because the first coordinate is $\zeta ^2$. On this fixed bounded disk the area factors are uniformly bounded for $r\leq 1$. Except at the zero parameter the planes converge to $P$, so dominated convergence gives the limit integral with position $(\zeta ^2,0)$ and factor $4|\zeta |^2$. The planar area formula for $\zeta \mapsto \zeta ^2$ identifies this with twice the integral over $P$; every nonzero image point has two preimages. The calculation holds along the full limit $r\downarrow 0$, proving uniqueness as a varifold. $\square$

Corollary 5.6.7 (A singular calibrated surface with a flat tangent).44CA40B3EEB1 The cusp varifold is stationary away from its outer boundary, smooth at every nonzero interior point, and singular at zero. Its origin is a flat singular point of density two with a unique tangent cone. Thus higher-codimension mass minimization permits this type of flat singularity.

Proof

Stationarity follows from Proposition 5.5.6. Injectivity and immersion give smoothness locally away from zero. If the support were a smooth embedded surface near zero, Proposition 5.6.6 would make its tangent plane horizontal, so it would be a single graph over $P$ on a smaller neighborhood. For each sufficiently small nonzero $v$, however, the two parameters solving $z^2=v$ give opposite nonzero values of $w=z^3$. Both points lie in that neighborhood, contradicting the graph property. Propositions 5.6.5 and 5.6.6 give the stated density and tangent. This is a concrete example under Definition 6.10.7, not a classification of all flat singularities. $\square$
