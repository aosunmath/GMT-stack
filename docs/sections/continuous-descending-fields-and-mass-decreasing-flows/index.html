8.6 Continuous descending fields and mass-decreasing flowsD6479908381E

D6479908381E

We give a continuous deformation underlying [MNIndex], Section 2.8, and the outline in [LiAP], Section 4.2. Fix a smooth closed connected $M^{n+1}$, $n\geq 1$, a mass bound $C>0$, and $K_ C,S_ C$ from Definition 8.5.1. Smooth norms are fixed using a finite atlas or a background connection. The construction below uses a uniformly summable selection of fields, so a locally finite partition of unity is unnecessary.

Lemma 8.6.1 (A negative first variation persists on a neighborhood).FEE57F22C3EB For every $V\in K_ C\setminus S_ C$ there are a smooth vector field $X_ V$, a number $b_ V>0$, and an open neighborhood

\[ U_ V=\{ W\in K_ C:\delta W(X_ V)<-b_ V\} \]

of $V$, disjoint from $S_ C$.

Proof

Nonstationarity gives a smooth field with nonzero first variation. Change its sign if necessary to make the variation negative, and choose $b_ V$ strictly between zero and $-\delta V(X_ V)$. Continuity from Lemma 8.5.2 makes $U_ V$ open. A stationary varifold has zero first variation on this field, so cannot belong to $U_ V$. $\square$

Lemma 8.6.2 (A continuous selection of smooth descending fields).ED073D8BA201 There is a map $V\mapsto Y_ V$ from $K_ C$ to smooth vector fields, continuous in every $C^ k$ norm and uniformly bounded in each such norm, with $Y_ V=0$ for $V\in S_ C$ and $\delta V(Y_ V)<0$ for $V\notin S_ C$.

Proof

The open subspace $K_ C\setminus S_ C$ is second countable, so the cover in Lemma 8.6.1 has a countable subcover $U_ i$, with associated fields $X_ i$. If this subspace is empty use $Y=0$. Otherwise, repeating sets if the cover is finite, define

\[ \chi _ i(V)=\frac{d(V,K_ C\setminus U_ i)}{1+d(V,K_ C\setminus U_ i)},\qquad B_ i=\max \{ 1,\| X_ i\| _{C^0},\ldots ,\| X_ i\| _{C^ i}\} ,\qquad Y_ V=\sum _{i=1}^\infty 2^{-i}\chi _ i(V)X_ i/B_ i. \]

The complements are nonempty because they contain zero. Each $\chi _ i$ is continuous, vanishes off $U_ i$, and is positive on $U_ i$. For any fixed $k$, the tail starting at $i\geq k$ is bounded in $C^ k$ by the summable series $\sum 2^{-i}$. Thus the series converges uniformly in every $C^ k$ norm and defines a spatially smooth field; uniform convergence of the continuous finite sums proves continuity of the selection and uniform bounds. On $S_ C$ every coefficient vanishes. For other $V$, all terms in $\delta V(Y_ V)$ are nonpositive and at least one is strictly negative. Passing first variation through the sum is justified by its $C^1$ convergence and the bound in Lemma 8.5.2. $\square$

Lemma 8.6.3 (A continuous stationarity defect).3E3D48604A0F For the selection in Lemma 8.6.2, set $a(V)=-\delta V(Y_ V)$. Then $a$ is continuous and nonnegative on $K_ C$, and its zero set is exactly $S_ C$. On any nonempty set $\{ V:d(V,S_ C)\geq \eta \} $ with $\eta >0$, it has a strictly positive minimum.

Proof

Joint continuity of first variation and $C^1$ continuity of $Y$ give continuity of $a$. Its sign and zero set follow from the selection. The set a positive distance from $S_ C$ is compact and contains no zero of $a$, so the extreme value theorem gives a positive minimum. This defect is auxiliary and depends on the chosen fields; it is not being identified with a norm of generalized mean curvature. $\square$

Lemma 8.6.4 (Uniform Taylor control along the selected flows).CC858385ED7D Let $\varphi _ t^ V$ be the flow of the fixed field $Y_ V$ and let $A_ V(t)=\| (\varphi _ t^ V)_\# V\| (M)$. There is $B\geq 1$, independent of $V\in K_ C$, such that for $0\leq t\leq 1$,

\[ A'_ V(0)=-a(V),\qquad |A''_ V(t)|\leq B,\qquad A'_ V(t)\leq -a(V)+Bt. \]
Proof

Smooth ODE flow existence and dependence on a vector field in smooth norms are the differential-equation inputs. The fields are complete since $M$ is compact, and have uniform bounds in every smooth norm. Consequently their flows and the needed spatial and time derivatives are uniformly bounded for $0\leq t\leq 1$. The rectifiable plane Jacobian, defined for every $P\in G_ n(M)$ regardless of rectifiability of $V$, gives

\[ A_ V(t)=\int _{G_ n(M)}J_ P\varphi _ t^ V(x)\, dV(x,P). \]

The first derivative at zero is the first-variation formula. The second time derivative of the Jacobian is bounded uniformly on $K_ C\times [0,1]\times G_ n(M)$: it is a continuous expression in the flow derivatives, and the flow differential stays invertible with a uniform inverse bound on this interval. Integrating this bound against mass at most $C$ gives $|A''_ V|\leq B$, after enlarging $B$ to at least one. Integration in time yields the last inequality. $\square$

Proposition 8.6.5 (A continuous time choice with a positive mass drop).8B58860B5F4F Fix $0<\tau _0\leq 1$ and put

\[ \tau (V)=\min \{ \tau _0,a(V)/(2B)\} ,\qquad D(V)=\tfrac 12 a(V)\tau (V),\qquad \mathscr H(s,V)=(\varphi _{s\tau (V)}^ V)_\# V. \]

Then $\mathscr H:[0,1]\times K_ C\to K_ C$ is continuous, fixes every stationary varifold, starts at the identity, and has nonincreasing mass in $s$. At its endpoint,

\[ \| \mathscr H(1,V)\| (M)\leq \| V\| (M)-D(V). \]

The continuous function $D$ vanishes exactly on $S_ C$.

Proof

For $0\leq t\leq \tau (V)$, Lemma 8.6.4 gives $A'_ V(t)\leq -a(V)/2$. Integrate from zero to $s\tau (V)$ to obtain monotonicity and the endpoint estimate. For stationary $V$, both the field and the chosen time are zero. For nonstationary $V$, both $a$ and $\tau $ are positive, so $D>0$. Continuity of the fields and times gives $C^1$ continuity of the diffeomorphisms in $(s,V)$. Lemma 8.5.5 gives continuity of $\mathscr H$. Its mass bound shows it stays in $K_ C$. $\square$

Corollary 8.6.6 (Uniform loss away from stationary varifolds).D31BFD6C3534 For every $\eta >0$ such that $K_\eta =\{ V\in K_ C:d(V,S_ C)\geq \eta \} $ is nonempty, there is $d_\eta >0$ with

\[ \| \mathscr H(1,V)\| (M)\leq \| V\| (M)-d_\eta \quad (V\in K_\eta ). \]

In particular, if the mass losses of a sequence tend to zero, every convergent subsequence of the original varifolds has stationary limit.

Proof

Take $d_\eta =\min _{K_\eta }D>0$ using compactness and positivity from Proposition 8.6.5. If $V_ j\to V$ and the actual losses tend to zero, the bound $0\leq D(V_ j)\leq \| V_ j\| (M)-\| \mathscr H(1,V_ j)\| (M)$ implies $D(V)=0$. Thus $V\in S_ C$. A fixed deformation and its fixed positive defect are used here; arbitrarily small losses for a changing sequence of unrelated deformations would not yield this conclusion. $\square$

Lemma 8.6.7 (Uniform closeness to the identity).52267311276E The choice of $\tau _0$ in Proposition 8.6.5 can make the deformation arbitrarily close to the identity in $\mathbf F_ V$, uniformly on $[0,1]\times K_ C$. For every integral mod-two cycle $T$ with mass at most $C$, the corresponding diffeomorphisms also satisfy

\[ \mathcal F((\varphi _ t^{|T|})_\# T-T)\leq A t\mathbf M(T),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1, \]

with $A$ independent of $T$.

Proof

Uniform smooth bounds give $d(\varphi _ t(x),x)\leq A_1t$, displacement of the tangent plane at most $A_1t$, and $|J_ P\varphi _ t-1|\leq A_1t$, with a fixed smooth metric on $G_ n(M)$. For a test function of absolute value and Lipschitz constant at most one, subtract the pushforward and original integrals to bound their difference by $A_2Ct$. Taking the supremum gives the varifold estimate. For cycles, use the external mod-two homotopy formula for $h(u,x)=\varphi _ u^{|T|}(x)$ on $[0,t]\times M$. Its time derivative is uniformly bounded and its spatial derivatives are bounded, hence the swept $(n+1)$-chain has mass at most $At\mathbf M(T)$. Its boundary is the required difference because $\partial T=0$. Taking $t\leq \tau _0$ makes the sum of the two bounds arbitrarily small. No mass-distance bound follows from this argument; Example 8.5.6 rules one out in general. $\square$

Theorem 8.6.8 (A continuous pull-tight homotopy on boundary cycles).90DB4B31CDD9 Let $M^{n+1}$ be smooth, closed and connected, $n\geq 1$, and let $C,\varepsilon >0$. On the mass-at-most-$C$ part of $\mathcal Z_ n^0(M;\mathbb Z_2)$ there is a jointly $\mathbf F$-continuous homotopy $H(s,T)$ which starts at $T$, fixes $T$ whenever $|T|$ is stationary, has nonincreasing mass in $s$, and satisfies

\[ \mathbf M(H(1,T))\leq \mathbf M(T)-D(|T|),\qquad \mathbf F(H(s,T),T)\leq \varepsilon , \]

where $D$ is continuous on $K_ C$ and positive precisely off $S_ C$. This proves the continuous deformation stated in [MNIndex], Section 2.8, from the current operations and ODE inputs specified above.

Proof

Choose $\tau _0$ using Lemma 8.6.7 and define $H(s,T)=(\varphi _{s\tau (|T|)}^{|T|})_\# T$. Diffeomorphic pushforward preserves integral mod-two cycles and commutes with boundary. In particular if $T=\partial U$, its image is the boundary of the image of $U$, so remains in the boundary component. The Jacobian formula with no cancellation identifies $|H(s,T)|=\mathscr H(s,|T|)$. Proposition 8.6.5 supplies mass monotonicity, the drop, and the fixed-point properties. Lemma 8.6.7 supplies the distance estimate. Finally $\mathbf F$ convergence of cycles gives weak convergence of their varifolds, hence $C^1$ convergence of the selected diffeomorphisms. Lemma 8.5.5 proves joint $\mathbf F$ continuity. No discrete mass-fineness assertion is used or proved. $\square$
