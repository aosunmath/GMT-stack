7.6 Positive Jacobi fields and graphical convergence0E19712752FF

0E19712752FF

The stability inequality, the linearized mean-curvature equation, and the separation of nearby sheets are different statements. This section develops their connections. We use the scalar convention $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $ from Definition 7.1.1.

Definition 7.6.1 (Stability on a noncompact hypersurface).35808222AC5B Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth two-sided minimal hypersurface in a smooth Riemannian manifold, possibly noncompact or with boundary. Put $q=|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu )$ and $J=\Delta _\Sigma +q$. Stability means

\[ Q(\phi ,\phi )=\int _\Sigma (|\nabla \phi |^2-q\phi ^2)\, dA\geq 0 \quad \text{for all }\phi \in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma \setminus \partial \Sigma ). \]

Thus stability on an open subset only tests variations supported there. The compactly supported second-variation formula is the local version of Theorem 7.1.3; see [ChMin], Theorem 23.4.

Proposition 7.6.2 (Linearization of scalar mean curvature).E4E4294F8594 Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, two-sided, and minimal. For a normal variation with velocity $f\nu $, differentiating scalar mean curvature at $t=0$ gives

\[ \left.\partial _ t H_{\Sigma _ t}\right|_{t=0}=-Jf. \]

The sign agrees with $Q(f,f)=-\int fJf$ and with the first variation $\delta A(f\nu )=\int Hf$.

Proof

Take a two-parameter normal graph variation with initial velocities $f\nu $ and $\phi \nu $, both compactly supported. Differentiate its first-variation identity in the other parameter. Terms differentiating the area form or normal component are multiplied by $H=0$, leaving $\int \phi \, \partial _ tH$. The mixed second derivative is the polarization of the second variation, $Q(f,\phi )=-\int \phi Jf$. Since $\phi $ is arbitrary, the identity follows pointwise. For a general smooth $f$, insert cutoffs near the point being tested. Compare [ChMin], the proof of Proposition 33.3, pp. 80–81. $\square$

Proposition 7.6.3 (Positive Jacobi fields imply stability).F531FEF8EC1D If $w>0$ is smooth on a two-sided minimal hypersurface and $Jw=0$, then for every compactly supported smooth $\phi $ in its interior,

\[ Q(\phi ,\phi )=\int _\Sigma w^2\left|\nabla \left(\frac\phi w\right)\right|^2dA\geq 0. \]

In particular the hypersurface is stable in the sense of Definition 7.6.1.

Proof

Set $\eta =\phi /w$. Expanding $|\nabla (w\eta )|^2$ and subtracting $q w^2\eta ^2$, the terms other than $w^2|\nabla \eta |^2$ integrate to $\int \nabla w\cdot \nabla (w\eta ^2)-qw^2\eta ^2=-\int w\eta ^2(\Delta w+qw)=0$. There is no boundary term because $\eta $ has compact support. This identity makes explicit the positive-Jacobi-field argument of [ChMin], Proposition 33.4. $\square$

Corollary 7.6.4 (The vertical angle makes a minimal graph stable).6FAB89AC045D For a smooth minimal graph in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ with upward normal $\nu $, the function

\[ w=\langle \nu ,e_{n+1}\rangle =(1+|Du|^2)^{-1/2} \]

is a positive Jacobi function. Hence the graph is stable under all compactly supported normal variations.

Proof

Vertical translations preserve minimality. Their normal speed is $w$; their tangential part differentiates the identically zero mean curvature and contributes zero. Proposition 7.6.2 therefore gives $Jw=0$. Apply Proposition 7.6.3. This also follows from the minimizing property in Proposition 7.2.13. $\square$

Theorem 7.6.5 (Separated sheets produce a positive Jacobi field).BB7E1EEFDC4F Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth connected two-sided embedded minimal hypersurface in a smooth Riemannian manifold. Let $U_1\Subset U_2\Subset \cdots $ be connected relatively compact open subsets exhausting its interior, and fix $p\in U_1$. Suppose smooth functions $u_{1,j}<u_{2,j}$ on $U_ j$ define minimal normal graphs by the normal exponential map, with both functions converging to zero in $C^\infty $ on every compact subset of $\Sigma \setminus \partial \Sigma $. Then a subsequence of

\[ v_ j=\frac{u_{2,j}-u_{1,j}}{(u_{2,j}-u_{1,j})(p)} \]

converges in $C^\infty $ on compact subsets to a function $v>0$ with $v(p)=1$ and $Jv=0$. Consequently the interior of $\Sigma $ is stable. This is the normal-graph formulation of [ChMin], Proposition 33.3, including its stated Riemannian extension. The local proof below retains Harnack and Schauder estimates as external analytic inputs. The conclusion assumes two globally ordered sheets on the exhaustion, not merely a numerical multiplicity at one point.

Proof

By Lemma 7.10.3, the positive gaps $w_ j=u_{2,j}-u_{1,j}$ solve linear equations $L_ jw_ j=0$, with smooth coefficients converging on compact subsets to those of $J$. Division by the positive value $w_ j(p)$ preserves the equation. Lemma 7.10.2 gives a subsequential smooth local limit $v>0$ with $v(p)=1$ and $Jv=0$. Proposition 7.6.3 then gives stability on the interior. The exhaustion hypothesis ensures that every compact set used in the Harnack and Schauder arguments is eventually in the domain of both graphs. No conclusion about extension across a singularity is needed for this theorem. $\square$

Theorem 7.6.6 (Stable curvature estimate in dimensions at most six).0B9CCB2E229B Let $2\leq n\leq 6$. Suppose $\Sigma ^ n$ is a smooth properly embedded two-sided minimal hypersurface without boundary in $B_{2R}^{n+1}(a)$, is stable for compactly supported normal variations there, and satisfies

\[ \mathcal H^ n(\Sigma \cap B_{2R}(a))\leq \Lambda R^ n. \]

Then $\sup _{\Sigma \cap B_ R(a)}|B|\leq C(n,\Lambda )/R$. This is the smooth Euclidean specialization of Schoen–Simon [SS81], as stated in [Sharp17], Theorem 2.1, pp. 320–321. Applying that local estimate at points of $\Sigma \cap B_ R(a)$ gives the stated interior bound. Smoothness removes the singular-set hypothesis in that source; for singular hypersurfaces the hypothesis must be restored. The area bound and dimension restriction are retained. The proof remains external.
