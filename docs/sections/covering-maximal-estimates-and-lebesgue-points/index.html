2.5 Covering, maximal estimates, and Lebesgue points33CC01B32E56

33CC01B32E56

Small-ball averages recover a function almost everywhere, but can behave very differently on the support of a singular measure. We prove the Euclidean Lebesgue-measure statements directly from a covering argument. The source is [Sim18], Chapter 1, Lemma 3.4 and Sections 3–4, printed pp. 15–24. Differentiation relative to an arbitrary Radon measure requires additional covering theory and is not asserted here. Balls in this section are open.

Lemma 2.5.1 (Fivefold covering for a bounded family of Euclidean balls).9B745ADFD001 Let $\mathcal B$ be a family of balls of positive radii in $\mathbb R^ n$, with bounded centers and uniformly bounded radii. There is a finite or countable pairwise disjoint subfamily $B_ j=B_{r_ j}(x_ j)$ such that

\[ \bigcup _{B\in \mathcal B}B\subset \bigcup _ j B_{5r_ j}(x_ j). \]

Moreover every original ball intersects a selected ball whose radius is at least half its own radius. This is a bounded Euclidean version of [Sim18], Lemma 3.4.

Proof

If no balls remain, stop. Otherwise select a remaining ball whose radius is greater than half the supremum of the remaining radii, and discard all remaining balls intersecting it. Continue. The selected balls are disjoint and lie in a fixed bounded region. If the process is infinite, their volumes have finite sum, so their radii tend to zero. An original ball of radius $r>0$ cannot remain forever: while it remains, every selected radius is greater than $r/2$, contradicting that limit. Thus it is selected or discarded at some step. If it intersects $B_{r_ j}(x_ j)$ at that step, its radius is at most $2r_ j$, so each of its points lies within distance $r_ j+2r\leq 5r_ j$ of $x_ j$. This proves both assertions, with the open-ball inequalities giving containment in the open dilation. $\square$

Definition 2.5.2 (Centered maximal functions).E382FA55D5F1 For a nonnegative Radon measure $\nu $ on $\mathbb R^ n$ define

\[ M\nu (x)=\sup _{r>0}\frac{\nu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ n r^ n}. \]

For $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^ n)$ write $Mf=M(|f|\, dx)$. The value may be infinite. These are measurable functions: for fixed $r$, Fatou's lemma makes $x\mapsto \nu (B_ r(x))$ lower semicontinuous, and the supremum retains this property. This maximal quantity controls the upper density defined in [Sim18], Chapter 1, equation (3.1).

Proposition 2.5.3 (The weak maximal estimate for finite measures).10768DA6E59B If $\nu $ is a finite nonnegative Radon measure on $\mathbb R^ n$ and $\lambda >0$, then

\[ \mathcal L^ n(\{ M\nu >\lambda \} )\leq \frac{5^ n}{\lambda }\nu (\mathbb R^ n). \]

In particular $M\nu <\infty $ almost everywhere for Lebesgue measure. The constant is not claimed to be sharp.

Proof

For each $x\in \{ M\nu >\lambda \} \cap B_ R(0)$ choose a ball centered at $x$ with $\nu (B_ r(x))>\lambda \omega _ n r^ n$. Its radius is bounded in terms of $\nu (\mathbb R^ n)$ and $\lambda $. Lemma 2.5.1 gives disjoint selected balls covering the set after fivefold dilation. Therefore its Lebesgue measure is at most

\[ 5^ n\sum _ j\omega _ n r_ j^ n \leq \frac{5^ n}{\lambda }\sum _ j\nu (B_{r_ j}(x_ j)) \leq \frac{5^ n}{\lambda }\nu (\mathbb R^ n). \]

Let $R\to \infty $. Letting $\lambda \to \infty $ then shows that the infinite-value set is null. If the measure is zero, the assertions are immediate. This is the maximal-estimate application of the covering principle in [Sim18], Lemma 3.4. $\square$

Theorem 2.5.4 (A strong maximal bound above the endpoint).4A2AA689E78F For $1<p<\infty $ and $f\in L^ p(\mathbb R^ n)$,

\[ \| Mf\| _ p^ p\leq \frac{2^ p5^ n p}{p-1}\| f\| _ p^ p. \]

This consequence of the covering estimate associated with [Sim18], Lemma 3.4, is proved here by integrating distribution functions.

Proof

Fix $\lambda >0$. Split $|f|$ into the part bounded by $\lambda /2$ and the tail $g_\lambda =|f|\mathbf1_{\{ |f|>\lambda /2\} }$. The first part has maximal function at most $\lambda /2$. The tail is integrable since $|f|\leq (\lambda /2)^{1-p}|f|^ p$ on its support. Subadditivity and Proposition 2.5.3 give

\[ \mathcal L^ n(\{ Mf>\lambda \} ) \leq \frac{2\cdot 5^ n}{\lambda }\int _{\{ |f|>\lambda /2\} }|f|\, dx. \]

The identity $a^ p=\int _0^ a p\lambda ^{p-1}\, d\lambda $, followed by Tonelli's theorem, gives

\[ \int (Mf)^ p\leq 2\cdot 5^ n p\int |f(x)| \left(\int _0^{2|f(x)|}\lambda ^{p-2}\, d\lambda \right)dx. \]

Evaluation of the inner integral proves the claim. Tonelli applies before finiteness is known because the integrands are nonnegative. Local integrability of $f$, needed to define $Mf$, follows from Holder's inequality on bounded sets. $\square$

Definition 2.5.5 (Lebesgue points).6B471E3298DE For a finite-valued representative of $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(U)$, a point $x\in U$ is a Lebesgue point if

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\omega _ n r^ n} \int _{B_ r(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|\, dy=0, \]

with radii small enough that the ball lies in $U$. Altering values on a null set can change individual Lebesgue points but not the almost-everywhere conclusion below. This is the absolute-oscillation form of differentiation associated with [Sim18], Corollary 4.10.

Theorem 2.5.6 (Lebesgue differentiation with absolute oscillation).2CA07074F4A7 If $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(U)$, almost every point of $U$ is a Lebesgue point of any representative finite almost everywhere. Consequently

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\omega _ n r^ n}\int _{B_ r(x)}f(y)\, dy=f(x) \quad \text{for almost every }x\in U. \]

Compare [Sim18], Corollary 4.10. Here the proof is restricted to Lebesgue measure on a Euclidean open set.

Proof

First take $U=\mathbb R^ n$ and $f\in L^1$. Denote the limsup in Definition 2.5.5 by $Df(x)$. For $g\in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$, continuity gives $Dg=0$, so the triangle inequality implies

\[ Df(x)\leq M(f-g)(x)+|f(x)-g(x)|. \]

The weak maximal estimate and Chebyshev's inequality yield, for $a>0$,

\[ \mathcal L^ n(\{ Df>2a\} )\leq \frac{5^ n+1}{a}\| f-g\| _1. \]

These sets are measurable: for each point with finite $f(x)$ the ball integrals vary continuously with the positive radius, so the limsup can be taken over rational radii; joint measurability follows from integration of the measurable integrand. By Theorem 2.3.8, choose $g$ with arbitrarily small $L^1$ error. Thus $\{ Df>2a\} $ is null for each $a>0$. A countable sequence $a\downarrow 0$ proves $Df=0$ almost everywhere.

For the local assertion, choose compact sets $K_ m$ with $K_ m\subset \operatorname {int}K_{m+1}$ exhausting $U$. Multiply $f$ by a compactly supported cutoff equal to one near $K_ m$, and extend by zero outside $U$. This gives an $L^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ function agreeing with $f$ near $K_ m$. The global result applies on each $K_ m$; their countable union gives the local assertion. Finally the absolute value of the difference between the average of $f$ and $f(x)$ is bounded by the average absolute oscillation. $\square$

Corollary 2.5.7 (The Lebesgue density theorem for measurable sets).C69197CB30B1 For every Lebesgue measurable $E\subset \mathbb R^ n$,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal L^ n(E\cap B_ r(x))}{\omega _ n r^ n} =\mathbf1_ E(x)\quad \text{for almost every }x\in \mathbb R^ n. \]

Neither finite total measure nor a regular boundary is assumed. See [Sim18], Corollary 3.10.

Proof

The indicator belongs to $L^1_{\rm loc}$. Apply Theorem 2.5.6 to it. At a point in $E$, its absolute oscillation average is the complementary volume fraction; outside $E$, it is the volume fraction of $E$ itself. The conclusion is almost everywhere in ambient Lebesgue measure, and makes no assertion at every topological boundary point. $\square$

Proposition 2.5.8 (Approximate continuity at a Lebesgue point).6196A7F42C62 If $x$ is a Lebesgue point of $f$ and $a>0$, then

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0} \frac{\mathcal L^ n(\{ y\in B_ r(x):|f(y)-f(x)|>a\} )}{\omega _ n r^ n}=0. \]
Proof

Chebyshev's inequality bounds the displayed fraction by $a^{-1}$ times the average of $|f(y)-f(x)|$ over the ball. That average tends to zero. Thus values far from $f(x)$ occupy a vanishing fraction of small balls; ordinary pointwise continuity is a stronger condition. $\square$

Proposition 2.5.9 (Mollifiers converge at every Lebesgue point).C54A732E0D36 Let $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^ n)$ and let $\rho $ be a bounded measurable function supported in $B_1$, with $\int \rho =1$. Put $\rho _\varepsilon (z)=\varepsilon ^{-n}\rho (z/\varepsilon )$. At every Lebesgue point $x$ of $f$,

\[ (\rho _\varepsilon *f)(x)\longrightarrow f(x). \]

Nonnegativity and smoothness of $\rho $ are not needed for this pointwise assertion.

Proof

Subtract $f(x)\int \rho _\varepsilon =f(x)$ from the convolution and estimate its absolute value by

\[ \| \rho \| _\infty \varepsilon ^{-n} \int _{B_\varepsilon (x)}|f(y)-f(x)|\, dy. \]

This is $\omega _ n\| \rho \| _\infty $ times a quantity tending to zero. The smooth-density approximation of measures in Proposition 2.4.8 concerns compact-test convergence; the present assertion describes values of mollified functions at individual Lebesgue points. $\square$

Proposition 2.5.10 (A singular measure has zero ambient density almost everywhere).752D2F6CE2C7 Let $\nu $ be a nonnegative Radon measure on $\mathbb R^ n$ singular with respect to $\mathcal L^ n$. Then

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\nu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ n r^ n}=0 \quad \text{for }\mathcal L^ n\text{-almost every }x. \]

This is a special case of the upper-density conclusions in [Sim18], Theorem 3.8 and Remark 3.9.

Proof

First assume $\nu $ is finite, and choose a Borel null set $N$ carrying all its mass. Given $\varepsilon >0$, inner regularity gives compact $K\subset N$ with $\nu (\mathbb R^ n\setminus K)<\varepsilon $. For $x\notin K$, all sufficiently small balls avoid $K$, so the upper density of $\nu $ at $x$ is at most $M(\nu \llcorner (\mathbb R^ n\setminus K))(x)$. Proposition 2.5.3 implies that the set where this upper density exceeds $a>0$ has Lebesgue measure at most $5^ n\varepsilon /a$, since $K$ is null. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and then take countably many $a\downarrow 0$.

For locally finite $\nu $, apply the finite result to its restriction to $B_{m+1}(0)$ and consider points of $B_ m(0)$. Restriction does not change sufficiently small balls there. Taking the union over positive integers $m$ proves the assertion. Restrictions to these Borel sets remain Radon measures. $\square$

Corollary 2.5.11 (Ball densities identify an absolutely continuous part).E5945F5D2962 Suppose a nonnegative Radon measure is written $\mu =f\, dx+\nu $ with $0\leq f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^ n)$ and $\nu \perp \mathcal L^ n$. Then

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{\omega _ n r^ n}=f(x) \quad \text{for }\mathcal L^ n\text{-almost every }x. \]
Proof

Add the limits supplied by Theorem 2.5.6 and Proposition 2.5.10, outside the union of their null exceptional sets. The decomposition is a hypothesis of this statement; existence of the Lebesgue decomposition and the Radon–Nikodym theorem are not proved by this argument. Compare [Sim18], Section 4, especially Theorem 4.18. $\square$

Example 2.5.12 (The maximal operator need not preserve integrability).798D622C77F0 Let $f=\mathbf1_{B_1(0)}$ on $\mathbb R^ n$. Then $f\in L^1$ but $Mf\notin L^1$.

Proof

For $|x|\geq 2$, the ball $B_{|x|+1}(x)$ contains $B_1(0)$, so

\[ Mf(x)\geq (|x|+1)^{-n}\geq (3|x|/2)^{-n}. \]

The integral of $|x|^{-n}$ over $\{ |x|\geq 2\} $ diverges: polar coordinates reduce it to a positive constant times $\int _2^\infty r^{-1}\, dr$. This separates the weak estimate in Proposition 2.5.3 from the strong estimates for $p>1$ in Theorem 2.5.4. $\square$
