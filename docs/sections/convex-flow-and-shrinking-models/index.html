14.2 Convex flow and shrinking modelsB6287A1ABCC9

B6287A1ABCC9

A round sphere is a model for a flow singularity, but the theory must explain when such a model is forced and when other models can occur. The supplied papers of Huisken [Hui86] and Chodosh–Choi–Mantoulidis–Schulze [CCMS20] provide two entry points.

Theorem 14.2.1 (Convex Euclidean hypersurfaces become round).7CECF8A8FAC9 Let $\Sigma _0^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth, connected, closed, embedded, strictly convex hypersurface, with $n\geq 2$. Its mean curvature flow exists smoothly until a finite time $T$, contracts to a single point, and, after dilation about that point to keep the area fixed, converges smoothly to a round sphere as $t\uparrow T$. This is the Euclidean specialization of [Hui86], Theorem 1.1. The introduction of that article attributes the Euclidean result to Huisken's earlier work. In a curved ambient manifold, the theorem requires quantitative pinching relative to ambient curvature; mere convexity is not substituted for those hypotheses. The proof remains external.

Definition 14.2.2 (Self-shrinkers).F2183BE1F075 A smooth hypersurface $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ is a self-shrinker with center zero and scale one when

\[ \vec H+\frac12 x^\perp =0. \]

Here $x^\perp $ is the normal projection of the position vector. The associated family is $\Sigma _ t=\sqrt{-t}\, \Sigma $ for $t<0$, evolving by mean curvature in its normal velocity. This normalization matches [CCMS20], Sections 1.2 and 2.

Proposition 14.2.3 (Round shrinking cylinders).B31B3405D469 For $1\leq k\leq n$, the cylinder $S^ k(\sqrt{2k})\times \mathbb R^{n-k}\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ is a self-shrinker. A hyperplane through the origin is also a self-shrinker.

Proof

On the radius-$r$ cylinder, let $\nu $ be the outward radial normal to the spherical factor. There are $k$ principal curvatures equal to $1/r$ and $n-k$ equal to zero. Our convention gives $\vec H=-(k/r)\nu $ and $x^\perp =r\nu $. The shrinker equation is therefore $-k/r+r/2=0$, equivalently $r^2=2k$. On a linear hyperplane both $\vec H$ and $x^\perp $ vanish. Differentiating the dilation $\sqrt{-t}$ shows that its normal velocity is exactly the rescaled mean curvature vector. $\square$

Proposition 14.2.4 (Gaussian area and the shrinker equation).12DACC1AA667 For a smooth closed hypersurface, put

\[ F(\Sigma )=(4\pi )^{-n/2}\int _\Sigma e^{-|x|^2/4}\, dA. \]

For a smooth normal variation with velocity $X$, its first variation is

\[ \delta F(X)=-(4\pi )^{-n/2}\int _\Sigma \left\langle \vec H+\tfrac 12x^\perp ,X\right\rangle e^{-|x|^2/4}\, dA. \]

Consequently the critical hypersurfaces for this functional satisfy the self-shrinker equation. Compare [CCMS20], Section 2. The noncompact compact-variation statement is Proposition 14.8.1, and the Hessian with moving center and scale is Theorem 14.9.2.

Proof

Since $X$ is normal, the pointwise first variation of area is $-\langle \vec H,X\rangle dA$. The derivative of the Gaussian factor is $-\tfrac 12\langle x,X\rangle e^{-|x|^2/4}$. Add these contributions and use $\langle x,X\rangle =\langle x^\perp ,X\rangle $. Testing with arbitrary smooth normal fields gives the final assertion. $\square$

Remark 14.2.5 (The supplied generic-flow paper has a specific scope).AAE9B0385275 The supplied version of [CCMS20] is arXiv:2003.14344v1, dated March 31, 2020. It studies one-sided ancient flows and perturbations avoiding certain unstable singularity models. Its introduction explicitly retains possible obstructions involving higher multiplicity and noncylindrical models with cylindrical ends. We do not turn this version into an unconditional classification of all generic flow singularities. Definitions 14.6.1, 14.4.6, and 14.6.10 now provide the weak-flow, entropy, and regularity vocabulary. The precise perturbation theorems still require further development; Theorem 6.2.3 is one connection to the chain and varifold framework.
