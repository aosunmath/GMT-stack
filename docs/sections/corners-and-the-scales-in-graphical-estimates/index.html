16.24 Corners and the scales in graphical estimates66A59ABFB12D

66A59ABFB12D

These examples connect the local estimates of [EH91] with the explicit expanding curves constructed in Section 16.5. They distinguish a sharp scaling exponent from a claim that every numerical constant in an estimate is optimal.

Example 16.24.1 (The time exponent one half cannot be improved for Lipschitz graphs).D3370A33F891 For the expanding curve $U(x,t)=\sqrt t\, u_ a(x/\sqrt t)$ of Proposition 16.5.4, the initial trace is $\ell _ a|x|$ and

\[ U(0,t)-U(0,0)=a\sqrt t. \]

Consequently a uniform time estimate with exponent $\alpha >1/2$ at zero cannot hold for all Lipschitz initial graphs, even for a single such nontrivial flow with its own finite constant. The curvature satisfies $|A|(0,t)=a/(2\sqrt t)$, so the positive-time curvature scale also occurs in an exact solution.

Proof

The profile has $u_ a(0)=a>0$, giving the height identity. Dividing it by $t^\alpha $ gives $a t^{1/2-\alpha }\to \infty $, excluding a finite constant for an estimate of that exponent near zero. Proposition 16.5.5 gives the curvature identity. These examples have bounded slope at all positive times by Lemma 16.5.2; the singular initial corner is why an initial curvature bound is unavailable. Products with Euclidean factors give the same height and curvature exponents in every hypersurface dimension. $\square$

Lemma 16.24.2 (The tip height parametrizes all positive symmetric cone slopes).6200E72780B0 For the even convex expander profiles, the map $a\mapsto \ell _ a$ is a continuous strictly increasing bijection from $(0,\infty )$ to $(0,\infty )$. Moreover

\[ \frac{a}{\sqrt2}\leq \ell _ a, \qquad \arctan \ell _ a\leq \frac{\sqrt\pi }{2}a. \]
Proof

The time estimate in Theorem 14.25.3, applied at the tip to the explicit expander with initial Lipschitz constant $\ell _ a$ and $n=1$, gives $a\leq \sqrt2\ell _ a$. For the upper angle estimate, the defect equation of Proposition 16.5.1 gives

\[ (\arctan p)'=q/2\leq (a/2)e^{-x^2/4}. \]

Integrate from zero to infinity and use $p(0)=0$, $p(\infty )=\ell _ a$ and $\int _0^\infty e^{-x^2/4}\, dx=\sqrt\pi $.

If $a<b$ but $\ell _ a\geq \ell _ b$, their expanding flows have ordered initial traces in the opposite order. Theorem 14.24.5 gives $U_ b\leq U_ a$, hence $b\leq a$ at the tip at time one, a contradiction. Thus the slope is strictly increasing.

To check continuity rather than assuming it from monotonicity, fix a compact interval of positive parameters. Smooth ODE dependence makes $p_ a(1)$ and $q_ a(1)$ bounded and continuous there. Lemma 16.5.2 bounds $p_ a(x)$ uniformly for $x\geq 1$. The defect bound therefore gives $p_ a'(x)\leq C e^{-x^2/4}$ uniformly over those parameters. Hence $p_ a(R)$ converges uniformly to $\ell _ a$ as $R\to \infty $. Every finite-$R$ value is continuous by ODE dependence, so its uniform limit is continuous. The angle bound implies $\ell _ a\to 0$ as $a\downarrow 0$, and the lower bound implies $\ell _ a\to \infty $ as $a\to \infty $. Continuity and strict increase finish the bijection proof. $\square$

Theorem 16.24.3 (The unique bounded-slope graphical expander for a symmetric corner).88EC1DC4D011 For every $\ell >0$ there is a unique smooth entire graphical self-expander whose slope is bounded and whose dilation flow converges locally uniformly to $\ell |x|$ at time zero. It is the even strictly convex profile $u_ a$ with $\ell _ a=\ell $. Uniqueness here is in this graphical bounded-slope class, not among arbitrary immersed networks or weak flows. The planar expander setting is studied in [Hal12], Section 6; the uniqueness proof here uses graphical comparison.

Proof

Lemma 16.24.2 supplies one parameter $a$. Theorem 16.5.3 and Proposition 16.5.4 supply the profile and its uniform initial trace. Any other profile in the stated class has a dilation flow smooth for positive time with the same locally uniform initial trace; dilation preserves the bound on its graph slope. Theorem 14.24.5 gives equality of the two flows. Their time-one slices are the profiles themselves. Evenness and strict convexity are therefore conclusions for every profile in this class, rather than extra assumptions imposed to prove uniqueness. $\square$

Example 16.24.4 (The spatial-radius term in local curvature estimates is necessary).87E95B631E65 For $\lambda >0$, consider the rescaled Scherk graph

\[ u_\lambda (x,y)=\lambda \bigl (\log \cos (y/\lambda )-\log \cos (x/\lambda )\bigr ). \]

It is a stationary graphical flow on $|x|,|y|<\pi \lambda /2$. On the base ball $B_\lambda (0)$ its slope is bounded independently of $\lambda $, whereas $|A|^2(0)=2/\lambda ^2$ at every time. Thus the $R^{-2}$ term in Theorem 14.23.5 cannot be omitted when $R$ is finite, and its power agrees with scaling.

Proof

Example 7.2.14 verifies the minimal graph equation at scale one. Euclidean dilation preserves minimality, hence stationarity under flow. On $B_\lambda $, both dimensionless coordinates have magnitude at most one, so $|Du_\lambda |^2\leq 2\tan ^2(1)$. At the origin the gradient is zero and the Hessian is $\operatorname {diag}(1/\lambda ,-1/\lambda )$. The graph metric is Euclidean there, giving $|A|^2=2/\lambda ^2$. If the local curvature bound involved only $C(V)/t$, sending $t\to \infty $ along this stationary flow would force its curvature to vanish, a contradiction. The ball lies strictly inside the graph domain, so failure is not due to a lack of smoothness on its closure. $\square$
