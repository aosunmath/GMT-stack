14.15 The translator metric and graphical calibrations3E434259EEC7

3E434259EEC7

Ilmanen's metric identifies translators with minimal hypersurfaces for a different ambient metric. The source discussion is [HIMW19], pp. 9–10. We distinguish the two metrics and prove the graphical comparison by a closed weighted form. The form is defined throughout the graph cylinder, which specifies where competitors may lie.

Proposition 14.15.1 (The conformal translator metric).9EAA75CE20C9 For fixed $v\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $n\ge 1$, put $h(x)=\langle x,v\rangle $ and $\bar g_ v=e^{2h/n}\bar g_{\rm Eucl}$. On every immersed $n$-hypersurface, the $\bar g_ v$ area measure is $e^ h dA$. Consequently its translator equation is equivalent to $\bar g_ v$ minimality. If $v\ne 0$, this ambient metric is incomplete.

Proof

The induced metric is multiplied by $e^{2h/n}$ in each of $n$ tangent directions; its volume density is multiplied by $e^ h$. Thus equality of the area functionals and Proposition 16.3.8 give equivalence of stationarity. Along the ray $x(t)=-tv/|v|$, $t\ge 0$, the metric length is

\[ \int _0^\infty e^{-|v|t/n}\, dt=\frac n{|v|}<\infty . \]

Points going to infinity along this ray form a metric Cauchy sequence with no limit in Euclidean space, whose usual local topology agrees with that of the smooth positive metric. Hence the metric is incomplete. The calculation does not assert incompleteness of every induced translator metric. $\square$

Proposition 14.15.2 (Normal speeds in the two metrics).40A3695B0742 If $\nu $ is a Euclidean unit normal, then $\bar\nu =e^{-h/n}\nu $ is a $\bar g_ v$ unit normal. A variation with Euclidean normal speed $f$ has $\bar g_ v$ normal speed $\bar f=e^{h/n}f$. In particular the normal component of an ambient translation is $e^{h/n}\langle a,\nu \rangle $ in the conformal metric.

Proof

Conformal scaling preserves orthogonality and multiplies vector lengths by $e^{h/n}$. Hence $f\nu =e^{h/n}f\bar\nu $, proving the assertions. The Euclidean angle solves the weighted equation of Proposition 14.14.4; the conformal normal speed solves the corresponding minimal Jacobi equation. These are related formulations, not the same scalar operator applied to the same normal-speed function. $\square$

Proposition 14.15.3 (A closed form calibrates a translating graph).F008CC73DD2C Suppose $u\in C^\infty (\Omega )$, with $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ n$ open, solves the graph translator equation with velocity $ce_{n+1}$. On $U=\Omega \times \mathbb R$, set

\[ N(x,z)=\frac{(-Du(x),1)}{\sqrt{1+|Du(x)|^2}},\qquad \rho (x,z)=e^{cz},\qquad \omega =\iota _{\rho N}(dx_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dx_ n\wedge dz). \]

Then $d\omega =0$, the Euclidean comass of $\omega $ is $\rho $, and its restriction to the graph with the orientation induced by $N$ is its weighted area form. Equivalently $\omega $ has comass one for the conformal metric of Proposition 14.15.1.

Proof

Put $W=\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}$. Because $N$ is independent of $z$,

\[ \operatorname {div}_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}(\rho N) =\rho \left(-\operatorname {div}_{\mathbb R^ n}\frac{Du}{W}+\frac cW\right)=0 \]

by Proposition 16.3.4. The exterior derivative of the contraction with ambient volume is this divergence times ambient volume, so the form is closed. Its Euclidean comass is the length of $\rho N$, namely $\rho $, with equality on the oriented plane normal to $N$. For the conformal metric an orthonormal $n$-frame is the Euclidean one multiplied by $e^{-cz/n}$, canceling that factor. This proves the claimed calibration without requiring a global minimal-foliation theorem. $\square$

Theorem 14.15.4 (Weighted minimization within the graph cylinder).8C900F72F8B6 Let $T$ be the current of integration over a compact smooth piece of the oriented translating graph in Proposition 14.15.3. If $S$ is a compactly supported integer rectifiable $n$-current of finite mass in $U$, and $S-T=\partial R$ for a compactly supported $(n+1)$-current $R$ in $U$, then

\[ \int \rho \, d\| S\| \ge \int \rho \, d\| T\| . \]

Equality holds exactly when the oriented approximate tangent of $S$ is calibrated by $\omega $ almost everywhere. This gives a current formulation of the graphical minimizing assertion in [HIMW19], pp. 9–10, with its ambient-domain condition explicit.

Proof

Choose a cutoff equal to one near all compact supports. Since $d\omega =0$ and its derivative vanishes near the support of $R$ after cutoff, Stokes' identity for currents gives $S(\omega )=T(\omega )$. The latter equals the weighted mass of $T$. The former is bounded above by the weighted mass of $S$, because its oriented rectifiable representation pairs a unit simple tangent vector with a form of comass $\rho $. The difference is the integral of a nonnegative calibration defect; it vanishes exactly when that defect is zero almost everywhere. The argument is the weighted version of Theorem 5.5.3. It compares homology classes inside $U$, not arbitrary competitors that leave the graph cylinder. $\square$

Corollary 14.15.5 (Strict minimizing comparison between graphs with the same boundary).8BE8E33ADABE Let $D\Subset \Omega $ be a connected smooth bounded domain and let $u$ be as in Proposition 14.15.3. For every smooth $w$ on $\overline D$ with $w=u$ on $\partial D$,

\[ \int _ D e^{cw}\sqrt{1+|Dw|^2}\, dx \ge \int _ D e^{cu}\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}\, dx. \]

Equality holds if and only if $w=u$. In particular there is at most one smooth solution of this translator Dirichlet problem. Existence is a separate problem.

Proof

The vertical homotopy $(x,t)\mapsto (x,(1-t)u(x)+tw(x))$ supplies a compact filling of the difference of the two graph currents in $\Omega \times \mathbb R$. Its side term is zero because the functions agree on $\partial D$. The preceding theorem gives the inequality. Equality makes the upward unit normals agree everywhere, first almost everywhere by the defect formula and then everywhere by continuity. Their last components are positive, so equality of normals gives $Dw=Du$. Connectedness makes $w-u$ constant, and the boundary condition makes the constant zero.

If two solutions are smooth on $\overline D$ and have the same boundary data, apply this calibration calculation in both directions. No extension as a solution beyond $\partial D$ is needed: pull the closed form back to the vertical homotopy on $\overline D\times [0,1]$ and use Stokes there. It is closed on the interior, smooth up to the boundary, and the side term vanishes because the two heights agree. Thus each graph has weighted area at most the other's. Their areas are equal, and the equality statement gives uniqueness. No joint convexity of the integrand in height and gradient is used. $\square$

Proposition 14.15.6 (A local area bound for a translating graph).B8579021D4D7 Let $M$ be the graph in Proposition 14.15.3. If $K\Subset \Omega \times \mathbb R$ is a smooth bounded domain whose boundary meets $M$ transversely, then

\[ 2\int _{M\cap K}e^{cz}\, dA\le \int _{\partial K}e^{cz}\, dA. \]

In particular, for a transverse Euclidean ball $B_ r(p)\Subset \Omega \times \mathbb R$,

\[ \mathcal H^ n(M\cap B_ r(p)) \le \tfrac 12 e^{2|c|r}\mathcal H^ n(\partial B_ r(p)). \]

The estimate alone is a mass bound, not a theorem of smooth convergence for arbitrary dimensions or boundary configurations.

Proof

The graph separates the cylinder into $U^+=\{ z>u(x)\} $ and $U^-=\{ z<u(x)\} $. Apply the divergence theorem to the divergence-free field $Y=e^{cz}N$ on $K\cap U^+$ and $K\cap U^-$, whose boundaries are piecewise smooth by transversality. The outward normal on the graph is respectively $-N$ and $N$. Therefore

\[ \int _{M\cap K}\rho =\int _{\partial K\cap U^+}\langle Y,\eta \rangle , \qquad \int _{M\cap K}\rho =-\int _{\partial K\cap U^-}\langle Y,\eta \rangle . \]

Since $|Y|=\rho $, summing proves the weighted bound. On $B_ r(p)$ the maximum of $e^{cz}$ divided by its minimum is at most $e^{2|c|r}$. Use the minimum on the interior integral and the maximum on the boundary integral to obtain the unweighted bound. These computations give the area comparison described in [HIMW19], p. 10, with the domain and transversality hypotheses specified. $\square$
