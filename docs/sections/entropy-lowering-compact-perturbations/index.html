14.12 Entropy-lowering compact perturbationsD74A98F1FACF

D74A98F1FACF

A negative second variation at one Gaussian center does not control the supremum over all centers and scales. We supply the additional estimates in [CM12], Theorem 0.15 and its proof in Section 7.3, pp. 789–792. Throughout, Gaussian scale is a positive parameter, not flow time. The absence of a Euclidean line factor is essential to this argument.

Definition 14.12.1 (A compact normal graph family).4257A80FD7E0 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth properly embedded two-sided hypersurface, and let $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$. For sufficiently small $|s|$, set

\[ \Sigma _ s=\{ x+sf(x)\nu (x):x\in \Sigma \} ,\qquad G(c,t,s)=F_{c,t}(\Sigma _ s). \]

The graph is taken within a fixed tubular neighborhood of the compact support. A finite coordinate cover and compactness give an interval on which the maps remain embeddings. All changed points then lie in a fixed ambient compact set; their areas and curvatures are uniformly bounded on a smaller closed parameter interval. This is the variation class specified in [CM12], p. 763, footnote 6. The unchanged part may have unbounded curvature.

Lemma 14.12.2 (A Gaussian scale derivative identity and inequality).F763BCA041B5 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth properly immersed two-sided boundaryless hypersurface with polynomial area growth and with $|H(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^ d$ for some finite $C,d$. For every $c$ and $t>0$,

\[ \partial _ t F_{c,t}(\Sigma ) =\int _\Sigma \left[\left(\frac{\langle x-c,\nu \rangle }{2t}-\frac H2\right)^2 -\frac{H^2}{4}\right]\rho _{c,t}\, dA \geq -\frac14\int _\Sigma H^2\rho _{c,t}\, dA. \]
Proof

Write $z=x-c$ and $\rho =\rho _{c,t}$. Kernel differentiation and $\Delta x=-H\nu $ give

\[ \partial _ t\rho =\rho \left(\frac{|z|^2}{4t^2}-\frac n{2t}\right),\qquad \Delta _\Sigma \rho =\rho \left(\frac{|z^ T|^2}{4t^2}-\frac n{2t} +\frac{H\langle z,\nu \rangle }{2t}\right). \]

Their difference is the integrand in the stated identity. The integral of $\Delta _\Sigma \rho $ is zero: multiply by compact radial cutoffs, integrate by parts, and bound the error by the Gaussian tail of $C(1+|x|)\rho $. Properness and polynomial growth justify this limit; the polynomial bound for $H$ also makes $\Delta _\Sigma \rho $ integrable. Differentiation under the integral is justified by Gaussian moments. Dropping the nonnegative square gives the inequality. This is the square completion behind [CM12], (7.3)–(7.5). $\square$

Lemma 14.12.3 (Uniform bounds for the changed compact part).8516998A23F4 Let $\Sigma _ s$ be a compact normal graph family over a smooth proper embedded hypersurface of polynomial area growth. On a sufficiently small fixed closed interval of $s$, there are constants $A_0,C_0$ such that

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq F_{c,t}(\Sigma )+A_0(4\pi t)^{-n/2} \exp \left(-\frac{\operatorname {dist}(c,B_{C_0}(0))^2}{4t}\right). \]

Consequently the added term is at most $A_0(4\pi t)^{-n/2}$, and for $|c|>C_0+R$ it is at most $C(n)A_0R^{-n}$ uniformly for all $t>0$. On each compact set of $(c,t)$, $G$ and its derivatives in $s$ are continuous, and $G(c,t,s)\to F_{c,t}(\Sigma )$ uniformly as $s\to 0$.

Proof

Choose a compact region in the parameter surface whose interior contains the support of $f$. Outside this region all embeddings are identical. Its perturbed image lies in $B_{C_0}$ and has area at most $A_0$, uniformly in $s$. Subtract the integrals on the common unchanged region and discard the nonnegative old contribution from the compact region to obtain the inequality.

For far centers the exponential is at most $e^{-R^2/(4t)}$. The substitution $t=R^2u$ gives

\[ \sup _{t>0}t^{-n/2}e^{-R^2/(4t)} =R^{-n}\sup _{u>0}u^{-n/2}e^{-1/(4u)}=C_ nR^{-n}. \]

The last supremum is finite since $n\geq 1$ and the function tends to zero at both ends. Shape derivatives involve only a fixed compact region and smooth bounded geometry. The unchanged part is controlled by polynomial Gaussian moments for parameters in compact positive-scale sets. Differentiation and uniform continuity therefore give the last assertions. Compare [CM12], (7.40)–(7.46). $\square$

Lemma 14.12.4 (Small-scale control for compact perturbations of a shrinker).815D2AE0FC46 Let $\Sigma _ s$ be a compact normal graph family over a connected complete smooth properly embedded two-sided boundaryless self-shrinker $\Sigma $ with polynomial area growth. Fix $R<\infty $. There are constants $A,B,C\geq 0$, independent of small $s$, $|c|\leq R$, and $0<t\leq 1$, such that

\[ \partial _ tG(c,t,s)\geq -A-BG(c,t,s). \]

In particular, for $0<t\leq \tau \leq 1$,

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq e^{B\tau }G(c,\tau ,s)+C\tau . \]
Proof

On the unchanged part of the surface, $|H|^2=|x^\perp |^2/4\leq |x|^2/4$. On the changed compact region the curvatures are uniformly bounded by some $C_ H$. Each perturbed surface has polynomial area and curvature growth, so Lemma 14.12.2 applies and gives

\[ \partial _ tG\geq -\frac1{16}\int _\Sigma |x|^2\rho _{c,t}\, dA -\frac{C_ H^2}{4}G. \]

Theorem 14.11.3 gives finite entropy of $\Sigma $. Proposition 14.10.5 bounds the first integral by $2\lambda (\Sigma )(R^2+C_ nt)$, proving the differential inequality with uniform constants. Multiply by $e^{Bt}$ and integrate from $t$ to $\tau $ to obtain

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq e^{B(\tau -t)}G(c,\tau ,s) +A e^{-Bt}\int _ t^\tau e^{Bu}\, du \leq e^{B\tau }G(c,\tau ,s)+Ae^ B\tau . \]

This proves the claim with $C=Ae^ B$. It is the uniform small-scale estimate in [CM12], (7.49)–(7.51); no curvature bound at infinity is assumed for the original shrinker. $\square$

Lemma 14.12.5 (Maximizing parameters cannot escape under a small compact perturbation).140F76BA7406 Let $\Sigma _ s$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 14.12.4, and suppose the constant tangent subspace of $\Sigma $ is zero. There exist $R,T>1$, $0<\tau <1$, $d>0$, and $\eta >0$ such that

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-d \]

for $|s|\leq \eta $ whenever $|c|>R$, or $t>T$, or $t<\tau $.

Proof

Theorem 14.11.8 gives a fixed positive deficit for the unperturbed surface whenever $|c|$ is large or $t\notin [1/2,2]$. Choose a smaller positive $\delta $ so that this deficit is at least $4\delta $ and $4\delta <\lambda (\Sigma )$. The far-center estimate of Lemma 14.12.3 then gives deficit at least $3\delta $ for every scale when $|c|>R$, after increasing $R$. Its large-scale estimate similarly gives deficit at least $3\delta $ for all centers when $t>T$. These choices are uniform on a fixed small shape interval.

It remains to consider $|c|\leq R$ at small scales. Take $A,B,C$ from Lemma 14.12.4. Choose $0<\tau <1/2$ sufficiently small that

\[ e^{B\tau }(\lambda (\Sigma )-3\delta )+C\tau \leq \lambda (\Sigma )-2\delta . \]

At this fixed scale, uniform convergence on the compact center ball makes $G(c,\tau ,s)\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-3\delta $ for $|s|\leq \eta $, after reducing $\eta $, because the unperturbed deficit is at least $4\delta $. The small-scale comparison propagates this upper bound to all $0<t<\tau $ with deficit $2\delta $. Combining the three regions proves the claim, for instance with $d=2\delta $. This is the escape-prevention part of [CM12], pp. 790–792. $\square$

Lemma 14.12.6 (An F-negative direction gives a strict joint local maximum).45661094ED28 Let $\Sigma $ be a connected complete smooth properly embedded two-sided boundaryless self-shrinker with polynomial area growth and zero constant tangent subspace. Suppose $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$ makes the joint Hessian in Theorem 14.9.2 negative for every center and scale derivative. For its compact graph family there are $r,\gamma >0$ such that

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq \lambda (\Sigma ) -\gamma \bigl (|c|^2+(t-1)^2+s^2\bigr ) \]

whenever $|c|^2+(t-1)^2+s^2<r^2$.

Proof

All first derivatives vanish at $(0,1,0)$ by Proposition 14.9.1. Consider a Hessian direction $(y,h,b)$ in center, scale, and graph amplitude. If $b\ne 0$, its value is $b^2$ times the joint Hessian for shape speed $f$ with center/scale derivatives $y/b,h/b$, so it is negative. If $b=0$ and $(y,h)\ne 0$, it is negative by the argument of Proposition 14.11.7. Thus the full finite-dimensional Hessian is negative definite. Smooth dependence of $G$ on these parameters follows from compact shape support and Gaussian domination of the unchanged part. Taylor's theorem and a uniform bound on the unit sphere of directions give the estimate. This is [CM12], step (1) on p. 790. $\square$

Theorem 14.12.7 (F-instability lowers entropy when no line splits).BEB1AB569895 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$, $n\geq 1$, be connected, complete, smooth, properly embedded, two-sided, without boundary, and a self-shrinker with polynomial area growth. Suppose it has no nonzero constant tangent vector and is $F$-unstable in Definition 14.9.3. Then there exist $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$ and $\eta >0$ such that the normal graphs $\Sigma _ s=\{ x+sf(x)\nu (x):x\in \Sigma \} $ satisfy

\[ \lambda (\Sigma _ s)<\lambda (\Sigma )\qquad (0<|s|<\eta ). \]

These perturbations can be arbitrarily small in every fixed $C^ m$ norm. This is a proper embedded formulation of [CM12], Theorem 0.15, p. 763, proved on pp. 789–792.

Proof

Choose $f$ from $F$-instability. Lemma 14.12.6 supplies a joint quadratic deficit near $(0,1,0)$. Lemma 14.12.5 supplies a compact parameter box $K=\{ |c|\leq R,\ \tau \leq t\leq T\} $ outside which all sufficiently small perturbations have a fixed deficit $d>0$.

Choose a parameter neighborhood $U$ of $(0,1)$ with closure in $K$, sufficiently small that the local quadratic estimate holds on $U$ for all sufficiently small $s$. On the compact set $K\setminus U$, the unperturbed surface has a uniform positive deficit by Theorem 14.11.8. Uniform convergence of $G(c,t,s)$ to $G(c,t,0)$ there retains a fixed positive deficit $d_1$ after reducing $|s|$. On $U$ the quadratic estimate gives deficit at least $\gamma s^2$. Consequently, for each small nonzero $s$,

\[ \sup _{c,\ t>0}G(c,t,s) \leq \lambda (\Sigma )-\min \{ d,d_1,\gamma s^2\} <\lambda (\Sigma ). \]

The graph family remains embedded for small amplitude by Definition 14.12.1. Since $f$ is fixed, smooth and compactly supported, the graph function $sf$ tends to zero in each fixed $C^ m$ norm. The exclusion of escaping parameters is what permits taking the global supremum. $\square$

Corollary 14.12.8 (Entropy-lowering perturbations of closed sign-changing shrinkers).7AABD65517DC A connected smooth closed embedded two-sided self-shrinker whose scalar mean curvature changes sign admits arbitrarily small compact normal perturbations with strictly smaller entropy.

Proof

Theorem 14.9.6 gives an $F$-negative direction, using the compact elliptic principal-eigenfunction theorem recorded there. A closed hypersurface cannot split a Euclidean line, since its image is bounded; Proposition 14.11.4 therefore makes its constant tangent subspace zero. All other hypotheses of Theorem 14.12.7 follow from closed smooth embeddedness. Applying it proves the result. No classification of mean-convex shrinkers is used, and this corollary is not stated for all nonround shrinkers without that additional input. $\square$
