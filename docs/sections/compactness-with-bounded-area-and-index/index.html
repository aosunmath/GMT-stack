7.3 Compactness with bounded area and index605F0FF85A8C

605F0FF85A8C

The Morse index measures the number of independent unstable directions, not the size of the second fundamental form. Its local consequence is particularly useful in compactness arguments.

Lemma 7.3.1 (Disjoint unstable regions consume index).3E0AAA9F69FA Let $\Sigma $ be a smooth closed two-sided minimal hypersurface with index at most $I$. Among any $I+1$ open subsets of $\Sigma $ with pairwise disjoint closures, at least one is stable for normal variations compactly supported in that subset.

Proof

Otherwise choose nonzero smooth compactly supported functions $f_1,\ldots ,f_{I+1}$, one in each subset, with $Q(f_ i,f_ i)<0$. The disjoint supports make every cross term $Q(f_ i,f_ j)$ vanish for $i\ne j$. Hence

\[ Q\left(\sum _ i a_ if_ i,\sum _ i a_ if_ i\right)=\sum _ i a_ i^2Q(f_ i,f_ i)<0 \]

for every nonzero coefficient vector. The functions are linearly independent, producing an $(I+1)$-dimensional negative subspace, contrary to the index bound. This is the localization mechanism used in [Sharp17], Lemma 3.1. $\square$

Theorem 7.3.2 (Sharp compactness).780986CC98B2 Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be smooth and closed, with $2\leq n\leq 6$. Let $\Sigma _ j$ be smooth, connected, closed, embedded minimal hypersurfaces satisfying

\[ \operatorname {Area}(\Sigma _ j)\leq \Lambda ,\qquad \operatorname {index}(\Sigma _ j)\leq I \]

for fixed finite $\Lambda $ and integer $I\geq 0$. After a subsequence, $|\Sigma _ j|$ converges to $m|\Sigma |$ for a positive integer $m$ and a smooth closed embedded minimal hypersurface $\Sigma $, with area at most $\Lambda $ and index at most $I$. The convergence is smooth and graphical away from a set $Y\subset \Sigma $ of at most $I$ points. If $m=1$, it is smooth and graphical everywhere. If $m\geq 2$ and $\Sigma $ is two-sided, $\Sigma $ is stable. Source: [Sharp17], Theorem 2.3, pp. 321–322. The more detailed nullity conclusions are not needed here. The proof remains external.

Remark 7.3.3 (Where regularity enters).EFD6BFF7085F Lemma 7.3.1 limits the number of regions where stability can fail. It does not itself give a curvature estimate. Theorem 7.6.6 now states the smooth Euclidean curvature estimate explicitly. Theorem 7.6.5 and Proposition 7.6.3 explain how separated sheets imply stability of a limit. Sharp's argument uses the local stable-hypersurface estimates of Schoen–Simon [SS81]; the initial use is stated in [Sharp17], Theorem 2.1. For singular stable hypersurfaces, stability must be accompanied by the appropriate assumptions on the singular set. Theorem 7.11.4 now proves stability across finite punctures on an already smooth hypersurface, and Theorem 7.12.6 proves index persistence when the weak limit is already smooth and two-sided. These deductions do not supply the regularity of a singular support. Neither stability nor a bound on index alone asserts that an arbitrary stationary varifold is smooth.
