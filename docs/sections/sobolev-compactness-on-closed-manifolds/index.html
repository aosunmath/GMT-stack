7.18 Sobolev compactness on closed manifolds47645FA91217

47645FA91217

The mollification argument used for BV compactness in Theorem 4.2.7 also gives the $L^2$ compactness needed for fixed-parameter elliptic problems. The translation-and-smoothing method parallels [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 2.6; the phase-field application is the compactness step in [ChAC], Exercise 4.4. We give the $H^1$ version explicitly.

Lemma 7.18.1 (The quadratic translation estimate).14BA3E3305C8 For $f\in H^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ and $h\in \mathbb R^ n$,

\[ \| f(\, \cdot +h)-f\| _{L^2}\leq |h|\, \| Df\| _{L^2}. \]

If $\rho _\delta $ is a nonnegative smooth mollifier of integral one supported in $B_\delta $, then $\| \rho _\delta *f-f\| _2\leq \delta \| Df\| _2$.

Proof

For smooth $f$, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives $f(x+h)-f(x)=\int _0^1 Df(x+sh)\cdot h\, ds$. Square, apply Cauchy–Schwarz in $s$, and integrate in $x$; translation invariance of Lebesgue measure gives the first estimate. For $H^1$ functions apply the calculation to their smooth mollifications and pass to the limit in $L^2$ for the functions and their weak derivatives. Approximation by mollifiers follows from translation continuity in $L^2$; it does not require any compactness theorem. Finally average the first estimate against $\rho _\delta (h)$ and use the triangle inequality for the $L^2$ integral to obtain the second. $\square$

Theorem 7.18.2 (Strong L2 compactness on a closed manifold).F7C78AA7EF28 On a smooth closed Riemannian manifold $(M^ n,g)$, every bounded sequence in $H^1(M)$ has a strongly $L^2(M)$ convergent subsequence. The same is true for an $H^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ bounded sequence whose members are supported in one fixed compact set. This is the compact embedding used in the fixed-parameter problem of [ChAC], Exercise 4.4.

Proof

First take the Euclidean sequence with common compact support $K$. For fixed $\delta >0$, its convolutions have support in $K+\overline B_\delta $ and uniform bounds on values and first derivatives:

\[ \| \rho _\delta *f_ j\| _\infty \leq \| \rho _\delta \| _2\| f_ j\| _2, \qquad \| D\rho _\delta *f_ j\| _\infty \leq \| D\rho _\delta \| _2\| f_ j\| _2. \]

Arzela–Ascoli makes this family precompact uniformly on the common compact set, hence in $L^2$. Lemma 7.18.1 bounds the $L^2$ distance from each original function to its convolution by $C\delta $. For each tolerance choose $\delta $ small and then a finite net for the convolutions. The same net, with the uniformly small smoothing error added, is a finite net for the original sequence. Total boundedness and completeness of $L^2$ yield a convergent subsequence.

On $M$, choose finitely many coordinate charts and a smooth partition of unity with each cutoff supported compactly in its chart. The coordinate representations of the cutoff functions times $f_ j$, extended by zero, have a common compact support and uniform Euclidean $H^1$ bounds. This follows from the product rule and bounded comparability of the coordinate metric and volume density on the compact supports; zero extension creates no boundary jump there. Apply the Euclidean result in each chart and take a subsequence common to this finite collection. Since the cutoffs sum to one and their volume densities are uniformly comparable, the resulting sequence is Cauchy in $L^2(M)$. This proves the compactness statement without imposing a bound on the values of $f_ j$. $\square$

Corollary 7.18.3 (A positive Poincare constant on a connected closed manifold).92C2144D9701 If $M$ is additionally nonempty, connected and positive-dimensional, then

\[ \lambda _1=\inf _{\substack {v\in H^1(M),\ \int _ Mv=0\\ v\ne 0}} \frac{\int _ M|\nabla v|^2}{\int _ Mv^2}>0. \]

Consequently $\lambda _1\int _ M|f-\bar f|^2\leq \int _ M|\nabla f|^2$ for every $f\in H^1(M)$, where $\bar f$ is its average.

Proof

If the infimum were zero, choose mean-zero $v_ j$ of $L^2$ norm one with $\| \nabla v_ j\| _2\to 0$. Theorem 7.18.2 supplies a strongly $L^2$ convergent subsequence. Distributional testing in each chart shows that the limit has zero weak gradient. Lemma 6.5.1 makes it constant locally, and connectedness propagates that constant over the manifold. Its mean is zero, so it is zero, contradicting the limiting $L^2$ norm one. The inequality is then the Rayleigh definition applied to $f-\bar f$. On a disconnected manifold, separate constants on different components show why a single global mean-zero condition would not suffice. $\square$
