13.1 Obstacle problems and variational inequalities7603C685327E

7603C685327E

This chapter begins the obstacle-problem branch with a model variational formulation. The Sobolev and weak-solution background is developed in [SimonPDE], Chapters 5–9. The classical free-boundary theory is developed in subsequent sections using [Fig18], with variational calculations and monotonicity proofs included and the deep regularity inputs identified.

Definition 13.1.1 (The classical scalar obstacle problem).C97A1383B3DD Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ d$ be bounded with Lipschitz boundary, $f\in L^2(U)$, and $g,\psi \in H^1(U)$. Assume the convex admissible set

\[ K=\{ v\in H^1(U):v-g\in H^1_0(U),\ v\geq \psi \text{ almost everywhere}\} \]

is nonempty. The obstacle problem is to minimize $\mathcal E(v)=\frac12\int _ U|Dv|^2-\int _ Ufv$ over $K$. The inequality is a unilateral constraint. Existence is proved in Theorem 13.2.1; regularity requires additional assumptions.

Proposition 13.1.2 (Variational inequality for the obstacle problem).422992397FE9 For $u\in K$, minimization of $\mathcal E$ over $K$ is equivalent to

\[ \int _ U Du\cdot D(v-u)\geq \int _ U f(v-u)\qquad \text{for every }v\in K. \]
Proof

If $u$ minimizes and $v\in K$, convexity gives $u+t(v-u)\in K$ for $0\leq t\leq 1$. Its energy has nonnegative right derivative at zero, which is exactly the inequality. Conversely, expand the quadratic energy:

\[ \mathcal E(v)-\mathcal E(u) =\int _ U Du\cdot D(v-u)-\int _ U f(v-u)+\tfrac 12\int _ U|D(v-u)|^2. \]

Every term on the right after combining the first two is nonnegative under the stated inequality. Hence $u$ minimizes. $\square$

Remark 13.1.3 (Scope of the classical obstacle theory).E2F2435B4E26 When $u$ and $\psi $ have continuous representatives, the contact set is $\{ u=\psi \} $ and the interior free boundary is $\partial \{ u>\psi \} \cap U$. The following sections develop existence, comparison, the reaction measure, quadratic blow-ups, and the Weiss and Monneau formulas. Theorem 13.3.1, Theorem 13.3.14, and Theorem 13.4.10 record precise external regularity inputs. Thin obstacles, variable-coefficient free-boundary regularity, and geometric obstacles require different hypotheses and remain future branches.
