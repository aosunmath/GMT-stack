7.14 Euclidean Sobolev inequalities and their endpoints974214D5CBD8

974214D5CBD8

The local estimates for elliptic equations use an integrability gain that comes from Euclidean geometry. We prove that input here, starting with iterated Holder inequalities. The source is [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Lemma 2 and Corollary 1, printed pp. 200–202. Throughout, $n\geq 2$ and $W^{1,p}_0(\Omega )$ denotes the closure of $C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$ in the $W^{1,p}$ norm. No regularity of the open set $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ n$ is required by that definition or by the zero-boundary inequalities below. Basic completion, weak derivatives, and Holder and Tonelli inequalities are background tools.

Lemma 7.14.1 (An iterated Holder inequality for coordinate projections).56D0A07041F8 For $i=1,\ldots ,n$, let $F_ i$ be a nonnegative integrable function of the $n-1$ coordinates $\widehat x_ i$ obtained by omitting $x_ i$. Then

\[ \int _{\mathbb R^ n}\prod _{i=1}^ n F_ i(\widehat x_ i)^{1/(n-1)}\, dx \leq \prod _{i=1}^ n\left(\int _{\mathbb R^{n-1}}F_ i\right)^{1/(n-1)}. \]
Proof

For $n=2$ this is the product identity from Tonelli. Suppose it is known in dimension $n-1$. Integrate in $x_ n$ first and apply Holder to the first $n-1$ factors, with all exponents equal to $n-1$. Put $G_ i(\widehat x_ i,\widehat x_ n)=\int _{\mathbb R}F_ i(\widehat x_ i)\, dx_ n$ for $i<n$, where the argument of $G_ i$ consists of the remaining $n-2$ coordinates. The integral in the statement is at most

\[ \int _{\mathbb R^{n-1}}F_ n(x')^{1/(n-1)} \prod _{i<n}G_ i(\widehat x_ i,\widehat x_ n)^{1/(n-1)}\, dx'. \]

Holder with exponents $n-1$ and $(n-1)/(n-2)$ bounds this by

\[ \| F_ n\| _1^{1/(n-1)} \left(\int _{\mathbb R^{n-1}}\prod _{i<n}G_ i^{1/(n-2)}\, dx'\right)^{(n-2)/(n-1)}. \]

Apply the induction hypothesis to the integral and use $\| G_ i\| _1=\| F_ i\| _1$. This gives the result. Zero factors are immediate; truncation to bounded functions on bounded boxes and monotone convergence justify the argument without any initial finiteness assumption on the product integral. $\square$

Theorem 7.14.2 (The first-power Sobolev inequality).3E1913FF6CC7 For every $u\in C_ c^1(\mathbb R^ n)$,

\[ \| u\| _{L^{n/(n-1)}} \leq \prod _{i=1}^ n\| \partial _ i u\| _{L^1}^{1/n} \leq \| Du\| _{L^1}. \]

This is the $p=1$ input in [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Lemma 2, pp. 200–201; no sharp constant is claimed.

Proof

On every coordinate line, the fundamental theorem of calculus and compact support give

\[ |u(x)|\leq F_ i(\widehat x_ i),\qquad F_ i(\widehat x_ i)=\int _{\mathbb R}|\partial _ i u(x)|\, dx_ i. \]

Multiply these $n$ inequalities and raise the result to the power $1/(n-1)$. Lemma 7.14.1 gives

\[ \int |u|^{n/(n-1)}\leq \prod _ i\| \partial _ i u\| _1^{1/(n-1)}. \]

Taking the power $(n-1)/n$ proves the first inequality. Each $\| \partial _ i u\| _1$ is at most $\| Du\| _1$, proving the second. If one coordinate derivative vanishes identically, the same argument forces $u=0$, consistently with compact support. $\square$

Theorem 7.14.3 (Sobolev gain below the critical derivative exponent).FB6C58CDB2CE Let $1\leq p<n$ and $p^*=np/(n-p)$. For every open $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^ n$ and every $u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Omega )$,

\[ \| u\| _{L^{p^*}(\Omega )}\leq C_{n,p}\| Du\| _{L^ p(\Omega )}. \]

One may take $C_{n,1}=1$ and $C_{n,p}=(n-1)p/(n-p)$ for $p>1$. These constants are convenient bounds, not optimal values. See [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Lemma 2.

Proof

First take $u\in C_ c^\infty (\Omega )$ and extend it by zero. The case $p=1$ is Theorem 7.14.2. For $p>1$, put $\alpha =(n-1)p/(n-p)>1$ and apply that theorem to $|u|^\alpha \in C_ c^1(\mathbb R^ n)$. The chain rule and Holder give

\[ \| u\| _{p^*}^{\alpha } \leq \alpha \int |u|^{\alpha -1}|Du| \leq \alpha \| u\| _{p^*}^{\alpha -1}\| Du\| _ p, \]

since $\alpha n/(n-1)=(\alpha -1)p/(p-1)=p^*$. If $u$ is nonzero, divide by the finite power of its norm; otherwise the conclusion is immediate.

For general $u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Omega )$ choose compactly supported smooth $u_ j\to u$ in $W^{1,p}$. Their zero extensions converge in $W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^ n)$, since both function and gradient norms of differences are unchanged by extension at this stage. The inequality for $u_ j-u_ k$ makes them Cauchy in $L^{p^*}$. The $L^{p^*}$ limit agrees almost everywhere with the zero extension of $u$, by taking subsequences of the $L^ p$ and $L^{p^*}$ convergences. Pass to the limit. This argument uses the closure definition and does not assume a trace theorem for a rough boundary. $\square$

Corollary 7.14.4 (A volume-dependent zero-boundary Poincare estimate).5B3E68252599 If $0<\mathcal L^ n(\Omega )<\infty $ and $1\leq p<n$, then

\[ \| u\| _ p\leq C_{n,p}\mathcal L^ n(\Omega )^{1/n}\| Du\| _ p \qquad (u\in W^{1,p}_0(\Omega )). \]
Proof

Holder gives $\| u\| _ p\leq \mathcal L^ n(\Omega )^{1/p-1/p^*}\| u\| _{p^*}$. The exponent difference is $1/n$; apply Theorem 7.14.3. The zero-boundary space matters: the constant function one belongs to $W^{1,p}(\Omega )$ and has zero gradient, so the estimate cannot hold on all of that space when $\Omega $ has positive finite volume. $\square$

Theorem 7.14.5 (Every finite integrability gain in dimension two).B277B3E87BF9 Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb R^2$ be open with $0<\mathcal L^2(\Omega )<\infty $. For every $2\leq q<\infty $ there is $C_ q<\infty $ such that

\[ \| u\| _{L^ q(\Omega )}\leq C_ q\mathcal L^2(\Omega )^{1/q}\| Du\| _{L^2(\Omega )} \qquad (u\in H_0^1(\Omega )). \]

The constants may depend on $q$. This includes the finite-power assertion in [SimonPDE], Lecture 17, Corollary 1, p. 202.

Proof

Set $p=2q/(q+2)$, so $1\leq p<2$ and $p^*=q$. Approximation and Holder on the finite-volume domain imply $H_0^1(\Omega )\subset W^{1,p}_0(\Omega )$. Theorem 7.14.3 and Holder for the gradient yield

\[ \| u\| _ q\leq C_{2,p}\| Du\| _ p \leq C_{2,p}\mathcal L^2(\Omega )^{1/p-1/2}\| Du\| _2. \]

The exponent difference is $1/q$. This proves the result, including $q=2$ through the first-power inequality. Letting $q\to \infty $ in the displayed assertion would require uniform control of its constants and is not justified. $\square$

Corollary 7.14.6 (The scaled gain on balls).15335E40C520 Let $\chi =n/(n-2)$ for $n>2$ and $\chi =2$ for $n=2$. For $R>0$ and $w\in H_0^1(B_ R(x_0))$,

\[ \left(\int _{B_ R(x_0)}|w|^{2\chi }\right)^{1/\chi } \leq C_ n R^{2-n+n/\chi }\int _{B_ R(x_0)}|Dw|^2. \]

The radius factor is one when $n>2$ and is $R$ when $n=2$.

Proof

On $B_1$, use Theorem 7.14.3 with $p=2$ for $n>2$, and Theorem 7.14.5 with $q=4$ for $n=2$, then square the inequality. For $v(y)=w(x_0+Ry)$, the left side scales by $R^{n/\chi }$ and the gradient integral by $R^{n-2}$. Rearranging gives precisely the displayed factor. Smooth approximation extends the change of variables to the stated spaces. $\square$

Example 7.14.7 (Concentration forbids a larger Sobolev exponent).474B02EE1263 For $1\leq p<n$ and finite $q>np/(n-p)$, no constant $C$ can make $\| u\| _ q\leq C\| Du\| _ p$ hold for every $u\in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$. The same failure holds with the full $W^{1,p}$ norm on the right.

Proof

Choose a nonzero $\phi \in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$ and set $u_\varepsilon (x)=\varepsilon ^{1-n/p}\phi (x/\varepsilon )$, $0<\varepsilon <1$. Direct change of variables gives

\[ \| Du_\varepsilon \| _ p=\| D\phi \| _ p,\qquad \| u_\varepsilon \| _ p=\varepsilon \| \phi \| _ p,\qquad \| u_\varepsilon \| _ q=\varepsilon ^{1-n/p+n/q}\| \phi \| _ q. \]

The last exponent is negative exactly when $q>np/(n-p)$, so that norm diverges while the right-hand norms remain bounded. The critical exponent is thus forced by concentration, independently of the particular proof of the inequality. $\square$

Example 7.14.8 (An unbounded function at the critical derivative exponent).87FC32996CA8 For every $n\geq 2$ there is an unbounded function in $W^{1,n}_0(B_1\subset \mathbb R^ n)$. In particular, $H_0^1(B_1\subset \mathbb R^2)$ does not embed in $L^\infty $.

Proof

Choose a smooth radial cutoff $\eta $, equal to one on $B_{1/4}$ and zero outside $B_{1/2}$, with $0\leq \eta \leq 1$. For $x\ne 0$ put

\[ u(x)=\eta (x)\log \log (e/|x|), \]

and assign any finite value at zero. This function is unbounded in essential supremum near zero. Its $L^ n$ norm is finite by polar integration. Near zero its gradient has length $1/(r\log (e/r))$, and

\[ \int _0^{1/4}\frac{dr}{r\log (e/r)^ n}<\infty \]

because $n>1$. Integration by parts off $B_\delta $ shows these classical derivatives are weak derivatives: the inner boundary term is bounded by a constant times $\delta ^{n-1}\log \log (e/\delta )$, which tends to zero.

For large $k$, cap the radial logarithmic function at height $k$ before multiplying by $\eta $. The resulting compactly supported functions are Lipschitz, agree with $u$ off a small ball, and converge to $u$ in $W^{1,n}$ by dominated convergence of their values and gradients. Their piecewise gradients are weak gradients because the capped functions are continuous across the matching sphere. Each can be approximated in $W^{1,n}$ by smooth compactly supported mollifications inside $B_1$, using translation continuity in $L^ n$ for the function and its weak derivatives. Hence $u\in W^{1,n}_0(B_1)$. The endpoint failure does not contradict any finite-$q$ assertion in Theorem 7.14.5. $\square$
