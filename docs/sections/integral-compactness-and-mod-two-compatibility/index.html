6.2 Integral compactness and mod-two compatibility0CE74FE3A047

0CE74FE3A047

Weak convergence of measures alone can lose the integer structure of a surface. First-variation bounds provide an additional geometric compactness principle. Compare the current compactness theorem in the currents chapter with the following varifold result.

Theorem 6.2.1 (Allard compactness for integral varifolds).5A050AC47DEE Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ be open, and let $V_ j$ be integral $m$-varifolds in $U$ with locally bounded first variation. Assume that for every relatively compact open $W\Subset U$,

\[ \sup _ j\bigl (\| V_ j\| (W)+\| \delta V_ j\| (W)\bigr )<\infty . \]

Here $\| \delta V_ j\| $ is the total variation measure of the first-variation functional. A subsequence converges as varifolds to an integral $m$-varifold $V$ with locally bounded first variation. Integrality of the limit is the substantive additional conclusion beyond weak compactness of Radon measures. Source: [Allard72], Theorem 6.4; see also [White09], Section 3. The proof remains external.

Definition 6.2.2 (The mod-two chain of an integral varifold).8706AD13DA84 For an integral varifold $V=V(S,\theta )$, let $[V]$ denote the rectifiable flat chain with coefficients in $\mathbb Z_2$ obtained by reducing the integer multiplicity $\theta $ modulo two. No orientation is required. Thus its density is $\theta \bmod 2$ at almost every point of $S$. Varifold convergence concerns measures on the Grassmann bundle, whereas flat convergence concerns chains; neither convergence is identified with the other by this definition. See [White09], Section 1.

Theorem 6.2.3 (Compatibility of varifold and mod-two limits).A428515E6D50 Suppose integral $m$-varifolds $V_ j$ in an open $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ converge to $V$ as varifolds, with uniformly locally bounded first variations. If $\partial [V_ j]$ converges in the local flat topology, then $[V_ j]$ converges in that topology to $[V]$. In particular, the integral limit is provided by Theorem 6.2.1, and the flat-chain limit agrees with its multiplicity modulo two. Source: [White09], Theorems 1.1 and 3.3, using the local conventions in Section 4. The boundary convergence assumption is retained. The proof remains external.

Example 6.2.4 (Even multiplicity is invisible modulo two).58C720EC2E67 If $P$ is an $m$-plane, the integral varifold $2|P|$ has twice the weight of $|P|$, but $[2|P|]=0$. Consequently the mod-two chain alone does not determine the area or multiplicity of the varifold. This elementary example explains why the two forms of convergence in Theorem 6.2.3 carry different information; see [White09], Section 2, for examples involving sequences.
