3.13 The area formula from metric piecesB74FEBA5FCE4

B74FEBA5FCE4

We expand the three cases in [Sim18], Chapter 2, Theorem 3.3 and its proof, printed pp. 57–60. The proof uses differentiability, countably many pieces on which distances are close to a fixed linear metric, and an injective graph regularization of the critical set. It uses the normalization $\mathcal H^ m=\mathcal L^ m$ on $\mathbb R^ m$, the diameter-cover Lipschitz estimate and ordinary linear change of variables. It does not use rectifiable-set tangent theorems, Whitney extension, or coarea. Throughout $1\leq m\leq n$.

Lemma 3.13.1 (An injective linear map determines a positive domain metric).249B82161066 Let $L:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be injective and linear, and put $T=(L^*L)^{1/2}$. Then $T$ is positive definite, $|Tv|=|Lv|$ for every $v$, and $\det T=J_ mL$. For every Lebesgue-measurable $E\subset \mathbb R^ m$,

\[ \mathcal H^ m(L(E))=\mathcal H^ m(T(E))=J_ mL\, \mathcal L^ m(E). \]

This is the linear normalization used in [Sim18], proof of Theorem 3.3, pp. 58–59.

Proof

The spectral theorem constructs the positive square root and gives $|Tv|^2=\langle L^*Lv,v\rangle =|Lv|^2$. Its determinant is the square root of $\det (L^*L)$. The map $LT^{-1}$ is an isometric linear embedding, so the diameter-cover definition gives invariance of Hausdorff measure under it. On the domain plane, the normalization recorded in Definition 2.2.1 identifies $\mathcal H^ m$ with Lebesgue measure; ordinary linear change of variables gives $\mathcal L^ m(T(E))=|\det T|\mathcal L^ m(E)$. This establishes the linear case without an area formula for nonlinear maps. Infinite values follow by increasing bounded pieces. $\square$

Lemma 3.13.2 (Measurability of a finite-volume Lipschitz image).E5CA65543835 Let $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be Lipschitz, and let $E\subset \mathbb R^ m$ be Lebesgue measurable with finite measure. Then $f(E)$ is $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable. If $|E|=0$, then $\mathcal H^ m(f(E))=0$.

Proof

Choose compact sets $K_ j\subset E$ with $|E\setminus \bigcup _ jK_ j|=0$ by inner regularity. Images of the compact sets are compact, so their union is Borel. Proposition 2.2.2 gives

\[ \mathcal H^ m(f(Z))\leq (\operatorname {Lip}f)^ m\mathcal H^ m(Z)=0 \quad \text{when }|Z|=0. \]

Thus $f(E)$ differs from the Borel union $\bigcup _ jf(K_ j)$ by a Hausdorff-null set and is measurable for the complete Hausdorff measure. This also proves the null assertion. The argument extends to arbitrary measurable $E$ by a countable bounded exhaustion. It requires neither injectivity nor any assertion that every continuous image of a Borel set is Borel. $\square$

Proposition 3.13.3 (A countable partition with controlled linear metric).8A21EBE8AE53 Let $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be Lipschitz, let $A$ be a measurable subset of its differentiability set on which $Df$ has rank $m$, and fix $0<\varepsilon <1$. There is a countable disjoint measurable partition $A=\bigcup _\ell E_\ell $ and positive definite symmetric maps $T_\ell :\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ m$ such that, for every $x,x'\in E_\ell $,

\[ (1-\varepsilon )|T_\ell (x-x')| \leq |f(x)-f(x')| \leq (1+\varepsilon )|T_\ell (x-x')|, \]

and at every $x\in E_\ell $,

\[ (1-\varepsilon )\det T_\ell \leq J_ mDf(x) \leq (1+\varepsilon )\det T_\ell . \]

In particular $f$ is injective on each piece. This is the quantified partition underlying [Sim18], pp. 58–59.

Proof

Choose a countable dense family of injective linear maps $L_ j:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ and put $T_ j=(L_ j^*L_ j)^{1/2}$. At a fixed full-rank differentiability point $x$, the smallest singular value of $Df(x)$ is positive. Differentiability makes $|f(x+v)-f(x)-Df(x)v|/|v|$ arbitrarily small for all sufficiently small nonzero $v$. Choose $L_ j$ close enough to $Df(x)$ in operator norm and its Jacobian close enough to $J_ mDf(x)$. These two facts give the displayed distance and Jacobian inequalities with $T_ j$, for all $0<|v|<1/i$ for some positive integer $i$. The positive least singular value converts the small error measured by $|v|$ into an error relative to $|L_ jv|=|T_ jv|$.

For fixed $j,i$, the set of qualifying $x$ is measurable. The Jacobian condition uses a measurable derivative representative; the distance inequalities can be tested only on rational nonzero vectors $v$ of length less than $1/i$. Continuity in $v$ then supplies the inequalities for all such vectors. This is a countable family of inequalities between measurable functions of $x$. The union of these qualifying sets covers $A$. Assign a point to the first pair $(j,i)$ in a fixed enumeration, and then partition each assigned set by a countable disjoint grid of sets of diameter strictly less than $1/i$. Both distance inequalities now apply to every pair in the resulting piece. Use $T_\ell =T_ j$ for that piece. Empty pieces may be omitted. The lower bound and invertibility of $T_ j$ imply injectivity. No area estimate has been used to construct this partition. $\square$

Lemma 3.13.4 (Area bounds on one metric piece).36D6E8A409D9 Suppose $E$ has finite Lebesgue measure and the two bounds in Proposition 3.13.3 hold on $E$ with one positive definite $T$. Then

\[ \frac{(1-\varepsilon )^ m}{1+\varepsilon }\int _ EJ_ mDf\, dx \leq \mathcal H^ m(f(E)) \leq \frac{(1+\varepsilon )^ m}{1-\varepsilon }\int _ EJ_ mDf\, dx. \]

Both comparison factors tend to one as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$.

Proof

The map $h=f\circ T^{-1}:T(E)\to f(E)$ is injective and has Lipschitz constant at most $1+\varepsilon $; its inverse has Lipschitz constant at most $(1-\varepsilon )^{-1}$. Proposition 2.2.2, applied separately to these two maps on their domains, gives

\[ (1-\varepsilon )^ m\mathcal H^ m(T(E)) \leq \mathcal H^ m(f(E)) \leq (1+\varepsilon )^ m\mathcal H^ m(T(E)). \]

Lemma 3.13.1 identifies the domain measure as $\det T\, |E|$. Integrate the pointwise bounds $(1-\varepsilon )\det T\leq J_ mDf\leq (1+\varepsilon )\det T$. Substitution gives exactly the two displayed ratios. The image is measurable by Lemma 3.13.2. Keeping the denominator factors explicit avoids reversing an inequality when comparing the Jacobian integral with $\det T\, |E|$. $\square$

Proposition 3.13.5 (The area identity on the full-rank differentiability set).64CD4DD34AB0 For Lipschitz $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ and a finite-volume measurable set $A$ of full-rank differentiability points,

\[ \int _ AJ_ mDf\, dx =\int _{\mathbb R^ n}\# (A\cap f^{-1}(y))\, d\mathcal H^ m(y). \]

The multiplicity function is measurable and finite almost everywhere. This is the full-rank case of [Sim18], Theorem 3.3.

Proof

For a fixed $\varepsilon $, partition $A$ by Proposition 3.13.3. Each restriction is injective, and each image is measurable by Lemma 3.13.2. Therefore, at every target point,

\[ \# (A\cap f^{-1}(y))=\sum _\ell \chi _{f(E_\ell )}(y). \]

This proves measurability, including the value infinity when infinitely many pieces contribute. Sum Lemma 3.13.4 over the disjoint domain pieces. Monotone convergence identifies the image-measure sum with the multiplicity integral, so the same two comparison factors bound it by $\int _ AJ_ mDf$. This last integral is finite because $J_ mDf\leq (\operatorname {Lip}f)^ m$ almost everywhere and $|A|<\infty $. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ to obtain equality. Finiteness of the integral gives finiteness of the multiplicity for almost every target point. The partitions may depend on $\varepsilon $; the function being integrated does not. $\square$

Lemma 3.13.6 (An injective graph regularization of a rank-deficient map).34F8CC476DF4 Let $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be Lipschitz with constant $L$, and set $F_\varepsilon (x)=(f(x),\varepsilon x)$ for $0<\varepsilon \leq 1$. This map is injective and Lipschitz. At every differentiability point of $f$, its derivative has rank $m$ and

\[ J_ mDF_\varepsilon =\sqrt{\det (Df^*Df+\varepsilon ^2I_ m)}. \]

If $\operatorname {rank}Df<m$, this is at most $\varepsilon (L^2+1)^{(m-1)/2}$.

Proof

The last coordinate block determines $x$, proving injectivity, and $\operatorname {Lip}F_\varepsilon \leq \sqrt{L^2+\varepsilon ^2}$. Its derivative has the vertical block $\varepsilon I_ m$, so is injective. Its Gram matrix is $Df^*Df+\varepsilon ^2I_ m$. At a rank-deficient point at least one eigenvalue of $Df^*Df$ is zero, and all eigenvalues are at most $L^2$. Thus one regularized eigenvalue is $\varepsilon ^2$, while the others are at most $L^2+1$. Taking the square root of their product gives the stated bound. Its order is $\varepsilon $ in the worst rank-$m-1$ case; a uniform quadratic error in $\varepsilon $ is unnecessary and generally false. $\square$

Proposition 3.13.7 (The critical differentiability set has a null image).0A1766CE1A05 Let $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be Lipschitz and let $A$ be a measurable set of differentiability points with $\operatorname {rank}Df<m$. Then $\mathcal H^ m(f(A))=0$.

Proof

First assume $|A|<\infty $. Lemma 3.13.6 and Proposition 3.13.5, now in target $\mathbb R^{n+m}$, give

\[ \mathcal H^ m(F_\varepsilon (A)) =\int _ AJ_ mDF_\varepsilon \, dx \leq \varepsilon (L^2+1)^{(m-1)/2}|A|. \]

Here injectivity of $F_\varepsilon $ turns its multiplicity into the indicator of its image. Orthogonal projection onto the first $n$ coordinates is one-Lipschitz and maps $F_\varepsilon (A)$ onto $f(A)$. Consequently the same upper bound holds for $\mathcal H^ m(f(A))$. Let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. General $A$ is a countable union of finite-volume intersections with bounded balls, so its image is a countable union of null images. This proves the critical case using the already established full-rank case, rather than assuming the desired area formula on critical points. $\square$

Theorem 3.13.8 (Euclidean area with measurable multiplicity).6695035EB27D Let $V\subset \mathbb R^ m$ be open, let $f:V\to \mathbb R^ n$ be locally Lipschitz, and let $A\subset V$ be Lebesgue measurable. Then the counting function $N_ A(y)=\# (A\cap f^{-1}(y))$ is $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable, and

\[ \int _ AJ_ mDf\, dx=\int _{\mathbb R^ n}N_ A(y)\, d\mathcal H^ m(y). \]

The Jacobian is assigned zero on the null set of nondifferentiability. Infinite values are permitted. This supplies the proof of [Sim18], Theorem 3.3, through its separate full-rank, critical and exceptional cases.

Proof

First let $f$ be globally Lipschitz and $|A|<\infty $. Partition $A$ into its full-rank differentiability part $R$, its rank-deficient differentiability part $C$, and its nondifferentiability part $Z$. Measurable derivative representatives make these measurable. Rademacher's theorem, Theorem 3.1.2, gives $|Z|=0$, so Lemma 3.13.2 gives $\mathcal H^ m(f(Z))=0$. Proposition 3.13.7 gives the same conclusion for $f(C)$. Their multiplicity functions vanish outside those null images and are measurable for complete Hausdorff measure, regardless of possibly infinite values on the images. Their Jacobian integrals are zero. Proposition 3.13.5 treats $R$. Counts add on disjoint domain subsets, and this proves the identity on $A$.

For the local case, cover $V$ by countably many bounded balls with closures in larger balls on which $f$ is Lipschitz. Extend its coordinate functions from each larger ball to all of $\mathbb R^ m$ by Proposition 3.4.2. The vector extension is Lipschitz, possibly with a larger constant. On the smaller ball it agrees with $f$ on a neighborhood of every point, so its derivative agrees wherever that of $f$ exists. Partition $A$ disjointly among the smaller balls and apply the established finite-volume formula on each part. Sum by monotone convergence. This proves both the identity and the measurability of its counting function for arbitrary $A$, while preserving every local derivative. The proof uses neither Whitney extension nor rectifiable-set decomposition or coarea. $\square$

Corollary 3.13.9 (A null image is equivalent to a vanishing area Jacobian).C5AE6A4945BD Under Theorem 3.13.8, $\mathcal H^ m(f(A))=0$ if and only if $J_ mDf=0$ almost everywhere on $A$. In particular, a measurable positive-volume set on which the area Jacobian is positive almost everywhere has an image of positive $m$-dimensional measure.

Proof

The image is measurable by countable local versions of Lemma 3.13.2. Its indicator is bounded above by $N_ A$, since every point of the image has at least one preimage. If the Jacobian vanishes almost everywhere, the area identity gives $\int N_ A=0$ and hence zero measure for the image. Conversely if the image is null, $N_ A$ vanishes off a null set, so its integral and the Jacobian integral are zero. Nonnegativity of the Jacobian gives its almost-everywhere vanishing. The final assertion is the contrapositive with a positive integral, which may be infinite. $\square$

Proposition 3.13.10 (Finite-volume domains have finite multiplicity almost everywhere).3AE746AD9F73 If $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ has Lipschitz constant $L$ and $A$ is measurable with finite volume, then

\[ \int _{\mathbb R^ n}N_ A\, d\mathcal H^ m\leq L^ m|A|, \qquad \mathcal H^ m(\{ N_ A\geq q\} )\leq \frac{L^ m|A|}{q} \quad (q=1,2,\ldots ). \]

In particular almost every target point has finitely many preimages in $A$. This conclusion permits exceptional infinite fibers.

Proof

All singular values of $Df$ are at most $L$, so $J_ mDf\leq L^ m$ at differentiability points. Theorem 3.13.8 gives the integral bound. On $\{ N_ A\geq q\} $ the counting function is at least $q$, giving the second inequality by integration. The set where $N_ A=\infty $ is contained in every such superlevel set, so its measure is zero on letting $q\to \infty $. A constant map can still have an entire domain as the fiber at its one exceptional image point. $\square$
