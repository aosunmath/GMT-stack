8.2 Almost minimizing varifolds and existence847AC51636CA

847AC51636CA

A critical limit needs more than stationarity. Pitts arranges that it resists small local mass-decreasing deformations in annuli; replacement arguments then connect this variational condition to regularity.

Definition 8.2.1 (Almost minimizing in an open set).5A3BD49B3997 Fix an open $U\subset M$ and $\varepsilon ,\delta >0$. A mod-two cycle $T$ is $(\varepsilon ,\delta )$-almost minimizing in $U$ in the mass formulation if every finite sequence $T=T_0,\ldots ,T_ q$ of cycles with

\[ \operatorname {spt}(T_ i-T)\subset U,\quad \mathbf M(T_ i-T_{i-1})\leq \delta ,\quad \mathbf M(T_ i)\leq \mathbf M(T)+\delta \]

satisfies $\mathbf M(T_ q)\geq \mathbf M(T)-\varepsilon $. A varifold $V$ is almost minimizing in $U$ in this formulation if for every $\varepsilon >0$ some $\delta >0$ and such a $T$ satisfy $\mathbf F_ V(V,|T|)<\varepsilon $. The annular condition requires, for every $p\in M$, a radius $r_ p>0$ such that this property holds in each $B_ t(p)\setminus \overline B_ s(p)$ with $0<s<t<r_ p$. Compare [LiAP], Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2, and [Pitts81], Sections 3.1–3.9. The cited sources compare flat and mass formulations after restricting to smaller open sets; those equivalence proofs are not included here.

Theorem 8.2.2 (Pitts' original smooth existence range).0B2FDF2F71CE Every smooth closed Riemannian manifold $M^{n+1}$ with $2\leq n\leq 5$ contains a nonempty smooth closed embedded minimal hypersurface. This is the smooth-metric specialization of [Pitts81], Introduction, Theorem A, and Theorem 7.13. Pitts' proof is an external dependency. This statement records the dimension range in the supplied book; later regularity extensions are not being attributed to that original statement.

Theorem 8.2.3 (Realization of a positive homotopy width).05D114DEC261 Let $M^{n+1}$ be smooth, closed, and connected, with $2\leq n\leq 6$. Let $\Pi $ and a minimizing sequence be as in Definition 8.1.2, and suppose $\mathbf L(\Pi )>0$. The critical set contains an integral varifold

\[ V=\sum _{j=1}^ N m_ j|\Sigma _ j|,\qquad \| V\| (M)=\mathbf L(\Pi ), \]

where the $m_ j$ are positive integers and the $\Sigma _ j$ are pairwise disjoint smooth closed embedded minimal hypersurfaces. This modern formulation, including the Schoen–Simon regularity input, is stated in [MNIndex], Section 1, p. 4; see also [LiAP], Section 5. Its proof remains external. Neither connectedness nor multiplicity one is part of this conclusion.

Remark 8.2.4 (The proof dependencies in Pitts' construction).F06EA6D2D607 The source route is: discrete homotopies and a nontrivial class ([Pitts81], Sections 4.1–4.7), pull-tight to obtain stationarity (Section 4.3), the combinatorial argument producing almost minimizing critical limits (Sections 4.8–4.13), and replacement and regularity arguments (Chapters 5–7). Li [LiAP], Sections 3–5, separates the same topological, variational, and analytic steps. Colding–De Lellis [CDL] supplies the three-dimensional smooth-family perspective. A deformation that establishes stationarity alone does not complete the regularity step. The continuous pull-tight bridge is now proved in Theorem 8.7.2, using the explicit deformation of Theorem 8.6.8. Discrete interpolation, the combinatorial almost-minimizing selection, and replacement regularity remain external dependencies.
