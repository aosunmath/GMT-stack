8.1 Cycles and min-max families6BE94E1EB61B

6BE94E1EB61B

Pitts' monograph [Pitts81] is the foundational source for discrete homotopies, almost minimizing varifolds, and regularity. We introduce the continuous notation used by Marques–Neves [MNIndex], Sections 1–2, and use Li's July 7, 2019 preliminary notes [LiAP] as a guide to the discrete construction. A homotopy-class width and a volume-spectrum width are different definitions.

Definition 8.1.1 (Mod-two boundary cycles and topologies).AEAF42CA9912 Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be smooth, closed, and connected, with $n\geq 1$. Write $\mathcal Z_ n^0(M;\mathbb Z_2)$ for the flat component of zero in the space of integral mod-two $n$-cycles: these are boundaries $T=\partial U$ of mod-two $(n+1)$-chains. We use $\mathcal F$ for the flat metric, $\mathbf M$ for mass, and $\mathbf F_ V$ for a metric inducing weak varifold convergence on bounded-mass sets. Set

\[ \mathbf F(T,S)=\mathcal F(T-S)+\mathbf F_ V(|T|,|S|). \]

Here $|T|$ is the associated unoriented varifold. The flat topology can lose area by cancellation, while the varifold term records area and tangent planes. Source: [MNIndex], Section 2. The integer currents of the foundational chapter and these mod-two boundary cycles have different coefficient conventions.

Definition 8.1.2 (A fixed homotopy-class width).073350371C3C Let $X$ be a finite simplicial complex and $\Phi :X\to \mathcal Z_ n^0(M;\mathbb Z_2)$ be $\mathbf F$-continuous. Let $\Pi $ contain all $\mathbf F$-continuous maps from $X$ that are homotopic to $\Phi $ in the flat topology. Its width is

\[ \mathbf L(\Pi )=\inf _{\Psi \in \Pi }\sup _{x\in X}\mathbf M(\Psi (x)). \]

A minimizing sequence $\Phi _ i\in \Pi $ has maximal masses converging to $\mathbf L(\Pi )$. Its critical set consists of varifold limits of slices $|\Phi _{i_ j}(x_ j)|$ whose masses converge to $\mathbf L(\Pi )$. This is the convention of [MNIndex], Section 1. The discrete homotopy formulation in [Pitts81], Section 4.1, requires a separate interpolation argument to compare the constructions.

Definition 8.1.3 (Mass fineness of a discrete family).92950BF01F3D On the vertices of a finite cubical subdivision, a map $\phi $ to cycles has mass fineness

\[ \mathbf f(\phi )=\max \{ \mathbf M(\phi (x)-\phi (y)):x,y\text{ are adjacent vertices}\} . \]

Almgren–Pitts sequences use successively finer subdivisions, uniformly bounded masses, and fineness tending to zero; their homotopies must satisfy corresponding fineness conditions. Small flat distances alone do not supply these conditions. Sources: [Pitts81], Section 4.1; [LiAP], Section 4.1; [LMN], Section 2.9.

Remark 8.1.4 (Smooth sweepouts in dimension three).E05332744D17 Colding–De Lellis [CDL], Section 1.1, formulate one-parameter families of surfaces on a closed three-manifold and allow specified degenerations at finitely many points. Their saturated classes are closed under parameter-dependent ambient isotopies. This provides a geometric route to the pull-tight and replacement arguments, alongside Pitts' cycle-space formulation. The parameter space, allowed degenerations, and saturation condition are part of that construction; they must be checked before transferring a width or a genus conclusion to another setting. The author's publication page also lists an erratum, which should be consulted when importing detailed proofs from this survey.
