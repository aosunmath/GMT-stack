5.5 Calibrated currents and mass comparisonA06646AFA6FA

A06646AFA6FA

The smooth comparison of Proposition 7.2.12 extends to currents and gives useful equality and convergence statements. Here $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ is open, $1\leq m\leq N$, and $\omega $ is a smooth closed $m$-form with comass at most one. The motivating source is [ChMin], Section 16, p. 46; current evaluation and mass use the conventions of [Sim18], Chapter 6, and Definition 5.1.3.

Definition 5.5.1 (A current calibrated by a form).5E8055ACA3A7 A compactly supported real $m$-current $T$ of finite mass in $U$ is calibrated by $\omega $ if

\[ T(\omega )=\mathbf M(T). \]

Evaluation uses a smooth cutoff equal to one near the support of $T$. For a locally integer rectifiable current, the local calibration condition is $\langle \omega (x),\tau _ T(x)\rangle =1$ for $\| T\| $-almost every $x$. No global finite mass is required for this local condition. Compare Definition 7.2.11 and [ChMin], Theorem 16.2.

Lemma 5.5.2 (The calibration defect and equality condition).0A0C249D90DB For a compactly supported integer rectifiable current $T$ of finite mass,

\[ \mathbf M(T)-T(\omega ) =\int \bigl (1-\langle \omega ,\tau _ T\rangle \bigr )\, d\| T\| \geq 0. \]

Equality holds exactly when the local calibration condition holds almost everywhere.

Proof

Use the oriented rectifiable representation and mass formula in Definition 5.1.5. Its unit tangent vector is simple, so the comass bound gives $\langle \omega ,\tau _ T\rangle \leq 1$. The difference is the integral of a nonnegative function, and it vanishes if and only if that function vanishes almost everywhere. The sign of the orientation is part of the condition. $\square$

Theorem 5.5.3 (Calibrated currents minimize in their compact homology class).249A6CDC9F2C Let $T$ be a compactly supported finite-mass real $m$-current calibrated by $\omega $ in $U$. If $S$ is another compactly supported finite-mass real $m$-current and $S-T=\partial R$ for a compactly supported $(m+1)$-current $R$ in $U$, then

\[ \mathbf M(S)-\mathbf M(T)=\mathbf M(S)-S(\omega )\geq 0. \]

If $S$ is integer rectifiable, equality holds precisely when $S$ is also calibrated. This is the current form of the argument in [ChMin], Theorem 16.2, p. 46.

Proof

Choose a cutoff $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ equal to one on a neighborhood of all three compact supports. Since $d\omega =0$,

\[ S(\omega )-T(\omega )=\partial R(\chi \omega ) =R(d\chi \wedge \omega +\chi d\omega )=0. \]

The mass definition gives $S(\omega )\leq \mathbf M(S)$, since the cutoff form has comass at most one. Substitute $T(\omega )=\mathbf M(T)$; Lemma 5.5.2 gives the equality statement. The filling is required to stay inside $U$. $\square$

Corollary 5.5.4 (Fixed-boundary minimization in Euclidean space).060B7E4721DF Suppose $\omega $ is defined on all of $\mathbb R^ N$. A compactly supported calibrated finite-mass $m$-current $T$, $m\geq 1$, minimizes mass among all compactly supported finite-mass real $m$-currents $S$ with $\partial S=\partial T$.

Proof

The difference $Z=S-T$ is a compactly supported normal cycle: its mass is finite and its boundary is zero. Proposition 5.3.19 gives a compactly supported filling $R=C_ aZ$ with $\partial R=Z$. Apply Theorem 5.5.3. If $S,T$ are integral, this filling is integral too. No corresponding filling inside an arbitrary nonconvex open subset is inferred from this argument. $\square$

Proposition 5.5.5 (Weak limits of calibrated currents retain mass).4C4FD4B0981B Let $T_ j$ be calibrated finite-mass real $m$-currents with supports in a common compact $K\subset U$, and suppose $T_ j\rightharpoonup T$. Then $T$ is calibrated, is supported in $K$, and

\[ \mathbf M(T_ j)\longrightarrow \mathbf M(T). \]

This conclusion does not by itself prove integrality of $T$.

Proof

A fixed cutoff equal to one near $K$ gives $\mathbf M(T_ j)=T_ j(\omega )\to T(\omega )$, a finite limit. Tests outside $K$ show $\operatorname {spt}T\subset K$. Mass lower semicontinuity and the comass inequality give

\[ \mathbf M(T)\leq \liminf _ j\mathbf M(T_ j)=T(\omega )\leq \mathbf M(T). \]

Thus all terms agree. The finite mass of $T$ follows from the first inequality; the last evaluation is justified by that finite mass. Integrality requires an additional closure theorem, such as Theorem 5.1.6 with its boundary-mass hypothesis. $\square$

Proposition 5.5.6 (Stationarity away from the boundary of a calibrated current).C4108F50C678 Let $T$ be a compactly supported calibrated integral $m$-current in $U$, and let $V_ T$ be its rectifiable varifold obtained by forgetting orientation and retaining the positive integer multiplicity. Then $V_ T$ is stationary in $U\setminus \operatorname {spt}\partial T$.

Proof

Take a smooth vector field $X$ compactly supported away from $\operatorname {spt}\partial T$, with flow $F_ t$. For small $t$, the trace of this flow on the compact support stays inside $U$. The homotopy formula, Proposition 5.3.13, shows that $(F_ t)_\# T-T$ is a compactly supported boundary in $U$. The term involving $\partial T$ vanishes because the flow is constant on a neighborhood of that boundary, so its derivative in the time direction is zero there. Theorem 5.5.3 gives $\mathbf M((F_ t)_\# T)\geq \mathbf M(T)$ for both signs of $t$.

A diffeomorphism causes no overlap cancellation; the area formula expresses this mass as $\int J_ m(F_ t|S)\, dV_ T(x,S)$. Differentiating at zero gives $\int \operatorname {div}_ S X\, dV_ T=0$. Bounded derivatives of the flow on a compact set justify differentiation under the finite measure. Smooth fields are dense in compactly supported $C^1$ fields, giving stationarity in the stated domain. $\square$

Example 5.5.7 (Why the homology condition matters).1B4F98280302 In the flat torus $M=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^{m+1}$, the oriented slice $\Sigma =(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^ m\times \{ 0\} $ is calibrated by $\omega =dx_1\wedge \cdots \wedge dx_ m$. It has empty boundary and positive area, while the zero chain has the same boundary. These two cycles are not homologous.

Proof

The angular one-forms are globally defined and closed; their wedge has comass one and restricts to the positive volume form of the slice. Hence $\int _\Sigma \omega =1$. If $\Sigma $ were a boundary, Stokes' theorem and $d\omega =0$ would force that integral to be zero. Thus the calibration theorem does not compare this cycle to zero in its homology class. In contrast, Corollary 5.5.4 uses a filling available in Euclidean space. $\square$
