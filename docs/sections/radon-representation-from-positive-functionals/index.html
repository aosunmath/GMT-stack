2.3 Radon representation from positive functionalsBFB627EE4234

BFB627EE4234

The examples of measures become useful in compactness arguments because one can construct a measure from limits of its integrals. We prove the positive Riesz representation theorem on an open set $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, following [Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 5, pp. 30–37. The elementary Caratheodory construction of a measure on the measurable sets of an outer measure is a background input. Metric separation and all regularity and representation steps used here are proved below.

Definition 2.3.1 (A positive functional on compactly supported tests).9E4845BBA4ED A positive linear functional is a map $L:C_ c(U;\mathbb R)\to \mathbb R$ which is linear and satisfies $L(f)\geq 0$ whenever $f\geq 0$. Values are finite real numbers. No single bound $|L(f)|\leq C\| f\| _\infty $ for all supports is assumed. Positivity will instead give bounds on each fixed compact support. See [Sim18], Theorem 5.12 and Remark 5.13.

Lemma 2.3.2 (Compact cutoffs and a finite partition of unity).065D9AC3265E If $K\subset U$ is compact and $O\subset U$ is open with $K\subset O$, there is $\eta \in C_ c(O)$, $0\leq \eta \leq 1$, equal to one on a neighborhood of $K$. If finitely many open sets $O_1,\ldots ,O_ N$ cover $K$, there are $\eta _ i\in C_ c(O_ i)$, $0\leq \eta _ i\leq 1$, whose sum is one on a neighborhood of $K$ and is at most one everywhere.

Proof

The empty-set cases are immediate. For the first assertion choose $\delta >0$ so that the closed $\delta $-neighborhood of $K$ is compact and lies in $O$. A continuous piecewise linear function of $\operatorname {dist}(x,K)$, equal to one for distance at most $\delta /2$ and zero for distance at least $\delta $, is the required cutoff. For the second assertion, cover $K$ by finitely many small balls whose closures each lie in one of the $O_ i$. Grouping their cutoffs gives nonnegative $\psi _ i\in C_ c(O_ i)$ with $S=\sum _ i\psi _ i>0$ on a neighborhood of $K$. Apply the first assertion to obtain $\chi \in C_ c(\{ S>0\} )$, equal to one near $K$ and between zero and one. Set $\eta _ i=\chi \psi _ i/S$ where $S>0$, and zero elsewhere. Its extension is continuous because $\operatorname {spt}\chi $ stays in $\{ S>0\} $. The sum equals $\chi $. This is the Euclidean version of [Sim18], Lemma 5.4 and Corollary 5.5. $\square$

Lemma 2.3.3 (Positivity gives continuity on a fixed support).059D87E3EFB0 If $L$ is positive, $K\subset U$ is compact, and $\eta \in C_ c(U)$ is nonnegative and at least one on $K$, then

\[ |L(f)|\leq L(\eta )\| f\| _\infty \quad \text{for every }f\in C_ c(U)\text{ supported in }K. \]

Thus uniform convergence with a common compact support implies convergence of the functional values.

Proof

The pointwise inequalities $-\| f\| _\infty \eta \leq f\leq \| f\| _\infty \eta $ and positivity give the bound. Apply it to the difference of two functions for the continuity assertion. This is [Sim18], Remark 5.13. $\square$

Proposition 2.3.4 (The open-set mass associated with a positive functional).D6FBFD2B5B8A For open $O\subset U$, put

\[ m(O)=\sup \{ L(f):f\in C_ c(O),\ 0\leq f\leq 1\} . \]

Then $m$ is monotone, $m(\varnothing )=0$, is countably subadditive on open sets, and is additive on disjoint open sets, allowing infinite values. The function

\[ \mu ^*(A)=\inf \{ m(O):A\subset O\subset U,\ O\text{ open}\} \]

for arbitrary $A\subset U$ is an outer measure, equals $m$ on open sets, is finite on compact sets, and satisfies

\[ \mu ^*(A\cup B)=\mu ^*(A)+\mu ^*(B) \quad \text{if }\operatorname {dist}(A,B)>0. \]
Proof

Monotonicity and the empty-set assertion are immediate. If $O\subset \bigcup _ jO_ j$ and $f$ is admissible for $m(O)$, compactness of its support and Lemma 2.3.2 give a finite subordinate partition, with $f=\sum _ j\eta _ jf$. Therefore $L(f)\leq \sum _ jm(O_ j)$; take the supremum. For disjoint opens, finitely many admissible tests have sum at most one, so taking their separate suprema gives $m(\bigcup _ jO_ j)\geq \sum _{j=1}^ Nm(O_ j)$ for every finite $N$. This proves countable additivity on disjoint opens.

The infimum definition is consistent on opens by monotonicity. To prove countable subadditivity of $\mu ^*$, the only nontrivial case has $\sum _ j\mu ^*(A_ j)<\infty $. Choose $O_ j\supset A_ j$ with $m(O_ j)\leq \mu ^*(A_ j)+\varepsilon 2^{-j}$, use open-set subadditivity, and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. For compact $K$, choose an open neighborhood $W$ with compact closure in $U$ and a cutoff $\eta \geq 1$ on $\overline W$. Every test for $m(W)$ is at most $\eta $, so $\mu ^*(K)\leq m(W)\leq L(\eta )<\infty $. Finally, if $A,B$ have positive distance, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $V_ A,V_ B$ of them. For every open $O\supset A\cup B$,

\[ m(O)\geq m(O\cap V_ A)+m(O\cap V_ B)\geq \mu ^*(A)+\mu ^*(B). \]

Taking the infimum and using subadditivity proves the metric separation identity. This follows the construction in [Sim18], proof of Theorem 5.12. $\square$

Lemma 2.3.5 (Metric outer measures make Borel sets measurable).87BA9817395E Let $\mu ^*$ be an outer measure on a metric space, additive on pairs of sets at positive distance. Then every closed set, and hence every Borel set, is Caratheodory measurable.

Proof

Fix a closed set $F$ and a set $E$ with $\mu ^*(E)<\infty $. The empty and whole-space cases are immediate, so assume $F$ is nonempty and proper. Write $d(x)=\operatorname {dist}(x,F)$ and

\[ A_ j=E\cap \{ 1/(j+1)<d\leq 1/j\} ,\qquad j\geq 1. \]

Any finite family of these annuli with indices of the same parity consists of sets separated from one another by positive distances, because $d$ is 1-Lipschitz. Their outer measures sum to at most $\mu ^*(E)$. Therefore $\sum _{j\geq 1}\mu ^*(A_ j)<\infty $, and the tails tend to zero. The part of $E\setminus F$ with $d>1/k$ is separated from $E\cap F$; hence

\[ \mu ^*(E)\geq \mu ^*(E\cap F)+\mu ^*(E\cap \{ d>1/k\} ). \]

The latter set together with $\bigcup _{j\geq k}A_ j$ covers $E\setminus F$. Subadditivity and the vanishing tail now give $\mu ^*(E)\geq \mu ^*(E\cap F)+\mu ^*(E\setminus F)$. The reverse inequality is subadditivity. For $\mu ^*(E)=\infty $ the required greater-than-or-equal inequality is automatic. Thus $F$ is measurable. The Caratheodory measurable sets form a sigma algebra, so all Borel sets are measurable. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 1, Theorem 1.15. $\square$

Proposition 2.3.6 (The constructed measure is Radon).DCE16B3F3D66 The restriction of $\mu ^*$ from Proposition 2.3.4 to Borel sets is a Radon measure $\mu $. In particular, for every open $O\subset U$,

\[ \mu (O)=\sup _{K\subset O,\ K\text{ compact}}\mu (K)=m(O). \]
Proof

Lemma 2.3.5 gives a Borel measure, and Proposition 2.3.4 gives local finiteness and outer regularity. If $f\in C_ c(O)$, $0\leq f\leq 1$, with compact support $K$, then $L(f)\leq m(V)$ for every open $V\supset K$, so $L(f)\leq \mu (K)$. Taking the supremum in $f$ proves inner regularity on open sets; the opposite inequality is monotonicity.

For a Borel set $A$ of finite measure, choose open $O\supset A$ with $\mu (O\setminus A)<\varepsilon $, compact $K\subset O$ with $\mu (O\setminus K)<\varepsilon $, and open $V\supset O\setminus A$ with $\mu (V)<2\varepsilon $. Then $K\setminus V$ is compact and contained in $A$, and $\mu (A\setminus (K\setminus V))<3\varepsilon $. Thus $A$ is inner regular. For arbitrary Borel $A$, exhaust $U$ by compact sets $K_ j$. The sets $A\cap K_ j$ have finite measure and increase to $A$, so their inner regularity and continuity from below give the general assertion. Compare [Sim18], Lemmas 5.7–5.9. $\square$

Theorem 2.3.7 (Positive Riesz representation on a Euclidean open set).D8B4638B4814 For every positive linear functional $L:C_ c(U;\mathbb R)\to \mathbb R$ there is a unique Radon measure $\mu $ on $U$ such that

\[ L(f)=\int _ U f\, d\mu \qquad (f\in C_ c(U;\mathbb R)). \]

Its values on open sets are given by $\mu (O)=\sup \{ L(\phi ):\phi \in C_ c(O),\ 0\leq \phi \leq 1\} $. This is [Sim18], Theorem 5.12, specialized to a Euclidean open set.

Proof

Construct the Radon measure by Proposition 2.3.6. First note that, for $0\leq h\leq 1$ with compact support,

\[ L(h)\leq \mu (\{ h>0\} ). \]

Indeed $(h-\varepsilon )_+$ has compact support in the open set $\{ h>0\} $ and is bounded by one, while the difference from $h$ tends uniformly to zero on a common compact support. Lemma 2.3.3 gives the inequality upon taking the limit.

For $f\geq 0$ with compact support, fix $\delta >0$ and an integer $N$ with $N\delta \geq \max f$. Set $h_ i=\min (1,(f-i\delta )_+/\delta )$ for $0\leq i<N$. Pointwise $f=\delta \sum _ i h_ i$. The upper bound just proved and the fact that $h_ i=1$ on $\{ f>(i+1)\delta \} $ give

\[ \delta \sum _{i=0}^{N-1}\mu (\{ f>(i+1)\delta \} ) \leq L(f)\leq \delta \sum _{i=0}^{N-1}\mu (\{ f>i\delta \} ). \]

For the lower bound, every admissible test supported in that open superlevel set is at most $h_ i$. The same two sums bound $\int f\, d\mu $ by pointwise simple-function comparison. Their difference is at most $\delta \mu (\{ f>0\} )$, which tends to zero by local finiteness. Thus $L(f)=\int f\, d\mu $. Subtract positive and negative parts for signed $f$.

Finally any representing Radon measure satisfies the displayed open-set formula: the upper bound is immediate, and cutoffs equal to one on compact subsets yield the reverse bound by inner regularity. Equal open-set masses and outer regularity imply equal Borel masses. This proves uniqueness. $\square$

Theorem 2.3.8 (Continuous compactly supported tests are dense in finite-power spaces).19396F96DC10 For any Radon measure $\mu $ on $U$ and $1\leq p<\infty $, $C_ c(U)$ is dense in $L^ p(\mu )$. The assertion concerns the completed measure as well, with functions identified almost everywhere. See [Sim18], Theorem 5.10.

Proof

Truncation of values and support, followed by approximation by simple functions, reduces the claim to $\mathbf1_ A$ with $\mu (A)<\infty $; a completed-measurable set can first be replaced by a Borel set modulo a null set. Choose compact $K\subset A$ and open $O\supset A$ such that $\mu (A\setminus K)<\varepsilon $ and $\mu (O\setminus A)<\varepsilon $. Lemma 2.3.2 gives $\phi \in C_ c(O)$, $0\leq \phi \leq 1$, equal to one on $K$. Then

\[ \int |\mathbf1_ A-\phi |^ p\, d\mu \leq \mu (O\setminus K)<2\varepsilon . \]

Finite linear combinations approximate simple functions by the $L^ p$ triangle inequality. This proves the result. The finite-power assumption is essential to this approximation argument; no density in $L^\infty $ is asserted. $\square$
