14.17 Curvature gaps and parallel self-shrinkers848E86003EC0

848E86003EC0

Throughout this section, $X:\Sigma ^ n\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ is a smooth two-sided immersion, $n\geq 1$, with $A=D\nu $, $H=\operatorname {tr}A$ and $H=\langle X,\nu \rangle /2$. Write $\rho =(4\pi )^{-n/2}e^{-|X|^2/4}$ and $\mathcal D=\Delta -\frac12\nabla _{X^ T}$. The local identities follow the mechanism in [CM12], Lemma 10.8, pp. 808–809. The complete gap result below is proved for hypersurfaces; compare the arbitrary-codimension theorem in [CL12], Theorem 1.1, whose normalization is $\vec H=-X^\perp $.

Proposition 14.17.1 (Tensor Simons equation on a shrinker).B8B380906F8F The shape tensor satisfies

\[ \mathcal D A=\left(\frac12-|A|^2\right)A,\qquad \mathcal D|A|^2=2|\nabla A|^2+(1-2|A|^2)|A|^2. \]

No completeness, embeddedness or sign condition on $H$ is needed.

Proof

Differentiate $H=\langle X,\nu \rangle /2$ to get $\nabla H=\frac12A(X^ T)$. Since $X^ T=X-2H\nu $, tangential differentiation gives $\nabla X^ T=I-2HA$. In a geodesic frame, Codazzi therefore yields

\[ \nabla ^2H=\frac12\nabla _{X^ T}A+\frac12A-HA^2. \]

The Euclidean case of Theorem 7.15.3 reads $\Delta A=\nabla ^2H+HA^2-|A|^2A$. Substitution proves the tensor equation. Applying the product rule for the rough Laplacian and drift to $|A|^2$ gives the scalar equation. This also explains the factor $1/2$ associated to the chosen shrinker normalization. $\square$

Lemma 14.17.2 (A weighted curvature Caccioppoli inequality).8C41334CC845 Suppose $f=|A|^2\leq 1/2$. For every Lipschitz function $\eta $ compactly supported in the interior,

\[ \int _\Sigma \eta ^2|\nabla A|^2\rho +\int _\Sigma \eta ^2(1-2f)f\rho \leq 2\int _\Sigma |\nabla \eta |^2\rho . \]

Both terms on the left are nonnegative.

Proof

First take a smooth test. Multiply Proposition 14.17.1 by $\eta ^2$ and integrate using Lemma 14.7.2. This gives

\[ 2\int \eta ^2|\nabla A|^2\rho +\int \eta ^2(1-2f)f\rho =-2\int \eta \langle \nabla \eta ,\nabla f\rangle \rho . \]

Metric compatibility gives $|\nabla f|\leq 2|A||\nabla A|$, including points where $A=0$. Put $K=1/\sqrt2$. Young's inequality bounds the absolute value of the right side by

\[ \int \eta ^2|\nabla A|^2\rho +4K^2\int |\nabla \eta |^2\rho . \]

Subtract the first term and use $4K^2=2$. Compactly supported Lipschitz tests follow by smooth approximation in coordinate charts on a fixed compact neighborhood. All coefficients there are bounded, so the integrals converge in the required local Sobolev norms. No division by $|A|$ occurs. $\square$

Proposition 14.17.3 (Finite Gaussian area forces parallel curvature at the gap).66389D683640 Suppose $\Sigma $ is complete and boundaryless, $\int _\Sigma \rho \, d\mu <\infty $, and $|A|^2\leq 1/2$. Then $\nabla A=0$ and $(1-2|A|^2)|A|^2=0$ everywhere. On each connected component, $|A|^2$ is identically zero or identically $1/2$.

Proof

Fix a point in a component. Hopf–Rinow makes closed intrinsic balls compact. The distance function supplies compactly supported Lipschitz cutoffs $\eta _ R$ equal to one on the radius-$R$ ball, zero outside the radius-$2R$ ball, with $|\nabla \eta _ R|\leq 2/R$. Lemma 14.17.2 bounds its left side by $8R^{-2}\int _\Sigma \rho $. On any fixed compact subset, both nonnegative integrands consequently have zero integral on letting $R\to \infty $. Smoothness makes both vanish pointwise. Parallelness makes the norm constant on a connected component, and its algebraic equation leaves the two stated values. Finite Gaussian area is the only global integration hypothesis used in this argument. $\square$

Lemma 14.17.4 (The image of a complete parallel hypersurface).69CCB32B46CE Let a connected complete boundaryless two-sided hypersurface in Euclidean space have $\nabla A=0$. Its image is either an affine hyperplane or a round cylinder $S^ k(r)\times \mathbb R^{n-k}$, $1\leq k\leq n$, up to an ambient rigid motion. This describes the immersed image and does not assert injectivity of the parametrization.

Proof

Parallel transport preserves the symmetric tensor $A$, so its eigenvalues and multiplicities are constant and its eigendistributions are parallel. If $v,w$ are unit vectors in distinct eigendistributions of eigenvalues $\lambda ,\mu $, their sectional curvature is zero. Indeed, the curvature operator preserves the eigendistribution of $w$, and hence $\langle R(v,w)w,v\rangle =0$. The Gauss equation identifies this sectional curvature with $\lambda \mu $. Thus distinct nonzero eigenvalues cannot occur.

If all eigenvalues vanish, the normal is constant and $X$ lies in one affine hyperplane. Otherwise let $\lambda \ne 0$ be the unique nonzero eigenvalue, of multiplicity $k$. The images of the nullspace under $dX$ form one fixed ambient plane $L$: parallel transport of a null vector along a curve has both zero covariant derivative and zero normal component of its ambient derivative. Let $\Pi $ be orthogonal projection onto $L^\perp $. Differentiation gives

\[ d\bigl (\Pi X-\nu /\lambda \bigr )(V)=\Pi dX(V)-dX(AV)/\lambda =0. \]

Therefore $\Pi X-\nu /\lambda =C$ is constant and the image lies in the cylinder $\{ z:|\Pi z-C|=1/|\lambda |\} $. Its tangent space agrees with that cylinder's tangent space at every point. In either case $X$ is a local isometry onto an open subset of the indicated connected complete model.

For completeness of the image, join an image point to any point of the model by a finite-length smooth path, subdivided into coordinate neighborhoods. A local isometry lifts it for as long as local inverse charts exist. If a lift stops at a finite parameter, its remaining lengths tend to zero, so it is Cauchy in the complete source and has a limit there. A local inverse chart at that limit continues it, a contradiction. Hence every such path lifts to its endpoint and the entire model is covered. The case $k=n$ has $L=\{ 0\} $ and gives a sphere. $\square$

Theorem 14.17.5 (The hypersurface curvature gap).7A3314CE0A68 Let $X:\Sigma ^ n\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be connected, complete, boundaryless, smooth and two-sided. Suppose it is a self-shrinker, has finite Gaussian area, and satisfies $|A|^2\leq 1/2$. Its image is a hyperplane through the origin or

\[ S^ k(\sqrt{2k})\times \mathbb R^{n-k},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n, \]

after an orthogonal ambient transformation. If $|A|^2<1/2$ at even one point, the image is a hyperplane. Polynomial area growth is a sufficient alternative to the finite-Gaussian-area hypothesis. Compare the normalization and gap theorem in [CL12]. The proof below treats hypersurfaces and uses finite Gaussian area; it does not import the arbitrary-codimension classification.

Proof

Proposition 14.17.3 and Lemma 14.17.4 give the plane or cylinder image. A plane has constant normal and $H=0$, so the shrinker equation forces its normal offset from the origin to vanish.

For a cylinder, use the notation of the lemma, with $C\in L^\perp $. Its normal ranges over the full unit sphere of $L^\perp $, and $H=k\lambda $. The shrinker equation becomes

\[ 2k\lambda =\langle C,\nu \rangle +1/\lambda . \]

The left side and last term are constant. Replacing a normal direction by its opposite gives $C=0$, and then $\lambda ^2=1/(2k)$. This is the claimed radius. Every such cylinder has $|A|^2=k\lambda ^2=1/2$, ruling it out under strict inequality at a point. Finally Lemma 14.7.4 implies finite Gaussian area from polynomial area growth. The proof concerns hypersurfaces; it does not silently apply the hypersurface Simons equation in higher codimension. $\square$

Corollary 14.17.6 (Compact and strict versions of the gap).D43AD8A2E6A9 A connected closed two-sided self-shrinker with $|A|^2\leq 1/2$ has image the sphere of radius $\sqrt{2n}$ centered at the origin. In particular every closed such shrinker satisfies $\max |A|^2\geq 1/2$, and equality gives that spherical image.

Proof

A closed immersed manifold has finite Gaussian area. Its image is compact, so the plane and the cylinders with a positive-dimensional Euclidean factor in Theorem 14.17.5 are impossible. If the maximum were strictly below $1/2$, that theorem would instead force a plane. The sphere has $|A|^2=1/2$ by its principal curvatures. $\square$

Proposition 14.17.7 (A mean-convex weighted curvature estimate).3C8B302A0B67 On a two-sided shrinker with $H>0$, every smooth $\phi $ compactly supported in the interior satisfies

\[ \int |A|^2\phi ^2\rho \leq \int |\nabla \phi |^2\rho +\frac12\int \phi ^2\rho . \]

This is the curvature estimate underlying [CM12], Proposition 10.14. It is not the definition of F-stability, which also varies center and scale.

Proof

Tracing Proposition 14.17.1 gives $\mathcal D H=(1/2-|A|^2)H$. The chain rule for the positive function $H$ yields

\[ \mathcal D\log H=\frac12-|A|^2-|\nabla \log H|^2. \]

Multiply by $\phi ^2$ and use compactly supported weighted integration by parts. Rearranging gives

\[ \int |A|^2\phi ^2\rho =\frac12\int \phi ^2\rho -\int \phi ^2|\nabla \log H|^2\rho +2\int \phi \langle \nabla \phi ,\nabla \log H\rangle \rho . \]

Completing the square bounds the last two terms by $\int |\nabla \phi |^2\rho $. Positivity is only needed on a neighborhood of the support of the test; completeness and growth assumptions play no role in this local estimate. $\square$
