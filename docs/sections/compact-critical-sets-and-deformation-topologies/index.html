8.5 Compact critical sets and deformation topologiesC15402F1FE6C

C15402F1FE6C

The continuous pull-tight argument in [MNIndex], Section 2.8, separates compactness, descent, and preservation of the homotopy class. Throughout this section $M^{n+1}$ is smooth, closed and connected, $n\geq 1$. All cycles have mod-two coefficients and lie in the boundary component of Definition 8.1.1. Varifolds need not be rectifiable.

Definition 8.5.1 (A mass cap and its stationary subset).7FF786CD9267 For $C>0$ let $K_ C$ be the space of all nonnegative $n$-varifolds $V$ on $M$ with $\| V\| (M)\leq C$, equipped with the bounded-Lipschitz metric $\mathbf F_ V$ on $G_ n(M)$. Write

\[ S_ C=\{ V\in K_ C:\delta V(X)=0\text{ for every smooth vector field }X\} . \]

Thus $0\in S_ C$. This compact ambient space, rather than a space restricted to integral varifolds, is used in [LiAP], Section 4.2. The first variation is $\delta V(X)=\int \operatorname {div}_ P X\, dV(x,P)$.

Lemma 8.5.2 (Compactness and joint continuity of first variation).6FFDB2ED1D18 The spaces $K_ C$ and $S_ C$ of Definition 8.5.1 are compact and metrizable. If $V_ j\to V$ weakly in $K_ C$ and $X_ j\to X$ in $C^1$, then $\delta V_ j(X_ j)\to \delta V(X)$.

Proof

The Grassmann bundle of a closed manifold is compact and metrizable. Weak compactness of bounded positive measures on a compact space, with the Riesz representation theorem, makes $K_ C$ compact. The bounded-Lipschitz metric induces this weak topology. These are the measure-compactness inputs. For fixed smooth $X$, the function $(x,P)\mapsto \operatorname {div}_ P X$ is continuous, so its integral is weakly continuous. Further,

\[ |\delta V_ j(X_ j-X)|\leq nC\| \nabla (X_ j-X)\| _\infty . \]

This proves joint continuity, first for smooth fields and then for $C^1$ fields by approximation. Each condition $\delta V(X)=0$ is closed; their intersection $S_ C$ is therefore compact. $\square$

Lemma 8.5.3 (A minimizing sequence has a compact nonempty critical set).FA1671BEBE2B Let $X$ be a finite simplicial complex, $\Pi $ a class in Definition 8.1.2, and $\Phi _ i\in \Pi $ a minimizing sequence with $L=\mathbf L(\Pi )<\infty $. Its critical set $\mathcal C$ is compact and nonempty, and every $V\in \mathcal C$ has mass $L$.

Proof

The maximal masses $m_ i$ converge to $L$, hence are uniformly bounded by some $C>0$. Mass is continuous in the $\mathbf F$ topology, so compactness of $X$ gives a maximizing slice for each $i$. Lemma 8.5.2 supplies a convergent subsequence of their varifolds, whose limit belongs to $\mathcal C$. The constant test function one shows that every critical limit has mass $L$. To prove closedness, suppose $V_ j\in \mathcal C$ and $V_ j\to V$. From a sequence defining $V_ j$ choose a slice of index $i_ j>\max \{ j,i_{j-1}\} $ whose varifold lies within $1/j$ of $V_ j$ and whose mass differs from $L$ by less than $1/j$. These slices converge to $V$ and certify that $V\in \mathcal C$. Thus $\mathcal C$ is closed in compact $K_ C$. $\square$

Lemma 8.5.4 (Late near-maximal slices concentrate near the critical set).1F1EF36EE50A In the setting of Lemma 8.5.3, let $\mathcal U$ be any open neighborhood of $\mathcal C$ in $K_ C$. There are $\eta >0$ and $i_0$ such that

\[ i\geq i_0,\quad \mathbf M(\Phi _ i(x))\geq L-\eta \quad \Longrightarrow \quad |\Phi _ i(x)|\in \mathcal U. \]
Proof

Otherwise choose $i_ j\to \infty $ and $x_ j$ with masses at least $L-1/j$ and varifolds outside $\mathcal U$. Their masses are at most $m_{i_ j}\to L$. A subsequence converges in $K_ C$ to a member of $\mathcal C$, but also to a point of the closed set $K_ C\setminus \mathcal U$. This contradicts $\mathcal C\subset \mathcal U$. $\square$

Lemma 8.5.5 (Joint continuity of diffeomorphic pushforward).D97A7755DDD4 Let $\varphi _ j,\varphi :M\to M$ be smooth diffeomorphisms with $\varphi _ j\to \varphi $ in $C^1$. If $V_ j\to V$ weakly with bounded mass, then $(\varphi _ j)_\# V_ j\to \varphi _\# V$ weakly. If boundary cycles $T_ j\to T$ in $\mathbf F$, then $(\varphi _ j)_\# T_ j\to \varphi _\# T$ in $\mathbf F$. The mod-two pushforward, product and homotopy formulas are external current-theory inputs here; the integer-current calculations alone do not establish this coefficient version. Compare the framework in [MNIndex], Section 2.

Proof

For a continuous test function $f$ on $G_ n(M)$, pushforward integrates

\[ f(\varphi _ j(x),D\varphi _ j(P))J_ P\varphi _ j(x) \]

against $V_ j$. These functions converge uniformly to the corresponding expression for $\varphi $. Uniform mass bounds and weak convergence give the first assertion. For the flat part, split the difference into $(\varphi _ j)_\# (T_ j-T)$ and $(\varphi _ j)_\# T-\varphi _\# T$. A uniform Lipschitz bound gives a uniform flat-norm operator bound for the first term: push forward a decomposition $T_ j-T=A+\partial B$ and bound the masses in dimensions $n$ and $n+1$. For the second, join the sufficiently close maps by the short ambient geodesic homotopy $h_ j$. Its time derivative is bounded by a constant times $\sup d(\varphi _ j,\varphi )$, and its spatial derivative is uniformly bounded. Since $\partial T=0$, the homotopy formula gives

\[ \mathcal F((\varphi _ j)_\# T-\varphi _\# T) \leq \mathbf M((h_ j)_\# ([0,1]\times T)) \leq A\sup d(\varphi _ j,\varphi )\mathbf M(T)\longrightarrow 0. \]

Here the injectivity radius is positive by compactness. Finally a diffeomorphism is injective, so the rectifiable Jacobian formula gives $|\varphi _\# T|=\varphi _\# |T|$ with no cancellation. Combining the varifold and flat conclusions proves $\mathbf F$ convergence. This identity is not asserted for general noninjective maps. $\square$

Example 8.5.6 (A smooth isotopy need not be continuous in mass).43B8B0899242 In the unit flat torus $M=\mathbb R^{n+1}/\mathbb Z^{n+1}$ let $T_ t$ be the mod-two boundary of the slab $t<x_{n+1}<t+a$, where $0<a<1/2$. Then $t\mapsto T_ t$ is $\mathbf F$-continuous, but for all sufficiently small nonzero $t$,

\[ \mathbf M(T_ t-T_0)=4=2\mathbf M(T_0). \]

This is the obstruction behind the interpolation warning in [LiAP], Section 4.2.

Proof

Each boundary is a pair of parallel unit $n$-tori. Translation is a smooth family of diffeomorphisms, so Lemma 8.5.5 gives $\mathbf F$ continuity. For sufficiently small nonzero $t$ the four boundary tori at heights $0,a,t,t+a$ are pairwise disjoint. There is no mod-two cancellation, and each has mass one. Thus mass distance stays four. Using a pair of tori ensures that every cycle lies in the boundary component; an individual coordinate torus would not have that property. $\square$
