14.13 Convexity and curvature pinching along the flowC6172D7FDBBC

C6172D7FDBBC

Throughout this section $F:M^ n\times [0,T)\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ is a smooth mean curvature flow of a closed manifold, with $n\geq 2$, and with a smooth choice of unit normal. We retain $h(X,Y)=\langle D_ X\nu ,Y\rangle $, $A=h^\sharp $, $H=\operatorname {tr}A$, and $\partial _ tF=-H\nu $. Weak convexity means $A\geq 0$; strict convexity means $A>0$. Mean convexity $H\geq 0$ is a weaker condition. The source is [Hui84], Sections 3–5, pp. 242–247. We prove the preservation and quotient estimates here; convergence of a normalized convex flow to a sphere requires further estimates.

Lemma 14.13.1 (Preserving a nonnegative endomorphism with scalar reaction).F00A2BFCC2AF Let $g(t)$ be a smooth family of metrics on a closed manifold for $0\leq t\leq \tau $. Suppose $S(t)$ is a smooth endomorphism, self-adjoint with respect to $g(t)$, satisfying

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta _{g(t)})S=aS \]

for a smooth scalar function $a$. If $S(0)\geq 0$, then $S(t)\geq 0$ for every $t\leq \tau $. The Laplacian on endomorphisms uses the connection of $g(t)$. This elementary special case suffices for the tensor-preservation arguments of [Hui84], Section 4.

Proof

Choose $C>\sup a$ on the compact space-time interval and put $S_\delta =S+\delta e^{Ct}I$, $\delta >0$. It is positive definite initially and satisfies

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta )S_\delta =aS_\delta +\delta e^{Ct}(C-a)I. \]

If positivity first fails, compactness supplies a first time $t_0>0$, a point $x_0$, and a nonzero vector $v$ with $S_\delta v=0$. Extend $v$ smoothly near this point with spatial covariant derivative zero there, and hold its coordinate components fixed in time. Before the first failure the scalar $\psi =g(S_\delta v,v)$ is positive; at $(x_0,t_0)$ it is zero and has a spatial minimum. Thus $(\partial _ t-\Delta )\psi \leq 0$ there.

At that point the derivative of the metric and the second derivatives of $v$ contribute zero, because $S_\delta v=0$ and $S_\delta $ is self-adjoint; first spatial derivatives of $v$ are zero by construction. Consequently

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta )\psi =g(((\partial _ t-\Delta )S_\delta )v,v) =\delta e^{Ct_0}(C-a)|v|^2>0, \]

a contradiction. Let $\delta \downarrow 0$. The argument treats the evolving metric explicitly and does not assume a time-independent orthonormal frame. $\square$

Proposition 14.13.2 (Preservation of convexity and linear pinching).483108BB132E Along the flow above, $A\geq 0$ is preserved. More generally, for any constants $\varepsilon ,\beta \in \mathbb R$, each inequality

\[ A\geq \varepsilon H I,\qquad A\leq \beta H I \]

is preserved if it holds initially. If the initial hypersurface is strictly convex, then it remains strictly convex at every smooth time. Compare [Hui84], Corollary 4.2 and Theorem 4.3, p. 245.

Proof

Proposition 14.3.4 gives $(\partial _ t-\Delta )A=|A|^2A$ and $(\partial _ t-\Delta )H=|A|^2H$. The identity endomorphism is parallel and has zero time derivative. Hence each of $A$, $A-\varepsilon HI$, and $\beta HI-A$ satisfies the scalar-reaction equation of Lemma 14.13.1. Apply it on any compact smooth time interval.

If $A>0$ initially, then $H>0$ and compactness supplies $\varepsilon >0$ with $A\geq \varepsilon HI$ initially. Mean curvature stays positive by Corollary 14.3.5, applied on each connected component. The preserved lower bound now implies $A>0$. No assertion about evolution beyond the maximal smooth time is made. $\square$

Proposition 14.13.3 (Evolution of the normalized curvature ratio).F7704EEF0D6B Wherever $H>0$, set $r=|A|^2/H^2$. Then

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta )r =\frac2H\langle \nabla H,\nabla r\rangle -\frac{2}{H^4}|H\nabla A-A\otimes \nabla H|^2. \]

This is the zero-parameter quotient identity in [Hui84], Lemma 5.2, pp. 246–247. The tensor in the last term has components $H\nabla _ kh_{ij}-h_{ij}\nabla _ kH$.

Proof

Write $Q=|A|^2$ and $L=\partial _ t-\Delta $. The evolution identities yield $LQ=-2|\nabla A|^2+2Q^2$ and $LH=QH$. The scalar chain and product rules therefore give

\[ L(QH^{-2})=-\frac{2|\nabla A|^2}{H^2} -\frac{6Q}{H^4}|\nabla H|^2 +\frac4{H^3}\langle \nabla Q,\nabla H\rangle . \]

The reaction terms cancel. Also

\[ \frac2H\langle \nabla H,\nabla r\rangle =\frac2{H^3}\langle \nabla H,\nabla Q\rangle -\frac{4Q}{H^4}|\nabla H|^2. \]

Subtract this expression. Expanding the square in the statement, and using $\nabla Q=2\langle A,\nabla A\rangle $, gives exactly the remaining three terms. The time-dependent metric has already been accounted for in the evolution of $Q$; treating the covariant components of $h$ as a fixed-metric matrix would not give that evolution correctly. $\square$

Corollary 14.13.4 (A nonincreasing curvature-ratio bound).401B923E6A7C If $H>0$ initially, then for every later smooth time

\[ \frac1n\leq \frac{|A|^2}{H^2}\leq \max _{M_0}\frac{|A|^2}{H^2}. \]

In particular an initial bound $|A|^2\leq CH^2$ is preserved, and the spatial maximum of $r$ is nonincreasing. This assertion does not by itself say that the maximum converges to $1/n$ at a singular time.

Proof

Positivity of $H$ persists. On each compact smooth time interval, $H$ has a positive minimum, so the drift $2\nabla H/H$ in Proposition 14.13.3 is smooth and bounded. The scalar parabolic maximum principle, applied from any starting time in the interval, makes the maximum of $r$ nonincreasing. The lower bound is the pointwise Cauchy–Schwarz inequality $(\sum _ i\kappa _ i)^2\leq n\sum _ i\kappa _ i^2$. Equality in this algebraic inequality means all principal curvatures agree. Compare [Hui84], Section 5; improved decay of the trace-free ratio requires more than this maximum-principle bound. $\square$

Lemma 14.13.5 (Parallel normalized curvature under strict convexity).B53165434118 Let a connected Euclidean hypersurface of dimension $n\geq 2$ be strictly convex and satisfy $\nabla (A/H)=0$. Then it is contained in a round sphere. If it is compact without boundary, its image is the entire sphere.

Proof

At a point use a principal orthonormal frame. The hypothesis says $\nabla _ i h_{jk}=h_{jk}\nabla _ iH/H$. Codazzi gives $h_{jk}\nabla _ iH=h_{ik}\nabla _ jH$. For each $i$ choose $j\ne i$ and put $k=j$; strict positivity of $\kappa _ j$ forces $\nabla _ iH=0$. Thus $H$ is constant and $\nabla h=0$.

Since $h$ is parallel, its derivative commutator is zero. Lemma 7.15.2, applied to $e_ i,e_ j,e_ i,e_ j$, yields $K_{ij}(\kappa _ i-\kappa _ j)=0$. The Euclidean Gauss equation gives $K_{ij}=\kappa _ i\kappa _ j>0$, so all eigenvalues agree: $A=\lambda I$ with $\lambda =H/n>0$ constant. Differentiating $F-\nu /\lambda $ now gives zero. Connectedness makes this vector a fixed center, and $|F-\text{center}|=1/\lambda $. The image of a compact immersed manifold of the same dimension as this sphere is open by the inverse function theorem and closed by compactness, hence is the full connected sphere. No global equality-classification theorem is used. $\square$

Theorem 14.13.6 (Rigidity when the maximum curvature ratio stops decreasing).10AE2686A175 Suppose $M$ is connected and the initial hypersurface is strictly convex. If for some smooth time $t_0>0$,

\[ \max _{M_{t_0}}r=\max _{M_0}r, \]

then every slice for $0\leq t\leq t_0$ has image a round sphere, and $r=1/n$ there. This is a consequence of the quotient identity in [Hui84], Lemma 5.2, together with the strong scalar maximum principle; it is distinct from the paper's general convergence theorem.

Proof

Let $C=\max _{M_0}r$. Proposition 14.13.2 preserves strict convexity, and Corollary 14.13.4 gives $r\leq C$. At time $t_0$, the nonnegative function $C-r$ vanishes somewhere. The strong parabolic maximum principle for the drift operator in Proposition 14.13.3 makes it zero on $M\times [0,t_0]$. To treat the top time as an interior time one can work on a slightly longer compact smooth interval, which exists because $t_0<T$; the conclusion propagates backward to the initial time by continuity.

The quotient identity then forces $H\nabla A-A\otimes \nabla H=0$ on each slice at positive time, which is $\nabla (A/H)=0$. Lemma 14.13.5 makes each such slice a round sphere, where $r=1/n$. Continuity gives the same identity and umbilicity at time zero; strict positivity and the lemma give the initial sphere as well. The scalar strong maximum principle is the analytic background input in this argument. $\square$

Example 14.13.7 (Cylinders have constant ratio without being umbilic).2BDD686532E1 For $1\leq k<n$, the shrinking round cylinder $\mathbb S^ k(R(t))\times \mathbb R^{n-k}$ with $R(t)^2=R(0)^2-2kt$ has

\[ H=\frac{k}{R(t)},\qquad |A|^2=\frac{k}{R(t)^2},\qquad r=\frac1k>\frac1n. \]

It has positive mean curvature and a constant curvature ratio, but is neither strictly convex nor compact. Thus it does not meet the hypotheses of Theorem 14.13.6.

Proof

The $k$ spherical principal curvatures are $1/R(t)$; the remaining $n-k$ principal curvatures are zero. Summing them and their squares gives the formulas. The radius equation $R'=-k/R$ is precisely the inward normal mean-curvature velocity. On each product slice the second fundamental form and $H$ are spatially parallel, so the negative square in Proposition 14.13.3 vanishes. The ratio remains $1/k$ because both numerator and denominator scale as $R^{-2}$. The zero principal curvatures explain exactly where the strict-convexity argument in Lemma 14.13.5 fails. $\square$
