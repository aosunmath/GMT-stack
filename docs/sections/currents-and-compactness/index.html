5.1 Currents and compactness9211826C5642

9211826C5642

Currents retain orientation and multiplicity. Their weak topology is defined by testing against differential forms. We use [Sim18], Chapter 6, and compare the overview in [Fle16], Sections 4–5.

Definition 5.1.1 (Current and boundary).C87631EB7AA5 Let $\mathcal{D}^ m(U)=C_ c^\infty (U;\Lambda ^ m(\mathbb {R}^ n)^*)$ be the space of smooth compactly supported $m$-forms, with its usual test-function topology. An $m$-current is a continuous linear functional $T:\mathcal{D}^ m(U)\to \mathbb {R}$. For $m\geq 1$ its boundary is the $(m-1)$-current

\[ \partial T(\eta )=T(d\eta ),\qquad \eta \in \mathcal{D}^{m-1}(U). \]

For $m=0$ set $\partial T=0$. Weak convergence $T_ j\rightharpoonup T$ means $T_ j(\omega )\to T(\omega )$ for every $\omega \in \mathcal{D}^ m(U)$. See [Sim18], Chapter 6, Section 2, pp. 159–161.

Proposition 5.1.2 (Boundary squared and weak continuity).7D8936D48805 Every current satisfies $\partial (\partial T)=0$. Moreover, $T_ j\rightharpoonup T$ implies $\partial T_ j\rightharpoonup \partial T$.

Proof

For an admissible test form $\eta $, $\partial ^2T(\eta )=T(d^2\eta )=0$. For a test form $\zeta $ of degree $m-1$, its exterior derivative is again smooth and compactly supported, so $\partial T_ j(\zeta )=T_ j(d\zeta )\to T(d\zeta )=\partial T(\zeta )$. The assertions in dimension zero follow from the convention in Definition 5.1.1. $\square$

Definition 5.1.3 (Mass and the comass convention).1EEA7973F645 The comass of an $m$-covector $\alpha $ is

\[ \| \alpha \| _* =\sup \{ |\langle \alpha ,v_1\wedge \cdots \wedge v_ m\rangle |: v_1,\ldots ,v_ m\text{ orthonormal}\} . \]

For $m=0$ it is the absolute value. For an open $W\subset U$ define

\[ \mathbf{M}_ W(T)=\sup \{ T(\omega ):\omega \in \mathcal{D}^ m(W), \sup _ x\| \omega (x)\| _*\leq 1\} . \]

Write $\mathbf{M}(T)=\mathbf{M}_ U(T)$. Test forms on $W$ are extended by zero to $U$.

We use the comass convention of [Fed69], Section 1.8, pp. 38–40. Simon initially uses the Euclidean norm on covectors in his mass definition, then discusses this distinction in [Sim18], Chapter 6, Remark 2.7, p. 160. Both conventions agree for the rectifiable currents in Definition 5.1.5; they need not agree for arbitrary currents. We will not identify their numerical values without this qualification.

Proposition 5.1.4 (Lower semicontinuity of mass).D219E445117D If $T_ j\rightharpoonup T$ in $U$, then for every open $W\subset U$,

\[ \mathbf{M}_ W(T)\leq \liminf _ j\mathbf{M}_ W(T_ j). \]
Proof

For each test form $\omega $ in the supremum defining $\mathbf{M}_ W(T)$, $T(\omega )=\lim _ jT_ j(\omega )\leq \liminf _ j\mathbf{M}_ W(T_ j)$. Take the supremum over $\omega $. This proof applies even if the limiting lower bound is infinite. Compare [Sim18], Chapter 6, Section 2. $\square$

Definition 5.1.5 (Integer rectifiable and integral currents).4220ABFF9A31 An $m$-current $T$ in $U$ is locally integer rectifiable if

\[ T(\omega )=\int _ M\theta (x)\langle \omega (x),\tau (x)\rangle \, d\mathcal{H}^ m(x), \]

where $M\subset U$ is $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and countably $m$-rectifiable, $\theta :M\to \{ 1,2,\ldots \} $ is measurable with $\int _{M\cap K}\theta \, d\mathcal{H}^ m<\infty $ for every compact $K\subset U$, and $\tau (x)$ is a measurable unit simple $m$-vector orienting $T_ xM$ almost everywhere. For $m=0$ this means a locally finite integer combination of point evaluations. It is locally integral if both $T$ and $\partial T$ are locally integer rectifiable. It is integral with finite mass and boundary mass if also $\mathbf{M}(T)+\mathbf{M}(\partial T)<\infty $.

For the displayed representation, $\mathbf{M}_ W(T)=\int _{M\cap W}\theta \, d\mathcal{H}^ m$. The mass formula and representation are cited facts; see [Sim18], Chapter 6, Definition 3.1 and Remark 3.2(3), pp. 172–173. Local finiteness and integrability are taken in the ambient open set, not merely on compact subsets of $M$.

Theorem 5.1.6 (Federer–Fleming compactness).558B04F2FD71 Let $1\leq m<n$. Suppose that $T_ j$ are integral $m$-currents in $\mathbb {R}^ n$, their supports lie in one compact set $K$, and

\[ \sup _ j\bigl (\mathbf{M}(T_ j)+\mathbf{M}(\partial T_ j)\bigr )<\infty . \]

Then a subsequence converges weakly to an integral current $T$ supported in $K$, and

\[ \mathbf{M}(T)\leq \liminf _ k\mathbf{M}(T_{j_ k}),\qquad \mathbf{M}(\partial T)\leq \liminf _ k\mathbf{M}(\partial T_{j_ k}). \]

Cited compactness and closure theorem; proof to be added. See [Sim18], Chapter 6, Theorem 3.15, p. 175, with its proof in Section 8, and [Fle16], Sections 4–5, pp. 5–7. The preservation of integer rectifiability is the substantial conclusion. The inequalities then follow from Propositions 5.1.2 and 5.1.4.
