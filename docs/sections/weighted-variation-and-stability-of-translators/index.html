14.14 Weighted variation and stability of translators67EE515CF47A

67EE515CF47A

Let $X:\Sigma ^ n\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a smooth immersed hypersurface, $n\ge 1$. For two-sided statements choose a smooth unit normal $\nu $, set $A=D\nu $ and $H=\operatorname {tr}A$, and use $\vec H=-H\nu $. Fix a constant velocity $v$. The translator equation is $H+\langle v,\nu \rangle =0$. We derive the local variation formulas in [HIMW19], Proposition 3.2, pp. 8–9, converting its downward velocity to this general convention. Compact support is always intrinsic and away from any boundary; finite total weighted area is not assumed.

Definition 14.14.1 (Translator weight and Jacobi operator).02ADA5490A9A Put $h=\langle X,v\rangle $ and $\rho _ v=e^ h$. Define

\[ \mathcal D_ v u=\Delta _\Sigma u+\langle v^ T,\nabla u\rangle , \qquad J_ vu=\mathcal D_ vu+|A|^2u, \qquad Q_ v(\phi )=\int _\Sigma (|\nabla \phi |^2-|A|^2\phi ^2)\rho _ v\, dA. \]

Weighted stability means $Q_ v(\phi )\ge 0$ for every smooth compactly supported interior test. This is a second variation for fixed $v$; it is distinct from dynamical stability under mean curvature flow and from ordinary minimal-surface stability. Compare [HIMW19], equations (3.5)–(3.7).

Lemma 14.14.2 (Translator drift in divergence form).00287673DB05 On any smooth immersed hypersurface, whether translating or not,

\[ \mathcal D_ vu=\rho _ v^{-1}\operatorname {div}_\Sigma (\rho _ v\nabla u), \qquad \int \phi \mathcal D_ vu\, \rho _ v=-\int \langle \nabla \phi ,\nabla u\rangle \rho _ v \]

for smooth $u$ and compactly supported smooth interior $\phi $.

Proof

The tangential gradient of $h$ is $v^ T$, hence $\nabla \rho _ v=\rho _ vv^ T$. Expand the divergence and integrate the compactly supported vector field $\phi \rho _ v\nabla u$. This proves both identities without an assumption on the total weighted area. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.3 (Linearization and second variation for fixed velocity).C1C8D24DC3BC At a two-sided translator, the derivative of $H+\langle v,\nu \rangle $ under normal speed $f\nu $ is $-J_ vf$. The second variation of $\int e^{\langle X,v\rangle }dA$ under compactly supported normal variations is $Q_ v(f)$.

Proof

The scalar variation formula, Proposition 11.3.4, gives $H'=-(\Delta +|A|^2)f$ in Euclidean space. Differentiating the orthogonality and unit-length equations for the normal gives $\nu '=-\nabla f$. Thus $\langle v,\nu \rangle '=-\langle v^ T,\nabla f\rangle $, proving the linearization.

The weighted first variation of Proposition 16.3.8 is $\int \rho _ v(H+\langle v,\nu \rangle )f$. At a translator, every term differentiating the weight, speed or area measure is multiplied by the vanishing Euler expression. The remaining term is $-\int \rho _ vfJ_ vf=Q_ v(f)$ by Lemma 14.14.2. This derives the sign of the formula rather than inferring it from the source's opposite velocity convention. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.4 (Translation angles and mean curvature are Jacobi fields).322C9B303954 For every constant ambient vector $a$, the normal component $w_ a=\langle a,\nu \rangle $ satisfies $J_ vw_ a=0$ on a two-sided translator. In particular $J_ vH=0$.

Proof

Ambient translations preserve both the Euclidean curvature and the equation with fixed $v$. Their normal speed is $w_ a$. The tangential part differentiates the identically zero Euler expression, so Proposition 14.14.3 gives $J_ vw_ a=0$. Since $H=-w_ v$, the last assertion follows. This argument concerns the translator equation even though translating the surface may multiply its weighted area by a constant. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.5 (Height and exponential drift identities).B857AAD27409 On a translator,

\[ \mathcal D_ vX=v,\qquad \mathcal D_ vh=|v|^2,\qquad |\nabla h|^2=|v|^2-H^2, \qquad J_ v(e^{-h})=(|A|^2-H^2)e^{-h}. \]

The first two identities do not require two-sidedness; the latter two use the scalar notation on a two-sided hypersurface.

Proof

The immersion identity gives $\Delta X=\vec H=v^\perp $, and the drift term applied to $X$ is $v^ T$. Pair their sum with $v$ to obtain the first two formulas. Since $v^\perp =-H\nu $, the orthogonal decomposition gives the gradient identity. The chain rule now yields

\[ \mathcal D_ ve^{-h}=e^{-h}(-\mathcal D_ vh+|\nabla h|^2)=-H^2e^{-h}. \]

Adding the potential proves the last formula. These identities are pointwise; integration over the whole surface would need additional integrability. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.6 (A weighted ground-state identity).6C4FB1EB0F30 Let $w>0$ be smooth on a two-sided translator. For every smooth compactly supported interior $\phi $,

\[ Q_ v(\phi )=\int w^2\left|\nabla \left(\frac\phi w\right)\right|^2\rho _ v -\int \frac{J_ vw}{w}\phi ^2\rho _ v. \]

Consequently a positive solution or supersolution $J_ vw\le 0$ proves weighted stability.

Proof

Set $\eta =\phi /w$. Expanding $|\nabla (w\eta )|^2$ leaves $w^2|\nabla \eta |^2$ and the terms $\langle \nabla w,\nabla (w\eta ^2)\rangle -|A|^2w^2\eta ^2$. Integrate the latter by Lemma 14.14.2; their sum is $-w\eta ^2J_ vw$. This is exactly the stated identity. Positivity of $w$ makes $\eta $ a legitimate compactly supported smooth test even when $w$ tends to zero at infinity. $\square$

Corollary 14.14.7 (Translating graphs are weighted stable).93A817E936B9 Every smooth translating graph over a nonempty connected open subset of $\mathbb R^ n$ is weighted stable. Its quadratic form is strictly positive on every nonzero compactly supported smooth test, although its variational spectral bottom need not be positive.

Proof

The upward normal has positive vertical angle $w=(1+|Du|^2)^{-1/2}$, and Proposition 14.14.4 gives $J_ vw=0$. Apply Proposition 14.14.6. If the quadratic form vanishes, $\phi /w$ has zero gradient, so connectedness makes it constant. The graph, as a manifold without its domain boundary, is noncompact; a nonzero constant multiple of $w>0$ cannot have compact support. Thus $\phi =0$. A sequence of compact tests can still have Rayleigh quotients tending to zero, as the grim-reaper example will show. No uniform bound for $w$ away from zero is needed. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.8 (A complete translator has infinite translator-weighted area).39F6B8FBFB5C Let a nonempty connected complete boundaryless immersed hypersurface translate with $v\ne 0$. Then $\int _\Sigma e^{\langle X,v\rangle }\, dA=\infty $, regardless of properness.

Proof

Assume the integral finite. Choose intrinsic Lipschitz cutoffs $0\le \eta _ R\le 1$, equal to one on the radius-$R$ intrinsic ball, supported in the radius-$2R$ ball, with $|\nabla \eta _ R|\le 2/R$. Completeness makes the supports compact. The integration-by-parts formula extends to these tests by smooth approximation on compact subsets. Proposition 14.14.5 and $|\nabla h|\le |v|$ give

\[ |v|^2\int \eta _ R\rho _ v=-\int \langle \nabla \eta _ R,\nabla h\rangle \rho _ v, \qquad \left|\int \langle \nabla \eta _ R,\nabla h\rangle \rho _ v\right| \le \frac{2|v|}{R}\int \rho _ v. \]

Dominated convergence makes the left side tend to a strictly positive number, while the right side tends to zero. This contradiction proves the claim. All cutoffs are intrinsic, so no unmentioned properness assumption enters. $\square$

Proposition 14.14.9 (A weighted gradient gap and boundary-volume inequality).9DC0106A8973 On a translator with $v\ne 0$, every compactly supported smooth interior $\phi $ satisfies

\[ \int |\nabla \phi |^2\rho _ v\ge \frac{|v|^2}{4}\int \phi ^2\rho _ v. \]

For a relatively compact smooth domain $D$ in the hypersurface,

\[ \int _{\partial D}\rho _ v\ge |v|\int _ D\rho _ v. \]

These are estimates for the drift-gradient energy; subtracting $|A|^2$ to form $Q_ v$ can remove the positive gap.

Proof

Test $\mathcal D_ vh=|v|^2$ against $\phi ^2$ and integrate by parts:

\[ |v|^2\int \phi ^2\rho _ v=-2\int \phi \langle \nabla \phi ,\nabla h\rangle \rho _ v \le 2|v|\left(\int \phi ^2\rho _ v\right)^{1/2} \left(\int |\nabla \phi |^2\rho _ v\right)^{1/2}. \]

Division and squaring prove the first estimate, including the zero-test case separately. For the second, the divergence formula gives $|v|^2\int _ D\rho _ v=\int _{\partial D}\rho _ v\langle v^ T,\eta \rangle \le |v|\int _{\partial D}\rho _ v$. The outward conormal and the pointwise bound $|v^ T|\le |v|$ fix the sign and constant. $\square$

Lemma 14.14.10 (A positive Jacobi field controls the weighted square-integrable kernel).53FAD32E9101 Let $\Sigma $ be connected, complete, boundaryless and two-sided, and suppose $w>0$ satisfies $J_ vw=0$. Every smooth solution $J_ vu=0$ with $\int u^2\rho _ v<\infty $ is a constant multiple of $w$. Thus the weighted square-integrable kernel is zero if $w\notin L^2(\rho _ vdA)$, and is spanned by $w$ if $w\in L^2(\rho _ vdA)$.

Proof

For an intrinsic cutoff $\eta _ R$ as in Proposition 14.14.8, integration by parts against $\eta _ R^2u$ gives the exact identity

\[ Q_ v(\eta _ Ru)=\int u^2|\nabla \eta _ R|^2\rho _ v. \]

Smooth approximation justifies Lipschitz cutoffs. Combining with Proposition 14.14.6 yields

\[ \int w^2\left|\nabla \left(\frac{\eta _ Ru}{w}\right)\right|^2\rho _ v \le \frac4{R^2}\int u^2\rho _ v. \]

On any fixed compact subset, $\eta _ R=1$ for large $R$. Letting $R$ tend to infinity therefore shows $\nabla (u/w)=0$ there. Exhaustion and connectedness give $u=cw$. No global integrability of $|A|^2u^2$ was assumed: both cancellations occur with compactly supported tests before the limit. $\square$
