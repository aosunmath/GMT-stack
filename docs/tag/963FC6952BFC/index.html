Theorem 5.12.8 (Local slicing of locally normal currents)963FC6952BFC

Theorem 5.12.8 (Local slicing of locally normal currents).963FC6952BFC Let $T$ be a locally normal $m$-current on $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $m\geq 1$, and let $f:U\to \mathbb R$ be locally Lipschitz. Outside a single Lebesgue-null set of levels, its slice is locally normal, is supported in $\operatorname {spt}T\cap f^{-1}(t)$, and satisfies

\[ \partial B_ t^ T=-B_ t^{\partial T}\quad (m\geq 2). \]

If $V\Subset U$ is open and $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz on an open neighborhood of $\overline V$, then

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\mathbf M_ V(B_ t^ T)\, dt\leq L\| T\| (V). \]

The analogous bound for its boundary holds with $\| \partial T\| $ when $m\geq 2$. This is the local normal-current setting of [Sim18], pp. 184–185.

Proof

First fix such a neighborhood and choose a smooth cutoff $0\leq \chi \leq 1$ supported there, equal to one near $\overline V$. The current $\chi T$, extended by zero to $\mathbb R^ N$, is compactly supported and normal by the smooth product rule. Proposition 3.4.2 extends the restriction of $f$ on that neighborhood to a globally $L$-Lipschitz function $\widetilde f$; explicitly one can take the infimum of $f(y)+L|x-y|$ over points $y$ in the neighborhood. It equals $f$ there by its Lipschitz inequality.

Apply Theorem 5.12.4 to $(\chi T,\widetilde f)$. Proposition 5.12.7 identifies its slice with that of $T$ on $V$. On this open set $\| \chi T\| =\| T\| $ and $\| \partial (\chi T)\| =\| \partial T\| $, because $\chi =1$ on a neighborhood. This proves the stated local mass estimates and normality on $V$ at almost every level.

Cover $U$ by countably many relatively compact balls on neighborhoods of which $f$ is Lipschitz. Remove the null sets furnished for those balls and, when needed, for $\partial T$. Local normality then holds simultaneously throughout $U$. The distributional boundary identity follows by $\partial ^2=0$ exactly as in the compact case. The support statement is local: on the open strict sublevel the two terms cancel, on the open strict superlevel they vanish, and tests off $\operatorname {spt}T$ vanish. This gives the conclusion even when neither $T$ nor its boundary has finite total mass in $U$. $\square$
