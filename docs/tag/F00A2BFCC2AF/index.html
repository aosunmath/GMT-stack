Lemma 14.13.1 (Preserving a nonnegative endomorphism with scalar reaction)F00A2BFCC2AF

Lemma 14.13.1 (Preserving a nonnegative endomorphism with scalar reaction).F00A2BFCC2AF Let $g(t)$ be a smooth family of metrics on a closed manifold for $0\leq t\leq \tau $. Suppose $S(t)$ is a smooth endomorphism, self-adjoint with respect to $g(t)$, satisfying

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta _{g(t)})S=aS \]

for a smooth scalar function $a$. If $S(0)\geq 0$, then $S(t)\geq 0$ for every $t\leq \tau $. The Laplacian on endomorphisms uses the connection of $g(t)$. This elementary special case suffices for the tensor-preservation arguments of [Hui84], Section 4.

Proof

Choose $C>\sup a$ on the compact space-time interval and put $S_\delta =S+\delta e^{Ct}I$, $\delta >0$. It is positive definite initially and satisfies

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta )S_\delta =aS_\delta +\delta e^{Ct}(C-a)I. \]

If positivity first fails, compactness supplies a first time $t_0>0$, a point $x_0$, and a nonzero vector $v$ with $S_\delta v=0$. Extend $v$ smoothly near this point with spatial covariant derivative zero there, and hold its coordinate components fixed in time. Before the first failure the scalar $\psi =g(S_\delta v,v)$ is positive; at $(x_0,t_0)$ it is zero and has a spatial minimum. Thus $(\partial _ t-\Delta )\psi \leq 0$ there.

At that point the derivative of the metric and the second derivatives of $v$ contribute zero, because $S_\delta v=0$ and $S_\delta $ is self-adjoint; first spatial derivatives of $v$ are zero by construction. Consequently

\[ (\partial _ t-\Delta )\psi =g(((\partial _ t-\Delta )S_\delta )v,v) =\delta e^{Ct_0}(C-a)|v|^2>0, \]

a contradiction. Let $\delta \downarrow 0$. The argument treats the evolving metric explicitly and does not assume a time-independent orthonormal frame. $\square$
