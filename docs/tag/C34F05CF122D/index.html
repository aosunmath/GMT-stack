Lemma 7.12.5 (Weak continuity of a finite family of ambient second variations)C34F05CF122D

Lemma 7.12.5 (Weak continuity of a finite family of ambient second variations).C34F05CF122D Let $M$ be smooth and closed, and let stationary $n$-varifolds $V_ j\to V$ weakly. Fix smooth ambient vector fields $X_1,\ldots ,X_ k$, put $X_ z=\sum _ a z_ aX_ a$, and define

\[ \varphi _ t^ z(x)=\exp _ x(tX_ z(x)),\qquad Q_{V_ j}(z)=\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\right|_{t=0}\| (\varphi _ t^ z)_\# V_ j\| (M). \]

These symmetric quadratic forms converge coefficientwise to $Q_ V$. For a smooth minimal hypersurface, the form is the usual second variation of its normal velocity. Compare [Sharp17], p. 324.

Proof

For $z$ in a fixed bounded set and $t$ sufficiently small, the maps are smooth diffeomorphisms close to the identity. The area formula gives their masses by integrating $J_ P\varphi _ t^ z(x)$ over $G_ n(M)$. Its second time derivative at zero is a continuous function of $(x,P)$ depending quadratically on $z$: it is the Hessian at zero of the smooth finite-parameter family $x\mapsto \exp _ x(\sum _ a s_ aX_ a(x))$, evaluated on $(z,z)$. All its derivatives in the finite parameters are uniformly bounded on the compact Grassmann bundle near zero, so differentiation under the integral is valid. Weak convergence makes the integral of each coefficient converge. The constant test function also gives uniform mass bounds. Stationarity of $V$ follows from weak continuity of first variation. For a smooth minimal hypersurface, tangential velocity is infinitesimal reparametrization and the acceleration term pairs with zero mean curvature; the second-variation formula therefore identifies the resulting form with the Jacobi quadratic form of $X_ z^\perp $. No bound on the second fundamental forms of the approximating hypersurfaces was used in the weak-continuity argument. $\square$
