Lemma 8.6.2 (A continuous selection of smooth descending fields)ED073D8BA201

Lemma 8.6.2 (A continuous selection of smooth descending fields).ED073D8BA201 There is a map $V\mapsto Y_ V$ from $K_ C$ to smooth vector fields, continuous in every $C^ k$ norm and uniformly bounded in each such norm, with $Y_ V=0$ for $V\in S_ C$ and $\delta V(Y_ V)<0$ for $V\notin S_ C$.

Proof

The open subspace $K_ C\setminus S_ C$ is second countable, so the cover in Lemma 8.6.1 has a countable subcover $U_ i$, with associated fields $X_ i$. If this subspace is empty use $Y=0$. Otherwise, repeating sets if the cover is finite, define

\[ \chi _ i(V)=\frac{d(V,K_ C\setminus U_ i)}{1+d(V,K_ C\setminus U_ i)},\qquad B_ i=\max \{ 1,\| X_ i\| _{C^0},\ldots ,\| X_ i\| _{C^ i}\} ,\qquad Y_ V=\sum _{i=1}^\infty 2^{-i}\chi _ i(V)X_ i/B_ i. \]

The complements are nonempty because they contain zero. Each $\chi _ i$ is continuous, vanishes off $U_ i$, and is positive on $U_ i$. For any fixed $k$, the tail starting at $i\geq k$ is bounded in $C^ k$ by the summable series $\sum 2^{-i}$. Thus the series converges uniformly in every $C^ k$ norm and defines a spatially smooth field; uniform convergence of the continuous finite sums proves continuity of the selection and uniform bounds. On $S_ C$ every coefficient vanishes. For other $V$, all terms in $\delta V(Y_ V)$ are nonpositive and at least one is strictly negative. Passing first variation through the sum is justified by its $C^1$ convergence and the bound in Lemma 8.5.2. $\square$
