Theorem 5.12.4 (Lipschitz slicing of compact normal currents)A55702EC34C6

Theorem 5.12.4 (Lipschitz slicing of compact normal currents).A55702EC34C6 Let $T$ be a compactly supported normal $m$-current in $\mathbb R^ N$, $m\geq 1$, and let $f:\mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R$ be $L$-Lipschitz. For almost every $t$, the common lower and upper slice $B_ t$ is a compactly supported normal $(m-1)$-current. For every open $W$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\mathbf M_ W(B_ t)\, dt\leq L\| T\| (W). \]

If $m\geq 2$, then $\partial B_ t=-B_ t^{\partial T}$ at almost every level and

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\mathbf M_ W(\partial B_ t)\, dt \leq L\| \partial T\| (W). \]

For $m=1$, the slice boundary is zero. No rectifiability or integer-multiplicity conclusion is asserted. These are the normal-current slicing estimates of [Sim18], equations (4.8)–(4.10), with compact support used to state global mass integrals.

Proof

Let $f_ j$ be standard smooth ambient mollifications with radii tending to zero. Proposition 3.4.7 gives uniform convergence to $f$ and $|Df_ j|\leq L$. The finite measure $f_\# (\| T\| +\| \partial T\| )$ has at most countably many atoms. At every other level, Lemma 5.12.3 gives flat, hence weak, convergence of the corresponding slices. For fixed open $W$, mass is lower semicontinuous under weak convergence: it is the supremum of the continuous evaluations on tests of comass at most one supported in $W$. Fatou and Proposition 5.12.2 yield

\[ \int \mathbf M_ W(B_ t^ f)\, dt \leq \liminf _ j\int \mathbf M_ W(B_ t^{f_ j})\, dt \leq L\| T\| (W). \]

Measurability follows from the same countable test family as in that proposition, applied to the order-one restriction formula. Taking $W=\mathbb R^ N$ proves finite mass at almost every level. The support is compact by Proposition 5.11.2.

When $m\geq 2$, repeat the mass argument for the normal current $\partial T$, whose boundary is zero. At every level the distributional algebra gives $\partial B_ t^ T=-B_ t^{\partial T}$. On the intersection of the two full-measure finite-mass sets this proves normality and the boundary integral estimate. In dimension one a zero-current has zero boundary, so the first mass conclusion already proves normality. Finally the two one-sided slices agree off the at most countable atomic set by Theorem 5.11.4. The quantitative measure inequality holds for each $W$; the full-measure set on which slices are normal is chosen once using their global masses. $\square$
