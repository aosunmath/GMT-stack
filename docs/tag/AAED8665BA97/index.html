Example 16.10.8 (The infinite cylinder is unstable under compactly supported volume constraints)AAED8665BA97

Example 16.10.8 (The infinite cylinder is unstable under compactly supported volume constraints).AAED8665BA97 The round cylinder $S_ R^1\times \mathbb R\subset \mathbb R^3$ has a smooth compactly supported normal speed $f$ with $\int f\, dA=0$ and $Q(f,f)<0$. Thus even the first-order volume constraint does not make the infinite cylinder stable.

Proof

Choose a nonzero odd smooth function $\eta \in C_ c^\infty (\mathbb R)$ and set $f_ T(\theta ,z)=\eta (z/T)$. The integral of the speed vanishes by oddness. Direct substitution into the cylinder form gives

\[ Q(f_ T,f_ T)=2\pi R\left(T^{-1}\int _{\mathbb R}|\eta '|^2 -\frac T{R^2}\int _{\mathbb R}\eta ^2\right)<0 \]

for sufficiently large $T$. The support is compact on the cylinder. If an exactly volume-preserving path is desired, add a second compactly supported normal variation with nonzero integral and solve for its coefficient by the same implicit-function argument as Proposition 11.3.2. Here volume means the signed change in the bounded region affected by the compactly supported perturbation, not the infinite total volume of the solid cylinder. The zero first derivative of that correction preserves the displayed negative second variation. $\square$
