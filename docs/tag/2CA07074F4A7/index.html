Theorem 2.5.6 (Lebesgue differentiation with absolute oscillation)2CA07074F4A7

Theorem 2.5.6 (Lebesgue differentiation with absolute oscillation).2CA07074F4A7 If $f\in L^1_{\rm loc}(U)$, almost every point of $U$ is a Lebesgue point of any representative finite almost everywhere. Consequently

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\omega _ n r^ n}\int _{B_ r(x)}f(y)\, dy=f(x) \quad \text{for almost every }x\in U. \]

Compare [Sim18], Corollary 4.10. Here the proof is restricted to Lebesgue measure on a Euclidean open set.

Proof

First take $U=\mathbb R^ n$ and $f\in L^1$. Denote the limsup in Definition 2.5.5 by $Df(x)$. For $g\in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$, continuity gives $Dg=0$, so the triangle inequality implies

\[ Df(x)\leq M(f-g)(x)+|f(x)-g(x)|. \]

The weak maximal estimate and Chebyshev's inequality yield, for $a>0$,

\[ \mathcal L^ n(\{ Df>2a\} )\leq \frac{5^ n+1}{a}\| f-g\| _1. \]

These sets are measurable: for each point with finite $f(x)$ the ball integrals vary continuously with the positive radius, so the limsup can be taken over rational radii; joint measurability follows from integration of the measurable integrand. By Theorem 2.3.8, choose $g$ with arbitrarily small $L^1$ error. Thus $\{ Df>2a\} $ is null for each $a>0$. A countable sequence $a\downarrow 0$ proves $Df=0$ almost everywhere.

For the local assertion, choose compact sets $K_ m$ with $K_ m\subset \operatorname {int}K_{m+1}$ exhausting $U$. Multiply $f$ by a compactly supported cutoff equal to one near $K_ m$, and extend by zero outside $U$. This gives an $L^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ function agreeing with $f$ near $K_ m$. The global result applies on each $K_ m$; their countable union gives the local assertion. Finally the absolute value of the difference between the average of $f$ and $f(x)$ is bounded by the average absolute oscillation. $\square$
