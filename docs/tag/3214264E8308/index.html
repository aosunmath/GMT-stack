Example 4.2.11 (Strict convergence need not be BV-norm convergence)3214264E8308

Example 4.2.11 (Strict convergence need not be BV-norm convergence).3214264E8308 In one dimension let $u=\chi _{(0,1)}$ and let $u_\varepsilon $ be its nonnegative smooth mollifications. Then $u_\varepsilon \to u$ strictly by Corollary 4.2.5, and $|Du|(\mathbb R)=2$. But $Du=\delta _0-\delta _1$ is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, whereas $Du_\varepsilon $ is absolutely continuous. Hence these derivative measures are mutually singular and

\[ |D(u_\varepsilon -u)|(\mathbb R)=\int |Du_\varepsilon |\, dx+2\longrightarrow 4. \]

Thus the stronger BV norm does not tend to zero. This example also distinguishes weak convergence of vector measures from convergence of their total variation norms.
