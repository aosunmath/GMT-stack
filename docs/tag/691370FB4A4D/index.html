Example 4.8.4 (Squaring a singular-continuous function)691370FB4A4D

Example 4.8.4 (Squaring a singular-continuous function).691370FB4A4D Let $C$ be the Cantor function on $(0,1)$. Then

\[ D(C^2)=2C\, \mu _ C, \]

a singular-continuous positive measure of total mass one. In particular $\int C\, d\mu _ C=1/2$.

Proof

The function $C$ is continuous, with $DC=\mu _ C$ by Example 16.1.11. Theorem 4.8.1 therefore reduces to the coefficient $2C$ everywhere. The derivative is singular because it is supported on the Cantor set, and nonatomic because $\mu _ C$ is nonatomic. It is positive since $C\geq 0$. Its mass equals $C(1-)^2-C(0+)^2=1$ by the trace identity, yielding the integral. This also provides the variation of $C^2$ without replacing its derivative by the almost-everywhere classical derivative, which vanishes off the Cantor set. $\square$
