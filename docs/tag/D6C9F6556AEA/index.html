Proposition 8.4.8 (A common parameter above several cost thresholds)D6C9F6556AEA

Proposition 8.4.8 (A common parameter above several cost thresholds).D6C9F6556AEA Let $\beta \in H^1(X;\mathbb Z_2)$ on a finite complex satisfy $\beta ^ p\ne 0$. Suppose $p_ i\geq 1$ and $\sum _{i=1}^ N p_ i\leq p$. Let $c_ i:X\to [0,\infty )$ be continuous, let $a_ i>0$, and assume $\beta ^{p_ i}$ restricts to zero on $U_ i=\{ c_ i<a_ i\} $. Then some $x\in X$ satisfies $c_ i(x)\geq a_ i$ for every $i$. If $F(x)\geq \sum _ i c_ i(x)$ for all $x$, then $\sup _ XF\geq \sum _ i a_ i$.

Proof

If the $U_ i$ covered $X$, Lemma 8.4.7 would give $\beta ^{\sum _ i p_ i}=0$. Multiplying by the remaining cup power, or using this equality directly if the sum is $p$, would give $\beta ^ p=0$, a contradiction. Thus a point lies outside every $U_ i$ and satisfies all thresholds. The bound for $F$ follows at that point. Applying this abstract lemma to cycle masses in subdomains requires additional work: those costs need not be continuous in the flat topology, and restriction need not give finite-mass relative boundaries. These are precisely the issues recorded in [LMN], p. 937; their geometric resolution is not proved by the present topological lemma. $\square$
