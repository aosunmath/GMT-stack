Example 16.15.4 (The Clifford torus shrinker in four dimensions)2E92C8C6AAA0

Example 16.15.4 (The Clifford torus shrinker in four dimensions).2E92C8C6AAA0 The map

\[ X(\theta ,\varphi )=(\sqrt2\cos \theta ,\sqrt2\sin \theta , \sqrt2\cos \varphi ,\sqrt2\sin \varphi ) \]

on $(\mathbb R/2\pi \mathbb Z)^2$ is a flat embedded torus in $\mathbb R^4$ with $|B|^2=1$, $|\vec H|^2=1$, area $8\pi ^2$ and $F_{0,1}=2\pi /e$. It is minimal in $S^3(2)$, where its squared second fundamental form is $1/2$.

Proof

The metric is $2(d\theta ^2+d\varphi ^2)$, so it is flat with area $8\pi ^2$. Apply Example 16.15.2 with two circle factors. Since $|X|^2=4$, direct substitution gives $F_{0,1}=(4\pi )^{-1}e^{-1}8\pi ^2=2\pi /e$. Proposition 16.15.1 subtracts $1/2$ from the Euclidean curvature norm to give the spherical value. This is a codimension-two shrinker and its Euclidean curvature exceeds the hypersurface gap threshold. $\square$
