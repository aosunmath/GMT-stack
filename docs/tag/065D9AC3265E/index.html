Lemma 2.3.2 (Compact cutoffs and a finite partition of unity)065D9AC3265E

Lemma 2.3.2 (Compact cutoffs and a finite partition of unity).065D9AC3265E If $K\subset U$ is compact and $O\subset U$ is open with $K\subset O$, there is $\eta \in C_ c(O)$, $0\leq \eta \leq 1$, equal to one on a neighborhood of $K$. If finitely many open sets $O_1,\ldots ,O_ N$ cover $K$, there are $\eta _ i\in C_ c(O_ i)$, $0\leq \eta _ i\leq 1$, whose sum is one on a neighborhood of $K$ and is at most one everywhere.

Proof

The empty-set cases are immediate. For the first assertion choose $\delta >0$ so that the closed $\delta $-neighborhood of $K$ is compact and lies in $O$. A continuous piecewise linear function of $\operatorname {dist}(x,K)$, equal to one for distance at most $\delta /2$ and zero for distance at least $\delta $, is the required cutoff. For the second assertion, cover $K$ by finitely many small balls whose closures each lie in one of the $O_ i$. Grouping their cutoffs gives nonnegative $\psi _ i\in C_ c(O_ i)$ with $S=\sum _ i\psi _ i>0$ on a neighborhood of $K$. Apply the first assertion to obtain $\chi \in C_ c(\{ S>0\} )$, equal to one near $K$ and between zero and one. Set $\eta _ i=\chi \psi _ i/S$ where $S>0$, and zero elsewhere. Its extension is continuous because $\operatorname {spt}\chi $ stays in $\{ S>0\} $. The sum equals $\chi $. This is the Euclidean version of [Sim18], Lemma 5.4 and Corollary 5.5. $\square$
