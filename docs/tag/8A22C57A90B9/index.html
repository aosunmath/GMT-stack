Proposition 12.10.3 (When the distance profile is an exact solution)8A22C57A90B9

Proposition 12.10.3 (When the distance profile is an exact solution).8A22C57A90B9 Put $u_\varepsilon (F(y,z))=q(z/\varepsilon )$ and $R_\varepsilon =\varepsilon ^2\Delta u_\varepsilon -W'(u_\varepsilon )$. Then $R_\varepsilon =\varepsilon H_ z\psi (z/\varepsilon )$, which is uniformly $O(\varepsilon )$, and is uniformly $O(\varepsilon ^2)$ when $\Sigma $ is minimal. If this profile is an exact solution throughout a tube for even one $\varepsilon >0$, then $A=0$ on its base; every connected base patch is contained in a plane. If $H(y_0)\ne 0$, the general $O(\varepsilon )$ estimate is sharp at $(y_0,0)$.

Proof

The equation $q''=W'(q)$ cancels the normal second derivative, leaving precisely the residual already identified in Proposition 12.4.4. The boundedness of $H_ z$ gives the general estimate. In the minimal case $|H_ z|\leq C|z|$, and $|t|\psi (t)$ is bounded, giving $C\varepsilon ^2$. If the residual vanishes identically, positivity of $\psi $ forces $H_ z=0$ for every $z$ in the tube. Differentiate at zero using Lemma 12.10.1: $0=-|A|^2$. The normal is therefore constant on each connected patch, and $y\cdot \nu $ is constant along it, proving planarity. Finally $R_\varepsilon (y_0,0)=\varepsilon H(y_0)\psi (0)$ proves sharpness when the mean curvature does not vanish. $\square$
