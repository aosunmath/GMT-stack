Definition 5.1.5 (Integer rectifiable and integral currents)4220ABFF9A31

Definition 5.1.5 (Integer rectifiable and integral currents).4220ABFF9A31 An $m$-current $T$ in $U$ is locally integer rectifiable if

\[ T(\omega )=\int _ M\theta (x)\langle \omega (x),\tau (x)\rangle \, d\mathcal{H}^ m(x), \]

where $M\subset U$ is $\mathcal{H}^ m$-measurable and countably $m$-rectifiable, $\theta :M\to \{ 1,2,\ldots \} $ is measurable with $\int _{M\cap K}\theta \, d\mathcal{H}^ m<\infty $ for every compact $K\subset U$, and $\tau (x)$ is a measurable unit simple $m$-vector orienting $T_ xM$ almost everywhere. For $m=0$ this means a locally finite integer combination of point evaluations. It is locally integral if both $T$ and $\partial T$ are locally integer rectifiable. It is integral with finite mass and boundary mass if also $\mathbf{M}(T)+\mathbf{M}(\partial T)<\infty $.

For the displayed representation, $\mathbf{M}_ W(T)=\int _{M\cap W}\theta \, d\mathcal{H}^ m$. The mass formula and representation are cited facts; see [Sim18], Chapter 6, Definition 3.1 and Remark 3.2(3), pp. 172–173. Local finiteness and integrability are taken in the ambient open set, not merely on compact subsets of $M$.
