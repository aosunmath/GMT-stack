Proposition 4.6.1 (Distributional derivatives as vector measures)B3DA56F70CE7

Proposition 4.6.1 (Distributional derivatives as vector measures).B3DA56F70CE7 Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be open. For $u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)$, local finiteness of the variation supremum in Definition 4.1.1 is equivalent to representation of its distributional gradient by a locally finite vector-valued Radon measure. There is a Radon measure $\mu $ and a Borel vector field $\sigma $ such that

\[ Du=\sigma \mu ,\qquad |\sigma |=1\quad \mu \text{-almost everywhere},\qquad \mu (W)=\sup _{\substack {X\in C_ c^1(W;\mathbb R^ n)\\ |X|\leq 1}}\int _ Wu\, \operatorname {div}X \]

for every open $W\subset U$. Thus $\mu =|Du|$ is exactly the total variation measure, including on open sets of infinite variation.

Proof

Define $L(X)=-\int u\, \operatorname {div}X$ on smooth compactly supported vector fields. The symmetry of the admissible tests under $X\mapsto -X$ bounds $|L(X)|$ by the local variation times $\| X\| _\infty $. Uniform approximation by smooth fields, with supports in a common compact neighborhood, extends $L$ uniquely to $C_ c(U;\mathbb R^ n)$. Independence of the approximation follows from the same local bound.

Apply Theorem 5.7.4 with the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb R^ n$. It gives $L(X)=\int X\cdot \sigma \, d\mu $, with $|\sigma |=1$ almost everywhere. To identify the open-set supremum, note first that each admissible smooth test has $|L(X)|\leq \mu (W)$. Conversely, for $0\leq g\leq 1$ compactly supported in $W$, the positive variation functional of Lemma 5.7.3 gives

\[ \int g\, d\mu =\sup \{ L(F):F\in C_ c(U;\mathbb R^ n),\ |F|\leq g\} . \]

Every such $F$ has support in $W$ and norm at most one. Extend it by zero and mollify with sufficiently small scale. Its smooth approximants stay supported in $W$, have norm at most one, and converge uniformly. Hence the original smooth supremum is at least $\int g\, d\mu $. Inner regularity and cutoff functions, exhausted over compact subsets of $W$, give the reverse inequality $\mu (W)$.

The coordinate distributional identities characterize $Du$ uniquely, since smooth tests determine Radon measures. Conversely, a locally finite vector measure representing $Du$ bounds the variation on $W\Subset U$ by its total variation there. The already established representation then identifies the minimal positive dominating measure with the stated supremum. This completes the equivalence without a separate vector-valued representation theorem left external. $\square$
