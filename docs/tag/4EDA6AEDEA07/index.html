Remark 1.1.2 (Notation and prerequisites)4EDA6AEDEA07

Remark 1.1.2 (Notation and prerequisites).4EDA6AEDEA07 Unless stated otherwise, $U$ is an open subset of $\mathbb {R}^ n$, $1\leq m\leq n$, and $B_ r(x)$ is the open Euclidean ball. We write $\mathcal{L}^ n$ for Lebesgue measure, $\omega _ m=\mathcal{L}^ m(B_1(0))$, and $\mu \llcorner E$ for the restriction of a measure to a measurable set $E$. The notation $W\Subset U$ means that $\overline W$ is compact and contained in $U$. Continuous compactly supported functions form $C_ c(U)$.

We assume elementary Lebesgue integration, the dominated and monotone convergence theorems, and basic differential forms. These prerequisites have not yet been developed in the stack. All almost-everywhere statements specify their measure. Sets of finite perimeter are considered modulo $\mathcal{L}^ n$-null sets. Our normal to a set of finite perimeter points outward; Simon uses the inward normal in Chapter 3, Section 4. For currents we use the comass convention for mass, as explained in Definition 5.1.3.
