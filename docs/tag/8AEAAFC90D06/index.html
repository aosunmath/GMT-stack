Proposition 6.9.7 (Integral product splitting along a proper spine subspace)8AEAAFC90D06

Proposition 6.9.7 (Integral product splitting along a proper spine subspace).8AEAAFC90D06 Suppose $L\subset S(C)$ and $1\leq d=\dim L<m$. There is a nonzero stationary integral $(m-d)$-cone $C_0$ on $L^\perp $ such that

\[ C=C_0\times |L|. \]

The product records the plane $T_ yC_0\oplus L$ and the multiplicity of $C_0$ at $(y,z)$. The rectifiable coarea input is [Sim18], Chapter 3, (2.8)–(2.9) and Remark 2.10, pp. 93–94; compare the conclusion of [Minter], Lemma 1.5.

Proof

Write $C=V(M,\theta )$. Lemmas 6.9.5 and 6.9.6 give $\mu =\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ d$ and $L\subset T_ xM$ almost everywhere. Thus the tangential Jacobian of the projection $p:M\to L$ is one. Weighted rectifiable coarea, Corollary 3.3.4, expresses $\mu $ as the integral over $z\in L$ of the measures

\[ \nu _ z=\theta (\, \cdot \, ,z)\mathcal H^{m-d}\llcorner \{ y:(y,z)\in M\} . \]

For almost every $z$, this is a locally finite integral rectifiable measure. Local finiteness and integrality follow on a countable compact exhaustion, with $\theta \geq 1$; exceptional sets of zero weighted measure meet almost every fiber in zero weighted measure. Coarea and the product identity give $\int _ B\nu _ z(A)\, dz=\nu (A)\mathcal L^ d(B)$. Apply this to a countable collection of bounded boxes generating the Borel sets of $L^\perp $. It follows that $\nu _ z=\nu $ as measures for almost every $z$.

Choose one such fiber and let $C_0$ be its integral rectifiable lift in $L^\perp $. The area formula on product charts gives a rectifiable product $C_0\times |L|$ with weight $\nu \otimes \mathcal L^ d$ and planes $T_ yC_0\oplus L$. Lemma 6.7.2 therefore identifies this product with $C$; no invariance of individual exceptional fibers was assumed.

For $Y\in C_ c^1(L^\perp ;L^\perp )$ choose $\chi \in C_ c^1(L)$ with $\int \chi =1$ and test with $X(y,z)=\chi (z)Y(y)$. Divergence in the $L$ directions is zero, and product integration gives $0=\delta C(X)=\delta C_0(Y)$. Finally homogeneity of $\mu $ gives $\nu (sA)=s^{m-d}\nu (A)$ by testing rectangles and scaling their $L$ factor. The dilated lift of $C_0$ has the same weight as $C_0$, so Lemma 6.7.2 proves conical invariance. Nonzeroness follows from the product identity. $\square$
