Theorem 6.4.5 (A density gap above one)5DBE38E6CA27

Theorem 6.4.5 (A density gap above one).5DBE38E6CA27 There is $\delta =\delta (n,k)>0$ such that every support point $a$ of a stationary integral $n$-varifold in $\mathbb R^{n+k}$ with $\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)<1+\delta $ is regular with multiplicity one. Equivalently, every singular point has density at least $1+\delta $. This is [Minter], Corollary 3.2, pp. 44–45. Its Allard regularity input remains external.

Proof

Let $\varepsilon =\varepsilon (n,k,2n)$ be the mass tolerance in Theorem 6.4.3, and take $\delta =\varepsilon /2$. At a point with density less than $1+\delta $, the mass ratio is less than $1+\varepsilon $ at all sufficiently small radii. Stationarity gives $H=0$, so the curvature hypothesis holds with $p=2n>n$. Allard regularity gives a multiplicity-one $C^{1,1/2}$ graph near the point. The stationary graph equation and elliptic bootstrapping make it smooth, as in Corollary 6.4.4. Thus the gap is a local consequence of the already stated Allard theorem. $\square$
