Lemma 4.9.3 (Radial phase volumes are absolutely continuous)8CD253C511F3

Lemma 4.9.3 (Radial phase volumes are absolutely continuous).8CD253C511F3 If $B_ R(x)\Subset U$, set $V(r)=|E\cap B_ r(x)|$ and $W(r)=|B_ r(x)\setminus E|$ for $0\leq r<R$. These functions are absolutely continuous, and for almost every $r$,

\[ V'(r)=\int _{\partial B_ r(x)}\chi _ E\, d\mathcal H^{n-1},\qquad W'(r)=n\omega _ n r^{n-1}-V'(r), \qquad 0\leq V'(r),W'(r)\leq n\omega _ n r^{n-1}. \]

Representatives of $E$ differing on a volume-null set give the same identities for almost every radius. This is the radial differentiation used in [Sim18], equations (4)–(6).

Proof

Translate $x$ to zero and use polar coordinates and Tonelli:

\[ V(r)=\int _0^ r s^{n-1}\int _{S^{n-1}}\chi _ E(s\theta )\, d\mathcal H^{n-1}(\theta )\, ds. \]

The integrand is measurable and bounded by $n\omega _ n s^{n-1}$. The fundamental theorem for Lebesgue integrals proves absolute continuity and the derivative formula. The sphere integral equals the displayed derivative after dilation. Subtract $V$ from $\omega _ n r^ n$ to obtain the claims for $W$. Fubini makes a volume-null modification invisible on almost every sphere. $\square$
