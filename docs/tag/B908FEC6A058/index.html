Lemma 3.4.3 (Lipschitz functions on lines are absolutely continuous)B908FEC6A058

Lemma 3.4.3 (Lipschitz functions on lines are absolutely continuous).B908FEC6A058 An $L$-Lipschitz real function on an interval is absolutely continuous on every compact subinterval, is differentiable almost everywhere, and its derivative has absolute value at most $L$. A locally Lipschitz real function on an open subset of $\mathbb R^ m$ therefore has directional derivatives in any one fixed direction at almost every point locally.

Proof

For finitely many disjoint subintervals $(a_ j,b_ j)$, the sum of the absolute increments is at most $L\sum _ j(b_ j-a_ j)$. This is the definition of absolute continuity. The one-variable fundamental theorem gives almost-everywhere differentiability, and each difference quotient is bounded by $L$. For the higher-dimensional assertion, restrict to a box lying in a neighborhood with a fixed Lipschitz constant, rotate the box to the chosen direction, and apply the one-dimensional assertion on its parallel lines. Fubini gives a null exceptional set in the box. Countably many such local boxes cover the domain. The nondifferentiability set is measurable since the limsup and liminf of difference quotients may be taken along rational increments. This is the line argument in [Sim18], proof of Theorem 1.4, p. 45. $\square$
