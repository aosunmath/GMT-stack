Proposition 2.3.4 (The open-set mass associated with a positive functional)D6FBFD2B5B8A

Proposition 2.3.4 (The open-set mass associated with a positive functional).D6FBFD2B5B8A For open $O\subset U$, put

\[ m(O)=\sup \{ L(f):f\in C_ c(O),\ 0\leq f\leq 1\} . \]

Then $m$ is monotone, $m(\varnothing )=0$, is countably subadditive on open sets, and is additive on disjoint open sets, allowing infinite values. The function

\[ \mu ^*(A)=\inf \{ m(O):A\subset O\subset U,\ O\text{ open}\} \]

for arbitrary $A\subset U$ is an outer measure, equals $m$ on open sets, is finite on compact sets, and satisfies

\[ \mu ^*(A\cup B)=\mu ^*(A)+\mu ^*(B) \quad \text{if }\operatorname {dist}(A,B)>0. \]
Proof

Monotonicity and the empty-set assertion are immediate. If $O\subset \bigcup _ jO_ j$ and $f$ is admissible for $m(O)$, compactness of its support and Lemma 2.3.2 give a finite subordinate partition, with $f=\sum _ j\eta _ jf$. Therefore $L(f)\leq \sum _ jm(O_ j)$; take the supremum. For disjoint opens, finitely many admissible tests have sum at most one, so taking their separate suprema gives $m(\bigcup _ jO_ j)\geq \sum _{j=1}^ Nm(O_ j)$ for every finite $N$. This proves countable additivity on disjoint opens.

The infimum definition is consistent on opens by monotonicity. To prove countable subadditivity of $\mu ^*$, the only nontrivial case has $\sum _ j\mu ^*(A_ j)<\infty $. Choose $O_ j\supset A_ j$ with $m(O_ j)\leq \mu ^*(A_ j)+\varepsilon 2^{-j}$, use open-set subadditivity, and let $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. For compact $K$, choose an open neighborhood $W$ with compact closure in $U$ and a cutoff $\eta \geq 1$ on $\overline W$. Every test for $m(W)$ is at most $\eta $, so $\mu ^*(K)\leq m(W)\leq L(\eta )<\infty $. Finally, if $A,B$ have positive distance, choose disjoint open neighborhoods $V_ A,V_ B$ of them. For every open $O\supset A\cup B$,

\[ m(O)\geq m(O\cap V_ A)+m(O\cap V_ B)\geq \mu ^*(A)+\mu ^*(B). \]

Taking the infimum and using subadditivity proves the metric separation identity. This follows the construction in [Sim18], proof of Theorem 5.12. $\square$
