Corollary 7.13.8 (An integrable entire subsolution vanishes)D0D8708926B9

Corollary 7.13.8 (An integrable entire subsolution vanishes).D0D8708926B9 Let $n\geq 2$ and let $A$ be measurable and symmetric on all of $\mathbb R^ n$, with fixed global bounds $\lambda I\leq A\leq \Lambda I$. If $u\geq 0$ is a weak subsolution in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n)$ and $\int _{\mathbb R^ n}u^ p<\infty $ for some $p>0$, then $u=0$ almost everywhere. A signed entire weak solution with finite $\int |u|^ p$ also vanishes.

Proof

Proposition 7.13.6, on balls of arbitrarily large radius, bounds $\operatorname *{ess\, sup}_{B_ R}u$ by $C R^{-n/p}(\int _{\mathbb R^ n}u^ p)^{1/p}$, which tends to zero. Every fixed ball lies in these expanding balls, so $u$ vanishes on every ball. For a signed solution, its positive and negative parts are subsolutions by Lemma 7.13.2, and each has finite $p$ integral. Apply the first assertion to both. The global ellipticity bounds and the integrability hypothesis are essential parts of this conclusion. $\square$
