Proposition 2.3.6 (The constructed measure is Radon)DCE16B3F3D66

Proposition 2.3.6 (The constructed measure is Radon).DCE16B3F3D66 The restriction of $\mu ^*$ from Proposition 2.3.4 to Borel sets is a Radon measure $\mu $. In particular, for every open $O\subset U$,

\[ \mu (O)=\sup _{K\subset O,\ K\text{ compact}}\mu (K)=m(O). \]
Proof

Lemma 2.3.5 gives a Borel measure, and Proposition 2.3.4 gives local finiteness and outer regularity. If $f\in C_ c(O)$, $0\leq f\leq 1$, with compact support $K$, then $L(f)\leq m(V)$ for every open $V\supset K$, so $L(f)\leq \mu (K)$. Taking the supremum in $f$ proves inner regularity on open sets; the opposite inequality is monotonicity.

For a Borel set $A$ of finite measure, choose open $O\supset A$ with $\mu (O\setminus A)<\varepsilon $, compact $K\subset O$ with $\mu (O\setminus K)<\varepsilon $, and open $V\supset O\setminus A$ with $\mu (V)<2\varepsilon $. Then $K\setminus V$ is compact and contained in $A$, and $\mu (A\setminus (K\setminus V))<3\varepsilon $. Thus $A$ is inner regular. For arbitrary Borel $A$, exhaust $U$ by compact sets $K_ j$. The sets $A\cap K_ j$ have finite measure and increase to $A$, so their inner regularity and continuity from below give the general assertion. Compare [Sim18], Lemmas 5.7–5.9. $\square$
