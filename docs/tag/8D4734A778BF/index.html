Proposition 11.3.14 (The CMC graph equation and oriented contact)8D4734A778BF

Proposition 11.3.14 (The CMC graph equation and oriented contact).8D4734A778BF For a graph $z=u(x)$ with upward normal, the equation $H=c$ is

\[ \operatorname {div}\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^2}}\right)=-c. \]

If two $C^2$ solutions with the same constant $c$ on a connected domain are ordered and have interior contact, they agree throughout the domain. In particular two smooth CMC hypersurfaces with the same mean-curvature constant and agreeing normals at a one-sided interior contact point agree locally. For the last assertion the ambient manifold may be Riemannian. Compare [ZZCMC], Lemma 2.7(i), p. 7.

Proof

The graph formula for $H$ follows from the normal convention in Proposition 14.3.13. Subtracting the two equations cancels $-c$, so the difference satisfies the divergence-form linear equation in Proposition 7.2.5, with locally uniformly elliptic coefficients. Harnack applied to the nonnegative difference gives the same open-and-closed argument as for minimal graphs. In Riemannian coordinates, the difference equation has smooth bounded lower-order terms on a smaller common graph chart; the local Harnack principle gives equality near the contact point. Agreement of the normals ensures the same signed equation. Oppositely oriented touching sheets are not covered by this conclusion. $\square$
