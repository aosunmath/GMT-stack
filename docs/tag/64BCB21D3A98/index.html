Theorem 14.8.8 (Every finite-Gaussian-area proper shrinker is unstable at fixed center)64BCB21D3A98

Theorem 14.8.8 (Every finite-Gaussian-area proper shrinker is unstable at fixed center).64BCB21D3A98 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be a nonempty smooth complete properly immersed two-sided self-shrinker without boundary and with $F(\Sigma )<\infty $. There is $f\in C_ c^\infty (\Sigma )$ with $Q(f)<0$. Thus no such shrinker is stable for Gaussian area with its center and scale fixed. This explains the distinction preceding [CM12], Section 4.3.

Proof

Choose smooth radial cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ R\leq 1$ equal to one on $B_ R$, zero outside $B_{2R}$, with $|\nabla _\Sigma \eta _ R|\leq C/R$. They are smooth at zero by being constant there, and properness makes their support compact on the parameter manifold. Dropping the nonpositive curvature term gives

\[ Q(\eta _ R)\leq \frac{C^2}{R^2}[1]-\tfrac 12[\eta _ R^2]. \]

Dominated convergence yields $[\eta _ R^2]\to [1]>0$. Hence the right side is negative for large $R$. This does not require a global integral bound for $|A|^2$: its contribution is finite on each compact support and only improves the inequality. The conclusion concerns fixed center and scale, unlike Definition 14.9.3. $\square$
