Example 8.7.6 (Stationary integral varifolds can have crossings)ADB5C190FC3B

Example 8.7.6 (Stationary integral varifolds can have crossings).ADB5C190FC3B On the unit flat $(n+1)$-torus, $n\geq 1$, the varifold sum of the coordinate tori $\{ x_1=0\} $ and $\{ x_2=0\} $ is integral and stationary. Its support is not a smooth embedded hypersurface near their intersection.

Proof

Both tori are closed and totally geodesic, hence stationary, and first variation is linear in the varifold. Their intersection has dimension $n-1$ and zero $n$-dimensional measure, so adding them gives an integral varifold with multiplicity one away from the intersection. At an intersection point its tangent cone is the sum of two distinct transverse $n$-planes. The support therefore cannot be locally a single smooth hypersurface, nor a disjoint collection of embedded hypersurfaces. Integrality and stationarity alone cannot replace the almost-minimizing and replacement arguments in Remark 8.2.4. $\square$
