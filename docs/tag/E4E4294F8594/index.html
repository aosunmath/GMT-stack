Proposition 7.6.2 (Linearization of scalar mean curvature)E4E4294F8594

Proposition 7.6.2 (Linearization of scalar mean curvature).E4E4294F8594 Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, two-sided, and minimal. For a normal variation with velocity $f\nu $, differentiating scalar mean curvature at $t=0$ gives

\[ \left.\partial _ t H_{\Sigma _ t}\right|_{t=0}=-Jf. \]

The sign agrees with $Q(f,f)=-\int fJf$ and with the first variation $\delta A(f\nu )=\int Hf$.

Proof

Take a two-parameter normal graph variation with initial velocities $f\nu $ and $\phi \nu $, both compactly supported. Differentiate its first-variation identity in the other parameter. Terms differentiating the area form or normal component are multiplied by $H=0$, leaving $\int \phi \, \partial _ tH$. The mixed second derivative is the polarization of the second variation, $Q(f,\phi )=-\int \phi Jf$. Since $\phi $ is arbitrary, the identity follows pointwise. For a general smooth $f$, insert cutoffs near the point being tested. Compare [ChMin], the proof of Proposition 33.3, pp. 80–81. $\square$
