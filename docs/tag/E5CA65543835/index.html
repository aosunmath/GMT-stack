Lemma 3.13.2 (Measurability of a finite-volume Lipschitz image)E5CA65543835

Lemma 3.13.2 (Measurability of a finite-volume Lipschitz image).E5CA65543835 Let $f:\mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ n$ be Lipschitz, and let $E\subset \mathbb R^ m$ be Lebesgue measurable with finite measure. Then $f(E)$ is $\mathcal H^ m$-measurable. If $|E|=0$, then $\mathcal H^ m(f(E))=0$.

Proof

Choose compact sets $K_ j\subset E$ with $|E\setminus \bigcup _ jK_ j|=0$ by inner regularity. Images of the compact sets are compact, so their union is Borel. Proposition 2.2.2 gives

\[ \mathcal H^ m(f(Z))\leq (\operatorname {Lip}f)^ m\mathcal H^ m(Z)=0 \quad \text{when }|Z|=0. \]

Thus $f(E)$ differs from the Borel union $\bigcup _ jf(K_ j)$ by a Hausdorff-null set and is measurable for the complete Hausdorff measure. This also proves the null assertion. The argument extends to arbitrary measurable $E$ by a countable bounded exhaustion. It requires neither injectivity nor any assertion that every continuous image of a Borel set is Borel. $\square$
