Corollary 7.5.7 (Hölder continuity from oscillation decay)482FCE0EEBCC

Corollary 7.5.7 (Hölder continuity from oscillation decay).482FCE0EEBCC There are $\alpha =\alpha (n,\Lambda /\lambda )\in (0,1)$ and $C=C(n,\Lambda /\lambda )$ such that every weak solution on $B_{2R}(a)$ has a $C^{0,\alpha }$ representative on $B_ R(a)$ and

\[ \| v\| _{C^0(B_ R(a))}+R^\alpha [v]_{\alpha ;B_ R(a)} \leq C\left(R^{-n}\int _{B_{2R}(a)}|v|^2\right)^{1/2}. \]
Proof

Local boundedness first bounds $v$ on $B_{3R/2}(a)$. On any ball $B_{2s}$ compactly contained there, let $M$ and $m$ be its essential supremum and infimum. Apply Harnack to $M-v$ and $v-m$. Adding the resulting inequalities gives

\[ \operatorname *{ess\, osc}_{B_ s}v\leq \frac{C_ H-1}{C_ H+1}\operatorname *{ess\, osc}_{B_{2s}}v, \]

where we may take $C_ H>1$. Iteration on concentric dyadic balls gives a bound $C(s/R)^\alpha $ for the oscillation, choosing a smaller exponent if necessary to have $\alpha <1$. The essential oscillations therefore define a unique continuous representative. Balls centered in $B_ R(a)$ of radius at most $R/4$, followed by the bound for pairs farther apart, give the stated seminorm estimate. This is the oscillation mechanism of De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory; see [SimonPDE], Chapters 17–18. $\square$
