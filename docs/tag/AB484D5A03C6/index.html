Theorem 10.6.3 (The sharp leading logarithmic neck cost)AB484D5A03C6

Theorem 10.6.3 (The sharp leading logarithmic neck cost).AB484D5A03C6 Fix $R>0$ and let $h\downarrow 0$. Choose the smaller waist solution $a(h)$ of Proposition 10.6.1. If $L=\log (R/h)$ and $T=h/a(h)$, then

\[ \frac{T}{L}\longrightarrow 1,\qquad T=L+\log L+\log 2+o(1),\qquad \frac{\bigl (A(h)-2\pi R^2\bigr )L}{h^2}\longrightarrow 2\pi . \]

Consequently, for every $\varepsilon >0$ and all sufficiently small $h>0$,

\[ A(h)\leq 2\pi R^2+\frac{2\pi (1+\varepsilon )h^2}{\log (R/h)}. \]

This is the dimensionless version of the leading estimate in [KMN], Proposition 2.1 and Remark 2.2; it concerns the explicit rotational neck.

Proof

The larger root $T$ tends to infinity: on the strictly decreasing branch of $t/\cosh t$, its value $h/R$ tends to zero, and no bounded sequence on that branch can have this limit. The boundary equation yields

\[ L=\log \cosh T-\log T =T-\log 2+\log (1+e^{-2T})-\log T. \]

Divide by $T$ to obtain $L/T\to 1$. Rearrangement and $\log T-\log L\to 0$ then give the second expansion. By Proposition 10.6.2,

\[ \frac{(A-2\pi R^2)L}{h^2} =2\pi \frac{L}{T}\left(1-\frac{1+e^{-2T}}{2T}\right)\longrightarrow 2\pi . \]

The asserted bound is the definition of this limit, with $L>0$ once $h<R$. The logarithm uses a ratio of lengths, so it is invariant under a common change of length units. $\square$
