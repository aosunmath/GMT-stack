Lemma 14.24.4 (A maximum principle with a time-integrable drift bound)2F5B0A379ED2

Lemma 14.24.4 (A maximum principle with a time-integrable drift bound).2F5B0A379ED2 Suppose $0\leq a(x,t)\leq A_0I$ and $|b(x,t)|\leq B(t)$ on $\mathbb R^ n\times (0,T]$, where $B$ is continuous, nonnegative and integrable on $(0,T]$. Let $f$ be continuous on $\mathbb R^ n\times [0,T]$, smooth enough for the following inequality at positive time, and satisfy

\[ f_ t-a^{ij}f_{ij}-b^ if_ i\leq 0,\quad f(x,0)\leq M, \quad f(x,t)\leq C(1+|x|^2)^ m \]

uniformly on the slab for some $C,m\geq 0$. Then $f\leq M$. Continuity at time zero is local; a uniform spatial rate of initial convergence is not assumed.

Proof

Repeat the barrier proof of Lemma 14.21.2 with $q>\max \{ m,1\} $ and

\[ E(x,t)=(1+|x|^2)^ q \exp \left((1+4nA_0q^2)t+q\int _0^ t B(s)\, ds\right). \]

The spatial derivative bounds in that lemma give $E_ t-a^{ij}E_{ij}-b^ iE_ i\geq E>0$ for $t>0$. Integrability of $B$ makes $E$ finite and continuous at time zero. For fixed $\eta >0$, the uniform polynomial upper bound confines every possible positive maximum of $f-M-\eta E$ to a fixed compact base ball, uniformly in time. Initial values there are strictly negative. Continuity and the strict parabolic inequality exclude a positive maximum at later time exactly as before. Let $\eta \downarrow 0$ at each point. The argument allows $B(t)=Ct^{-1/2}$, but not a nonintegrable coefficient merely by ignoring its singularity. $\square$
