Proposition 14.16.3 (What a maximum of the ratio implies)1CC5369E3654

Proposition 14.16.3 (What a maximum of the ratio implies).1CC5369E3654 If $R$ has a local maximum at a point where $H\ne 0$, there is a connected neighborhood on which $R$ is constant and $B=A/H$ is parallel. If $R$ attains its global maximum on a connected component of $\{ H\ne 0\} $, the same conclusion holds throughout that component.

Proof

Choose a relatively compact connected coordinate neighborhood where $H$ stays away from zero and the local maximum is a maximum on the whole neighborhood. The operator in Proposition 14.16.2 is uniformly elliptic there and has bounded smooth drift and zero zeroth-order term. The scalar strong maximum principle gives constancy of $R$ on this neighborhood. Its equation then gives $\nabla B=0$.

For a global maximum on a connected component, the set where that maximum is attained is relatively closed by continuity and is relatively open by the same argument. It is nonempty, hence is the whole component. No uniform ellipticity constants at infinity are needed, since the argument is local at each maximum point. The scalar strong maximum principle is a background analytic input. A local maximum alone is not silently promoted to a global conclusion by that principle. $\square$
