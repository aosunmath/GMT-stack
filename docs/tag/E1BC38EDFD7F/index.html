Lemma 5.14.4 (Full-rank image tangents agree with the image of the differential)E1BC38EDFD7F

Lemma 5.14.4 (Full-rank image tangents agree with the image of the differential).E1BC38EDFD7F Let $M\subset \mathbb R^ N$ be measurable and countably $m$-rectifiable with finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, $m\geq 1$, and let $f:\mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R^ q$ be Lipschitz, with $q\geq m$. Its image is countably $m$-rectifiable and has finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure. At almost every $x\in M$ with $J_ m(d^ Mf_ x)>0$,

\[ T_{f(x)}f(M)=d^ Mf_ x(T_ xM). \]

Null sets in the image are understood using measurable representatives. This is the tangent assertion preceding [Sim18], equation (3.6), p. 174.

Proof

Compose the countably many Lipschitz parametrizations of $M$ with $f$. Their images cover $f(M)$ except for the image of a Hausdorff-null set, which is null by the Lipschitz measure bound. The same bound gives finite image measure and measurability follows from compact cores.

Work on a regular $C^1$ source chart $F$ and put $u=f\circ F$. By Corollary 3.5.6, countably many $C^1$ maps agree with $u$ and its derivative on measurable sets covering almost every parameter point. On the sets where the derivative has rank $m$, subdivide further among neighborhoods where the corresponding $C^1$ map is an injective immersion. The inverse function theorem applied to a nonzero coordinate minor supplies these neighborhoods; their images are embedded $C^1$ manifolds. The chain rule in Theorem 3.9.5 identifies their tangent images with $d^ Mf(T_ xM)$ at the retained points.

The measurable image piece lies in both this smooth image manifold and $f(M)$. Corollary 3.6.8 makes their tangents agree outside an image-null set. Enlarge the exceptional image set to a Borel Hausdorff-null set before taking its preimage. Its preimage on the positive-Jacobian part is domain-null: apply Theorem 3.10.2 with its indicator to get integral of $J_ m(d^ Mf)$ zero there, and use its strict positivity. Thus the tangent equality holds at almost every full-rank domain point. Countably many pieces remove only one null set. The rank-deficient domain part has null image by the same area formula and the indicator argument; it creates no additional almost-everywhere image tangent. This justifies both the domain and image exceptional-set conventions. $\square$
