Example 11.6.7 (An equator separates two notions of minimal stability)8B1EF7479D38

Example 11.6.7 (An equator separates two notions of minimal stability).8B1EF7479D38 An equator $S^ n\subset S^{n+1}$ is minimal, has $A=0$, and is stable for the enclosed-volume constraint but unstable for unrestricted area variations. Its unrestricted index is one. This example is emphasized in [BdCE88], Remark 3.5.

Proof

At geodesic radius $\pi /2$ the space-form formula gives $H=0$ and $q=n$ because the ambient normal Ricci curvature is $n$. Thus $Q(f,f)=\int (|\nabla f|^2-nf^2)$. The sharp spherical Poincare inequality makes this nonnegative for zero-mean $f$, while $Q(1,1)<0$. The index statement follows as above. In particular the minimal equation $H=0$ does not remove the distinction between testing all normal speeds and testing only those that preserve enclosed volume. $\square$
