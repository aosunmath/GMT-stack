Proposition 16.12.2 (Linear ball bounds and finite positive length)51869A534A53

Proposition 16.12.2 (Linear ball bounds and finite positive length).51869A534A53 For $x\in K$ and $0<r\le \sqrt2$,

\[ \frac{r}{8\sqrt2}\le \mu (B_ r(x))\le 100r. \]

The upper bound also holds for balls centered outside $K$. Consequently $0<\mathcal H^1(K)<\infty $ and $\dim _{\rm H}K=1$; more precisely $1/100\le \mathcal H^1(K)\le \sqrt2$.

Proof

For $r<1$, choose $k$ with $\ell =4^{-k}\le r<4\ell $. The lower-left corners of level-$k$ squares lie on the grid $\ell \mathbb Z^2$. A square meeting a ball of radius $r$ has each lower-left coordinate in an interval of length at most $2r+\ell <9\ell $. There are at most ten grid choices per coordinate and hence at most one hundred such squares. Each has $\mu $ mass $\ell $, so $\mu (B_ r)\le 100\ell \le 100r$. For $r\ge 1$ the bound follows from total mass one.

For the lower bound choose the least $k$ with $\sqrt2\, 4^{-k}\le r/2$. The level-$k$ cylinder containing $x$ lies entirely in $B_ r(x)$, and minimality gives $4^{-k}>r/(8\sqrt2)$. Thus its mass proves the lower estimate. The same cylinder masses show that every point has zero mass.

The level-$k$ squares cover $K$ with total diameter $4^ k\sqrt2\, 4^{-k}=\sqrt2$, proving the Hausdorff upper bound. For any sufficiently small countable cover $K\subset \bigcup U_ i$, discard sets missing $K$ and choose $x_ i\in U_ i\cap K$. Then $U_ i\cap K\subset \overline B_{\operatorname {diam}U_ i}(x_ i)$. The ball estimate, obtained for closed balls by enlarging the radius and taking a limit, gives $1\le 100\sum _ i\operatorname {diam}U_ i$. Infimizing and shrinking the cover diameters gives the lower bound. A set of finite positive $\mathcal H^1$ measure has Hausdorff dimension one by the comparison of diameter powers in the Hausdorff definition. $\square$
