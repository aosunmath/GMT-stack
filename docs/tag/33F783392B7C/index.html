Proposition 4.7.5 (Zero extension and endpoint traces)33F783392B7C

Proposition 4.7.5 (Zero extension and endpoint traces).33F783392B7C Let $u\in BV(a,b)$ and extend it by zero to $\widetilde u\in L^1(\mathbb R)$. Then

\[ D\widetilde u=(Du)\lfloor (a,b)+u(a+)\delta _ a-u(b-)\delta _ b, \]

where the first term means the derivative measure extended by zero. Consequently

\[ |D\widetilde u|(\mathbb R)=|Du|((a,b))+|u(a+)|+|u(b-)|. \]
Proof

Use the representation $u(x)=c+\mu ((a,x])$ almost everywhere. For $\phi \in C_ c^1(\mathbb R)$, Fubini as in Lemma 4.7.1, now retaining the endpoint term, gives

\[ -\int _ a^ bu\phi ' =\int _{(a,b)}\phi \, d\mu +c\phi (a)-(c+\mu ((a,b)))\phi (b). \]

The two coefficients are the endpoint traces. The three measures live on pairwise disjoint Borel sets, so their total variations add. This is why zero extension is not generally variation preserving. $\square$
