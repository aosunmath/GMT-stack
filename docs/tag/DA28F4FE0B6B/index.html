Theorem 4.3.2 (Half-space blow-up at a reduced-boundary point)DA28F4FE0B6B

Theorem 4.3.2 (Half-space blow-up at a reduced-boundary point).DA28F4FE0B6B Let $E$ have locally finite perimeter in $U$, and let $x\in \partial ^*E\cap U$. With outward normal $\nu _ E(x)$, set $E_{x,r}=(E-x)/r$. As $r\downarrow 0$,

\[ \chi _{E_{x,r}}\longrightarrow \chi _{\{ y:y\cdot \nu _ E(x)<0\} } \quad \text{in }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n), \]

and the perimeter measures converge locally to $\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner \nu _ E(x)^\perp $. In particular,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{|E\cap B_ r(x)|}{\omega _ nr^ n}=\frac12, \qquad \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{P(E;B_ r(x))}{\omega _{n-1}r^{n-1}}=1. \]

This is the blow-up conclusion in the proof of [Sim18], Chapter 3, Theorem 4.4, especially equations (9)–(13), pp. 98–100, with the project's outward-normal convention. The local proof below uses the separately developed polar, compactness, and two-phase estimates. The convergence holds as $r\downarrow 0$, not merely for one selected subsequence.

Proof

For $n\geq 2$, Theorem 4.11.6 gives the characteristic-function convergence at every vanishing scale, with the outward-normal sign stated here. Theorem 4.12.3 gives the perimeter-measure limit: its proof uses polar alignment in addition to weak convergence of vector derivatives to rule out cancellation. Corollary 4.12.4 gives both normalized densities. The intermediate two-phase bound ensures the limiting half-space passes through the origin rather than being empty, full, or translated. None of these steps invokes the full De Giorgi structure theorem; countable rectifiability and the global measure representation are separate conclusions.

For $n=1$, choose a bounded interval about $x$ with compact closure in $U$. Theorem 4.7.4 identifies $E$ there, up to null sets, with a finite union of intervals. A reduced-boundary point must be one of their genuine endpoints; in a sufficiently small neighborhood it is the only endpoint. There the phase is exactly the half-line on the negative side of the outward normal and its perimeter is the unit atom at $x$. After dilation these identities hold on every fixed bounded interval for all sufficiently small scales. The two densities are $1/2$ and $1$, with $\omega _1=2$ and $\omega _0=1$. Thus this case does not require the higher-dimensional Sobolev inequality. $\square$
