Proposition 14.11.2 (Gaussian area decreases along parabolic parameter paths)6CD90D8527E9

Proposition 14.11.2 (Gaussian area decreases along parabolic parameter paths).6CD90D8527E9 Let $\Sigma $ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 14.11.1. Fix $y\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $a\in \mathbb R$, set $t_ s=1+as^2$, and put $g(s)=F_{sy,t_ s}(\Sigma )$ whenever $t_ s>0$. Then for $s\geq 0$,

\[ g'(s)=-\frac{s}{2t_ s^2} \bigl [|(asx+y)^\perp |^2\bigr ]_{sy,t_ s}\leq 0. \]
Proof

Write $z=x-sy$ and suppress the weighted subscript. The preceding identities imply

\[ \left[\frac{\langle z^ T,y^ T\rangle }{t_ s}\right] =-[\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle +s|y^\perp |^2], \]
\[ \left[\frac{|z^ T|^2}{t_ s}\right] =[2n-|z^\perp |^2-s\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle ]. \]

For the second equation substitute $x=z+sy$ into the second shifted identity and use the first. Differentiation under the Gaussian integral is justified by polynomial growth on any compact interval with $t_ s>0$. It gives

\[ g' =\frac{t_ s'}{4t_ s} \left[\frac{|z^ T|^2}{t_ s}+\frac{|z^\perp |^2}{t_ s}-2n\right] +\frac12\left[\frac{\langle z^ T,y^ T\rangle }{t_ s} +\frac{\langle z^\perp ,y^\perp \rangle }{t_ s}\right]. \]

Insert the two identities and use $t_ s'=2as$ and $t_ s^{-1}-1=-as^2/t_ s$. Completing the square yields $g'=-s[|as z^\perp +t_ s y^\perp |^2]/(2t_ s^2)$. Since $as z+t_ s y=asx+y$, this is the stated formula. It is [CM12], (7.27), with the square simplified. $\square$
