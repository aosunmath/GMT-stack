Proposition 5.14.2 (Weak continuity of minors under a uniform Lipschitz bound)3C7149CCAEEE

Proposition 5.14.2 (Weak continuity of minors under a uniform Lipschitz bound).3C7149CCAEEE Let $u_ j,u:V\subset \mathbb R^ m\to \mathbb R^ q$ be locally Lipschitz. Suppose $u_ j\to u$ locally uniformly and their Lipschitz constants are uniformly bounded on each relatively compact ball. Every minor of $Du_ j$ of a fixed order $1\leq r\leq \min \{ m,q\} $ converges weakly against compactly supported $L^1$ coefficients to the corresponding minor of $Du$. Equivalently the coefficient forms

\[ du_ j^{i_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge du_ j^{i_ r} \ \rightharpoonup ^*\ du^{i_1}\wedge \cdots \wedge du^{i_ r} \quad \text{in }L^\infty _{\mathrm{loc}}. \]

Repeated target indices simply give zero forms.

Proof

For order one, distributional integration by parts and local uniform convergence give convergence against smooth tests. The uniform derivative bound extends it to every $L^1$ test by approximation with smooth functions on a common compact neighborhood.

Induct on $r$. By Lemma 5.14.1, test the order-$r$ form by integrating by parts onto the complementary test form, leaving $u_ j^{i_1}du_ j^{i_2}\wedge \cdots \wedge du_ j^{i_ r}$. The difference made by replacing the undifferentiated factor $u_ j^{i_1}$ with $u^{i_1}$ tends to zero by uniform convergence and the bounded lower-order minors. With that factor fixed, induction gives convergence, since its product with a smooth test coefficient is an $L^1$ coefficient. Apply the same distributional identity to the limit. This proves distributional convergence of every order-$r$ coefficient. All these coefficients have a common local bound, from the uniform derivative bound and the determinant expansion. Smooth approximation of an arbitrary $L^1$ coefficient again gives weak-star convergence. A finite partition of unity covers the support of a test meeting several balls. This argument proves convergence of minors, not strong or pointwise convergence of the derivative matrices. $\square$
