Lemma 3.14.1 (Measurability of the measure of a compact fiber)A4D9D83AD84A

Lemma 3.14.1 (Measurability of the measure of a compact fiber).A4D9D83AD84A Let $K\subset \mathbb R^ N$ be compact, let $f:K\to \mathbb R^ k$ be continuous, and let $d$ be an integer with $1\leq d\leq N$. Then

\[ y\longmapsto \mathcal H^ d(K\cap f^{-1}(y)) \]

is a Borel function with values in $[0,\infty ]$.

Proof

For each positive integer $j$, define $c_ j(y)$ as the infimum of $\sum _ i\omega _ d(\operatorname {diam}O_ i/2)^ d$ over finite covers of the compact fiber by open sets $O_ i$ of diameter less than $1/j$. Restrict to the countable family of finite unions of balls with rational centers and radii. There are countably many finite cover candidates. A candidate covers the fiber precisely when $y\notin f(K\setminus \bigcup _ iO_ i)$. The set on the right being excluded is compact, so the set of levels where the candidate is a cover is open. Consequently $\{ y:c_ j(y)<a\} $ is open for every $a$, and $c_ j$ is Borel.

The family restrictions do not change the limit: $c_ j(y)\geq \mathcal H^ d_{1/j}(K\cap f^{-1}(y))$. Conversely, start with a diameter cover of the fiber by sets of diameter less than $1/(2j)$, with total cost within $\varepsilon $ of the corresponding Hausdorff content. Enlarge each set slightly to an open finite union of rational balls, increasing its cost by at most $\varepsilon 2^{-i}$ and keeping its diameter below $1/j$. Such an enlargement exists by a finite fine grid cover of each bounded set. Compactness of the fiber selects a finite subcover from these open sets. Thus $c_ j(y)\leq \mathcal H^ d_{1/(2j)}(K\cap f^{-1}(y))+2\varepsilon $ whenever the latter content is finite. Infinite content causes no upper-bound restriction. Sending $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ and then $j\to \infty $ proves that the increasing functions $c_ j$ converge to the Hausdorff measure of the fiber. Their pointwise limit is Borel. Empty fibers have cost zero throughout. $\square$
