Theorem 2.6.4 (Absolute oscillation vanishes at almost every Radon-measure point)96707809DDCF

Theorem 2.6.4 (Absolute oscillation vanishes at almost every Radon-measure point).96707809DDCF If $\lambda $ is a positive Radon measure on $\mathbb R^ n$ and $g\in L^1(\lambda )$, then for $\lambda $-almost every $x$,

\[ \lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda (\overline{B_ r(x)})} \int _{\overline{B_ r(x)}}|g(y)-g(x)|\, d\lambda (y)=0. \]

This is an absolute-oscillation form of the differentiation conclusion in [Sim18], Corollary 4.10. Its covering input is the cited Besicovitch theorem above.

Proof

Almost every point lies in the support of $\lambda $ and has a finite value of $g$. At these points all the denominators are positive. Let $Dg(x)$ be the limsup on the left. It is measurable: the same joint-kernel argument and rational-radius reduction as in Proposition 2.6.3 apply to the locally integrable function $|g(y)-g(x)|$ for fixed $x$.

For $\phi \in C_ c(\mathbb R^ n)$, the triangle inequality and continuity at $x$ give

\[ Dg(x)\leq M_\lambda (g-\phi )(x)+|g(x)-\phi (x)|. \]

The average oscillation of $\phi $ tends to zero because it is bounded by its supremum oscillation on the shrinking ball, regardless of the measure of that ball. For $\varepsilon >0$, the maximal estimate and Chebyshev's inequality therefore give

\[ \lambda (\{ Dg>2\varepsilon \} ) \leq \frac{N_ n+1}{\varepsilon }\| g-\phi \| _{L^1(\lambda )}. \]

Theorem 2.3.8 supplies continuous compactly supported approximants with arbitrarily small right side. Hence this exceptional set has measure zero. Taking a countable sequence $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ proves $Dg=0$ almost everywhere. This proves the oscillation conclusion from the stated geometric covering input, rather than assuming a doubling property for a potentially singular measure. $\square$
