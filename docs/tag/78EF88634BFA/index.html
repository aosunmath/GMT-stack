Lemma 12.6.4 (The gradient defect and regular level geometry)78EF88634BFA

Lemma 12.6.4 (The gradient defect and regular level geometry).78EF88634BFA Let $u$ be smooth, $w=|\nabla u|$, and $\nu =\nabla u/w$ on the regular set $O=\{ w>0\} $. If $A$ is the second fundamental form of a regular level set with normal $\nu $, then on $O$,

\[ |\nabla ^2u|^2-|\nabla w|^2 =w^2|\nabla \nu |^2 =w^2|A|^2+|\nabla _{\{ u=\mathrm{constant}\} }w|^2\geq 0. \]

Also $w$ is locally Lipschitz and $|\nabla w|\leq |\nabla ^2u|$ almost everywhere on the whole manifold.

Proof

Differentiate $\nabla u=w\nu $. The derivatives of the unit vector are perpendicular to it, so the squared norm gives $|\nabla ^2u|^2=|\nabla w|^2+w^2|\nabla \nu |^2$. In a frame tangent to the level set, the tangential derivatives of $\nu $ are its second fundamental form. Symmetry of the Hessian gives, for tangent $X$, $w\langle \nabla _\nu \nu ,X\rangle =Xw$, so the remaining normal-direction contribution is $w^{-2}|\nabla _{\{ u=\mathrm{constant}\} }w|^2$.

The norm of a smooth vector field is locally Lipschitz. The usual norm chain rule gives the inequality where $w>0$; on its zero set the weak derivative of $w$ vanishes almost everywhere. Thus the a.e. inequality holds globally. This proves the identities behind [ChAC], Exercise 5.5(b),(c),(f). $\square$
