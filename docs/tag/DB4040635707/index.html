Proposition 5.12.2 (Smooth slicing bounds the integral of mass)DB4040635707

Proposition 5.12.2 (Smooth slicing bounds the integral of mass).DB4040635707 Under the preceding assumptions, for every open $W\subset \mathbb R^ N$ and Borel $A\subset \mathbb R$,

\[ \int _ A\mathbf M_ W(B_ t)\, dt \leq \int _{W\cap f^{-1}(A)}|Df|\, d\| T\| . \]

The integrand is measurable, with infinity allowed before the estimate is proved. In particular $B_ t$ has finite mass for almost every level. Compare [Sim18], equations (4.9)–(4.10).

Proof

Each $B_ t$ has order at most one on every compact support, directly from its definition in terms of $T(d\omega )$ and $\partial T(\omega )$. Proposition 5.8.2 therefore provides a countable family $\{ \omega _ j\} _{j\geq 1}$ of smooth $(m-1)$-forms compactly supported in $W$, each of comass at most one, with $\mathbf M_ W(B_ t)=\sup _ j B_ t(\omega _ j)$. Include zero as the first test and include negatives. This test-space construction is independent of rectifiability.

For the first $k$ tests, partition $A$ into disjoint measurable sets $A_1,\ldots ,A_ k$ on which the corresponding test realizes their maximum, breaking ties by the smallest index. By Lemma 5.12.1,

\[ \int _ A\max _{1\leq j\leq k}B_ t(\omega _ j)\, dt =\sum _{j=1}^ k T(\chi _{f^{-1}(A_ j)}df\wedge \omega _ j) \leq \int _{W\cap f^{-1}(A)}|Df|\, d\| T\| . \]

Indeed the inverse images of the $A_ j$ are disjoint, and $df\wedge \omega _ j$ has comass at most $|Df|$. This bound holds for arbitrary polar $m$-vectors by mass/comass duality; simplicity is unnecessary. Ensure zero is the first test, so the maxima are nonnegative and increase. Monotone convergence as $k\to \infty $ gives the result and measurability. Taking $W=\mathbb R^ N$ makes the right side finite because $T$ has compact support and $Df$ is bounded there. $\square$
