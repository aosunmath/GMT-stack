Theorem 4.14.5 (Unit density identifies the perimeter measure exactly)DCBDD31CBE35

Theorem 4.14.5 (Unit density identifies the perimeter measure exactly).DCBDD31CBE35 For $E$ of locally finite perimeter in $U\subset \mathbb R^ n$, $n\geq 2$,

\[ |D\chi _ E|=\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner (\partial ^*E\cap U). \]

The proof uses the arbitrary-Radon differentiation theorem proved from Besicovitch selection and the locally proved density theorem for rectifiable sets, in addition to the local arguments developed above. This is the final measure-identification step of [Sim18], Theorem 4.4, p. 100.

Proof

Put $\mu =|D\chi _ E|$, $S=\partial ^*E\cap U$ and $\sigma =\mathcal H^{n-1}\llcorner S$. Corollary 4.14.4 gives locally finite mutually absolutely continuous measures. The abstract Radon–Nikodym theorem gives $\mu =f\sigma $ for a nonnegative locally $\sigma $-integrable function $f$; one may apply it on a countable finite-measure exhaustion and use uniqueness on overlaps. This abstract representation is recalled in [Sim18], Chapter 1, equation (4.17), p. 27.

By Theorem 2.6.5,

\[ f(x)=\lim _{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\mu (B_ r(x))}{\sigma (B_ r(x))} \quad \text{for }\sigma \text{-almost every }x. \]

Theorem 4.13.6 makes $S$ countably rectifiable, and the preceding corollary supplies the required local Hausdorff finiteness. Corollary 3.6.7 therefore gives $\sigma (B_ r(x))/(\omega _{n-1}r^{n-1})\to 1$ at $\sigma $-almost every point. At every point of $S$, Corollary 4.12.4 gives the same unit limit for $\mu $. Dividing the two limits yields $f=1$ almost everywhere for $\sigma $, proving the measure identity. The density theorem is used only after its rectifiability and local-finiteness hypotheses have been established, so this step does not assume the perimeter representation it is proving. $\square$
