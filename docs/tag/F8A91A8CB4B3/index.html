Lemma 12.4.2 (Tubular coordinates near a smooth boundary)F8A91A8CB4B3

Lemma 12.4.2 (Tubular coordinates near a smooth boundary).F8A91A8CB4B3 Let $E$ be open with compact $C^2$ boundary $\Sigma =\partial E\Subset U$. For some $\rho >0$, the map $F(y,t)=y+t\nu (y)$, $y\in \Sigma $, $|t|<\rho $, is one-to-one onto a tubular neighborhood, where $\nu $ is outward. Its volume element is $J(y,t)d\mathcal H^{n-1}(y)dt$, with

\[ J(y,t)=\det (I+tD_\Sigma \nu ),\qquad J(y,0)=1, \]

and $J>0$ and $J\to 1$ uniformly as $t\to 0$. The signed distance $d$, positive in $E$, satisfies $d(F(y,t))=-t$, $|Dd|=1$, and $\Delta d|_\Sigma =-H$ for $H=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $.

Proof

At $t=0$, the derivative of $F$ maps tangent directions identically and the $t$ direction to $\nu $, so the inverse function theorem gives local coordinates. Compactness supplies a uniform radius. Global injectivity for a smaller radius follows by contradiction: two colliding normal segments with lengths tending to zero would have base points tending to the same boundary point, contradicting local injectivity. Nearest-point minimization shows these normal coordinates give the signed distance. Tangential differentiation of $F$ yields $I+tD_\Sigma \nu $, and its normal derivative is $\nu $; this gives the Jacobian. Positivity and uniform convergence follow by reducing $\rho $. Since $Dd=-\nu $ on the boundary and has zero normal derivative along normal segments, its divergence there is $-\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nu $. These are the coordinate facts used in [ChAC], p. 10. $\square$
