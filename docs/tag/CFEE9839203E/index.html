Theorem 7.12.6 (Index cannot increase at a smooth weak limit)CFEE9839203E

Theorem 7.12.6 (Index cannot increase at a smooth weak limit).CFEE9839203E Let $M^{n+1}$ be smooth and closed, $n\geq 1$. Let $\Sigma _ j$ and $\Sigma $ be smooth closed two-sided embedded minimal hypersurfaces. If

\[ |\Sigma _ j|\longrightarrow m|\Sigma |\quad \text{as varifolds},\qquad m>0, \]

then $\operatorname {index}(\Sigma )\leq \liminf _ j\operatorname {index}(\Sigma _ j)$. The smoothness and two-sidedness of the limit are assumptions, not conclusions. This proves the index-persistence step underlying [Sharp17], Section 4, Claim 3, in this setting.

Proof

Take any finite-dimensional negative subspace for the Jacobi form of $\Sigma $, with smooth basis $f_1,\ldots ,f_ k$. Extend $f_ a\nu $ to smooth ambient fields $X_ a$ using a tubular neighborhood and a cutoff. At the limit varifold, Lemma 7.12.5 identifies the form on coefficient vectors with

\[ Q_{m|\Sigma |}(z)=mQ_\Sigma \left(\sum _ a z_ af_ a,\sum _ a z_ af_ a\right), \]

which is negative definite because $m>0$. Lemma 7.12.4 makes the corresponding forms on $\Sigma _ j$ negative definite for all large $j$. Their normal velocities are the functions $\langle X_ a,\nu _ j\rangle $. These functions are linearly independent: a zero linear combination would have second variation zero, contradicting strict negativity for its nonzero coefficient vector. Their span therefore gives $\operatorname {index}(\Sigma _ j)\geq k$. Since every finite negative subspace of the limit can be used, the stated inequality follows, including when its index is infinite. This argument neither establishes smooth graphical convergence nor removes singularities of a weak limit. $\square$
