Lemma 5.14.7 (Localization of a proper Lipschitz map on a normal current)FE42F5542170

Lemma 5.14.7 (Localization of a proper Lipschitz map on a normal current).FE42F5542170 Let $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$ and $W\subset \mathbb R^ q$ be open. Let $T$ be locally normal in $U$ and $f:U\to W$ locally Lipschitz and proper on $\operatorname {spt}T$. There is a locally normal pushforward defined on each compactly supported target test by the compact normal construction with a source cutoff. It is independent of the cutoff and local extension, has support in $f(\operatorname {spt}T)$, and satisfies

\[ \partial (f_\# T)=f_\# (\partial T). \]

For $m=0$ the boundary assertion has the usual zero-boundary meaning.

Proof

For a compact target set $K\Subset W$ choose a relatively compact open target neighborhood $O$ of $K$. Properness makes $C=\operatorname {spt}T\cap f^{-1}(\overline O)$ compact in $U$. Choose $\chi \in C_ c^\infty (U)$ equal to one on a neighborhood of $C$. The cutoff $Q=T\llcorner \chi $ is a compact normal current in the whole ambient Euclidean space by Proposition 5.3.5, extending it by zero outside $U$.

Choose a globally Lipschitz map $F:\mathbb R^ N\to \mathbb R^ q$ equal to $f$ near $\operatorname {spt}\chi $. Such an extension exists: multiply $f$ by a smooth compactly supported function in $U$ equal to one on a slightly larger neighborhood and extend the product by zero. The resulting map is locally Lipschitz everywhere with a common derivative/Lipschitz bound, by a finite cover of its compact support; subdividing line segments proves a global Lipschitz bound. Its target may leave $W$, which causes no difficulty because target tests are extended by zero outside $W$. Define the value of the desired pushforward on tests supported in $K$ by $F_\# Q$.

Two extensions agree near the support of $Q$, so Proposition 5.13.3 makes their pushforwards agree. For two cutoffs use one extension on the union of their supports. Their difference current $T\llcorner (\chi _1-\chi _2)$ is compact and normal; its support has no point mapping into $O$, since both cutoffs equal one near $C$. The compact support theorem therefore makes its pushforward vanish on $O$. This proves agreement on every test in $K$. It also supplies linearity by choosing common cutoffs for finitely many tests. On a fixed target compact set, the mass and boundary bounds of the compact normal construction give local normality and continuity. Tests supported off $f(\operatorname {spt}T)$ vanish by the same support argument. That image is relatively closed in $W$ by properness.

For $m\geq 1$, finally use

\[ \partial Q=(\partial T)\llcorner \chi -T\llcorner d\chi . \]

The first term is compact normal. The second term is also compact normal, being the difference of that term and $\partial Q$; its support is contained in $\operatorname {spt}T\cap \operatorname {spt}d\chi $ and therefore maps outside $O$. Its pushforward vanishes on target tests supported in $K$. Boundary commutation for $Q$ consequently gives the desired identity there. Since $\operatorname {spt}\partial T\subset \operatorname {spt}T$, the same properness applies to the boundary. In dimension zero both boundaries vanish. Exhausting target compact sets proves the statement. This proof uses normal cutoffs without claiming that those cutoffs preserve integer multiplicity. $\square$
