Lemma 14.12.5 (Maximizing parameters cannot escape under a small compact perturbation)140F76BA7406

Lemma 14.12.5 (Maximizing parameters cannot escape under a small compact perturbation).140F76BA7406 Let $\Sigma _ s$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 14.12.4, and suppose the constant tangent subspace of $\Sigma $ is zero. There exist $R,T>1$, $0<\tau <1$, $d>0$, and $\eta >0$ such that

\[ G(c,t,s)\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-d \]

for $|s|\leq \eta $ whenever $|c|>R$, or $t>T$, or $t<\tau $.

Proof

Theorem 14.11.8 gives a fixed positive deficit for the unperturbed surface whenever $|c|$ is large or $t\notin [1/2,2]$. Choose a smaller positive $\delta $ so that this deficit is at least $4\delta $ and $4\delta <\lambda (\Sigma )$. The far-center estimate of Lemma 14.12.3 then gives deficit at least $3\delta $ for every scale when $|c|>R$, after increasing $R$. Its large-scale estimate similarly gives deficit at least $3\delta $ for all centers when $t>T$. These choices are uniform on a fixed small shape interval.

It remains to consider $|c|\leq R$ at small scales. Take $A,B,C$ from Lemma 14.12.4. Choose $0<\tau <1/2$ sufficiently small that

\[ e^{B\tau }(\lambda (\Sigma )-3\delta )+C\tau \leq \lambda (\Sigma )-2\delta . \]

At this fixed scale, uniform convergence on the compact center ball makes $G(c,\tau ,s)\leq \lambda (\Sigma )-3\delta $ for $|s|\leq \eta $, after reducing $\eta $, because the unperturbed deficit is at least $4\delta $. The small-scale comparison propagates this upper bound to all $0<t<\tau $ with deficit $2\delta $. Combining the three regions proves the claim, for instance with $d=2\delta $. This is the escape-prevention part of [CM12], pp. 790–792. $\square$
