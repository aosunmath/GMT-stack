Theorem 16.5.3 (A complete convex expander asymptotic to two rays)56C7AF70B331

Theorem 16.5.3 (A complete convex expander asymptotic to two rays).56C7AF70B331 For every $a>0$, there is a unique smooth even solution $u_ a:\mathbb R\to \mathbb R$ of the expander graph equation with $u_ a(0)=a$ and $u_ a'(0)=0$. Its graph is properly embedded, complete, and strictly convex. For a finite $\ell _ a>0$,

\[ u_ a(x)-\ell _ a|x|\longrightarrow 0\quad (|x|\to \infty ), \qquad 0<u_ a(x)-\ell _ a|x|\leq a. \]

Its two asymptotic rays meet at the origin. This constructs a family; it does not assert that a prescribed cone angle determines a unique expander among arbitrary immersions.

Proof

The ODE is invariant under $x\mapsto -x$, so local uniqueness makes the solution even; Lemma 16.5.2 extends both halves. Positivity of $q$ gives $u_ a''>0$. As an entire smooth graph it is proper, embedded, and complete. Write $\ell =\ell _ a$. Boundedness of $p$ and the Gaussian bound for $q$ give $0<p'(x)\leq Ce^{-x^2/4}$, hence for $x\geq 1$,

\[ 0<\ell -p(x)\leq C\int _ x^\infty e^{-s^2/4}\, ds \leq \frac{2C}{x}e^{-x^2/4}. \]

Since $u_ a-x\ell =q-x(\ell -p)$, both terms tend to zero. Its derivative is $p-\ell <0$ for $x\geq 0$; thus it decreases from $a$ to zero and is strictly positive. Reflecting gives the negative half. These properties agree with the expanding-curve models in [Hal12], Section 6, after converting its dilation normalization. $\square$
