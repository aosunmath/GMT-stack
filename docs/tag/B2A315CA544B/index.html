Proposition 14.8.5 (Translation and dilation modes)B2A315CA544B

Proposition 14.8.5 (Translation and dilation modes).B2A315CA544B On a smooth two-sided self-shrinker, for each constant vector $v$ put $a_ v=\langle v,\nu \rangle $. Then

\[ La_ v=\tfrac 12a_ v,\qquad LH=H. \]

These pointwise identities do not require compactness. A mode is an eigenfunction only when it is nonzero and belongs to the domain under consideration. Compare [CM12], Theorem 5.2.

Proof

Translate the immersion to $x+sv$. Its scalar curvature and normal are unchanged, so its residual is $\Phi _ s=-s a_ v/2$. The normal velocity is $a_ v$; its tangential component contributes zero because $\Phi =0$. Linearization therefore yields $-La_ v=-a_ v/2$.

For the dilation $x_ s=(1+s)x$, scalar curvature is $H/(1+s)$ and the normal is unchanged. Thus $\Phi _ s=H((1+s)^{-1}-(1+s))$, with derivative $-2H$. The normal velocity is $\langle x,\nu \rangle =2H$. Linearization gives $-L(2H)=-2H$. These arguments are local calculations and do not assert convergence of noncompact integrals. $\square$
