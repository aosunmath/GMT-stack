Theorem 7.1.3 (Second variation in the two-sided case)30FBA471ADE5

Theorem 7.1.3 (Second variation in the two-sided case).30FBA471ADE5 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset (M^{n+1},g)$ be a smooth, closed, two-sided minimal hypersurface. For a smooth normal variation with initial velocity $f\nu $, the second derivative of area is

\[ Q(f,f)=\int _\Sigma \bigl (|\nabla _\Sigma f|^2-(|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu ))f^2\bigr )\, dA. \]

With $\Delta _\Sigma =\operatorname {div}_\Sigma \nabla _\Sigma $, define $J=\Delta _\Sigma +|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}_ M(\nu ,\nu )$; then $Q(f,f)=-\int _\Sigma fJf\, dA$. Source: [MM], Theorem 6.2, specialized to a closed hypersurface; also [ChMin], Section 23.

Proof

Differentiate the first-variation formula $\frac d{dt}A(\Sigma _ t)=\int _{\Sigma _ t}H_ tf_ t\, dA_ t$. At a minimal initial hypersurface, the terms from differentiating $f_ t$ and $dA_ t$ vanish because $H_0=0$. Thus $A''(0)=\int _\Sigma f\dot H$. The Riemannian calculation in Proposition 11.3.4, which does not use the second variation, gives $\dot H=-\Delta _\Sigma f-(|B|^2+\operatorname {Ric}(\nu ,\nu ))f$. Integration by parts on the closed hypersurface proves the formula. A tangential initial velocity contributes the tangential derivative of $H_0=0$, so only the normal speed enters. The same proof works for variations compactly supported away from a boundary. $\square$
