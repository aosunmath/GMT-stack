Theorem 13.2.1 (Existence and uniqueness for the scalar obstacle problem)A38AB8D7E124

Theorem 13.2.1 (Existence and uniqueness for the scalar obstacle problem).A38AB8D7E124 Under the hypotheses of Definition 13.1.1, there is a unique minimizer $u\in K$ of $\mathcal E_ f$. Uniqueness is as an $H^1$ function, hence up to almost-everywhere equality. No regularity of its free boundary is part of this assertion.

Proof

Fix $v_0\in K$ and write $v=v_0+w$, $w\in H^1_0(U)$. Poincare and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[ \mathcal E_ f(v)\geq \tfrac 14\| Dw\| _2^2-C, \]

where $C$ depends on $v_0,f,U$. Thus a minimizing sequence is bounded in $H^1$. Pass to a weakly convergent subsequence. The affine space $g+H^1_0(U)$ is weakly closed. The obstacle constraint is also weakly closed: for every nonnegative $\eta \in L^2(U)$, the inequalities $\int (v_ j-\psi )\eta \geq 0$ pass to the limit and imply $v\geq \psi $ almost everywhere. The squared gradient norm is weakly lower semicontinuous and the forcing term is weakly continuous, so the limit minimizes.

If $u_1,u_2$ minimize, their midpoint is admissible. The parallelogram identity shows that its energy is the average energy minus $\frac18\| D(u_1-u_2)\| _2^2$. Thus the gradients agree. Since $u_1-u_2\in H^1_0(U)$, Poincare gives equality of the functions. This extends the proof of [Fig18], Proposition 2.1, to the stated forcing class. $\square$
