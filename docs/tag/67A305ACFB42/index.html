Example 6.11.8 (A flat singular point from two smooth minimal graphs)67A305ACFB42

Example 6.11.8 (A flat singular point from two smooth minimal graphs).67A305ACFB42 Let $\Omega =(-\pi /2,\pi /2)^2$ and

\[ u(x,y)=\log (\cos y)-\log (\cos x). \]

In $\Omega \times \mathbb R$, the sum of the varifolds of the graph of $u$ and the plane $z=0$ is stationary and integral. The origin is a flat singular point with density two and a unique tangent cone equal to twice the horizontal plane.

Proof

Here $u_ x=\tan x$, $u_ y=-\tan y$, $u_{xx}=\sec ^2x$, $u_{yy}=-\sec ^2y$, and $u_{xy}=0$. The minimal graph equation in two variables is

\[ (1+u_ y^2)u_{xx}-2u_ xu_ yu_{xy}+(1+u_ x^2)u_{yy}=0, \]

which these derivatives satisfy. Thus both smooth graphs are stationary for compactly supported variations in the indicated open cylinder. At zero, $u=0$ and $Du=0$, so the tangent cone of their sum is twice the horizontal plane; smoothness makes this blow-up unique.

The support is not a single smooth surface near zero. If it were, its horizontal tangent plane would make it a graph on a smaller neighborhood by the inverse function theorem. But the support contains both the zero graph and nonzero values of $u$ arbitrarily close to zero, giving two heights over some base points. This contradiction proves singularity. The graphs cross along $y=\pm x$; this is not one-sided contact prohibited by the strong maximum principle. It also differs from a regular multiplicity-two plane, which has only one geometric sheet as a set. $\square$
