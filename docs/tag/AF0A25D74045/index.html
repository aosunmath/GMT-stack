Lemma 12.6.9 (Bounded stable one-dimensional profiles)AF0A25D74045

Lemma 12.6.9 (Bounded stable one-dimensional profiles).AF0A25D74045 A bounded $C^2$ solution $f:\mathbb R\to [-1,1]$ of $f''=W'(f)$ is stable on $\mathbb R$ if and only if it is a constant well or a translated, possibly reversed heteroclinic. If a solution $u(x)=f(e\cdot x)$ is stable on $\mathbb R^ n$, then $f$ is stable on $\mathbb R$.

Proof

For the second assertion rotate $e$ to the last coordinate and test with $\phi (t)\eta (y/R)$, where $\phi $ is compactly supported and $\eta \not\equiv 0$ is a fixed compactly supported transverse cutoff. Divide the stability inequality by $\int \eta (y/R)^2$. The transverse gradient contribution tends to zero like $R^{-2}$, giving the one-dimensional stability inequality. The case $n=1$ needs no reduction.

The constant zero is unstable by testing with $\phi (t/L)$ and taking $L$ large: its negative potential term is of order $L$ and the gradient term of order $L^{-1}$. A nonconstant solution cannot attain $\pm 1$ or attain $0$ with zero derivative, by ODE uniqueness. If it has a turning point with value $a\in (-1,1)\setminus \{ 0\} $, its conserved Hamiltonian gives a periodic orbit between $-|a|$ and $|a|$. Indeed $f'^2=2(W(f)-W(a))$, positive between the two turning values, and the separated travel-time integrals are finite because their endpoint roots are simple.

Let $(s,t)$ lie between successive extrema of this periodic orbit, and extend $h=f'$ on $[s,t]$ by zero. Then $h\in H^1_ c(\mathbb R)$, $h(s)=h(t)=0$, and integration of its linearized equation gives $Q(h)=0$. Nonnegative quadratic forms obey $Q(h,\zeta )=0$ for all compact tests, by applying stability to $h+\tau \zeta $ for both signs of $\tau $. But integration by parts gives $Q(h,\zeta )=h'(t)\zeta (t)-h'(s)\zeta (s)$, and $h'=f''=W'(f)\ne 0$ at either extremum. Choosing a test supported near just one endpoint contradicts this identity. Thus a nonconstant stable profile has no turning point.

It is strictly monotone and bounded, so it has distinct limits at both ends. Uniform derivative bounds and convergence of translates show that these limits are equilibria in $\{ -1,0,1\} $, with derivative tending to zero. Conservation of the Hamiltonian requires equal potential values at the two ends; the only distinct pair is $-1,+1$. Separation of variables now gives the heteroclinic. Conversely constant wells are stable, and heteroclinics are stable by their positive translation derivative and Proposition 12.6.2. This resolves the ODE step in [ChAC], Exercise 5.5(e). $\square$
