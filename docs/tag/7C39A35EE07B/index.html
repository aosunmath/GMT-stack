Lemma 4.15.1 (The inverse derivative is a pushforward of interval length)7C39A35EE07B

Lemma 4.15.1 (The inverse derivative is a pushforward of interval length).7C39A35EE07B The positive measure $\mu =f_\# (\mathcal L^1\llcorner [a,b])$ has no atoms, is supported on $[c,d]$, and has mass $b-a$. For $c\leq u<v\leq d$,

\[ \mu ((u,v])=g(v)-g(u), \qquad Dg=\mu \llcorner (c,d). \]

The distributional derivative is taken on the open interval $(c,d)$.

Proof

Strict increase and continuity give $f^{-1}((u,v])=(g(u),g(v)]$, so pushing forward length gives the increment identity. Every target singleton has at most one preimage and therefore zero mass. Support and total mass follow directly from the definition of pushforward. In particular $g(t)=a+\mu ((c,t])$ for $c\leq t\leq d$. Lemma 4.7.1 identifies its distributional derivative on $(c,d)$ with $\mu $. The endpoint atoms vanish, so the total mass of this derivative is also $b-a$. This establishes the BV assertion directly, without assuming absolute continuity of the inverse. $\square$
