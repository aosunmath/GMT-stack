Lemma 5.15.2 (Mass distance equals the L1 distance of signed multiplicities)74859C11798E

Lemma 5.15.2 (Mass distance equals the L1 distance of signed multiplicities).74859C11798E Let $T_ j=\llbracket M_ j,\theta _ j,\tau _ j\rrbracket $ be locally integer rectifiable currents and use $M,\eta $ from Lemma 5.15.1. Define the signed integer function $a_ j$ on $M$ by $a_ j\eta =\theta _ j\tau _ j$ on $M_ j$ and $a_ j=0$ elsewhere. Then, for each relatively compact open $W\subset U$,

\[ \mathbf M_ W(T_ j-T_ k) =\int _{M\cap W}|a_ j-a_ k|\, d\mathcal H^ m, \qquad \mathbf M_ W(T_ j)=\int _{M\cap W}|a_ j|\, d\mathcal H^ m. \]

The equalities concern mass on open sets; no boundary mass hypothesis is required.

Proof

The common orientation makes $a_ j$ well defined almost everywhere and integer valued, with $|a_ j|=\theta _ j$ on its carrier. Both currents act by integration against the vector density $a_ j\eta $ relative to the same sigma-finite Hausdorff measure. Their difference has density $(a_ j-a_ k)\eta $. This density is integrable locally since $|a_ j-a_ k|\leq |a_ j|+|a_ k|$. Its vector-measure variation is $|a_ j-a_ k|\mathcal H^ m$, because the simple vector $\eta $ has mass norm one. Theorem 5.3.2 and Proposition 5.7.6 identify this variation on $W$ with current mass. The same argument with one coefficient zero proves the second equality. The reference measure itself need not be locally finite: the integrable densities define locally finite vector measures, to which the representation and variation statements apply. $\square$
