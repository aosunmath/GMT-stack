Lemma 2.5.1 (Fivefold covering for a bounded family of Euclidean balls)9B745ADFD001

Lemma 2.5.1 (Fivefold covering for a bounded family of Euclidean balls).9B745ADFD001 Let $\mathcal B$ be a family of balls of positive radii in $\mathbb R^ n$, with bounded centers and uniformly bounded radii. There is a finite or countable pairwise disjoint subfamily $B_ j=B_{r_ j}(x_ j)$ such that

\[ \bigcup _{B\in \mathcal B}B\subset \bigcup _ j B_{5r_ j}(x_ j). \]

Moreover every original ball intersects a selected ball whose radius is at least half its own radius. This is a bounded Euclidean version of [Sim18], Lemma 3.4.

Proof

If no balls remain, stop. Otherwise select a remaining ball whose radius is greater than half the supremum of the remaining radii, and discard all remaining balls intersecting it. Continue. The selected balls are disjoint and lie in a fixed bounded region. If the process is infinite, their volumes have finite sum, so their radii tend to zero. An original ball of radius $r>0$ cannot remain forever: while it remains, every selected radius is greater than $r/2$, contradicting that limit. Thus it is selected or discarded at some step. If it intersects $B_{r_ j}(x_ j)$ at that step, its radius is at most $2r_ j$, so each of its points lies within distance $r_ j+2r\leq 5r_ j$ of $x_ j$. This proves both assertions, with the open-ball inequalities giving containment in the open dilation. $\square$
