Theorem 14.9.7 (Nontrivial round cylinders are F-unstable)C6F03108FD3A

Theorem 14.9.7 (Nontrivial round cylinders are F-unstable).C6F03108FD3A For $1\leq k<n$, the self-shrinker $S^ k(\sqrt{2k})\times \mathbb R^{n-k}$ is $F$-unstable for compactly supported normal variations. This is the cylinder case of [CM12], Section 11.1, pp. 816–817. In particular the roles of cylinders in entropy and singularity theory cannot be summarized as $F$-stability under Definition 14.9.3.

Proof

Write $z\in \mathbb R^{n-k}$ for the axial coordinate. The cylinder has $H=\sqrt{k/2}$, $|A|^2=1/2$, and $Lz_1=z_1/2$. Choose smooth radial cutoffs $0\leq \eta _ R(z)\leq 1$, equal to one for $|z|\leq R$, zero for $|z|\geq 2R$, and with $|\nabla \eta _ R|\leq C/R$. Set $f_ R=\eta _ Rz_1$. This has compact support because the spherical factor is compact. Its oddness in $z_1$ gives $[f_ R]=0$ exactly. The normal depends only on the spherical factor, and its integral on that sphere is zero, so $[f_ Ra_ y]=0$ for every $y$.

The Gaussian product measure has all polynomial moments. Therefore $f_ R\to z_1$ and $\nabla f_ R\to \nabla z_1$ in Gaussian $L^2$: the cutoff errors are supported in Gaussian tails and $|z_1\nabla \eta _ R|\leq C|z|/R$. Since $|A|^2+1/2=1$,

\[ Q(f_ R)\longrightarrow [|\nabla z_1|^2-z_1^2] =-\tfrac 12[z_1^2]<0. \]

The last equality follows from the one-dimensional Gaussian identity $[z_1^2]=2[1]$, obtained by integrating the derivative of $z_1e^{-z_1^2/4}$ on the real line. Choose one large $R$ with $Q(f_ R)<0$. Its joint Hessian is $Q(f_ R)-h^2[H^2]-[a_ y^2]/2$, strictly negative for every center and scale derivative. Exact oddness makes this conclusion uniform in $h$, without passing a nonuniform limit through an optimization. $\square$
