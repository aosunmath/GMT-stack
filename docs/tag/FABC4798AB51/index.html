Lemma 4.11.1 (Zero tangential derivatives imply one-dimensional dependence)FABC4798AB51

Lemma 4.11.1 (Zero tangential derivatives imply one-dimensional dependence).FABC4798AB51 Suppose $u\in L^\infty (\mathbb R^ n)$ and $D_ i u=0$ in distributions for $i=1,\ldots ,n-1$. Then there is $h\in L^\infty (\mathbb R)$ such that

\[ u(x',s)=h(s)\quad \text{for almost every }(x',s)\in \mathbb R^{n-1}\times \mathbb R. \]

If $u$ is a characteristic function, $h$ is zero or one almost everywhere. This makes explicit the cylindrical-limit step after equation (8) in [Sim18], p. 98.

Proof

Convolve $u$ with a standard smooth compactly supported approximate identity on $\mathbb R^ n$. Boundedness makes the convolution well defined even when $u$ is not globally integrable. Each smooth $u_\delta $ has zero derivative in every $x'$ direction, hence is constant on each connected horizontal plane: $u_\delta (x',s)=h_\delta (s)$. On any bounded product $Q\times I$, with $0<|Q|<\infty $, local $L^1$ convergence of convolutions implies

\[ |Q|\int _ I|h_\delta -h_\gamma |\, ds =\int _{Q\times I}|u_\delta -u_\gamma |\, dx\longrightarrow 0. \]

Thus $h_\delta $ has an $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R)$ limit $h$. Limits on overlapping intervals agree, and the uniform bound passes to $h$ by almost-everywhere convergence along a sequence. On every product cylinder $u=h(s)$ in $L^1$, hence almost everywhere; a countable exhaustion gives the global assertion. If $u$ is zero or one almost everywhere, Fubini on any such product shows that $h$ has the same property. $\square$
