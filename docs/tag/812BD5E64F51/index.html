Example 16.29.5 (Distance is constant on a sphere and its volume function jumps)812BD5E64F51

Example 16.29.5 (Distance is constant on a sphere and its volume function jumps).812BD5E64F51 Let $R>0$, $M=S_ R^2\subset \mathbb R^3$ and $\rho (x)=|x|$. Its tangential gradient vanishes everywhere, while

\[ \rho _\# (\mathcal H^2\llcorner M)=4\pi R^2\delta _ R. \]

The function $V(t)=\mathcal H^2(M\cap B_ t(0))$ equals zero for $t\leq R$ and $4\pi R^2$ for $t>R$. It is not absolutely continuous. Coarea nevertheless holds: all level intersections except the single level $R$ are empty.

Proof

The function $\rho |M$ is the constant $R$, so its tangential derivative is zero and its unweighted pushforward is the claimed atom. The area is $4\pi R^2$ by Example 16.29.2. The strict inequality in the open-ball definition accounts for $V(R)=0$. At the one nonempty level the fiber is the entire two-sphere and has infinite one-dimensional measure, by the same dimension comparison as in Example 16.29.4, applied to a regular chart. That value occurs on a null set of radii, and the integral of the fiber length is zero, agreeing with the zero Jacobian integral. Proposition 3.12.7 concerns the empty regular part in this example and makes no incorrect absolute-continuity claim for $V$ itself. $\square$
