Theorem 2.3.7 (Positive Riesz representation on a Euclidean open set)D8B4638B4814

Theorem 2.3.7 (Positive Riesz representation on a Euclidean open set).D8B4638B4814 For every positive linear functional $L:C_ c(U;\mathbb R)\to \mathbb R$ there is a unique Radon measure $\mu $ on $U$ such that

\[ L(f)=\int _ U f\, d\mu \qquad (f\in C_ c(U;\mathbb R)). \]

Its values on open sets are given by $\mu (O)=\sup \{ L(\phi ):\phi \in C_ c(O),\ 0\leq \phi \leq 1\} $. This is [Sim18], Theorem 5.12, specialized to a Euclidean open set.

Proof

Construct the Radon measure by Proposition 2.3.6. First note that, for $0\leq h\leq 1$ with compact support,

\[ L(h)\leq \mu (\{ h>0\} ). \]

Indeed $(h-\varepsilon )_+$ has compact support in the open set $\{ h>0\} $ and is bounded by one, while the difference from $h$ tends uniformly to zero on a common compact support. Lemma 2.3.3 gives the inequality upon taking the limit.

For $f\geq 0$ with compact support, fix $\delta >0$ and an integer $N$ with $N\delta \geq \max f$. Set $h_ i=\min (1,(f-i\delta )_+/\delta )$ for $0\leq i<N$. Pointwise $f=\delta \sum _ i h_ i$. The upper bound just proved and the fact that $h_ i=1$ on $\{ f>(i+1)\delta \} $ give

\[ \delta \sum _{i=0}^{N-1}\mu (\{ f>(i+1)\delta \} ) \leq L(f)\leq \delta \sum _{i=0}^{N-1}\mu (\{ f>i\delta \} ). \]

For the lower bound, every admissible test supported in that open superlevel set is at most $h_ i$. The same two sums bound $\int f\, d\mu $ by pointwise simple-function comparison. Their difference is at most $\delta \mu (\{ f>0\} )$, which tends to zero by local finiteness. Thus $L(f)=\int f\, d\mu $. Subtract positive and negative parts for signed $f$.

Finally any representing Radon measure satisfies the displayed open-set formula: the upper bound is immediate, and cutoffs equal to one on compact subsets yield the reverse bound by inner regularity. Equal open-set masses and outer regularity imply equal Borel masses. This proves uniqueness. $\square$
