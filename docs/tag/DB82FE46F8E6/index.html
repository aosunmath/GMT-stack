Theorem 12.7.9 (Monotone bounded solutions in three dimensions)DB82FE46F8E6

Theorem 12.7.9 (Monotone bounded solutions in three dimensions).DB82FE46F8E6 Let $u:\mathbb R^3\to [-1,1]$ solve $\Delta u=W'(u)$ smoothly and satisfy $\partial _{x_3}u>0$. Then

\[ u(x)=\tanh \left(\frac{e\cdot x-b}{\sqrt2}\right) \quad \text{with }|e|=1,\ e\cdot e_3>0. \]

No prescribed limit as $x_3\to \pm \infty $ is assumed.

Proof

Strict monotonicity and boundedness give $-1<u<1$ and stability. The upper end limit is a bounded stable solution on $\mathbb R^2$ by Proposition 12.7.7. Corollary 12.6.11 makes it a constant well or a planar heteroclinic. Regarded on $\mathbb R^3$, either has energy in $B_ R$ at most $c_ W\pi R^2$: for a layer, slice in its transition direction, bound each transverse disk area by $\pi R^2$, and integrate its one-dimensional density of total mass $c_ W$.

Proposition 12.7.8 now gives $E_1(u;B_ R)\leq CR^2$. Theorem 12.6.10 applies. The constant alternatives are excluded by strict monotonicity, which also forces the displayed direction to have positive third component. This is the argument of [ChAC], Theorem 5.3 and Section 5.5, with the end-limit stability and growth steps separated. $\square$
