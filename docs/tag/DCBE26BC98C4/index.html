Proposition 16.1.7 (Cylinder masses and singular continuity)DCBE26BC98C4

Proposition 16.1.7 (Cylinder masses and singular continuity).DCBE26BC98C4 The set $C$ is compact and has Lebesgue measure zero. The measure $\mu _ C$ is a Radon probability measure supported on $C$, and every level-$k$ retained interval $I$ has mass $2^{-k}$. It has no atoms. Thus a measure may be singular with respect to Lebesgue measure without being atomic.

Proof

The sets $C_ k$ are nested compact sets with total length $(2/3)^ k$, so their intersection is compact and null. The ternary series defining $T$ belongs to every $C_ k$. Specifying a word of $k$ binary digits selects a half-open dyadic interval of Lebesgue measure $2^{-k}$ and the corresponding retained ternary interval; endpoint conventions affect at most a countable null set. This proves the cylinder mass. Every point of $C$ lies in a retained interval at every level; its singleton mass is therefore at most $2^{-k}$ for all $k$. These same shrinking intervals show that every neighborhood of every point of $C$ has positive mass, so the support is exactly $C$. A finite Borel measure on $\mathbb R$ is regular ([Sim18], Chapter 1, Section 1); hence this probability measure is Radon. Alternatively, regularity follows by compact cylinder approximations and finite total mass. $\square$
