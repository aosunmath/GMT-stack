Example 14.19.6 (Why the grim reaper does not violate ancient rigidity)A7AD2D7C46AE

Example 14.19.6 (Why the grim reaper does not violate ancient rigidity).A7AD2D7C46AE For the grim-reaper graph $u(x,t)=-\log \cos x+t$ on $(-\pi /2,\pi /2)$, and for its products with Euclidean factors,

\[ v=\sec x,\qquad |A|=\cos x,\qquad v^2|A|^2=1. \]

The tensor $vA$ is parallel along each time slice. Thus it realizes the zero right-hand side of Proposition 14.19.1, even though its curvature is not zero. Its unbounded slope and its strip domain exclude both the periodic smoothing conclusion with finite $V_ s$ and Corollary 14.19.5.

Proof

The derivatives are $u_ x=\tan x$ and $u_{xx}=\sec ^2x$. With upward normal and $A=D\nu $, the signed principal curvature of the curve is $-u_{xx}/(1+u_ x^2)^{3/2}=-\cos x$. Multiplication by $v$ makes it $-1$. In dimension one this is a constant multiple of the identity tensor, hence parallel. For its product with a flat factor, the product connection preserves the curve and flat tangent factors, and $vA$ is minus the projection onto the curve factor, again parallel. The displayed identities follow. The slope tends to infinity at both strip edges, so the finite starting supremum used in the smoothing proof is unavailable. This example checks the exact square without extending a compact maximum principle to an unbounded domain. $\square$
