Theorem 4.8.1 (The divided-difference chain rule on an interval)24962712EC88

Theorem 4.8.1 (The divided-difference chain rule on an interval).24962712EC88 Let $u\in BV(I)$ and $\Phi \in C^1(\mathbb R)$. Then $\Phi (u)\in BV(I)$ and

\[ D(\Phi (u))=K_\Phi (u^-,u^+)\, Du, \qquad K_\Phi (s,t)=\int _0^1\Phi '((1-\theta )s+\theta t)\, d\theta . \]

Equivalently $K_\Phi (s,t)=(\Phi (t)-\Phi (s))/(t-s)$ for $s\ne t$, and $K_\Phi (s,s)=\Phi '(s)$. In particular, at a jump point $x$ the new atom has mass $\Phi (u^+(x))-\Phi (u^-(x))$. Away from the countable jump set the coefficient equals $\Phi '(u^+(x))$. We prove this one-dimensional formula directly from the measure formulation of BV in [Sim18], including its jump and singular-continuous parts.

Proof

The regulated representative of $u$ is bounded. On an interval containing its range, $\Phi '$ is bounded; extend the restriction of $\Phi $ to a globally Lipschitz function. Proposition 4.4.6, together with boundedness of $I$ and the composition, gives $\Phi (u)\in BV(I)$. Its right-continuous representative is $\Phi (u^+)$. The integral defining $K_\Phi $ shows that it is continuous and bounded on the compact square containing all one-sided values.

Fix $a<s<t<b$ and take partitions $s=x_0<\cdots <x_ N=t$ with mesh tending to zero. Write $u_ i=u^+(x_ i)$ and $\mu =Du$. Telescoping and the interval representation give

\[ \Phi (u^+(t))-\Phi (u^+(s)) =\sum _ iK_\Phi (u_{i-1},u_ i)\mu ((x_{i-1},x_ i]). \]

Regard the coefficients as a bounded step function on $(s,t]$. For each fixed $r$ in that interval, the upper endpoint of its half-open cell tends to $r$ from the right or equals $r$, and the lower endpoint tends to $r$ strictly from the left. Therefore the corresponding values converge to $u^+(r)$ and $u^-(r)$. Continuity of $K_\Phi $ and dominated convergence with respect to the finite measure $|\mu |$ yield

\[ \Phi (u^+(t))-\Phi (u^+(s)) =\int _{(s,t]}K_\Phi (u^-,u^+)\, d\mu . \]

The left side is $D(\Phi (u))((s,t])$ by Theorem 4.7.2. Equality on these half-open intervals determines the finite signed measure on $I$, by exhaustion and the uniqueness of a measure on its interval generators. Multiplying the coefficient at an atom by $Du(\{ x\} )=u^+(x)-u^-(x)$ gives the asserted jump mass. $\square$
