Proposition 5.8.2 (Mass as a countable supremum for order-one currents)470B6DE01C14

Proposition 5.8.2 (Mass as a countable supremum for order-one currents).470B6DE01C14 Let $B$ be a $q$-current on $W$ satisfying, for each compact $K\subset W$, an estimate $|B(\omega )|\leq C_ K\| \omega \| _{C^1}$ for tests supported in $K$. For the countable unit-comass family $\{ \omega _ j\} $ of Lemma 5.8.1, chosen to include zero and negatives,

\[ \mathbf M_ W(B)=\sup _ j B(\omega _ j), \]

allowing infinite mass. If $t\mapsto B_ t(\omega )$ is measurable for every test and each $B_ t$ has this order-one property, then $t\mapsto \mathbf M_ W(B_ t)$ is measurable. This formalizes the testing step in the slicing estimates of [Sim18], Section 4.

Proof

Every chosen test is admissible in the definition of mass, giving one inequality. Conversely approximate any unit-comass test by the family in $C^1$ with common compact support. The assumed estimate implies convergence of its evaluations, so its value is bounded by the countable supremum. Taking the supremum over all tests proves the reverse inequality. The supremum of countably many measurable functions is measurable, which proves the last assertion. No uniformity in the constants $C_ K$ as $t$ varies is required. $\square$
