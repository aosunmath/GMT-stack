Lemma 5.9.2 (Weak tangential convergence of ambient mollifications)4AAAD381706B

Lemma 5.9.2 (Weak tangential convergence of ambient mollifications).4AAAD381706B Let $M$ be countably $m$-rectifiable with locally finite $\mathcal H^ m$ measure, and let $d\mu =\theta \, d\mathcal H^ m\llcorner M$ be locally finite, where $\theta \geq 0$ is measurable. Suppose $h$ is locally Lipschitz in an ambient open neighborhood. On a fixed compact subset, let $h_\varepsilon $ be smooth ambient mollifications of $h$. If $v$ is a $\mu $-integrable tangent vector field supported in that compact set, then

\[ \lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0}\int _ M v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh_\varepsilon \, d\mu =\int _ M v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh\, d\mu . \]

This is a weak convergence statement against fixed $L^1(\mu )$ fields. It does not assert pointwise convergence of ambient derivatives on $M$. See [Sim18], p. 182, equation (1) in the proof of Lemma 4.5.

Proof

Localize to a slightly larger compact neighborhood on which $h$ has a finite Lipschitz constant $L$. A cutoff and an extension can be used before convolution; sufficiently small convolution radii give uniform convergence to $h$ and a uniform gradient bound on the compact set being tested.

First work in a relatively compact regular $C^1$ chart $F:V\subset \mathbb R^ m\to M_ j$, with $DF$ and the inverse metric bounded. The functions $h_\varepsilon \circ F$ converge uniformly to $h\circ F$, while their first derivatives are uniformly bounded. For a smooth compactly supported scalar test $a$, integration by parts gives

\[ \int _ V a\, \partial _ i(h_\varepsilon \circ F) =-\int _ V(\partial _ i a)(h_\varepsilon \circ F) \longrightarrow \int _ V a\, \partial _ i(h\circ F). \]

Approximation of an $L^1$ test by smooth tests, together with the uniform derivative bound, extends this convergence to every fixed $L^1$ coefficient. Expressing $v\cdot \nabla ^ Mh_\varepsilon $ in chart coordinates introduces only bounded coefficients from $DF$ and the inverse metric. The factor $\theta $ and the chart area density are absorbed into the $L^1$ test; the indicator of a measurable rectifiable piece is absorbed there as well. No derivative of a merely continuous chart metric is taken.

Partition $M$ into disjoint measurable pieces covered by such charts, using the countable $C^1$ decomposition. A finite number of compact chart pieces captures all but an arbitrarily small amount of $\int |v|\, d\mu $. On that remainder both tangential gradients have the same uniform bound, so the difference of the integrals is bounded by a constant times this small tail. First pass to the limit on the finite union, then let the tail tend to zero. This proves the assertion for the original field. $\square$
