Theorem 14.16.6 (Complete rigidity with a global curvature-ratio maximum)4DDD1D6498E3

Theorem 14.16.6 (Complete rigidity with a global curvature-ratio maximum).4DDD1D6498E3 Let $X:\Sigma ^ n\to \mathbb R^{n+1}$ be connected, complete, boundaryless, smooth and two-sided, with $H$ nowhere zero. Suppose it translates with a fixed velocity $v$ and $|A|^2/H^2$ attains a global maximum. Then its image is a complete grim-reaper curve times an $(n-1)$-dimensional Euclidean space, possibly tilted relative to $v$. This is a restricted complete formulation of the rigidity mechanism in [MSHS14], Theorem A, pp. 10–12; the explicit hypotheses avoid an unstated continuation across $\{ H=0\} $.

Proof

Proposition 14.16.3 and Lemma 14.16.4 give a parallel rank-one projection $B$ on all of $\Sigma $. The images under $dX$ of its kernel define one fixed ambient $(n-1)$-plane $L$. Indeed, parallel transport of a kernel vector along any curve has zero ambient derivative, since both its covariant derivative and its second fundamental form with the curve tangent vanish. Connectedness makes this ambient plane independent of the base point.

For any $a\in L$, the unique tangent field $V_ a$ with $dX(V_ a)=a$ has constant length. It is complete: an integral curve on a finite time interval stays in a bounded intrinsic ball, compact by completeness, so the smooth ODE can be continued. Along its flow $X$ changes by $ta$. Therefore the image is invariant under all translations in $L$.

Project onto $L^\perp $, an affine two-dimensional factor, and let $v_0$ be the projection of $v$. It is nonzero because $H\ne 0$. Choose a unit vector $q\in L^\perp $ perpendicular to $v_0$. The projection $q^ T$ on the surface never vanishes: otherwise $q$ would be normal, giving $\langle v_0,\nu \rangle =0$ and $H=0$. Thus $E=q^ T/|q^ T|$ is a global smooth unit tangent field spanning the range of $B$, and its flow exists for all real time by the same bounded-speed argument. Its image, after projection onto $L^\perp $, is a complete unit-speed planar translator with nonzero curvature and everywhere positive $q$ component of its tangent. The graph ODE in Proposition 16.3.5 identifies it with the full grim reaper: any omitted endpoint strictly inside its maximal graph interval would have bounded slope and finite arclength, contradicting continuation of this complete unit-speed flow; the two genuine asymptotes have infinite arclength.

The flows of $E$ and the fields $V_ a$ span the tangent space everywhere. Their accessible orbit from any point is therefore open, and the orbits partition the connected manifold into open sets, so there is only one. The $L$ flows do not change the two-plane projection; the $E$ flow stays on the same grim-reaper curve by its ODE uniqueness. Hence no additional image component occurs, and translation invariance in $L$ gives the asserted full product. The conclusion describes the image; it does not rely on a separate analytic unique-continuation theorem. $\square$
