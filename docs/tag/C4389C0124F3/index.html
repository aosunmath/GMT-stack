Lemma 12.10.4 (The bounded odd normal corrector)C4389C0124F3

Lemma 12.10.4 (The bounded odd normal corrector).C4389C0124F3 There is exactly one bounded solution $j$ of

\[ j''-Vj=t\psi ,\qquad j(0)=0. \]

It is odd, belongs to $H^2(\mathbb R)$, and is given by

\[ j(t)=\psi (t)\int _0^ t\psi (s)^{-2} \left(\int _{-\infty }^ s\tau \psi (\tau )^2\, d\tau \right)ds. \]

It is negative for $t>0$, orthogonal to $\psi $, and satisfies $|j|+|j'|+|j''|\leq C(1+t^2)e^{-\sqrt2|t|}$.

Proof

The total integral of $\tau \psi (\tau )^2$ is zero by oddness. Its primitive $I(s)$ from minus infinity is even and strictly negative at every finite $s$: for $s\leq 0$ this is immediate, and for $s>0$ use $I(s)=-\int _ s^\infty \tau \psi (\tau )^2d\tau $. Thus $\psi ^{-2}I$ is even and negative, proving the claimed parity and sign of the formula. Writing $j=\psi v$ gives

\[ (\psi ^2v')'=t\psi ^2,\qquad j''-Vj=\psi v''+2\psi 'v'=t\psi , \]

so the formula solves the correct equation with the prescribed value at zero.

For $s\geq 0$, the exponential bounds on $\psi $ give $|I(s)|\leq C(1+s)e^{-2\sqrt2s}$ and $\psi (s)^{-2}|I(s)|\leq C(1+s)$. Therefore $|v(t)|\leq C(1+t^2)$ and $|v'(t)|\leq C(1+|t|)$; parity handles the other end. These bounds give the estimates for $j$ and $j'$, and the differential equation gives the bound for $j''$. They imply $j\in H^2$ and justify its orthogonality to the even function $\psi $. The difference of two bounded solutions is a multiple of $\psi $ by Proposition 12.8.2; its zero value at the origin forces that multiple to vanish. Both boundedness and the value at zero are needed for this uniqueness statement. The linear coefficient is $V=W''(q)$, as dictated by linearization of $W'$. $\square$
