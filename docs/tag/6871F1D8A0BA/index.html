Theorem 6.8.3 (Exponential monotonicity for bounded mean curvature)6871F1D8A0BA

Theorem 6.8.3 (Exponential monotonicity for bounded mean curvature).6871F1D8A0BA Let $V$ be an $m$-varifold in $U\subset \mathbb R^ N$, $1\leq m<N$, with $\mu =\| V\| $. Suppose $\delta V(X)=-\int H\cdot X\, d\mu $ for all $X\in C_ c^1(U;\mathbb R^ N)$ and $|H|\leq \Lambda $ almost everywhere, where $\Lambda \geq 0$. If $\overline{B_ R(a)}\subset U$, then

\[ r\longmapsto e^{\Lambda r}\frac{\mu (B_ r(a))}{\omega _ m r^ m} \]

is nondecreasing for $0<r<R$. The density $\Theta ^ m(\mu ,a)$ exists, is finite and nonnegative, and $\mu (\{ a\} )=0$. This is the bounded-mean-curvature case of [Sim18], Chapter 8, (3.5), p. 241.

Proof

The function $r\mapsto \mu (B_ r(a))$ is nondecreasing and left-continuous. Therefore $f_ a$ is locally bounded and of locally bounded variation away from zero. Proposition 6.8.1 and Lemma 6.8.2 give exponential monotonicity. Its nonnegative ratios have a finite limit at zero, bounded above by any fixed-radius value. Since $e^{\Lambda r}\to 1$, that limit is the usual density after division by $\omega _ m$. The resulting small-ball bound $\mu (B_ r(a))\leq Cr^ m$ excludes atoms. $\square$
