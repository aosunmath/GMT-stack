Theorem 17.1.3 (Radial energy monotonicity)7B92D7E65AAC

Theorem 17.1.3 (Radial energy monotonicity).7B92D7E65AAC Let $m\ge 2$, let $u$ be a stationary weak harmonic map, and assume $\overline B_ R(a)\subset \Omega $. Define

\[ \Theta _ u(a,r)=r^{2-m}\frac12\int _{B_ r(a)}|Du|^2. \]

For $0<\rho <R$,

\[ \Theta _ u(a,R)-\Theta _ u(a,\rho ) =\int _{B_ R(a)\setminus B_\rho (a)}|x-a|^{2-m}|\partial _ r u|^2\, dx\ge 0, \]

where $\partial _ r u=Du(x)(x-a)/|x-a|$. In particular $\Theta _ u(a,r)$ is nondecreasing and has a finite nonnegative limit as $r\downarrow 0$. The proof derives the radial identity from the domain-variation framework of [Hel01], Theorem 3.2, rather than importing weak regularity results.

Proof

Translate $a$ to zero and write $e=|Du|^2/2$ and $E(r)=\int _{B_ r}e$. In the domain-stationarity identity use $X(x)=\eta (|x|)x$, with $\eta $ smooth and constant near zero. Since $\operatorname {tr}S=(m-2)e$ and $S(x,x)/|x|=|x|(e-|\partial _ ru|^2)$, this yields

\[ 0=\int \big ((m-2)\eta e+r\eta '(e-|\partial _ ru|^2)\big ). \]

Approximate the indicator of $[0,s]$ by such cutoffs. Polar integration and one-dimensional Lebesgue differentiation give, for almost every $s$,

\[ (m-2)E(s)=s\int _{\partial B_ s}(e-|\partial _ ru|^2). \]

Here all surface integrals are the almost-everywhere slices given by polar integration of integrable functions. Since $E'(s)=\int _{\partial B_ s}e$ almost everywhere,

\[ \frac{d}{ds}(s^{2-m}E(s))=s^{2-m}\int _{\partial B_ s}|\partial _ ru|^2. \]

Both sides are integrable on every closed radius interval away from zero, and $E$ is absolutely continuous there. Integrating and applying polar integration proves the formula for all $0<\rho <R$. Monotonicity and the finite value at $R$ give the finite limit at zero. The argument uses domain stationarity; it does not need to differentiate the weak harmonic-map equation. $\square$
