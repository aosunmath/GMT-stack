Example 16.26.1 (Endpoint jumps for the rectangle)D3422C8E82DC

Example 16.26.1 (Endpoint jumps for the rectangle).D3422C8E82DC Let $T=\llbracket [0,1]^2,e_1\wedge e_2\rrbracket $ and $f(x,y)=x$, as in Example 5.4.5. Denote by $I_ t$ the upward-oriented segment $\{ t\} \times [0,1]$. At the two endpoints,

\[ B_0^-=0,\qquad B_0^+=I_0,\qquad B_1^-=I_1,\qquad B_1^+=0. \]

Thus the lower slice jumps from the right at zero and is left continuous at one. The two jumps come entirely from boundary mass on the cutting level, since $\| T\| (\{ f=0\} )=\| T\| (\{ f=1\} )=0$.

Proof

At zero the open sublevel contains no mass of $T$ or $\partial T$, so $B_0^-=0$. The closed sublevel contains no two-dimensional mass, but the boundary restriction is the left edge with downward orientation, namely $-I_0$. Hence $B_0^+=I_0$. At one, the open restriction of $T$ is all of $T$, while the boundary restriction is $\partial T-I_1$, giving $B_1^-=I_1$. The closed sublevel contains both currents completely, so $B_1^+=0$. These direct computations agree with the jump identity in Theorem 5.11.4. The interior values $B_ t^-=B_ t^+=I_ t$, $0<t<1$, were computed in Example 5.4.5. $\square$
