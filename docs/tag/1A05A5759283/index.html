Example 16.20.3 (A disk has zero classical bending energy but infinite full first-variation energy)1A05A5759283

Example 16.20.3 (A disk has zero classical bending energy but infinite full first-variation energy).1A05A5759283 Let $V$ be the multiplicity-one varifold of a closed planar disk $D_ R\subset \mathbb R^3$, considered in all of $\mathbb R^3$. Its smooth interior mean curvature is zero, but $\mathcal E_{\mathbb R^3}(V)=+\infty $. The singular boundary first variation is essential.

Proof

The divergence theorem gives $\delta V(X)=\int _{\partial D_ R}X\cdot \eta \, ds$. Choose smooth compactly supported ambient fields $X_\varepsilon $ that equal the outward radial unit vector on the boundary circle and are supported, on the disk, in an annulus of width at most $\varepsilon $, with uniformly bounded magnitude. Such fields come from smooth radial cutoffs near $r=R$ and a cutoff in the normal coordinate. Then

\[ \delta V(X_\varepsilon )=2\pi R,\qquad b_\varepsilon =\int |X_\varepsilon |^2d\| V\| =O(\varepsilon ). \]

The energy definition tested with $tX_\varepsilon $ gives a supremum over $t$ equal to $(2\pi R)^2/(4b_\varepsilon )$, which tends to infinity. If instead one works in an ambient open set not meeting the boundary circle, its first variation is zero there. The ambient domain is therefore part of the assertion. $\square$
