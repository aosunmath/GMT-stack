Lemma 4.2.3 (Mollification and the variation bound)33DD11902347

Lemma 4.2.3 (Mollification and the variation bound).33DD11902347 Let $u\in BV(\mathbb R^ n)$ and let $\rho \in C_ c^\infty (B_1)$ be nonnegative with integral one. Put $\rho _\varepsilon (x)=\varepsilon ^{-n}\rho (x/\varepsilon )$ and $u_\varepsilon =\rho _\varepsilon *u$. Then $u_\varepsilon \to u$ in $L^1(\mathbb R^ n)$ and

\[ Du_\varepsilon (x)=\int \rho _\varepsilon (x-y)\, dDu(y), \qquad |Du_\varepsilon (x)|\leq \int \rho _\varepsilon (x-y)\, d|Du|(y). \]

In particular, $\int |Du_\varepsilon |\leq |Du|(\mathbb R^ n)$, and for a Borel set $A$, $\int _ A|Du_\varepsilon |\leq |Du|(A_\varepsilon )$, where $A_\varepsilon =\{ y:\operatorname {dist}(y,A)<\varepsilon \} $. See [Sim18], Chapter 2, Lemma 2.5.

Proof

Convolution is an approximate identity on $L^1$, giving the first convergence. Distributional differentiation commutes with convolution: test against a compactly supported smooth function, use Fubini, and then apply the defining integration-by-parts identity for $Du$. The triangle inequality for integration against a vector measure gives the pointwise bound. Integrate it and use Fubini again. The kernel has total integral one, and it vanishes when $|x-y|\geq \varepsilon $, which proves the two integral bounds. $\square$
