Theorem 6.3.6 (Existence and structure of tangent cones)4897B19C7602

Theorem 6.3.6 (Existence and structure of tangent cones).4897B19C7602 Let $a\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| $. For every sequence $r_ j\downarrow 0$, a subsequence of $V_{a,r_ j}$ converges locally as varifolds on $\mathbb R^{n+k}$ to a nonzero stationary integral $n$-varifold $C$. The limit is a cone:

\[ (\eta _{0,s})_\# C=C\quad (s>0),\qquad \| C\| (B_ R(0))=\omega _ n R^ n\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)\quad (R>0). \]

Such limits are called tangent cones; their set is denoted $\operatorname {Tan}(V,a)$. No uniqueness is asserted. Sources: [Minter], Section 1, Lemmas 1.1–1.2 and Definition 1.3. The compactness input remains external; the conical-structure deduction follows below.

Proof

Choose $r_0>0$ with $\overline{B_{r_0}(a)}\subset U$. For fixed $R$ and all sufficiently large $j$, scaling and stationary monotonicity give

\[ \| V_{a,r_ j}\| (B_ R)=r_ j^{-n}\| V\| (B_{r_ jR}(a)) \leq R^ n r_0^{-n}\| V\| (B_{r_0}(a)). \]

The dilates are integral and stationary by Proposition 6.3.2; hence their first variations vanish on every fixed compact set for large $j$. Allard compactness, Theorem 6.2.1, on an exhaustion and a diagonal subsequence give a stationary integral limit $C$ on all of $\mathbb R^{n+k}$. Stationarity also follows directly from Lemma 6.7.1.

At any radius $R$ with $\| C\| (\partial B_ R)=0$, weak convergence and scaling imply

\[ \| C\| (B_ R)=\lim _ j r_ j^{-n}\| V\| (B_{r_ jR}(a)) =\omega _ nR^ n\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a). \]

Only countably many radii fail this continuity condition. Approximating any radius from below by continuity radii and using continuity from below of open-ball masses proves the identity for every $R>0$. Theorem 6.7.4 now proves dilation invariance, including the tangent-plane distribution. Corollary 6.3.4 gives $\Theta ^ n(\| V\| ,a)\geq 1$, so the limit is nonzero. The construction uses subsequences and supplies no uniqueness statement. $\square$
