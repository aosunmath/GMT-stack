Theorem 4.7.2 (The regulated representative of an interval BV function)6B835FA933C4

Theorem 4.7.2 (The regulated representative of an interval BV function).6B835FA933C4 For $u\in BV(I)$, with $\mu =Du$, there is a constant $c$ such that the right-continuous representative

\[ u^+(x)=c+\mu ((a,x]) \]

equals $u$ almost everywhere. It has left limits $u^-(x)=c+\mu ((a,x))$, finite endpoint traces, and

\[ u^+(x)-u^-(x)=\mu (\{ x\} ). \]

There are at most countably many nonzero jumps. Its pointwise variation, defined by the supremum of $\sum _{j=1}^ N|u^+(x_ j)-u^+(x_{j-1})|$ over finite partitions $a<x_0<\cdots <x_ N<b$, equals $|Du|(I)$. The proof below derives the interval statement directly from the distributional BV framework of [Sim18].

Proof

Subtract the cumulative function in Lemma 4.7.1. The difference has zero distributional derivative, so Lemma 4.6.2 gives the constant $c$. The one-sided limits and jump identity follow from that lemma. For every $\varepsilon >0$, at most $|\mu |(I)/\varepsilon $ points can have $|\mu (\{ x\} )|\geq \varepsilon $; a countable union gives countability.

Each increment is $\mu ((x_{j-1},x_ j])$, so the variation is at most $|\mu |(I)$. For the reverse inequality, take $\psi \in C_ c(I)$ with $|\psi |\leq 1$. Choose endpoints outside its support and subdivide their compact interval into intervals of length at most $h$. Step functions constant on these half-open intervals, zero outside, approximate $\psi $ uniformly as $h\to 0$. Their integrals against $\mu $ have absolute value at most the sum of the absolute interval masses, hence at most the pointwise variation. Passing to the limit gives $|\int \psi \, d\mu |$ bounded by that variation. The continuous-test characterization of total variation in Proposition 4.6.1, or the scalar case of its proof, gives $|\mu |(I)$ as the supremum of these integrals. This proves equality. The right-continuous representative is unique: two such representatives equal almost everywhere agree at every interior point by taking a sequence of equality points approaching from the right. $\square$
