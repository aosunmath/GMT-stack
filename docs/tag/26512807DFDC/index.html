Example 16.1.12 (Diffuse approximations to a singular continuous measure)26512807DFDC

Example 16.1.12 (Diffuse approximations to a singular continuous measure).26512807DFDC Let $C_ k$ be the level-$k$ Cantor approximation and define

\[ d\nu _ k=(3/2)^ k\mathbf1_{C_ k}\, dx. \]

Then $\nu _ k$ is an absolutely continuous probability measure and $\nu _ k\rightharpoonup \mu _ C$. Each retained interval has the same mass under $\nu _ k$ and $\mu _ C$, although their behavior inside it is different.

Proof

The total length of $C_ k$ is $(2/3)^ k$. Multiplying by $(3/2)^ k$ gives mass one, and each interval has mass $2^{-k}$. For a continuous test $\varphi $ on $[0,1]$, select one point $x_ I$ in each level-$k$ interval. The difference between either measure's integral and $\sum _ I2^{-k}\varphi (x_ I)$ is bounded by the modulus of continuity of $\varphi $ at $3^{-k}$. These two errors tend to zero, proving weak convergence. Thus concentration can produce a singular continuous limit as well as the Dirac limit in Example 16.1.2. $\square$
