Lemma 11.5.3 (Zero quadratic form and stability)758BCB624841

Lemma 11.5.3 (Zero quadratic form and stability).758BCB624841 Suppose $Q\geq 0$ on $\mathcal Z$. For $f\in \mathcal Z$, the condition $Q(f,f)=0$ is then equivalent to the constrained Jacobi condition. Without stability, the implication from zero quadratic form need not hold.

Proof

For every $g\in \mathcal Z$ and $t\in \mathbb R$,

\[ 0\leq Q(f+tg,f+tg)=2tQ(f,g)+t^2Q(g,g) \]

when $Q(f,f)=0$. A nonzero linear coefficient would make the expression negative for a sufficiently small $t$ of one sign. Thus $Q(f,g)=0$ for every $g$. The converse follows by taking $g=f$. For an indefinite form, $B((a,b),(a,b))=a^2-b^2$ has $B((1,1),(1,1))=0$ but $B((1,1),(1,0))=1$. This algebraic example explains why the stability assumption cannot be removed from the inference. $\square$
