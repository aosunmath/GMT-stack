Example 6.11.6 (The triple junction and its products)9A121EC9BA8B

Example 6.11.6 (The triple junction and its products).9A121EC9BA8B Three unit-multiplicity planar rays with angles $120$ degrees form a stationary integral $1$-cone $Y$ of density $3/2$. For $m\geq 1$, $Y\times \mathbb R^{m-1}$ is a stationary integral $m$-cone in $\mathbb R^{m+1}$. Its spine and singular set are both $\{ 0\} \times \mathbb R^{m-1}$, and its density there is $3/2$.

Proof

The three unit direction vectors sum to zero, so Proposition 6.11.2 gives stationarity. Product integration preserves stationarity: the transverse divergence integrates to zero by stationarity of $Y$, while coordinate derivatives along the Euclidean factor integrate to zero by compact support. Away from the common axis the support is a single smooth multiplicity-one half-plane interior. On the axis three half-planes meet and the support is not a smooth manifold. Their three half-ball masses give density $3/2$ there and density one elsewhere. Proposition 6.9.4 identifies the axis as the spine. All tangent cones at axis points equal this same cone, so these points lie in $\mathcal S^{m-1}$ and, for $m\geq 2$, in no lower stratum. $\square$
