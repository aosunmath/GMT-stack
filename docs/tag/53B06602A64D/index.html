Lemma 12.9.2 (Uniform decay in the normal direction)53B06602A64D

Lemma 12.9.2 (Uniform decay in the normal direction).53B06602A64D Let $w\in C^2(\mathbb R^{d+1})\cap L^\infty $ solve

\[ \Delta _ yw+w_{tt}-V(t)w=0. \]

For every $0<\sigma <\sqrt2$ there is $C_\sigma $ such that

\[ |w(y,t)|+|Dw(y,t)|+|D^2w(y,t)| \leq C_\sigma e^{-\sigma |t|} \]

for every $(y,t)$. The constants are uniform in the tangential variable $y$.

Proof

Choose $0<\delta <1$ and then $\Lambda $ so large that $V(t)>\max \{ \sigma ^2,2\delta ^2\} $ for $|t|\geq \Lambda $. On the upper half-space $t\geq \Lambda $, put

\[ B_\eta (y,t)=e^{\sigma (\Lambda -t)}+ \eta \cosh (\delta t)\sum _{i=1}^ d\cosh (\delta y_ i),\qquad \eta >0. \]

The operator $P=\Delta _ y+\partial _ t^2-V(t)$ is strictly negative on each positive summand. In particular the product summands have Laplacian $2\delta ^2$ times themselves; the coefficient is not multiplied by the number of tangential dimensions. The barrier is at least one on $t=\Lambda $ and tends to infinity along every sequence escaping to infinity in the half-space.

Let $M=\| w\| _\infty $. If $M=0$ there is nothing to prove. Each of $\pm w-MB_\eta $ is nonpositive on the finite boundary, negative outside a sufficiently large compact set, and has $P(\pm w-MB_\eta )>0$. A positive interior maximum would have $P(\pm w-MB_\eta )<0$ because $V>0$, a contradiction. Therefore $|w|\leq MB_\eta $. Send $\eta \downarrow 0$ at a fixed point to obtain $|w(y,t)|\leq Me^{\sigma (\Lambda -t)}$. The lower half-space is identical, and the remaining strip is handled by increasing the constant.

The coefficient $V$ and all its derivatives are uniformly bounded. Interior elliptic estimates on unit balls, followed by bootstrapping, bound first and second derivatives by a constant times the supremum of $|w|$ on a slightly larger ball. These estimates are the external local elliptic input used here. The proved exponential bound on that larger ball differs by at most a fixed factor, so it gives the displayed derivative bounds uniformly in $y$. This justifies the decay step requested in [ChAC], Problem 2(e). $\square$
