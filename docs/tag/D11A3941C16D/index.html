Proposition 10.5.6 (A containing ball bounds area by energy)D11A3941C16D

Proposition 10.5.6 (A containing ball bounds area by energy).D11A3941C16D If $R>0$ and the image lies in $\overline B_ R(y)$, then $\mu (M)\leq R^2\mathcal W(F)$. Equality holds exactly when the immersion lies in $\partial B_ R(y)$ and is minimal as a surface of that sphere.

Proof

Use the vector field $F-y$ in the closed-surface first variation. Its tangential divergence is two, so

\[ 2\mu (M)=-\int _ M(F-y)\cdot \vec H \leq R\sqrt{\mu (M)}\left(\int _ M|\vec H|^2\right)^{1/2}. \]

This gives the inequality. Equality in the two estimates requires $|F-y|=R$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere, and $\vec H$ to be a fixed negative scalar multiple of $F-y$. The first-variation integral identifies that scalar as $2/R^2$. For a surface in a radius-$R$ sphere, the Euclidean and spherical mean curvature vectors satisfy $\vec H_{\mathbb R^ N}=\vec H_{S^{N-1}(R)}-2(F-y)/R^2$. Therefore equality is equivalent to spherical minimality. Conversely that identity and $|F-y|=R$ give $\mathcal W=\mu (M)/R^2$ directly. $\square$
