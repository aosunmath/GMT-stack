Lemma 5.12.3 (Continuity under uniform perturbation of the slicing function)945B4DB69E85

Lemma 5.12.3 (Continuity under uniform perturbation of the slicing function).945B4DB69E85 Let $T$ be compactly supported and normal, and suppose $f_ j,f$ are continuous near its support and $f_ j\to f$ uniformly there. If

\[ (\| T\| +\| \partial T\| )(\{ f=t\} )=0, \]

then their lower distributional slices satisfy

\[ \mathcal F_{\mathbb R}(B_ t^{f_ j}-B_ t^ f)\longrightarrow 0. \]

The lower slices are defined by their restrictions even when they have infinite mass. This justifies the approximation in the final paragraph of [Sim18], Chapter 6, Section 4.

Proof

Let $\delta _ j=\sup _{\operatorname {spt}T}|f_ j-f|$. The symmetric difference of the two strict sublevels is contained in $\{ |f-t|\leq \delta _ j\} $ on the current support. Subtraction of the defining formulas gives a flat decomposition with filling $T\llcorner (\chi _{\{ f_ j<t\} }-\chi _{\{ f<t\} })$ and residual the negative analogous restriction of $\partial T$. Their mass sum is at most

\[ (\| T\| +\| \partial T\| )(\{ |f-t|\leq \delta _ j\} ). \]

This tends to zero by finiteness of the measure and the assumed zero mass on the level. The conclusion also holds when some $\delta _ j=0$, by equality of the restrictions. Uniform approximation is used with respect to the current's support, rather than almost everywhere in ambient volume. $\square$
