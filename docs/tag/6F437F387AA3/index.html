Example 16.9.1 (The radial projection is a singular stationary map)6F437F387AA3

Example 16.9.1 (The radial projection is a singular stationary map).6F437F387AA3 For $m\ge 3$, the map $u(x)=x/|x|$ on $\mathbb R^ m\setminus \{ 0\} $, with any value assigned at zero, belongs to $W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ m;S^{m-1})$ and is a stationary weak harmonic map. It has

\[ |Du|^2=\frac{m-1}{|x|^2},\qquad \Theta _ u(0,r)=\frac{(m-1)|S^{m-1}|}{2(m-2)}. \]

It has no continuous representative at the origin. Stationarity here does not assert energy minimization or stability.

Proof

For $r=|x|>0$, $Du=r^{-1}(I-u\otimes u)$, so its squared norm is $(m-1)r^{-2}$, its radial derivative is zero, and $\Delta u=-(m-1)r^{-2}u$. The energy is locally integrable precisely when $m>2$. Integration by parts for first derivatives on a punctured ball has an inner boundary term bounded by $C\varepsilon ^{m-1}$, which tends to zero; thus the displayed classical derivative is the weak derivative across zero.

For the weak equation, integrate against a compactly supported smooth vector test outside $B_\varepsilon $. The normal derivative of $u$ on the inner sphere is zero, so the Laplacian integration has no inner boundary term. The integrable quantities pass to the limit and give the weak equation. For domain stationarity, the stress tensor is divergence-free away from zero. On a sphere its radial flux is $S\nu =e\nu $, with $e=(m-1)/(2\varepsilon ^2)$. In the boundary integral against $X$, the constant term $X(0)$ integrates to zero by symmetry. Since $X(x)-X(0)=O(\varepsilon )$, the remaining flux is $O(\varepsilon ^{m-2})\to 0$. This proves stationarity across zero. Polar integration gives $E(B_ r)=(m-1)|S^{m-1}|r^{m-2}/(2(m-2))$, proving the density. Distinct radial limits rule out a continuous representative. $\square$
