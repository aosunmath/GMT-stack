Example 16.14.2 (A diffuse finite-mass two-current)4B74DFD48D85

Example 16.14.2 (A diffuse finite-mass two-current).4B74DFD48D85 Let $Q=[0,1]^4$ and define a two-current on $\mathbb R^4$ by

\[ T(\omega )=\int _ Q\bigl (\omega _{12}(x)+\omega _{34}(x)\bigr )\, dx. \]

Then $\| T\| =2\mathcal L^4\llcorner Q$, its polar bivector is $\zeta /2$, and $\mathbf M(T)=2$. Its support is $Q$, and its mass measure is non-atomic.

Proof

The bound $|\langle \omega ,\zeta \rangle |\leq 2\| \omega \| _*$ shows that the formula is a current and bounds its mass by $2\mathcal L^4\llcorner Q$. For any open $W$ and any smooth scalar cutoff $0\leq g\leq 1$ compactly supported in $W$, the form $g\omega _0$ has comass at most one and $T(g\omega _0)=2\int _ Qg\, dx$. Taking the supremum of these cutoffs yields the reverse open-set mass bound. This identifies the measure and the polar field. Every ball about a point of $Q$ meets $Q$ in positive four-dimensional measure, while tests outside $Q$ vanish; hence the support is $Q$. Singletons have zero Lebesgue measure. $\square$
