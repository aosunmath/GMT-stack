Proposition 16.18.4 (Product tori in the unit three-sphere)F65993A50A9F

Proposition 16.18.4 (Product tori in the unit three-sphere).F65993A50A9F For $a,b>0$ with $a^2+b^2=1$, the product torus

\[ F(\theta ,\varphi )=(a\cos \theta ,a\sin \theta ,b\cos \varphi ,b\sin \varphi ) \]

has area $4\pi ^2ab$, intrinsic Gauss curvature zero, and spherical principal curvatures $b/a$ and $-a/b$. Its spherical Willmore energy is $\pi ^2/(ab)\geq 2\pi ^2$, with equality exactly at $a=b=1/\sqrt2$. Within this family, only that Clifford torus is a Willmore critical surface.

Proof

The product metric is $a^2d\theta ^2+b^2d\varphi ^2$, flat with the claimed area. The field $\nu =(b\cos \theta ,b\sin \theta ,-a\cos \varphi ,-a\sin \varphi )$ is a unit vector tangent to $S^3$ and normal to the torus. Its derivatives in the two product directions give $b/a$ and $-a/b$. Thus $H=(b^2-a^2)/(ab)$ is constant, and

\[ 1+\frac{H^2}{4}=\frac{4a^2b^2+(b^2-a^2)^2}{4a^2b^2} =\frac1{4a^2b^2}. \]

Multiplication by area gives the energy. The inequality $ab\leq (a^2+b^2)/2=1/2$ proves its minimum. The trace-free curvature is nonzero everywhere, because the two principal curvatures have opposite signs. Hence the equation of Theorem 10.3.6 reduces to $H=0$, or $a=b$. The sphere here has radius one; it is different from the radius-two sphere containing the Clifford self-shrinker of Example 16.15.4. $\square$
