Proposition 14.4.9 (Entropy and uniform Euclidean area growth)9867B85F190C

Proposition 14.4.9 (Entropy and uniform Euclidean area growth).9867B85F190C If $\lambda (\mu )\leq \Lambda $, then for every $x\in \mathbb R^{n+1}$ and $r>0$,

\[ \mu (B_ r(x))\leq e^{1/4}(4\pi )^{n/2}\Lambda r^ n. \]

Conversely, if $\mu (B_ r(x))\leq Kr^ n$ for every center and radius, then $\lambda (\mu )\leq C(n)K$. The uniformity in the center is part of the hypothesis.

Proof

In $B_ r(x)$ the exponential in $F_{x,r^2}$ is at least $e^{-1/4}$, proving the first inequality. Conversely fix a scale $s=r^2$. Bound the contribution of $B_ r(x)$ by $(4\pi )^{-n/2}K$. On the annulus $2^ jr\leq |y-x|<2^{j+1}r$, the exponential is at most $e^{-4^ j/4}$ and the mass is at most $K(2^{j+1}r)^ n$. Thus the whole integral is bounded by

\[ (4\pi )^{-n/2}K\left(1+\sum _{j=0}^\infty 2^{(j+1)n}e^{-4^ j/4}\right). \]

The sum converges independently of $x,r$. Compare the bounded-area-ratio class used in [WhiMCF15], Section 10.1. $\square$
