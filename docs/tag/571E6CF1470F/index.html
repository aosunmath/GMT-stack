Proposition 7.1.2 (First variation of a closed hypersurface)571E6CF1470F

Proposition 7.1.2 (First variation of a closed hypersurface).571E6CF1470F Let $\Sigma $ be smooth, compact, and without boundary. If $F_ t$ is a smooth variation with velocity $X$, then

\[ \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\operatorname {Area}(F_ t(\Sigma )) =\int _\Sigma \operatorname {div}_\Sigma X\, dA =-\int _\Sigma \langle \vec H,X\rangle \, dA. \]

For $X=f\nu $ this equals $\int _\Sigma Hf\, dA$. This is the smooth counterpart of the varifold first variation. Compare [MM], Theorems 2.3 and 2.6.

Proof

In local coordinates, differentiating the induced metric gives $\dot g_{ij}=\langle \nabla _ iX,e_ j\rangle +\langle e_ i,\nabla _ jX\rangle $. The identity $\frac{d}{dt}\sqrt{\det g}=\frac12\operatorname {tr}(g^{-1}\dot g)\sqrt{\det g}$ therefore gives $\dot{dA}=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X\, dA$. Decompose $X=X^ T+X^\perp $. Differentiating $\langle X^\perp ,e_ i\rangle =0$ along an orthonormal tangent frame yields $\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X=\operatorname {div}_\Sigma X^ T-\langle \vec H,X\rangle $. The tangential divergence integrates to zero because $\Sigma $ has no boundary. Finally substitute $\vec H=-H\nu $. $\square$
