Proposition 7.17.9 (Normal second variation at a free boundary)4E8A8B534883

Proposition 7.17.9 (Normal second variation at a free boundary).4E8A8B534883 Let $\Sigma ^ n\subset \overline B^{n+1}$ be a smooth compact two-sided embedded free-boundary minimal hypersurface, with global unit normal $\nu $. For every smooth scalar function $f$ on $\Sigma $, the second variation in normal direction $f\nu $ is

\[ Q(f,f)=\int _\Sigma (|\nabla f|^2-|A|^2f^2)-\int _{\partial \Sigma }f^2. \]

In particular $Q(1,1)<0$. The boundary is allowed to move on the sphere; this quadratic form differs from the fixed-boundary Dirichlet problem.

Proof

The field $f\nu $ is tangent to the sphere along $\partial \Sigma $, because $\eta =x$ is tangent to $\Sigma $. It extends to a smooth ambient vector field tangent to the sphere: use boundary charts in which the transverse intersection of the hypersurface and the sphere is a coordinate intersection, extend its component functions, and combine using a partition of unity. Along $\Sigma $, $D_{e_ i}(f\nu )=f_ i\nu +fAe_ i$. Therefore the squared derivative sum is $|\nabla f|^2+f^2|A|^2$, the tangential divergence is $fH=0$, and the symmetric derivative is $2fh$. Lemma 7.17.7 gives the formula. Its value at $f=1$ is strictly negative because $|\partial \Sigma |>0$. Compare [Fraser00], Section 2.1, p. 960. $\square$
