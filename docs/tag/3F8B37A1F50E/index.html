Proposition 6.7.6 (Local convergence of stationary integral supports)3F8B37A1F50E

Proposition 6.7.6 (Local convergence of stationary integral supports).3F8B37A1F50E Suppose stationary integral $m$-varifolds $V_ j$ converge to a stationary integral varifold $V$ in $U$. For every compact $K\subset U$ and $\varepsilon >0$, for all sufficiently large $j$,

\[ \operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| \cap K\subset \{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,\operatorname {spt}\| V\| )<\varepsilon \} , \]
\[ \operatorname {spt}\| V\| \cap K\subset \{ x:\operatorname {dist}(x,\operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| )<\varepsilon \} . \]

Distances are to the full supports in $U$, not their intersections with $K$; the distance to an empty support is infinite.

Proof

If the first inclusion fails, choose support points $a_ j\in K$ staying at least $\varepsilon $ from the limiting support, and pass to $a_ j\to a\in K$. Choose $r>0$ small enough that $\overline{B_{2r}(a)}\subset U$ misses that support. A nonnegative compactly supported cutoff equal to one on $B_ r(a)$ then has integrals against $\| V_ j\| $ tending to zero. But $B_{r/2}(a_ j)\subset B_ r(a)$ for large $j$, so Proposition 6.7.5 gives a fixed positive lower bound, a contradiction.

If the second inclusion fails, choose $a_ j\in \operatorname {spt}\| V\| \cap K$ tending to $a$ while $B_\varepsilon (a_ j)$ misses $\operatorname {spt}\| V_ j\| $. The relative support is closed, so $a$ belongs to it. A fixed sufficiently small ball about $a$ lies in these empty balls for large $j$. All compactly supported test integrals in that ball vanish in the limit, contradicting that $a$ is a support point. Only the first direction needs the uniform integral mass lower bound. $\square$
