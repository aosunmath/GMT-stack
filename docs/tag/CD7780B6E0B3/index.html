Theorem 13.3.11 (Compactness and nontriviality of obstacle blow-ups)CD7780B6E0B3

Theorem 13.3.11 (Compactness and nontriviality of obstacle blow-ups).CD7780B6E0B3 Let $u$ be a normalized obstacle solution and $x_0\in \Gamma $. Every sequence $r_ j\downarrow 0$ has a subsequence for which $u_{x_0,r_ j}\to u_0$ in $C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^ n)$. The limit is a nonzero global normalized obstacle solution, with $0\in \partial \{ u_0>0\} $, $u_0(0)=|Du_0(0)|=0$, and a global bound on the Lipschitz constant of its gradient. No homogeneity or classification is assumed in this compactness statement.

Proof

Choose a fixed ball about $x_0$ on which $Du$ is Lipschitz with constant $L$. Scaling gives $D^2u_{x_0,r}(x)=D^2u(x_0+rx)$ almost everywhere, so the same gradient Lipschitz constant works on every fixed ball once $r$ is small. The values and gradients vanish at zero and are consequently bounded on fixed balls. Arzela–Ascoli and a diagonal subsequence give $C^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ convergence and a $C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ limit with the bound $L$.

Each rescaling satisfies $\Delta u_{x_0,r}=\chi _{\{ u_{x_0,r}>0\} }$. Where $u_0>0$, uniform convergence makes the approximants positive locally, so $\Delta u_0=1$ there. On its contact set the Hessian vanishes almost everywhere by Lemma 13.3.2. Since the limit has a bounded weak Hessian, these two conclusions give the normalized equation on the whole space. This does not assume convergence of the characteristic functions on the limiting contact set. Nondegeneracy and convergence on every $\overline{B_ s}$ give $\sup _{B_ s}u_0\geq s^2/(2n)$. Thus the limit is nontrivial and zero remains on its free boundary. This expands the compactness discussion in [Fig18], pp. 10–11. $\square$
