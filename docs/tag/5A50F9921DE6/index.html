Lemma 3.9.3 (Restriction to a regular chart defines a tangential differential)5A50F9921DE6

Lemma 3.9.3 (Restriction to a regular chart defines a tangential differential).5A50F9921DE6 Let $F:V\subset \mathbb R^ m\to N\subset U\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be an injective regular $C^1$ chart of an embedded manifold, and let $f:U\to \mathbb R^ k$ be locally Lipschitz. At almost every chart point $a$, the derivative $D(f\circ F)(a)$ exists and defines a linear map on $T_{F(a)}N$ by

\[ L_{F(a)}(DF(a)v)=D(f\circ F)(a)v. \]

On a neighborhood where $f$ has Lipschitz constant $L$, this map has operator norm at most $L$. Changing regular $C^1$ coordinates gives the same map at points where the derivatives exist.

Proof

On relatively compact base balls $F$ has bounded derivative, so $f\circ F$ is Lipschitz. Rademacher gives its derivative almost everywhere; a countable exhaustion covers the chart. Regularity makes $DF(a)$ an isomorphism onto its tangent image, giving a well-defined linear map. For each $v$, the Lipschitz bound for $f$ gives

\[ |D(f\circ F)(a)v| =\lim _{t\to 0}\frac{|f(F(a+tv))-f(F(a))|}{|t|} \leq L|DF(a)v|. \]

The chain rule for composition with a $C^1$ coordinate diffeomorphism proves coordinate independence. Its derivative and inverse derivative are locally bounded, so null sets are preserved under such coordinate changes. The area formula, with the strictly positive chart Jacobian, identifies coordinate-null exceptional sets with $\mathcal H^ m$-null exceptional subsets of $N$. An ambient derivative of $f$ need not exist at these surface points; only derivatives of its chart restriction are used. $\square$
