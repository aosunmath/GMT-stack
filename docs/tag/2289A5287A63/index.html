Theorem 10.5.7 (Area-energy bounds for extrinsic diameter)2289A5287A63

Theorem 10.5.7 (Area-energy bounds for extrinsic diameter).2289A5287A63 If $M$ is connected and nonempty, its extrinsic diameter $D=\operatorname {diam}F(M)$ satisfies

\[ \sqrt{\frac{\mu (M)}{\mathcal W(F)}}\leq D \leq \frac{29}{2\pi }\sqrt{\mu (M)\mathcal W(F)}. \]

The upper constant is merely an explicit choice. The qualitative estimate is Lemma 1.1 of [SimW93], p. 283; the proof below also works with immersion multiplicity.

Proof

Fix a point of the image. The entire image lies in its closed radius-$D$ ball, so Proposition 10.5.6 gives the lower estimate. Write $A=\mu (M)$, $W=\mathcal W(F)>0$ and $\ell =\sqrt{A/W}$. Choose image points whose distance is $D$ and use one as center $y_0$. Connectedness makes the continuous distance function on the image attain every value in $[0,D]$. Hence for $j=0,\ldots ,\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor $ choose image points $y_ j$ with $|y_ j-y_0|=j\ell $. Distinct points have pairwise distance at least $\ell $, so their open radius-$\ell /2$ balls are disjoint.

Each $y_ j$ has at least one preimage. Apply Proposition 10.5.3 with outer radius $\ell /2$ at that center and sum. Disjointness of the preimages of these balls gives

\[ \pi \bigl (1+\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor \bigr ) \leq \frac{12A}{\ell ^2}+\frac52W=\frac{29}{2}W. \]

Since $D/\ell <1+\lfloor D/\ell \rfloor $, the upper bound follows, with a non-strict inequality as stated. This is an extrinsic diameter bound; it does not control intrinsic distances along a surface with long narrow geometry. $\square$
