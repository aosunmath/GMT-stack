Example 5.2.4 (Thin boundaries and loss of mass)2113703E7856

Example 5.2.4 (Thin boundaries and loss of mass).2113703E7856 In $\mathbb {R}^2$ let $Q_\varepsilon $ be the oriented rectangle $[0,1]\times [0,\varepsilon ]$, with $\varepsilon >0$. Then

\[ \mathcal{F}(\partial Q_\varepsilon )\leq \mathbf{M}(Q_\varepsilon )=\varepsilon , \qquad \mathbf{M}(\partial Q_\varepsilon )=2+2\varepsilon . \]

Thus $\partial Q_\varepsilon $ converges to zero in the flat norm as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$, although its mass tends to $2$. The first estimate uses $Q_\varepsilon $ as a filling and the second sums its four edge lengths. This is the example in [WhiCho14], Example 2.7, p. 6. Flat convergence does not imply convergence of mass.
