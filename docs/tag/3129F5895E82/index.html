Proposition 9.4.8 (First variation with respect to the ambient metric)3129F5895E82

Proposition 9.4.8 (First variation with respect to the ambient metric).3129F5895E82 Let $g_ t$ be a smooth family of metrics with $g_0=g$ and $\dot g_0=h$. For a fixed smooth closed immersed $n$-dimensional hypersurface $\Sigma $,

\[ \left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0\operatorname {Area}_{g_ t}(\Sigma ) =\frac12\int _\Sigma \operatorname {tr}_{T\Sigma ,g}h\, dA_ g, \qquad \left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0\operatorname {Vol}(M,g_ t) =\frac12\int _ M\operatorname {tr}_ g h\, dV_ g. \]

For $g_ t=e^{2tf}g$, these become $n\int _\Sigma f\, dA_ g$ and $(n+1)\int _ M f\, dV_ g$. The hypersurface formula is the metric derivative appearing in [MNS19], Lemma 2, p. 426.

Proof

In fixed local coordinates on $\Sigma $, let $G(t)$ be the induced metric matrix. The determinant identity gives

\[ \frac d{dt}\sqrt{\det G(t)}\Big|_0 =\frac12\sqrt{\det G(0)}\operatorname {tr}(G(0)^{-1}\dot G(0)). \]

The trace is precisely the restriction of $h$ to the tangent space, traced with its induced metric. A partition of unity and compactness permit differentiation under the integral. The same calculation in ambient coordinates proves the volume formula. For the conformal variation, $h=2fg$ has tangential trace $2nf$ and ambient trace $2(n+1)f$. The calculation is local and does not need orientability of the hypersurface. $\square$
