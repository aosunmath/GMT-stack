Proposition 10.6.1 (Two rotational catenoids with the same circular boundary)81AF723B08AA

Proposition 10.6.1 (Two rotational catenoids with the same circular boundary).81AF723B08AA Fix $R>0$ and $h>0$. A rotational catenoid of waist radius $a>0$, centered at height zero, meets the planes $z=\pm h$ in circles of radius $R$ exactly when

\[ R=a\cosh (h/a). \]

There is a unique $t_*>0$ satisfying $t_*\tanh t_*=1$. Set $q_*=t_*/\cosh t_*$. If $0<h/R<q_*$, there are exactly two such waist radii. At equality there is exactly one, and above it there is none. The smaller waist corresponds to the larger solution $t=h/a>t_*$. The same threshold parameter occurs in the free-boundary catenoid of Example 16.7.5; the present assertion counts rotational solutions for prescribed boundary circles.

Proof

The catenoid parametrization in Example 16.2.2 has radius $a\cosh v$ at height $av$, giving the equation. On putting $t=h/a$, it becomes $h/R=t/\cosh t$. The derivative of the right side is $(1-t\tanh t)/\cosh t$. The function $t\tanh t$ is strictly increasing from zero to infinity, since its derivative is $\tanh t+t/\cosh ^2t>0$. Thus the ratio increases strictly to a unique maximum and then decreases strictly to zero; its limit at zero is also zero. The number of roots follows by the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity. Since $a=h/t$, the larger root has the smaller waist. This argument counts rotational solutions and does not assert uniqueness among all surfaces spanning the two circles, or prove their stability indices. $\square$
