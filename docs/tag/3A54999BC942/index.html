Proposition 16.17.1 (A radial profile with a prescribed contact ball)3A54999BC942

Proposition 16.17.1 (A radial profile with a prescribed contact ball).3A54999BC942 Let $n\geq 2$ and $a>0$. Put $U_ a(r)=0$ for $0\leq r\leq a$ and, for $r>a$, set

\[ U_ a(r)=\int _ a^ r\frac{s^ n-a^ n}{ns^{n-1}}\, ds. \]

Then $u_ a(x)=U_ a(|x|)$ is a globally $C^{1,1}$ normalized obstacle solution on $\mathbb R^ n$, with contact set $\overline B_ a$ and positivity outside that ball. Explicitly,

\[ U_ a(r)=\begin{cases} \dfrac {r^2-a^2}{4}-\dfrac {a^2}{2}\log (r/a),& n=2,\\[4pt] \dfrac {r^2-a^2}{2n}+\dfrac {a^ n}{n(n-2)}(r^{2-n}-a^{2-n}),& n>2 \end{cases}\qquad (r>a). \]
Proof

The radial equation is $(r^{n-1}U')'=r^{n-1}$. Integrating with $U'(a)=0$ gives $U'(r)=(r^ n-a^ n)/(nr^{n-1})$, and a second integration with $U(a)=0$ gives the stated formulas. For $r>a$ the derivative is positive, so the contact set is exactly the closed ball. The tangential and radial eigenvalues of its Hessian there are

\[ \frac{U_ a'}r=\frac1n\left(1-(a/r)^ n\right),\qquad U_ a''=\frac1n+\frac{n-1}{n}(a/r)^ n. \]

They lie in $[0,1]$ and have sum, with tangential multiplicity $n-1$, equal to one. On the contact ball the gradient and Hessian vanish. Since $U_ a(a)=U_ a'(a)=0$, the gradient is continuous across the sphere and no surface measure appears in its distributional derivative. Its bounded weak Hessian makes the gradient globally Lipschitz, with bound one. The function is therefore $C^{1,1}$ and has Laplacian equal to one outside contact and zero inside, as required. It is not $C^2$ across the sphere: the radial second derivative jumps from zero to one. $\square$
