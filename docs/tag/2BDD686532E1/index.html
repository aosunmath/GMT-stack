Example 14.13.7 (Cylinders have constant ratio without being umbilic)2BDD686532E1

Example 14.13.7 (Cylinders have constant ratio without being umbilic).2BDD686532E1 For $1\leq k<n$, the shrinking round cylinder $\mathbb S^ k(R(t))\times \mathbb R^{n-k}$ with $R(t)^2=R(0)^2-2kt$ has

\[ H=\frac{k}{R(t)},\qquad |A|^2=\frac{k}{R(t)^2},\qquad r=\frac1k>\frac1n. \]

It has positive mean curvature and a constant curvature ratio, but is neither strictly convex nor compact. Thus it does not meet the hypotheses of Theorem 14.13.6.

Proof

The $k$ spherical principal curvatures are $1/R(t)$; the remaining $n-k$ principal curvatures are zero. Summing them and their squares gives the formulas. The radius equation $R'=-k/R$ is precisely the inward normal mean-curvature velocity. On each product slice the second fundamental form and $H$ are spatially parallel, so the negative square in Proposition 14.13.3 vanishes. The ratio remains $1/k$ because both numerator and denominator scale as $R^{-2}$. The zero principal curvatures explain exactly where the strict-convexity argument in Lemma 14.13.5 fails. $\square$
