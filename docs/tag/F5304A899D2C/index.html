Theorem 13.4.2 (Weiss monotonicity and its exact derivative)F5304A899D2C

Theorem 13.4.2 (Weiss monotonicity and its exact derivative).F5304A899D2C Let $u$ be a normalized obstacle solution with $0\in \Gamma $ and $\overline{B_ R}\subset U$. The function $r\mapsto W(r,u)$ is locally absolutely continuous and

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(r,u) =\frac{2}{r^{n+2}}\int _{\partial B_ r} \left(\partial _\nu u-\frac{2u}{r}\right)^2\, d\mathcal H^{n-1} \geq 0 \]

for almost every $r$. It has a finite limit $W(0+,u)$ as $r\downarrow 0$.

Proof

Write $u_ r(x)=r^{-2}u(rx)$. On a compact interval of positive radii, the $C^{1,1}$ bounds justify differentiation under the fixed-ball integrals and integration by parts for almost every radius. In particular $W(1,u_ r)$ is absolutely continuous and

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(1,u_ r) =2\int _{B_1}(1-\Delta u_ r)\partial _ ru_ r +2\int _{\partial B_1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)\partial _ ru_ r. \]

The interior integrand vanishes: where $u_ r>0$ the first factor is zero, while on contact both $u_ r$ and $Du_ r$ vanish and $\partial _ ru_ r=r^{-1}(x\cdot Du_ r-2u_ r)=0$. On $\partial B_1$ this last identity becomes $r^{-1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)$. Hence

\[ \frac{d}{dr}W(1,u_ r)=\frac2r\int _{\partial B_1}(\partial _\nu u_ r-2u_ r)^2. \]

Scaling the surface integral gives the displayed formula. Quadratic upper growth and $|Du(x)|\leq L|x|$ bound both terms in $W$ uniformly at small radii, so its monotone limit is finite. This is the computation in [Fig18], Proposition 8.2, with its regularity justification and scaling factors explicit. $\square$
