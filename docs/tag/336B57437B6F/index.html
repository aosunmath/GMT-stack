Proposition 3.14.8 (C1 agreement reduces a Lipschitz map to smooth coarea)336B57437B6F

Proposition 3.14.8 (C1 agreement reduces a Lipschitz map to smooth coarea).336B57437B6F Let $f:\mathbb R^ n\to \mathbb R^ k$ be globally Lipschitz, with $1\leq k<n$, and let $A\subset \mathbb R^ n$ be bounded and Lebesgue measurable. Then

\[ \int _ AJ_ kDf\, dx=\int _{\mathbb R^ k}\mathcal H^{n-k}(A\cap f^{-1}(y))\, dy. \]

Here $Df$ is supplied by Rademacher and the Jacobian is zero on its exceptional set. The proof uses Whitney-based $C^1$ approximation and the preceding $C^1$ formula.

Proof

For every positive integer $j$, Corollary 3.5.6 gives a $C^1$ map $g_ j$ agreeing with both $f$ and its derivative off a set of measure less than $1/j$. Let $E_ j$ be the differentiability points where both equalities hold. Their union has full measure, since its complement lies in each exceptional set and thus has measure at most $1/j$ for every $j$. Make the $E_ j$ disjoint by assigning each point to the first qualifying index. These agreement sets are measurable because $f,g_ j$ and the derivative representatives are measurable.

Apply Theorem 3.14.7 to $g_ j$ on $A\cap E_ j$. On this set $J_ kDg_ j=J_ kDf$, and its fiber under $g_ j$ is exactly its fiber under $f$. Sum the identities over $j$. Additivity of measure on the disjoint fiber pieces and monotone convergence identify the sum with the coarea identity for $A\cap \bigcup _ jE_ j$. The omitted subset has zero volume, and Lemma 3.10.3 gives zero lower-dimensional fiber measure at almost every level. It affects neither side. This proves the formula without using Euclidean coarea in the $C^1$ approximation or null-fiber steps. $\square$
