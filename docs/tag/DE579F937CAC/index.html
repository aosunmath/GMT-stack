Example 14.6.3 (A plane that suddenly disappears)DE579F937CAC

Example 14.6.3 (A plane that suddenly disappears).DE579F937CAC Let $P$ be an affine $n$-plane, let $q\geq 1$ be an integer, and fix a time $t_*$. Set $\mu _ t=q\mathcal H^ n\llcorner P$ for $t<t_*$ and $\mu _ t=0$ for $t\geq t_*$. This is an integral Brakke flow. To verify the inequality, note that $\vec H=0$ whenever the measure is nonzero. On an interval crossing $t_*$, the time integral of $\partial _ t\phi $ is

\[ q\int _ P\phi (x,t_*)\, d\mathcal H^ n-q\int _ P\phi (x,a)\, d\mathcal H^ n, \]

while the difference of the endpoint measures is just the negative second term. Nonnegativity of $\phi $ gives the required inequality. On intervals not crossing $t_*$ it is an equality. Thus the Brakke inequality alone permits disappearance even though curvature was zero. Compare [WhiMCF15], Remark 9.2.
