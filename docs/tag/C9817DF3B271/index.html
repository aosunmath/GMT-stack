Theorem 16.17.2 (All constant-height Dirichlet cases in a ball)C9817DF3B271

Theorem 16.17.2 (All constant-height Dirichlet cases in a ball).C9817DF3B271 Fix $n\geq 2$, $R>0$ and $h\geq 0$. The unique minimizer of

\[ J(v)=\int _{B_ R}\left(\tfrac 12|Dv|^2+v\right),\qquad v\geq 0,\quad v-h\in H^1_0(B_ R), \]

is as follows, with $h_*=R^2/(2n)$:

\[ u_ h(x)=\begin{cases} 0,& h=0,\\ U_ a(|x|),\quad U_ a(R)=h,\ 0<a<R,& 0<h<h_*,\\ |x|^2/(2n),& h=h_*,\\ h-h_*+|x|^2/(2n),& h>h_*. \end{cases} \]

In the middle range the contact radius $a$ is uniquely determined and decreases strictly as $h$ increases. These explicit solutions illustrate the normalized obstacle equation in [Fig18]; the proof below verifies minimality against all admissible competitors.

Proof

Existence and uniqueness follow from Theorem 13.2.1 with forcing $f=-1$. For $0<a<R$ set $g(a)=U_ a(R)$. Differentiating its integral expression, whose integrand vanishes at the moving lower endpoint, gives

\[ g'(a)=-a^{n-1}\int _ a^ R s^{1-n}\, ds<0. \]

The explicit formulas show $g(a)\to h_*$ as $a\downarrow 0$ and $g(a)\to 0$ as $a\uparrow R$. Continuity and strict monotonicity supply exactly one radius for each intermediate $h$.

Every displayed function is nonnegative, has boundary value $h$, and is $C^{1,1}$ with $\Delta u_ h=\chi _{\{ u_ h>0\} }$ almost everywhere. For a competitor $v$, integration by parts yields

\[ \int _{B_ R}Du_ h\cdot D(v-u_ h)+\int _{B_ R}(v-u_ h) =\int _{\{ u_ h=0\} \cap B_ R}v\geq 0. \]

Thus each candidate satisfies the variational inequality and is the unique minimizer. This verifies the global boundary-value problem directly; rotational symmetry alone is not used to assume away possible nonradial competitors. $\square$
