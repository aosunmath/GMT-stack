Corollary 12.8.7 (The gap at a physical interface scale)FCE16961822E

Corollary 12.8.7 (The gap at a physical interface scale).FCE16961822E For $\varepsilon >0$, if $f\in H^1(\mathbb R)$ and $\int f(t)\psi (t/\varepsilon )dt=0$, then

\[ \int \left(\varepsilon |f'|^2+\varepsilon ^{-1}V(t/\varepsilon )f^2\right)dt \geq \frac3{2\varepsilon }\int f^2dt. \]

The operator $-\varepsilon ^2d^2/dt^2+V(t/\varepsilon )$ instead has gap $3/2$, while $-d^2/dt^2+\varepsilon ^{-2}V(t/\varepsilon )$ has gap $3/(2\varepsilon ^2)$. These are different normalizations of the same one-dimensional estimate.

Proof

Use $s=t/\varepsilon $ and apply Theorem 12.8.4 to $f(\varepsilon s)$. The energy on the left becomes its unscaled quadratic form, while its squared norm is $\varepsilon ^{-1}\int f^2$. Multiplying the resulting inequality by $\varepsilon $ or by $\varepsilon ^{-1}$ gives the other two normalizations. Equality is attained by the correspondingly rescaled function $\zeta $, so the constants remain sharp. $\square$
