Lemma 14.10.2 (Uniform polynomial Gaussian tails)52A6BD047625

Lemma 14.10.2 (Uniform polynomial Gaussian tails).52A6BD047625 Suppose $\lambda (\mu )\leq \Lambda $. Fix $q\geq 0$, $A\geq 0$ and $0<a\leq b<\infty $. Then

\[ \sup _{|c|\leq A,\ a\leq t\leq b} \int _{|x|>R}(1+|x|)^ q\rho _{c,t}(x)\, d\mu (x)\longrightarrow 0 \]

as $R\to \infty $, uniformly over all such measures. More precisely, for $R\geq 2A+2$ the integral is at most

\[ C(n,a)\Lambda \sum _{j=\lfloor R\rfloor }^{\infty } (j+2)^{n+q}\exp \left(-\frac{(j-A)^2}{4b}\right). \]
Proof

Proposition 14.4.9 bounds $\mu (B_{j+1}(0))$ by $C(n)\Lambda (j+1)^ n$. In the annulus $j\leq |x|<j+1$, the polynomial factor is at most $(j+2)^ q$, $|x-c|\geq j-A$, and the prefactor is at most $(4\pi a)^{-n/2}$. Sum these bounds. The series converges, with tails tending to zero independently of the measure and parameters. The same estimate with the bounded inner ball included gives uniform finite polynomial Gaussian moments. This is the annular method of [CM12], (7.31)–(7.32). $\square$
