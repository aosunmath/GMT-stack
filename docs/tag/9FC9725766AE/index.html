Example 7.19.7 (Linear and trace-free quadratic modes)9FC9725766AE

Example 7.19.7 (Linear and trace-free quadratic modes).9FC9725766AE The degree-zero space consists of constants. The degree-one space has dimension $n+1$ and consists of $x\mapsto a\cdot x$ restricted to the sphere. Degree two consists of $x\mapsto x^ TBx$ with $B$ symmetric and $\operatorname {tr}B=0$; its dimension is $(n+1)(n+2)/2-1$. On the shrinker sphere $R=\sqrt{2n}$, the sharp inequality $Q(g)\geq [g^2]/n$ after removing constants and linear modes has equality exactly on this quadratic space. Here $[\, \cdot \, ]$ denotes Gaussian integration on the sphere, and $Q$ is the existing Gaussian quadratic form.

Proof

All degree-one polynomials are harmonic. Every real homogeneous quadratic has a unique symmetric-matrix expression, and $\Delta (x^ TBx)=2\operatorname {tr}B$, giving the stated characterization and dimension. On a sphere centered at the origin the Gaussian density is a positive constant, so weighted and ordinary orthogonality agree. At radius $\sqrt{2n}$, one has $|A|^2=1/2$ and therefore $Q(g)=[|\nabla g|^2-g^2]$. Corollary 7.19.6 with $m=2$ gives $2(n+1)/(2n)-1=1/n$, with its equality characterization. On $S^1$, these degree-two modes are the familiar $\cos (2\theta )$ and $\sin (2\theta )$, so the dimension formula and equality case also include $n=1$. $\square$
