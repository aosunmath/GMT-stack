Example 16.2.7 (Higher-dimensional catenoids fit in a finite-height slab)E86BB8933722

Example 16.2.7 (Higher-dimensional catenoids fit in a finite-height slab).E86BB8933722 Let $r(0)=a>0$, $r'(0)=0$ in Proposition 16.2.6. The even solution has upper half described by

\[ z=\int _ a^{r(z)}\frac{d\rho }{\sqrt{(\rho /a)^{2n-2}-1}}. \]

When $n=2$, its height tends to infinity and $r(z)=a\cosh (z/a)$. When $n\geq 3$, the limiting height is finite:

\[ Z_ a=a\int _1^\infty \frac{ds}{\sqrt{s^{2n-2}-1}}<\infty . \]

In every dimension the resulting hypersurface is complete and proper, with its two ends escaping horizontally; the finite height for $n\geq 3$ is not a finite intrinsic distance to an edge.

Proof

Smooth ODE existence and uniqueness apply near $(r,r')=(a,0)$ since $r>0$. The equation gives $r''>0$, so $r'>0$ for $z>0$, and uniqueness gives evenness. The constant is $c=a^{n-1}$. Solving the first integral for $r'$ and separating variables yields the formula. Near $s=1$ the integrand is bounded by a constant times $(s-1)^{-1/2}$, which is integrable. At infinity it is comparable to $s^{-(n-1)}$, giving divergence for $n=2$ and convergence for $n\geq 3$. The solution can continue as long as $r$ stays bounded, because its derivative is then bounded by the first integral. Thus its end has $r\to \infty $. Intrinsic lengths dominate changes in the radial coordinate, so each end has infinite distance. Properness follows from the same radial divergence and compactness of the spherical factor. Compare [ChMin], Exercises 14.2–14.3. $\square$
