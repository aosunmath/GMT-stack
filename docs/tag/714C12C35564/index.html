Lemma 4.9.4 (A radial flux bound without a smooth boundary)714C12C35564

Lemma 4.9.4 (A radial flux bound without a smooth boundary).714C12C35564 For any unit vector $e$ and almost every $r\in (0,R)$ with $B_ R(x)\Subset U$,

\[ \left|\int _{B_ r(x)}\nu \cdot e\, d\mu \right|\leq V'(r). \]

The corresponding bound for $U\setminus E$ has $-\nu $ in place of $\nu $ and $W'(r)$ in place of $V'(r)$. This is a distributional version of the flux estimate in [Sim18], equations (3)–(5); no boundary trace on the sphere is required.

Proof

Choose a smooth nonincreasing function $h$ on $\mathbb R$, equal to one on $(-\infty ,0]$ and zero on $[1,\infty )$, with $\int _0^1-h'(s)\, ds=1$. Put $\phi _\delta (y)=h((|y-x|-r)/\delta )$ for $0<\delta <R-r$. It is smooth, including near $x$, because it is constant there. The identity $D\chi _ E=-\nu \mu $ gives

\[ \int \phi _\delta \nu \cdot e\, d\mu =\int _ E e\cdot D\phi _\delta \, dy, \qquad \left|\int _ E e\cdot D\phi _\delta \right| \leq \int _0^1(-h'(s))V'(r+\delta s)\, ds. \]

For almost every $r$, the right side tends to $V'(r)$ by Lebesgue differentiation applied to the integrable radial derivative from Lemma 4.9.3. The exceptional radii where $\mu (\partial B_ r(x))>0$ are at most countable, since these spheres are disjoint and $\mu $ is finite on each smaller compact ball. At every remaining radius, dominated convergence gives convergence of the left integral to its open-ball integral. Taking absolute values proves the result. The complement satisfies $D\chi _{U\setminus E}=-D\chi _ E$ within $U$, so the same proof applies to it. $\square$
