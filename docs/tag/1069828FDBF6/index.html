Example 16.15.2 (Products of round factors and a Euclidean factor)1069828FDBF6

Example 16.15.2 (Products of round factors and a Euclidean factor).1069828FDBF6 Let $p\geq 1$, $d_1,\ldots ,d_ p\geq 1$ and $q\geq 0$ be integers, and put $m=\sum _ i d_ i$, $n=m+q$. The embedded product

\[ M=\prod _{i=1}^ p S^{d_ i}(\sqrt{2d_ i})\times \mathbb R^ q\subset \mathbb R^{n+p} \]

is a complete proper self-shrinker. Its curvature satisfies $|B|^2=p/2$ and $|\vec H|^2=m/2$. It has polynomial area growth, is compact exactly when $q=0$, and in that case is minimal in $S^{n+p-1}(\sqrt{2n})$.

Proof

For arbitrary radii $r_ i$, the mean-curvature vector has $i$th spherical block $-d_ iX_ i/r_ i^2$ and zero Euclidean block. The normal projection of $X=(X_1,\ldots ,X_ p,y)$ is $(X_1,\ldots ,X_ p,0)$. Thus the shrinker equation holds exactly when $r_ i^2=2d_ i$ for every $i$. The different radial normals are orthogonal, so

\[ |B|^2=\sum _ i\frac{d_ i}{r_ i^2}=p/2, \qquad |\vec H|^2=\sum _ i\frac{d_ i^2}{r_ i^2}=m/2. \]

A product of complete round metrics and a Euclidean metric is complete. Compactness of the spherical factors and boundedness of the Euclidean coordinate on ambient compact sets prove properness. The portion in an ambient ball is bounded by the product of the total spherical volumes and a radius-$R$ Euclidean ball volume, hence has growth at most $C(1+R)^ q$. When $q=0$, the squared ambient radius is $\sum _ i2d_ i=2n$ and Proposition 16.15.1 proves spherical minimality. Compare [CL12], Examples 5.1–5.2. $\square$
