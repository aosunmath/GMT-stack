Lemma 6.11.3 (Polar measure of a one-dimensional cone)D981C02BEF95

Lemma 6.11.3 (Polar measure of a one-dimensional cone).D981C02BEF95 If $C$ is a stationary $1$-cone on $\mathbb R^ N$ with weight $\mu $, there is a finite measure $\sigma $ on $S^{N-1}$ such that

\[ \int \phi (x)\, d\mu (x)=\int _{S^{N-1}}\int _0^\infty \phi (tv)\, dt\, d\sigma (v) \]

for every nonnegative Borel function $\phi $. Moreover $\Theta ^1(\mu ,v)=\sigma (\{ v\} )$ for every unit vector $v$.

Proof

There is no atom at zero by Corollary 6.6.4. Define $\sigma (A)=\mu (\{ tv:0<t<1,\ v\in A\} )$ for Borel $A\subset S^{N-1}$. Homogeneity gives the corresponding truncated-sector mass $r\sigma (A)$ for every $r>0$. Centered spheres have zero mass because $\mu (B_ r(0))$ is a constant times $r$. Subtracting truncated sectors proves that the mass of a polar rectangle $(a,b)\times A$ is $(b-a)\sigma (A)$. Uniqueness of measures on rectangles proves the formula.

For $0<r<1/2$, the part of the ray through $v$ inside $B_ r(v)$ has length $2r$. Conversely, if $tu\in B_ r(v)$, then $1-r<t<1+r$ and $|u-v|<2r$. Hence

\[ 2r\sigma (\{ v\} )\leq \mu (B_ r(v)) \leq 2r\sigma (\{ u:|u-v|<2r\} ). \]

Divide by $2r$ and let $r\downarrow 0$; finiteness of $\sigma $ gives the claimed density. $\square$
