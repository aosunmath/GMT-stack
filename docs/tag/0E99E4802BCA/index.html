Theorem 5.4.2 (Slicing of integral currents)0E99E4802BCA

Theorem 5.4.2 (Slicing of integral currents).0E99E4802BCA Let $T=\llbracket M,\theta ,\tau \rrbracket $ be a locally integral $m$-current in $U$, $m\geq 1$, and let $f:U\to \mathbb R$ be locally Lipschitz. For almost every $t$, the slice $\langle T,f,t\rangle $ is a locally integral $(m-1)$-current supported in $\operatorname {spt}T\cap f^{-1}(t)$; the restriction $T\llcorner \{ f<t\} $ is locally integral as well. For a Borel set $E\subset U$,

\[ \int _{\mathbb R}\| \langle T,f,t\rangle \| (E)\, dt =\int _ E|\nabla ^ M f|\, d\| T\| . \]

In particular, if $f$ is $L$-Lipschitz, this is at most $L\| T\| (E)$. On the rectifiable part of $M\cap f^{-1}(t)$ where $\nabla ^ M f\ne 0$, the slice carries multiplicity $\theta $ and orientation $\tau \mathbin {\lrcorner }(\nabla ^ M f/|\nabla ^ M f|)$, where $\langle \tau \mathbin {\lrcorner }v,\eta \rangle =\langle \tau ,v\wedge \eta \rangle $.

Sources: [Sim18], Definition 4.4 and Lemma 4.5, pp. 181–183. The proof below identifies the geometric slice with the distributional boundary in Definition 5.4.1, including a single exceptional null set for all test forms. Rectifiable coarea and the almost-everywhere fiber tangent theorem are now proved in Theorem 3.10.6. The manifold decomposition is proved in Theorem 3.5.7, with locally proved Euclidean area/coarea and Whitney extension retained as inputs. All conclusions concerning a slice are almost-everywhere in the level.

Proof

Proposition 5.10.3 constructs the geometric slices, fixes their orientations and proves the mass identity. Its dimension-one endpoint uses the separately proved curve coarea statement. Proposition 5.10.5 identifies these currents with the distributional slices outside one null set, using the Lipschitz scalar product rule and countable $C^1$ tests. Proposition 5.10.6 then proves integrality of both the restrictions and slices, including the boundary sign.

The support assertion follows locally from the distributional identity. A test supported in $\{ f<t\} $ sees the cancellation $\partial T-\partial T$; a test supported in $\{ f>t\} $ sees zero in both terms. A partition of unity extends this to tests supported off the level, and tests supported off $\operatorname {spt}T$ vanish as well. Finally $|\nabla ^ Mf|\leq L$ gives the Lipschitz mass bound. None of these steps assumes finite global mass when only local integrality is given. $\square$
