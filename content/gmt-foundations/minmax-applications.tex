\chapter{The Willmore energy and the catenoid estimate}\label{sec:willmore-catenoid}
The same general min-max machinery can be applied to very different geometric families. The energy bound and topological detection must be supplied anew for each application.

\begin{definition}[Willmore energy and normalization]\label{def:willmore-energy}
For a smooth immersed closed surface $\Sigma\subset S^3$ in the unit round sphere, set
\[
 \mathcal W(\Sigma)=\int_\Sigma(1+H_{\mathrm{av}}^2)\,dA,
 \qquad H_{\mathrm{av}}=(k_1+k_2)/2.
\]
For a surface in $\mathbb R^3$ the corresponding energy is $\int_\Sigma H_{\mathrm{av}}^2\,dA$. The subscript distinguishes averaged mean curvature from the trace convention in Definition \ref{def:smooth-minimal}. Source: \cite{MNWill}, Definition 1.2, p. 685.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The Willmore bound for embedded positive-genus surfaces]\label{thm:willmore-bound}
A smooth connected closed embedded surface $\Sigma\subset S^3$ of genus at least one satisfies $\mathcal W(\Sigma)\geq2\pi^2$. Equality holds precisely for conformal images of the Clifford torus
$S^1(1/\sqrt2)\times S^1(1/\sqrt2)$.
Source: \cite{MNWill}, Theorem A, p. 685. The proof is external. Its main bridge constructs a canonical family controlled by the Willmore energy and uses its topology to force a min-max lower bound; it is not a consequence of the Weyl asymptotic formula.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[What the catenoid estimate supplies]\label{rem:catenoid-estimate}
For a closed two-sided unstable embedded minimal surface in a three-manifold, the first negative direction for area produces nearby sheets with a quadratic area decrease. Ketover--Marques--Neves \cite{KMN}, Section 2 and Theorem 2.4, control the cost of inserting and opening connecting necks, obtaining suitable families whose areas remain strictly below twice the central area. Their logarithmic neck estimate resolves the critical two-dimensional comparison. A complete formulation of the deformation and its topology is still an external dependency here. Applications must retain the particular sweepout class; the estimate is not a general theorem that all min-max limits have multiplicity one.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[One-parameter width in positive Ricci curvature]\label{thm:ricci-one-parameter}
Let $(M^{n+1},g)$ be smooth, closed, connected, and orientable, with $2\leq n\leq6$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_g>0$. Consider the classical Almgren--Pitts width of the integral fundamental class, using one-parameter integer-cycle sweepouts based at the zero cycle. This width is realized with multiplicity one by an orientable embedded minimal hypersurface of Morse index one. Source: \cite{KMN}, Theorem 1.5; proof external. This is a fixed fundamental-class width. The source's Remark 1.6 cautions that allowing periodic families in other components of the mod-two cycle space changes the problem.
\end{theorem}

\chapter{Low widths of the round three-sphere}\label{sec:round-sphere-widths}
The unit round metric gives concrete checks on both the normalization and the possible repetitions in the volume spectrum. The following records results from the supplied 2016 thesis, without presenting them as a complete list of presently known widths.

\begin{theorem}[Nurser's low-width results]\label{thm:nurser-widths}
For the mod-two volume spectrum of the unit round three-sphere,
\[
 \omega_1=\omega_2=\omega_3=\omega_4=4\pi,\qquad
 \omega_5=\omega_6=\omega_7=2\pi^2,
\]
\[
 2\pi^2<\omega_9<8\pi,\qquad \omega_{13}\leq8\pi.
\]
Source: \cite{Nurser}, Theorem 8, p. 17. The proof uses explicit detecting families and Lusternik--Schnirelmann arguments, and is not included here. In particular, the final inequality is an upper bound, not a determination of $\omega_{13}$. No value of $\omega_8$ is asserted in this entry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{example}[The latitude family]\label{ex:latitude-area}
In $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$, the slice $\{x_4=t\}$ has area $4\pi(1-t^2)$ for $-1<t<1$: it is a Euclidean two-sphere of radius $\sqrt{1-t^2}$. The maximal slice has area $4\pi$, and the endpoint slices collapse to points. The boundaries of the corresponding sublevel regions form the basic one-parameter sweepout. This calculation alone supplies the maximal area, not the lower bound for $\omega_1$ or the detection of a higher cup power. See \cite{Nurser}, Sections 1.3.1 and 3.1--3.2.
\end{example}
