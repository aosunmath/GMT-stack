A growing, subject-based mathematical reference for geometric measure theory and variational problems, built on [Mingchen Xia’s Pome framework](https://github.com/MingchenXia/Pome). The website presents connected exposition, definitions, examples, theorem statements, and proofs with stable tags and cross-references. It is intended to develop into a Stacks-style reference for these subjects.

## Current scope and sources

The project covers geometric measure theory, minimal surfaces, min-max theory, the volume spectrum, constant mean curvature surfaces, Willmore geometry, Allen–Cahn and phase transitions, obstacle problems, and mean curvature flows. The first chapter is **Introduction**.

The latest expansions develop smooth and weak mean curvature flow, Gaussian monotonicity and entropy, BV compactness, current operations and slicing, Plateau existence, volume-constrained CMC stability, scalar-curvature consequences of stability, Bernstein’s theorem, the spectral criterion for smooth-link minimal cones, weighted and BV coarea, level-set calculus, and smooth-support varifold constancy. They follow the expanded minimal-graph, elliptic, Jacobi-field, and Allard regularity sections.

The current draft contains **978 mathematical and historical entries in 141 sections and 17 chapters**, including 755 local proofs and 42 cited theorems whose proofs remain external. The website's total of 1119 addressable entries includes the 141 section headings.

- The measure-theory foundations now prove positive Riesz representation, local compactness of Radon measures, convergence on continuity sets, tightness and probability compactness, smooth and atomic approximation, maximal estimates, and Lebesgue differentiation.
- The Examples chapter develops concentration and oscillation of measures, Cantor dimension and BV, catenoid and helicoid geometry, Enneper self-intersections, the complete bowl construction, expanding corners, exact ancient-oval limits, free-boundary spectra, CMC reconstruction, and harmonic-map energy concentration, unduloid limits, Cantor-set perimeter and rectifiability, weighted grim-reaper Jacobi fields, and diffuse normal currents.

- The analytic foundations now prove scalar Lipschitz extension and Rademacher differentiability, the Euclidean Sobolev inequalities and their endpoint counterexamples, truncated power tests, Moser iteration, bounds from every positive integrability exponent, and Sobolev boundary maximum principles.

- The minimal-surface chapter now proves Gauss--Codazzi and Simons identities, improved Kato and weak curvature inequalities, the Schoen--Simon--Yau integral estimate, volume-growth rigidity through dimension five, normalized Jacobi convergence, the separated-sheets theorem, removable stability across finite punctures, local isolation of nondegenerate normal graphs, and index persistence at smooth weak limits. Free-boundary additions include Steklov coordinates, the area/boundary identity, and instability in every positive codimension.

- The min-max chapter now proves compactness of critical sets, constructs continuous descending vector fields and a mass-decreasing pull-tight homotopy, and obtains stationary critical limits while retaining discrete interpolation and almost-minimizing regularity as external steps. Its foundations distinguish flat continuity from uniform concentration control, prove parameter-space and cup-product tests, and derive metric comparison, uniform normalized-width continuity and uniform Weyl convergence on compact metric families. Width differentiation along a smooth realizing family is conditional on that family existing.

- The CMC chapter now proves Minkowski identities, Euclidean and spherical volume-stability rigidity, constrained Jacobi equations, geodesic-sphere stability and nullity, explicit unstable modes on product tori, trace-free curvature identities, the sharp cubic inequality, and the first CMC pinching gap with product-sphere examples. Harmonic Gauss maps and Hopf differentials give sphere rigidity, while reconstruction formulas include explicit period and immersion conditions.

- The current and perimeter chapters now prove finite-mass current representation from scalar Riesz representation and norm duality, and weak compactness by countable tests. BV now has a local vector-measure representation proof, interval representatives and traces, finite-interval perimeter structure, and exact nonlinear jump and product rules. They also include calibrated mass comparison, equality and weak-limit results, a singular calibrated complex cusp, and strict Simons cone perimeter minimization for arbitrary compact perturbations.

- The varifold foundations now prove exact stationary monotonicity, tangent-cone homogeneity, spine splitting, qualitative singular stratification, one-dimensional cone classification, convergence of supports, bounded-curvature monotonicity, and the density gap as a consequence of Allard regularity. General-varifold examples distinguish weight homogeneity, rectifiability, sphere atoms, and boundary contributions.

- The Allen–Cahn branch now develops transition profiles, sharp energy bounds, BV phase compactness, perimeter lower bounds, equipartition, recovery layers, Modica gradient bounds, diffuse monotonicity, geometric stability, sliding minimization, and bounded monotone rigidity in dimensions two and three. It also proves the sharp normal spectral gap and inverse, the bounded flat kernel, local curved-layer residuals and the projected Jacobi equation, periodic-profile classification and instability, exact geometric energies, and fixed-parameter compactness using a local Sobolev compactness proof. Its Gamma limit and local-minimizer consequences explicitly depend on the external relative-boundary recovery theorem.

- The classical obstacle branch now includes existence, comparison, reaction measures, quadratic blow-ups, Weiss and Monneau monotonicity, and singular blow-up uniqueness. Deep obstacle regularity and singular-stratum manifold containment retain external proof dependencies; thin and geometric obstacles remain in development.

- The flow chapter includes evolution equations, preservation of convexity and linear pinching, the normalized curvature-ratio equation and its rigidity case, comparison, Gaussian monotonicity, entropy, rescaling, Brakke flow, weighted shrinker identities, Gaussian second variation, symmetry modes, the distinct stability behavior of spheres and cylinders, Gaussian tails and convergence, entropy maximizers, and entropy-lowering compact perturbations; translators now have weighted variation, graph calibration, ground-state identities, exact grim-reaper and plane calculations, and curvature-ratio rigidity with explicit local and global hypotheses. The self-shrinker gap theorem now has a cutoff proof and a complete-image classification, and higher-codimension examples distinguish spherical minimal submanifolds from round hyperspheres. Level-set flows and finer singularity theory remain in development.

- The harmonic-map chapter proves stress-energy conservation, radial energy monotonicity, homogeneity in the equality case, and finite-total-energy rigidity above dimension two. Weak domain stationarity is an explicit hypothesis; singular radial maps and concentrating spherical bubbles are worked out in Examples.

- Willmore geometry now includes conformal calculus, multiplicity and inversion, local area and diameter estimates, explicit torus equations and sphere second variation. Quadratic varifold energy has a dual representation and a lower-semicontinuity proof; Allard integrality and the sharp Marques–Neves theorem remain external. The catenoid section derives its Euclidean logarithmic neck cost while retaining the manifold sweepout as an external construction.

No independent human review or formal verification is claimed.

## Local preview

Requires Python 3.11+, Node.js/npm, and Git. Clone the repository, install dependencies, and build:

```sh
git clone https://github.com/aosunmath/GMT-stack.git
cd GMT-stack
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
npm ci --prefix vendor --ignore-scripts --no-audit --no-fund
.venv/bin/python scripts/build.py
python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory _site
```

Open [localhost:8000](http://localhost:8000/). To preview the already-built edition without installing build dependencies, run `python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory docs` from the repository root.

The registered local manuscript builds without fetching remote sources. Its exact input files are recorded in a SHA-256 manifest. Remote source manuscripts still require `scripts/build.py --fetch` and exact Git revisions.

## Contributing mathematical content

Propose corrections or additions through a pull request. Include precise references and explain changes to hypotheses, notation, or proof status. Preserve existing tags and source labels so citations remain stable. Read [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) and the [integration rules](documentation/integration-layout.md) before reorganizing material.

- Edit `content/gmt-foundations/` for the current original exposition. Keep `entry-review.json` and source citations in sync.
- Register additional local manuscripts with `local_path` in `books.json`, or register remote repositories; add an adapter in `adapters/`.
- Organize chapters, subjects, and canonical entries in `collection.json`.
- Add formal proof evidence to `formalizations.json` only after verification.
- Optional proof expansions, reading edits, and historical exposition must be explicitly registered in `collection.json`. No inherited registrations remain.

See [integration rules](documentation/integration-layout.md), [adapter contract](adapters/README.md), and [deployment](documentation/deployment.md).

## Publishing

GitHub Pages serves the complete `docs/` directory from `main` at **[aosunmath.github.io/GMT-stack/](https://aosunmath.github.io/GMT-stack/)**. Builds and validation run locally; there is no custom CI or scheduled content update. After an authorized update has been committed and checked, the publisher replaces the generated edition and verifies its public revision. See the [deployment guide](documentation/deployment.md).

[Ao Sun's homepage](https://aosunmath.github.io/) links to this project.

## Checks

```sh
npm ci --prefix vendor --ignore-scripts --no-audit --no-fund
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests
node --test tests/*.test.mjs
.venv/bin/python scripts/build.py --fetch
```

Tests use small synthetic mathematical fixtures to verify the renderer; those fixtures are never published as project content. Source-specific Pome integration tests have been removed with their adapters.
