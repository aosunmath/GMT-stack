## Critical limits and limitations

Applying one fixed deformation to a minimizing sequence preserves its flat-homotopy class and width. Critical output slices force the input mass drops to vanish. Compactness, positivity of the defect, and continuity then show that every new critical limit is stationary and belongs to the original critical set. Positive width therefore yields a nonzero stationary varifold of the correct mass.

An ambient-volume varifold with fixed parallel tangent plane on a flat torus is stationary but nonrectifiable. Two intersecting coordinate tori give an integral stationary varifold with crossing support. These examples isolate the missing conclusions: stationarity does not give integrality, embedded regularity, almost-minimizing behavior, multiplicity one or Morse index control.
