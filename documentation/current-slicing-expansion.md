# Current slicing and exceptional levels

This expansion develops six subject sections and supplies the boundary-identification proof for the existing integral-current slicing theorem. Its permanent tag and reader location are preserved. The source is Leon Simon's supplied 2017/2018 Introduction to Geometric Measure Theory, Chapter 6, Section 4, printed pages 180–185. The source inventory records the exact edition and file hash. Source documents are not redistributed.

## Local proof coverage

Countable test forms approximate first derivatives as well as coefficients, allowing one exceptional null set to work for all test forms. The tangential calculus develops scalar coarea on curves from the area formula, weak convergence of ambient mollifications on a rectifiable support, the chain rule and the Lipschitz current product rule. Geometric fiber orientation and signed coarea then identify a slice with the boundary created by a sublevel restriction. Applying the argument to the boundary proves integrality of both the cuts and slices.

For general normal currents, finite measurable selections of test forms give the integrated smooth slicing mass estimate. Uniform approximation of the slicing function passes to Lipschitz functions at non-atomic levels. Smooth cutoffs and scalar Lipschitz extension give the local theorem. The interval estimate uses only mass in the intervening open slab.

The lower distributional slice is left continuous in the real flat norm and its right limit is the upper slice. The jump records both current mass and boundary mass on the level. A slab bounds flat changes, and the full slice map has bounded total flat variation when the current has compact support and finite mass and boundary mass. A countable exceptional set can be dense.

Seven examples calculate rectangle endpoint jumps, a closed hemispherical cap, a dense set of jumps, the exact real flat norm of opposite point masses, circle slice cancellation, an atom with no jump, and diffuse slices of a nonrectifiable normal current.

## Remaining inputs and conventions

Euclidean area, rectifiable coarea, countable C1 decomposition and the almost-everywhere fiber tangent assertion remain external geometric inputs. The expansion does not prove Federer–Fleming compactness, the deformation theorem, or equality of real-current and integer-polyhedral flat norms. Ambient differentiability almost everywhere in volume is never used to define derivatives on a potentially volume-null rectifiable support. Normality alone does not imply rectifiability or integer multiplicity.

All scalar slices use the lower-sublevel boundary convention. Proofs retain the distinction between almost-everywhere level assertions and distributional formulas defined at every level. Local hypotheses are distinguished from compact-support hypotheses needed for global flat-norm estimates.
