## Compactness and perimeter

Quartic coercivity and well concentration give phase compactness without a uniform pointwise bound. BV compactness for the primitive and uniform integrability give global Lp convergence for p below four on any bounded open domain. The binary-phase normalization retains the factor two between the derivative of a plus/minus-one function and perimeter.

Separate statements give the liminf inequality, domination of perimeter by the limiting energy measure, and equipartition when the total energies attain the perimeter bound. A two-layer example converges to a constant phase but retains two transition costs at one point. It is not asserted to solve the Euler equation.

## Recovery and variational limits

A flat layer is computed as a measure limit with its translation kept inside the scaled argument. Tubular coordinates then give smooth-interface recovery and an explicit curvature error in the Euler equation. A diagonal argument combines that construction with the project's smooth finite-perimeter approximation to prove recovery for compactly contained sets.

For interfaces meeting a Lipschitz domain boundary, the relative recovery theorem remains an external proof dependency. The Gamma-limit and fixed-radius local-minimizer proofs identify that dependency explicitly. No boundary trace or volume constraint is imposed. The local-minimizer argument keeps a strict comparison margin and does not claim that strictness passes to the limit.
