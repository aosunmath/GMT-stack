The Euclidean convention is the trace vector `H = ΔF`, with energy one quarter of its squared norm. For a two-sided hypersurface, the scalar shape operator is `A = Dν` and the vector mean curvature is `−Hν`. In a unit sphere the energy includes area. The space-form first variation retains the ambient curvature in the averaged formulation. Unit spherical Clifford tori are distinguished from the radius-two Clifford self-shrinker used elsewhere.

## Local proofs and explicit models

The conformal argument derives the connection transformation, trace-free curvature density and intrinsic curvature-density identity. Integrating a Laplacian proves total-curvature invariance here; the argument does not invoke an unproved Gauss–Bonnet theorem. An ambient conformal map must be defined near the entire closed image. Inversion at a point of the image is treated separately, with one energy defect of `4π` per parameter sheet.

A puncture-flux calculation gives the radial square identity, the Li–Yau multiplicity bound, and the strict low-energy embedding threshold. Equality at energy `4π` is proved by varying the pole, obtaining an umbilic second fundamental form, a parallel normal mean-curvature vector and a round sphere in an affine three-plane. The proof does not assume orientability. The positive-genus corollary combines the multiplicity estimate with the externally proved Marques–Neves theorem; it is a deduction from that theorem, not a replacement for its min-max proof.

The local estimates keep annular energy and radius terms in the exact two-radius identity. They prove a corrected monotone ratio, uniform quadratic area growth from total energy, a Gaussian-area bound and extrinsic diameter bounds. Open balls at nonregular radii are handled by increasing regular radii, avoiding an assumption that every radial level has zero surface measure.

Examples compute circular torus metrics, curvatures, energy and the full Euler equation, rather than infer unrestricted criticality from optimization in the circular family. Product tori, stereographic projection and spherical latitudes provide normalization checks. At a round sphere the Willmore Hessian is the integral of the squared trace-free Hessian of the normal speed; its four-dimensional kernel is derived directly from an ambient constant-vector identity. No spherical harmonic completeness theorem is needed for that kernel proof.

The inverted catenoid has energy `8π`, but its two closing sheets are only continuously differentiable and have no twice-differentiable graph extension at their common endpoint. Finite energy alone does not justify a smooth compactification or application of a smooth closed-immersion theorem there.

## Weak energy and proof dependencies

The varifold energy is defined as a supremum of full first-variation tests minus their squared weight norm. Hilbert-space duality identifies finite energy with a globally square-integrable generalized mean curvature representing the entire first variation. The supremum formulation proves lower semicontinuity under varifold convergence. Integrality of subsequential limits still uses Allard compactness externally; the two compactness corollaries identify that input explicitly.

Collapsing spheres distinguish vanishing area from a point concentration of curvature energy. Translating spheres show loss at infinity. A planar disk has zero interior bending energy but infinite energy for its full first variation in an ambient domain containing its boundary. A sphere with noninteger real multiplicity demonstrates why the smooth multiplicity bound cannot be transferred to every nonzero varifold without hypotheses.

## What the neck calculation proves

The catenoid section counts the two rotational branches for prescribed coaxial circles, computes exact excess over two disks, and derives the leading `2π h² / log(R/h)` thin-neck cost for fixed outer radius `R`. A cylindrical connector has a larger maximum excess of `2π h²`. The cylindrical comparison has corners; its area formula is not itself a smooth sweepout construction. A sharp annulus Dirichlet-energy identity complements the existing point-capacity cutoff lemma without duplicating it.

For a positive smooth negative second-variation direction, the paired normal graphs lose area quadratically. A separate conditional proposition shows that a uniform logarithmic neck error is dominated by that gain. It assumes the comparison family and its uniform estimate. The manifold deformation, topology, continuity, smoothing errors and min-max application remain external. No unrestricted multiplicity-one claim follows from these elementary calculations.
