# Rectifiable tangents, weights and chart derivatives

This expansion adds seven sections with 46 entries and 42 new proofs, and supplies a proof of the existing tangent-plane characterization. Four guarded edits preserve existing statement labels and reader locations while updating the perimeter and tangential-differential dependency explanations.

## Proof structure and sources

The source is the supplied Simon Tsinghua/NTU edition, `Sim18`: Chapter 2, printed pages 46–48; Chapter 3, pages 85–94; and Chapter 8, pages 235–236. The source inventory records exact checked passages. The exposition is original; source PDFs and private extracts are not redistributed.

Whitney extension is isolated as a cited theorem. Rademacher, Egoroff and Lusin give compatible jets off a small exceptional set, hence scalar and vector C1 approximation. The Euclidean area formula removes critical images, and the inverse function theorem supplies countably many embedded graph pieces.

A graph has its tangent-plane measure limit by rescaling the area integral. Differentiation with respect to the measure of a union then controls the symmetric difference of a rectifiable set and a graph piece. This proves the forward tangent theorem and density one without assuming different pieces are separated. The converse uses finite nets of projection matrices, cone exclusions, uniform scale classes and coordinatewise scalar Lipschitz extensions. The original iff theorem now links these complete local arguments.

Positive weights are treated first on locally finite area measures and then on bounded-weight pieces. This extends the tangent statement to sets with infinite local unweighted area and proves independence of the positive weight. The varifold proof additionally controls the tangent-plane coordinate through matrix-field differentiation; scalar mass convergence alone is not used as a substitute.

The tangential differential is defined in regular charts. Density-one directional approximation proves agreement on common measurable parts, so the definition is independent of the chosen C1 decomposition. Ambient differentiability of a Lipschitz function at almost every ambient point is not assumed to give differentiability on a surface of zero ambient Lebesgue measure.
