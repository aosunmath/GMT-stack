## Variational foundations

The direct method proves existence and uniqueness under the project's original Sobolev-data hypotheses. Separate proofs establish a quantitative energy gap, forcing stability in the H-minus-one norm, comparison for ordered boundary/obstacle/forcing data, a nonnegative reaction measure, the equation away from contact, and the least-supersolution characterization. Subtracting the obstacle is treated explicitly: with the original energy sign, the new coefficient is minus the forcing minus the obstacle Laplacian.

## Regularity and blow-ups

The optimal interior regularity theorem is recorded under smooth-data hypotheses with its proof external. A density-point argument proves that the Hessian vanishes almost everywhere on the contact set, yielding the strong complementarity equation when the solution has the required regularity.

The normalized equation, its variational equivalence, half-parabola and quadratic models, upper growth, nondegeneracy, quadratic rescaling, and compactness of nontrivial global blow-ups are developed individually. The compactness argument identifies the equation on the limiting positivity and contact sets without assuming convergence of characteristic functions on contact. The regular--singular dichotomy is a separate external theorem for the normalized classical equation.

## Monotonicity and singular uniqueness

The Weiss derivative is derived with all scaling factors. Equality gives homogeneity, and the limit of the Weiss energy makes every blow-up homogeneous. Explicit computations distinguish the half-parabola and trace-one quadratic energies.

The Monneau distance is a spherical integral. Its derivative is written as a sum of two nonnegative quantities at a singular center. This proves uniqueness of the quadratic blow-up along all radii and continuity of its matrix across singular points. The singular-stratum definition then leads to the classical manifold-containment theorem, whose extension-theoretic proof remains external. Manifold containment is not identified with the stratum itself being a manifold.
