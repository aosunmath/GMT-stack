## Translator proofs and examples

The variation section derives the drift integration formula, second variation, translation Jacobi fields, height identities, ground-state factorization and graph stability. Intrinsic completeness and the absence of boundary justify compact exhaustion cutoffs in the infinite-weighted-area and square-integrable-kernel arguments; properness is not silently assumed. The weighted gradient gap is distinguished from the Jacobi spectral bottom, whose curvature potential changes the answer.

The conformal metric section tracks its normal-speed scaling and proves the graphical calibration. Competitors must be homologous within the graph cylinder, where the form is defined. Dirichlet uniqueness uses a vertical homotopy and remains valid for solutions smooth up to the boundary without extension as solutions beyond it. Local area comparison uses transversality and gives a mass bound; it does not assert smooth compactness in arbitrary dimensions.

The curvature-ratio section derives the tensor and scalar identities before using the strong maximum principle. A local maximum gives a local result. The complete image theorem explicitly assumes connectedness, intrinsic completeness, no boundary, two-sidedness, nowhere-zero mean curvature and attainment of a global ratio maximum. Parallel null directions and complete bounded-speed tangent flows supply the global product conclusion. No analytic continuation across the zero-mean-curvature set is included.

Worked examples compute the grim reaper's arclength, both bounded Jacobi solutions, its one-dimensional weighted square-integrable kernel, and its Schrödinger form. Strict positivity on compact tests coexists with spectral bottom zero. Grim products also have bottom zero but no weighted square-integrable null field. A tangentially translating plane has the different bottom (|v|^2/4).

## Vector representation and current examples

The current representation proof develops Hilbert functional representation and sigma-finite (L^1) duality, then applies the already proved positive Riesz theorem to a positive variation functional. Coordinate densities and a countable dense unit ball give the polar field without an unproved measurable-selection step. The finite-dimensional norm is arbitrary; for currents, forms carry comass and multivectors carry its dual mass norm. Smooth approximation and scalar cutoffs identify the measure with open-set current mass.

Weak compactness follows by diagonal extraction on countably many tests and uniform approximation on a common compact support. The boundary estimate passes through test forms directly. This statement does not establish integer rectifiability of limits.

The Examples chapter constructs a two-current distributed over a four-dimensional cube, with mass two and boundary mass at most eight. Its polar bivector is nonsimple. A separate ambient-volume argument proves nonrectifiability. Riemann sums of parallel oriented squares approximate it by rectifiable normal currents with real multiplicities, uniform mass and boundary mass, and real flat-norm error at most (5\sqrt2/j). Their noninteger coefficients explain why this does not contradict integral-current compactness.

Background inputs remain explicit: completeness of (L^1/L^2), elementary integration and finite-dimensional norm duality, smooth approximation on manifolds, the scalar strong maximum principle, ordinary differential equations and the flat-connection parallel-frame construction. Deep rectifiability, integral-current compactness and singular-flow regularity are not supplied by these calculations. No independent human review or formal verification is claimed.
