## Mathematical development

- **Radial variation and exact monotonicity:** radial divergence, the exact annular identity, density defect and absence of point atoms, equality and quantitative radial tilt, two general-varifold examples, and exclusion of compact interior support.
- **Conical structure and convergence of supports:** closure of stationarity, uniqueness of a rectifiable lift from its weight, radial tangency and homogeneous weight, dilation invariance of rectifiable stationary cones, integral mass lower bounds, and local convergence of supports.
- **Bounded curvature and corrected mass ratios:** the signed mean-curvature error, a BV integrating-factor argument, exponential monotonicity, support density bounds, and a flat-disk boundary example.

All balls are open. The exact annulus includes its inner sphere and excludes its outer sphere. A stationary general varifold formed from a circle and a diffuse family of outward rays has positive mass on the circle and a discontinuous mass ratio. Thus the proofs use continuity radii and measure derivatives, rather than assuming absolute continuity of the mass function. Rectifiability is explicitly used when homogeneous weight is lifted to equality of varifolds. Weak convergence of rescaled ball masses is first established at continuity radii. Support convergence measures distance to the full local supports, not their intersections with a chosen compact set.

The bounded-curvature statements assume the entire first variation is represented by a bounded mean-curvature field. A singular boundary measure is excluded. The flat disk demonstrates why zero interior curvature alone is insufficient.
