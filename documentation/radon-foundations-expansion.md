## Coverage and proof dependencies

The representation section constructs compact cutoffs and finite partitions of unity, proves continuity of a positive functional on each fixed support, defines its open-set mass and metric outer measure, proves Borel measurability and Radon regularity, and establishes positive Riesz representation and uniqueness. A superlevel-set approximation identifies the functional with integration without assuming the representation theorem in advance. Continuous compactly supported functions are then proved dense in Lp for finite p.

The convergence section builds countably many tests with controlled supports, extracts a positive limit functional by a diagonal argument, and completes the local Radon compactness proof. It then proves the compact-set upper bound, continuity-set convergence and the countable exceptional-radius statement, the upgrade from compact tests to bounded continuous tests under uniform tightness, and sequential compactness for tight probability measures. Smooth densities and finite atomic measures are both shown to approximate arbitrary Radon measures locally. Links to the Examples chapter explain concentration, mass escape, and changes of measure type.

The arguments take place on an open subset of Euclidean space. Countable test families justify sequential extraction; weak-star compactness alone is not used to infer sequential compactness on a general nonmetrizable dual space. Tightness is uniform over the sequence, compact sets stay inside the open domain, and the limit measure's boundary-null condition is required for convergence on a relatively compact Borel set. Mollification does not require finite total mass.

The Caratheodory construction of a measure on the measurable sets of an outer measure, elementary integration and convergence theorems, approximation by simple functions, and compactness of bounded real sequences remain stated background tools. The metric outer-measure criterion is proved here, but this expansion does not claim a complete construction of Lebesgue integration. No independent human review or formal verification is claimed.

The third section proves a fivefold covering lemma for bounded families of Euclidean balls, the weak maximal estimate for finite Radon measures, and a strong Lp estimate for p greater than one. These yield absolute-oscillation Lebesgue differentiation, the density theorem, approximate continuity, convergence of mollifiers at Lebesgue points, and zero ambient Lebesgue density for singular measures. The final example shows why the maximal operator does not map L1 into itself. Differentiation relative to arbitrary Radon measures and existence of the Lebesgue decomposition are not claimed by these proofs.
