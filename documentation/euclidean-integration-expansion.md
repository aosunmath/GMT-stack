# Euclidean area and coarea, with critical-set examples

This expansion supplies local proofs of the two previously cited Euclidean integration theorems. Thirty new entries have proofs, and two existing statements gain proofs without changing their tags or reader locations. The primary source is the supplied Simon Tsinghua/NTU edition (`Sim18`), Chapter 2, Theorem3.3 and proof, printed57–60, the diameter-cover estimate on49–51, and Theorem7.3 on76–79. Source PDFs and private extracts are not redistributed.

The area proof starts with a positive linear metric and a countable measurable partition of the full-rank differentiability set. Lipschitz bounds for a piece and its inverse compare its image measure to the Jacobian integral. Summing counts every sheet. Adding small domain coordinates gives an injective full-rank graph of a critical map; projection and a Jacobian bound make its critical image null. Rademacher handles the remaining exceptional set. The construction is independent of Whitney extension, rectifiable decomposition, tangent theorems and coarea.

The coarea proof first establishes fiber-measure measurability and a coarse slicing inequality directly from diameter covers. Full-rank C1 maps are treated in inverse coordinates. Adding small target variables makes a critical map regular, and the coarse slicing estimate controls the original critical fibers. C1 approximation then reduces Lipschitz coarea to the smooth formula. Whitney extension remains an external input to that approximation. The proof does not use a coarea theorem to justify its own critical-set or null-set steps.

The Jacobian estimates keep square roots and comparison denominators explicit. In graph regularization, the worst rank-deficient case has a linear error in the small parameter; a diagonal example verifies why a quadratic bound is unavailable. This does not change the critical-image conclusion, which only needs convergence to zero.

Twelve examples cover a nonorthogonal linear graph, repeated circle traversal, a cusp, a fat Cantor critical map and its graph, regularization rates, infinite exceptional fibers, rank-deficient projections, complex power maps, a logarithmic product density, radial powers and distance slices of an offset plane. The fat Cantor example gives a singular continuous pushforward of positive-volume critical mass under a strictly increasing Lipschitz map.

Remaining inputs include Borel regularity and normalization of Hausdorff measure, ordinary measure/calculus results, Whitney extension, and the Besicovitch/Radon–Nikodym inputs to the separate differentiation chain. No claim of a full smooth Sard theorem, Besicovitch–Federer projection theorem, independent human review, or formal verification is made.
