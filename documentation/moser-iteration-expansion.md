The argument is for scalar divergence-form equations with bounded measurable symmetric uniformly elliptic principal coefficients and no lower-order terms. Convex approximation proves that positive parts are subsolutions. Truncated powers give legitimate Sobolev test functions before local boundedness is known. An energy estimate, an external Sobolev gain, and Fatou's lemma improve integrability. Iterating on shrinking balls gives the local L2-to-supremum bound. Dimension two uses a separate finite Sobolev gain; no critical exponent n/(n-2) is used there.

Interpolation and a second scalar iteration give bounds from every positive exponent, including exponents below one without invoking a norm triangle inequality. A weak maximum principle uses an explicit H0^1 boundary condition on an arbitrary bounded open set. The large-ball estimate gives a Liouville conclusion for entire subsolutions with a finite positive-power integral and global ellipticity bounds.

The old boundedness theorem now follows by applying the subsolution estimate to both positive and negative parts.
