## Mathematical content

Gaussian calculus is developed through the drift divergence formula, compact and global integration by parts, polynomial Gaussian tails, coordinate and radial identities, first and mixed moments, the fourth moment and scale variance, and a radial rigidity statement for closed shrinkers. The global integration lemma lists the three integrability conditions; completeness is used for compact intrinsic cutoffs.

The variation section supplies compact-support first variation on noncompact surfaces, linearization of the shrinker residual, the Gaussian Hessian, translation and dilation modes, rotation Jacobi fields, closed-mode orthogonality, and fixed-center instability using radial cutoffs. The second-variation section derives the joint shape/center/scale Hessian, defines F-stability with its quantifiers, removes the geometric modes by orthogonal projection, proves sphere F-stability from the spherical harmonic spectrum, and proves closed instability when mean curvature changes sign.

A separate theorem gives a compactly supported negative direction on each nontrivial round cylinder. Its axial cutoff is radial, so the mixed mean-curvature term vanishes exactly by oddness. This avoids a nonuniform limit in the scale adjustment. Cylinders are F-unstable under this definition. Their role in entropy and generic singularity theory is a different question; no full entropy-stability or generic-flow classification is inferred here.

The centered scale is one and the center is zero. The parameter `h` in the joint Hessian is the derivative of the Gaussian time scale, not the derivative of the radius or its logarithm. Pointwise symmetry equations are not automatically eigenfunction statements on an unspecified noncompact domain. Formal symmetry on compact tests is not asserted to prove a particular self-adjoint extension or discrete spectrum.
