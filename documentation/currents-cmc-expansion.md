## Current operations and Plateau theory

The development separates locally finite-mass currents, normal currents, and integral currents. It introduces vector-measure representation, Borel restrictions, smooth cutoff boundary identities, proper pushforwards, mass bounds, Lipschitz images, and cancellation. The vector-measure representation and the Lipschitz extension retain explicit external proof dependencies.

The interval product leads to the homotopy identity and a quantitative affine-homotopy estimate. Cone fillings include their mass bound and a separate explanation of the zero-dimensional augmentation condition. A real-current flat norm is distinguished from the previously defined integer polyhedral flat norm; their infima are not identified. The test-form estimate proves nondegeneracy of both constructions in their stated categories.

The slicing section fixes the sign by restricting to a lower sublevel set. Its theorem records almost-everywhere integrality, the geometric orientation, and the exact coarea mass formula. Separate proofs explain the boundary sign, selection of a controlled cutting radius, compact-container Plateau existence, and reduction of the compactly supported Euclidean Plateau problem to a ball. Existence does not assert smoothness, connectedness, or a prescribed genus.

## CMC variations

An implicit-function argument realizes every zero-mean smooth normal speed by an exactly volume-preserving path. This gives the fixed-volume Euler equation and explains why a total-volume constraint imposes one common mean-curvature constant across components.

The Riemannian mean-curvature variation is derived from covariant-derivative commutation, independently of the second variation. It then proves the existing minimal second-variation formula and the CMC area-minus-volume formula, avoiding a circular argument. The section distinguishes strong and volume-constrained stability, compares their indices, and calculates the round sphere using the sharp Poincare inequality. The compact Laplace spectral theorem and Bochner identity remain recorded external inputs.

Further entries treat instability under nonnegative Ricci curvature, connectedness under a positive Jacobi potential, prescribed-mean-curvature energies and their extra normal-derivative term, and oriented CMC graph comparison.
