# Lipschitz current images, mass closure and singular inverses

This expansion adds 31 entries with proofs and supplies a proof of the existing locally integral Lipschitz pushforward theorem. It connects the analytic definition for normal currents to the geometric formula for oriented images, while retaining the difference between mass convergence and weak convergence. Five new subject sections are appended without changing existing tags or reader locations.

For compact normal currents, smooth vector mollification preserves the Lipschitz constant. The homotopy estimate gives convergence on tests, a mass bound, boundary commutation, and independence of approximation. Explicit weak limits of fillings and residuals give flat stability without assuming a completeness theorem for the flat norm. Composition is proved using smooth maps on arbitrary finite-mass flat competitors.

The geometric identification uses weak convergence of derivative minors on Euclidean charts and fixed integrable weights. At full-rank points, the image tangent agrees with the image of the tangential differential. The area formula sums signed integer multiplicities on overlapping sheets. The exact mass lost to cancellation is twice the smaller of the positive and negative contributions. Properness permits localization with smooth normal-current cutoffs; a separate restricted integer current proves local integer rectifiability, since smooth cutoffs themselves need not preserve integer multiplicity.

The mass-closure argument uses signed integer coefficients on a common sigma-finite rectifiable carrier. The union of carriers need not have locally finite Hausdorff measure. Its limiting nonzero multiplicity set does, which permits the tangent identification needed for an integer rectifiable limit. Both zero-dimensional currents and boundaries are covered. This proves closure for mass-Cauchy sequences; it does not prove Federer–Fleming weak compactness or the deformation theorem.

The BV section derives the derivative measure of a monotone inverse as the pushforward of interval length. It gives exact criteria for absolute continuity and a Lipschitz bound of the inverse. A fat-Cantor example has inverse derivative one almost everywhere but an additional singular continuous derivative measure. Absolutely continuous measures of fixed mass concentrate to that singular continuous measure.

The Examples section computes a folded rectangle with zero oriented image and nonzero pushed area measure, a complex power map with positive disk multiplicity, uniformly small oscillations with weakly convergent minors but no strong derivative convergence, and an injective fat-Cantor map whose current mass differs from its ordinary pushed length. A mass-convergent sequence of integral currents has an infinite-length carrier union and boundaries that converge weakly but not in mass. Further examples in the normal-current section show why finite mass alone does not determine an image from support values and why a nonproper parametrization can make a proposed integral infinite.
