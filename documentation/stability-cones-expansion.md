## Scalar curvature and topology

The traced Gauss equation fixes the scalar-curvature convention and rewrites the Jacobi potential for a minimal surface. Testing stability by the constant function proves the genus-zero conclusion and area bound in positive scalar curvature. The hypotheses include closedness, connectedness, orientability, and two-sidedness. A separate polarization argument proves the pointwise rigidity of a stable torus under nonnegative scalar curvature. Product examples explain why positive curvature and orientability cannot simply be dropped. No ambient splitting theorem is claimed.

## Logarithmic cutoffs and Bernstein

The cutoff argument is divided into independently usable steps: a nonnegative stability potential vanishes under cutoffs of small Dirichlet energy; properness and quadratic extrinsic area growth produce such cutoffs; and a divergence-free normal field gives an area bound for every entire minimal graph. Together with graphical stability these prove Bernstein's theorem in two dimensions. The proof records why the entire graph is proper and complete. The nonproper Scherk graph illustrates the scope of the result.

## Smooth links and cone stability

The cone metric and curvature formulas lead to the sharp radial Hardy inequality and the exact spectral stability criterion. The link operator is distinguished from the full Jacobi operator of a hypersurface in a sphere. Product-sphere examples exhibit the dimension-seven threshold for stability without asserting area minimization.

A local derivation of the spherical Simons identity supplies the Rayleigh bound for a nonflat smooth link. Combining it with the Hardy threshold proves flatness of stable cones with smooth closed connected embedded two-sided links in cone dimensions two through six. The statement does not cover links with additional singularities. The norm test is justified by smooth approximation at zeros of the second fundamental form.
