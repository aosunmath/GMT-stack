## Positive solutions and graph limits

The external Harnack estimate now explicitly includes bounded drift and potential terms, nonnegative solutions and an interior-ball margin. This is the version needed for Jacobi operators; the earlier homogeneous zero-lower-order estimate alone was insufficient. Harnack chains, Schauder estimates and a diagonal argument prove compactness of normalized positive solutions.

The normal-graph mean-curvature operator is linearized along the interval between two graphs, with its sign fixed by H=div(nu) and DH(0)=-J. This gives the missing local proof that globally ordered sheets produce a positive Jacobi field. Sheet-gap ratios, a positive-supersolution identity, simplicity of a closed positive Jacobi kernel, and the degeneracy forced by accumulating minimal normal graphs are proved separately. ## Removable stability

Small-radius cutoffs have energy of order epsilon^(n-2) in dimensions above two; in dimension two a logarithmic cutoff has energy of order 1/|log epsilon|. Finite products and smooth approximation give convergence of the full quadratic form. Stability therefore extends across finite punctures on an already smooth hypersurface with locally bounded Jacobi potential. The proof does not require extending the positive Jacobi field itself through the punctures.

A one-dimensional interval example shows that point removal can change stability. Ordered sheets away from finitely many points give global stability in dimensions at least two; the constant test then rules out that specific configuration for a closed two-sided limit in positive ambient Ricci curvature. Global ordering, smoothness and two-sidedness remain explicit hypotheses.

## Index and weak convergence

Finite index gives stability outside a compact set and in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood, by producing disjoint negative tests if either conclusion fails. These statements supply no curvature estimate by themselves.

Plane-Jacobian Hessians give weak continuity of every coefficient of the ambient second-variation form. Negative definiteness persists under matrix convergence, with all cross terms controlled. Extending a finite negative basis from a smooth two-sided limit to ambient fields therefore proves index lower semicontinuity for varifold convergence to a positive multiple of that limit. The limit is assumed smooth; this argument does not prove the regularity part of Sharp compactness.
