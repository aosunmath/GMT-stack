This expansion adds eight sections and 46 entries, with 44 new proofs and local proofs for the two existing blow-up and De Giorgi theorems. Signs are derived directly from this identity, including the limiting half-space on the negative side of the outward normal.

## Proof structure

Polar alignment yields radial perimeter bounds. A compactly supported BV Sobolev estimate and radial differential inequality keep both phases present at every sufficiently small scale. Compactness, vanishing tangential derivatives and the limiting normal derivative sign classify each subsequential limit. Two-phase persistence centers the limiting half-space, and a subsequence contradiction gives convergence at every vanishing scale.

Polar alignment also prevents cancellation of the vector derivative, yielding the scalar perimeter tangent and exact densities. Uniform-scale cone pieces give Lipschitz graphs. Separate Hausdorff/Radon comparisons establish local finiteness and mutual absolute continuity before differentiation identifies the measure exactly. The Gauss–Green identity then follows from distributional integration by parts.

The measure-theory section states Besicovitch selection as an external theorem and proves its symmetric Vitali consequence, a nondoubling maximal estimate, absolute-oscillation differentiation, and the local open-ball version. The remaining inputs are Besicovitch selection, abstract Radon–Nikodym representation, the existing rectifiable-set density theorem, and the standard normalization of Hausdorff measure on a plane. A local proof does not assert that these dependencies have also been proved here.

## Examples and limitations

Six worked examples treat smooth subgraphs, sectors, crossing quadrants, collapsing slabs, a quadratic cusp and a dense null modification. They separate phase density from perimeter density, vector cancellation from surviving positive mass, and the reduced boundary from a chosen topological boundary. Smooth graph rates are local model estimates; no uniform rate is asserted for arbitrary finite-perimeter sets. Sharp isoperimetry, BV traces, and quantitative regularity remain further development.
